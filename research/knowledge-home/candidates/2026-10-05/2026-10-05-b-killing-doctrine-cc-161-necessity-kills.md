## Body
Original lock (Batch 352): his no-killing stance is a deliberately costed default, never an absolute. The Scrip-Forge Raid, Dredge-Line Ambush, and Maw-9 demolition stay bloodless because bloodlessness was the better weapon. When the ratio demands death, he chooses it explicitly and records the cost. Governing precedents: the Black Trench (MCD-232), the Falling Bridge (MCD-243), the Sovereign Pier (MCD-245/MCD-1880, nine killed, three deliberately spared). His kills are cold, ledgered, calm and chosen, never rage. The urge to destroy his enemies stays reserved for the turn caused by Maro Rexmar's death at the Fulfillment Ceremony; nothing before Book 1 may read as that turn arriving early.

Amendment (Batch 355, refined Batch 356): before Maro's death every kill by Kanja's own hand is a necessity kill: the person is an active, immediate threat to life in that moment (attacking, or working the wheel, lever, or blade killing others). He never names or recites a person's crimes, asks them to confirm guilt, or pronounces a verdict then kills; he never executes a surrendering or no-longer-threatening man, deterrence included; an offered surrender is honored. Any deterrent effect is a consequence others carry away, never his stated reason. Judgment of the dead belongs to Onyx's narration afterward (the Black Ledger's Iron/Rust verdicts), never to Kanja beforehand. Batch 356 added: fleeing or disengaging counts as no threat; a brand check to decide whom to spare is allowed; terms are stated once; no announced deaths. The author's replies: "go with your recommendation on the CC-161 amendment" and "approved." A read-only audit of locked material found zero violations; the only breaches were in unlocked drafts plus one borderline beat in Chronicle IV.
