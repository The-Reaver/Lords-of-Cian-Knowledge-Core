# Bane — Profile & Game Plan

**Status:** walkthrough drafted
**Track:** Alias Chronicle
**Gate cleared:** NO — no Chronicle prose may be drafted or presented until this file reaches "game plan approved."

This file is the standing gate artifact for this character, per the Character Chronicle Launch
Protocol locked in `CLAUDE.md`. It is the single source of truth for who this character is before
any Chronicle is written or rewritten — not a summary produced after the fact.

**Note on this alias specifically:** Bane is a register/mask Kanja wears, not a distinct
psychology. This profile should ground itself in Kanja's own single underlying psychology,
expressed through this alias's own narrative register (Rebellion-era, ages 19-30; fear-as-doctrine;
tactical-terrain-physics; a reputation whose only real power is that every claim behind it is
literally true), gear-era (the complete Trinity — Mafesto, Onyx of Oblivion, Obsidian Malice —
pre-Awakening, no Density Spike), and established voice quirks (VB-060's "Already-Finished
Negotiation" presence trait, first dramatized under this alias), rather than inventing a separate
psychology for "Bane" as if he were a different person.

## 1. Rules Walkthrough

### Biography / stats

- `MCD-004` / `MCD-005` — Kanja's Rebellion (ages 18-30) is against the Sovereign Trust, the
  visible government; "the Directorate" is the informal name for the Trust's enforcement
  apparatus (used in labor-district/rebellion-era speech) that actually classifies and names
  threats like Bane; the SBD is a separate, clandestine layer beneath both, uninvolved in the
  Bane classification itself.
- `MCD-230` — The Twenty-Two Victories (ages 18-30, 12 years) break down as 10 Conventional
  Victories, 6 Unwinnable Victories (each earning a Directorate-classified alias), and 6
  Operational/labor-logistics Campaigns. Aliases in acquisition order: the Trench Monarch (18),
  **Bane (19, the Battle of the Black Trench, Unwinnable)**, the Industrial Myth (19, first
  iteration), the Blue-Collar Titan (20), the Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea (21), the Scourge
  (22), the Crow King (23), the Iron Bastard (25), the Lord of Embers (27), the Storm That Walks
  (29), and Captain (never a Directorate classification). Bane is the second alias earned and the
  first born from an "Unwinnable" Victory.
- `MCD-232` — The Battle of the Black Trench itself: facing Suppression Brigade Kethane's 2,000
  soldiers and three Titan-class enforcers, Kanja deliberately collapsed both exits of a rock
  ravine to seal himself inside with the Brigade, compressing their numerical advantage into
  uselessness; fought nine hours in chemical fog, emerged with 93 of 120 fighters against 1,400+
  Brigade losses. This is the Trinity's **first combat deployment as a complete system** — Mafesto
  had already been bonded roughly two years earlier and worn dormant, but Black Trench is the
  first time Onyx, Obsidian Malice, and Mafesto all went live together in the field. "Bane" is the
  Directorate's own classification for **a threat that destroys the force built to destroy it**.
- `MCD-400` — Dramatizes the origin of that classification language directly: a Directorate
  analyst's own difficulty formally taxonomizing the alias — no standard threat category fit him,
  and the label itself was an admission of that failure, not a confident one.
- `ARS-020` — Onyx of Oblivion, bonded age 17: a sentient Living Drakma longsword (SBD blackscribe
  origin, royal Karesian, predating Kanja's own smithing career by millennia per `MCD-023`/
  `ARS-341`), recovered from a dockside pawn shop. Powers: Cadence Ruin, Whisper of Shadows, Veil
  Piercer, Soulbound Edge, the Black Ledger.
- `ARS-030` — Obsidian Malice, Kanja's Trinity war club, forged at the Mao Volcano — Mafesto's
  discharge weapon, 3-5 second recharge after a max-output strike, capable of a much slower
  passive two-year dormant-charge accumulation (per Batch 47's reconciliation) as an alternate
  discharge mode.
- Kanja's Density Spike is **not active during the Bane era**. `CC-002` locks the Density Spike as
  post-Awakening only; `MCD-003`/`CC-006` lock the Pi-Awakening as occurring only between his
  314th and 315th birthday — nearly three centuries after the Trinity is even surrendered
  (`MCD-246`, age 30). `ARS-310` explicitly names the entire post-Trinity persona system the
  "Pre-Awakening Theatrics System." This means Bane's terror and battlefield results at age 19 are
  produced entirely by Trinity gear, Rexmar-Mar tactical instinct, and craft/reputation — not raw
  density-scaled physical power. `MCD-311` grounds the tactical side of this: the Rexmar combat
  tradition is an engineering discipline applied to violence (structural-failure-point analysis,
  not forms), biological rather than trained, which is why "an eighteen-year-old Kanja with no
  formal military training could produce results the Sovereign Trust" couldn't counter.
  `MCD-1568` extends this further: Kanja doesn't arrive to his (pre-Rebellion, unrecorded) mutual
  mentorship with Daba a blank slate — he already carries inherited, instinctive tactical mastery
  via the Rexmar-Haku convergence, which Daba's guerrilla teaching then sharpens rather than
  originates.
- `ARS-344` — The Valen Protocol begins within the Bane-active window, at age 26 (Battle of the
  Forge Quarter): Valen Sinisterblade, Kanja's Master-at-Arms ("the White Lotus"), orders him to
  begin documenting what Mafesto's armor does that his own body can't, as a hedge against its
  eventual loss — meaning by the alias's later years, Kanja is already quietly preparing for a
  Trinity-less future, though the actual replacement gear (Forge-Coat, Sovereign Eyes, etc.) is
  not built until after the surrender.
- `MCD-246` — At age 30, Kanja surrenders the entire Trinity (Mafesto, Onyx, Obsidian Malice) to
  the vault at L9 under his father's peace deal with the Sovereign Trust — a hard endpoint for any
  Bane material that uses the Trinity in combat; everything after this point belongs to the Long
  Mask era and different aliases (the Scourge, etc.), not Bane.
- `MCD-708` — The Directorate **formally retires** the "Bane" threat classification at some point
  within the Rebellion, concluding his behavior no longer fits the fear-based profile it was built
  around.
- `MCD-1120` — Directly counterpoints `MCD-708`/`MCD-710`'s retirement framing: Efa Gol asks Bane,
  after ninety operations, whether the name still fits, and his answer is that the alias isn't his
  to set down — it's a door that still reaches people who need it — and the run continues rather
  than ending. **Read together, the institutional classification can lapse while the mask itself
  keeps being worn** whenever it's still useful; see Reserved/unresolved threads for what this
  means for placing future material.

### Relationships

- **Corren Halst** and **Danne Sok** — two of Kanja's three earliest crew members (with Maret
  Vos), all freed independently before Maw-9, by the Black Trench itself (age 19) — `MCD-234`
  locks them as forming "the nucleus of his earliest crew" before the mass Maw-9 liberation added
  further recruits. Both are extremely heavily recurring across the whole Bane run (Corren Halst
  as senior lieutenant and the one person whose authority can override Bane's own — `MCD-1059`
  orders him to sleep on flat authority; `MCD-941` has Halst's publicly argued objection settled by
  a polled vote rather than Bane's command, and Halst turns out to be right; Danne Sok as
  silent-vigilance tracker/infiltration partner). **Neither has a dedicated `CC-` biographical
  dossier** the way Garren Hask, Callum Breck, Efa Gol, and Dol Maren do (Batch 48/226) — they
  exist in the ledger only via `MCD-234`'s one-line origin and the Chronicle corpus itself. Flagged
  as a real gap in the walkthrough, not a story gap.
- **Toran** (`MCD-1099`) — a new named second-tier field commander personally trained by Corren
  Halst, extending the crew's operational discipline beyond the founding circle for the first
  time. Tests whether Bane's own hard-won standing orders (the sleep-deprivation rule, `MCD-1059`)
  survive being taught secondhand. Runs his first fully unsupervised extraction in `MCD-1393`, and
  marries within the column in `MCD-1429` ("The Wedding at the Waystation"), with Bane standing as
  witness.
- **Colonel Serrin Draeth** (introduced `MCD-1104`) — Bane's only recurring (not one-scene)
  antagonist across the run: a defeated Directorate officer who returns for a personal rematch
  rather than under orders. Escalates across three entries — declined a personal fight (`MCD-1104`),
  drills himself to avoid entering Bane's presence at all to resist VB-060's trait (`MCD-1106`,
  the avoidance itself becoming the tell), and finally confronts him in person only to recognize
  the trait for himself mid-confrontation — that there was never a personal fight to win — and
  requests reassignment to a rear post rather than being killed or court-martialed (`MCD-1115`,
  closing the thread).
- **Efa Gol** (`CC-130`/`CC-131`) — recruited early, commands decoy/diversion forces repeatedly
  across the Rebellion (Iron Shallows, `MCD-233`; implied at Maw-9). Within the Bane run
  specifically: `MCD-433` establishes her as the one crew member who "still checks on the man
  rather than the reputation," and the one who confirms directly that the Bane persona is a
  deliberate, costly performance, not an involuntary transformation. Given unsupervised command of
  a raid for the first time in `MCD-684`. Discovers a coerced insider betrayal by pattern-reading
  in `MCD-1392`. Closes the ninety-operation run in `MCD-1120` by asking whether the name still
  fits.
- **Garren Hask** (`CC-115`/`CC-116`) — the crew's ledger-keeper/"counter." Within the Bane run:
  a private late-night conversation (`MCD-693`) is the alias's first direct interior/psychological
  entry, revealing Bane's undiminished emotional weight over every death and his fear of ever
  becoming numb to it. `MCD-695` and `MCD-1119`/`MCD-1117` use him as the reflective voice tallying
  the mixed combat/non-combat ledger of the era and explicitly declining to write down some costs
  at all, on principle.
- **Callum Breck** (`CC-117`-`CC-119`) — during his own four-month silent period after Nev Torr's
  death at the Black Trench, `MCD-707` has him hand Bane a hand-carved marker as wordless comfort
  after a broken promise costs lives — a gesture-only scene predating Breck's full voice recovery.
- **Maret Vos** (he/him, per the Batch 226 pronoun reconciliation) — trusted to run a solo
  interception in `MCD-938`; `MCD-1061` gives him his own dedicated reflective perspective within
  Bane's run specifically (distinct from his Trench Monarch and Captain-alias entries), on Bane
  deliberately not fixing a plan Maret Vos needed to prove to himself he didn't need fixed.
- **The unnamed Sovereign Trust Undersecretary** (`MCD-365`) — Bane's first on-page antagonist,
  one-scene, offering a House seat/name/protection in exchange for standing down; the origin scene
  for `VB-060`.
- **Orven Castellan** (`CC-148`) — a Regional Magistrate-General, image-obsessed and politically
  ambitious, who has spent two years inflating his brigade's engagement record. `MCD-1856` locks
  that he is **eventually captured and broken (not killed) by Bane** in a parley built entirely
  around `VB-060` — Bane arrives already holding evidence Castellan's victory reports are
  fabricated and states it before Castellan can begin the negotiation he came for, ending his
  career and Council ambitions without a blow struck. **This is locked as character-plus-outcome
  only — no Chronicle prose has been drafted for it; it's an explicitly queued future Bane-wave
  beat pending review**, not part of the existing 102-entry corpus.

### Abilities / gear

- **The full Trinity, deployed as a complete system for the first time at the Black Trench**:
  Mafesto (Kinetic Transfer System — redirects/absorbs momentum through grounded contact, reads
  impact through plating rather than sight), Onyx of Oblivion (five named powers, each getting a
  dedicated corpus showcase across the run — see below), and Obsidian Malice (discharge club, 3-5s
  active recharge or up to two-year passive dormant accumulation).
- `VB-060` — "Already-Finished Negotiation": Kanja's presence, under any alias, reads to a POV
  character at real stakes not as someone deciding or negotiating in real time but as someone who
  already reached his conclusion elsewhere and is only now reporting the result — a character
  trait, not a supernatural power. **First put on the page under Bane** (`MCD-365`, the
  Undersecretary), explicitly designed to recur across every alias and era. Across the Bane corpus
  specifically it has been shown at: individual-negotiation scale (`MCD-365`), a trap-defuser's own
  hesitation rather than a spoken line (`MCD-366`), collective/army scale — a third of a 900-strong
  line deserting on dread alone before contact (`MCD-367`), zero-leverage hostage-crisis scale
  (`MCD-1427`), and in reverse against genuine sincerity rather than performance/fear (`MCD-1027`,
  where Bane is the one unsettled, finding no deception to read). It has also been shown to
  **fail**: against a disciplined garrison commander who refuses to feel it (`MCD-688`), and
  against Colonel Draeth's own trained avoidance (`MCD-1106`) — established as a real, non-universal
  trait rather than an unbeatable aura.
- Bane's own signature tactical doctrine is terrain-as-weapon / ravine-and-chokepoint sealing,
  descending directly from the Black Trench: an enemy detachment spends months reverse-engineering
  it into a countermeasure against him (`MCD-366`); the run's most intense full-Trinity showcase
  restages the same ravine shape deliberately (`MCD-709`, "The Last Night the Ravine Remembered").
- The Trinity's mechanical limits were **systematically explored and documented across the corpus**,
  completing at least one failure case for all three pieces: Mafesto fails on unstable
  marsh/silt ground (`MCD-697`) and has reduced (not zero) footing reliability on wet rock
  (`MCD-1424`); Obsidian Malice suffers a premature partial discharge from ambient electrical-storm
  interference (`MCD-1097`) and is deliberately withheld near open water on a safety principle
  established at `MCD-1100`/reused at `MCD-1424`; Onyx's Whisper of Shadows has no effect on animal
  senses (nearly exposing an infiltration to a guard dog, `MCD-1094`) and can be partially
  countered by a purpose-built sound-deadened chamber (`MCD-933`); Sovereign Eyes-style visual
  enhancement (not yet built as dedicated gear in this era, but implied baseline vision) is
  stripped to nothing in a whiteout blizzard, forcing an entire ambush defense onto sound/vibration
  alone (`MCD-1391`).
- Each of Onyx's five named powers received its own dedicated corpus showcase: Cadence Ruin
  (`MCD-398`, unmaking a rehearsed ambush), Whisper of Shadows (`MCD-1094`), Veil Piercer
  (`MCD-910`, completing a deliberate trilogy with Black Ledger and Soulbound Edge), Soulbound Edge
  (`MCD-696`, detecting a coerced informant), and the Black Ledger (`MCD-690`, forensically
  exposing a false-flag atrocity).
- One entry (`MCD-1425`) stages an engagement with the Trinity **excluded from the terms before it
  begins** — formal single combat, blade only, Onyx sheathed, Mafesto and Obsidian Malice left with
  the column — isolating Kanja's raw swordsmanship (via Valen Sinisterblade's training,
  `ARS-291`/`292`) as the sole deciding factor.

### Reserved / unresolved threads

- **The Orven Castellan defeat** (`MCD-1856`) is the one explicitly queued, not-yet-written beat
  for this alias — locked character-plus-outcome, pending Abad's review before any prose is
  drafted. A natural Chronicle-I-or-early candidate, or a later-wave entry, depending on the game
  plan.
- **The retirement/continuation tension between `MCD-708`/`710` and `MCD-1120`.** `MCD-708`-`710`
  (wave 15) were originally framed as closing "the full fifteen-wave arc" with Bane's own private
  sense that the alias was ready to be set down. `MCD-1120` (wave 30) directly and deliberately
  counterpoints this, with Bane's own later answer that the mask isn't his to retire — it still
  reaches people who need it — and the run continues. This is not a contradiction needing a fix;
  it's already been reconciled in-corpus. But it constrains future placement: **new Bane material
  should be understood as filling in more episodes within the same active ages-19-30 window**
  (matching how every wave since is explicitly tagged "within the Bane window," regardless of
  production order — mirroring the Arturo-prequel and Ozmund-strand precedent of write-order not
  matching in-universe order), not as a sequel picking up after some point where Bane was
  definitively laid down. Whether a *true* final chronological endpoint for Bane (something that
  actually happens at/near age 30, paralleling how the Scourge's Long Mask has a locked final
  night) should ever be drafted is open and untouched by the corpus so far.
- **No dedicated `CC-` dossier for Corren Halst or Danne Sok** despite being two of the three
  founding crew members and extremely heavily recurring across not just Bane but nearly every
  alias. This is a walkthrough gap worth flagging to Abad — it doesn't block this alias's gate, but
  a future dedicated pass (matching Batch 48's Hask/Breck/Maren treatment) could give them the same
  depth.
- The corpus's own recurring legend-drift throughline — the false massacre rumor (`MCD-681`), the
  paid storyteller's embellishment (`MCD-692`), the folk marching song (`MCD-1108`), profit-motive
  impersonators (`MCD-1428`), and finally an unverified distant uprising invoking his name in a
  region he's never been and can't reach or correct (`MCD-1432`, deliberately left **permanently
  unresolved**) — is an open-ended thread by design, not something meant to close.
- The friction with the ideologically opposed rival partisan commander (`MCD-1095`, unnamed,
  one-scene) who refuses to coordinate with Bane's methods on principle is explicitly "never
  resolved into alliance or agreement" — open by design.

### Existing Chronicle corpus

102 Chronicles locked across 34 waves (`MCD-365`-`367`, `398`-`400`, `431`-`433`, `476`-`478`,
`528`-`530`, `681`-`710`, `909`-`911`, `933`-`941`, `1026`-`1028`, `1059`-`1061`, `1094`-`1120`,
`1391`-`1393`, `1424`-`1432`), the deepest track-record of any single alias alongside the other
ten. Read representatively across early, middle, and recent waves. What the corpus has already
established, synthesized rather than re-invented:

- **The core operating principle, stated explicitly and tested repeatedly: "the fear only works if
  it's true"** (`MCD-431`). This governs almost everything Bane does that isn't combat: he
  personally carries six wounded enemy soldiers back to their own lines rather than leave them
  (`MCD-431`); stops a young recruit from killing a surrendering scout, stating the reputation
  exists to *prevent* killing, not license it (`MCD-528`); insists on publicly correcting a false
  massacre rumor even at real recruitment cost (`MCD-681`), but later draws the actual line as
  correcting a story only when it costs real people something, not to protect his own image
  (`MCD-1098`, deliberately contrasted against `MCD-681`); publicly exposes profit-motive
  impersonators rather than executing them (`MCD-1428`); and extends the doctrine to the
  *dependents* of enemy soldiers, redirecting captured grain to a garrison town's civilian quarter
  over his own lieutenant's strategic objection (`MCD-1426`).
- **Genuine failures are dramatized without narrative rescue, repeatedly and deliberately**, not
  softened into near-misses: his own cautious extra-day-verification habit costs two prisoners
  their lives to an informant (`MCD-477`) — the same caution that keeps his reputation honest; a
  stale-intelligence misjudgment nearly costs a 40-person column (`MCD-682`); a specific,
  date-certain promise is broken by an unavoidable elsewhere-crisis, costing four lives, and he
  permanently changes how he frames future assurances rather than excusing it (`MCD-706`); his own
  amnesty/mercy doctrine gets a freed garrison captain re-arming and killing six Rebellion soldiers
  weeks later, and he does not reverse the doctrine, naming the cost as inevitable rather than a
  mistake (`MCD-1102`); a founding crew member (Danne Sok) has his own real operational failure
  attributed directly to him rather than to Bane, bad intelligence, or exhaustion (`MCD-1114`); and
  — the alias's most interior failure — eleven sleepless days into holding three operations open,
  Bane's own judgment silently slips on something he'd reflexively have caught any other night,
  caught only by Danne Sok's habit rather than suspicion (`MCD-1059`), producing a standing order he
  applies to himself for the first time.
- **The Trinity's mechanical limits were treated as real and worth dramatizing, not glossed over**
  (see Abilities/gear above) — every piece has at least one documented failure or partial-failure
  case, and several entries are built entirely around operating *without* one or more pieces (a
  formal duel with Onyx sheathed and Mafesto/Obsidian Malice left behind, `MCD-1425`; a fire fought
  with pure physical labor since no Trinity ability applies to it, `MCD-911`; captivity chosen
  deliberately as an infiltration tool, `MCD-685`).
- **A heavy, deliberate non-combat register runs throughout**: multi-day restorative labor with no
  fight at all (a five-day well/granary rebuild, `MCD-691`; a six-week whole-district roof-rebuild
  ahead of winter, `MCD-1118`); deferring all medical authority to a camp healer during a fever
  outbreak rather than directing treatment himself (`MCD-1101`); declining to rule as judge over an
  internal captive dispute and instead facilitating a community-led process (`MCD-704`); and the
  run's first purely celebratory, zero-conflict entry — standing witness at Toran's wedding
  (`MCD-1429`).
- **Institutional/legal register**, distinct from combat entirely: a neutral arbiter council
  summons him to answer fabricated atrocity claims, and he testifies disarmed, conceding real
  failures while refusing unsupported accusations (`MCD-939`); the council's mixed ruling six weeks
  later is deliberately not framed as a clean win (`MCD-1028`); and later he deliberately absents
  himself from a proceeding he could dominate by presence alone, to confirm the process is real
  rather than borrowed through him (`MCD-1112`).
- **Command is not absolute and is shown failing to be**: Corren Halst's public objection to a
  planned strike is settled by a polled vote rather than Bane's authority, and Halst turns out to
  be right (`MCD-941`); Efa Gol and later Toran are each handed genuinely unsupervised command
  (`MCD-684`, `MCD-1393`) and succeed on their own judgment.
- **Private/interior vulnerability is rare but direct when it appears**: a late-night confession to
  Garren Hask of undiminished grief and fear of becoming numb to death (`MCD-693`); Efa Gol's
  confirmation that the persona is a deliberate, costly performance, not an involuntary
  transformation (`MCD-433`); a freed child too young to know the stories telling him plainly he
  isn't scary, just tired (`MCD-478`); and Danne Sok's own kept-private memory of the boy's hands
  shaking for an hour after freeing him, before any alias existed at all (`MCD-530`) — tracing the
  "tired, not scary" theme back to its literal origin.
- **Bane's relationship to his own growing legend is itself a running thread**, culminating in two
  directly counterpointed reflections roughly fifteen waves apart: his own private sense at wave 15
  that the alias has done what it was for and is ready to be set down (`MCD-710`), versus his
  answer at wave 30 that it isn't his to set down at all — it's a door that reaches whoever needs
  it, and the run continues (`MCD-1120`). Corren Halst's own reflection frames all of Kanja's
  aliases collectively as "the same truth... offered through enough different doors" rather than
  truth split into pieces (`MCD-1119`).
- Recurring but non-load-bearing texture: mends his own clothes and rations himself last when
  supplies run short (`MCD-686`); refuses treatment for a year to preserve crew morale until a
  serious wound forces it (`MCD-701`); invented locations across the run (Kessic Overwatch,
  Threshbend depot, Drennock Bridge, the Amitane flats, the Threndale relief operation) are
  collision-checked but largely non-recurring one-off settings rather than a fixed home geography.
