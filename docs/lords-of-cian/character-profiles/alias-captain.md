## 1. Rules Walkthrough

### Biography / stats

**Underlying Kanja layer (applies to every alias, Captain included):**
- `CC-001` — Full name Kanja Haku Rexmar; "Haku" taken from the ancestor who once deposed T.D.K.
- `CC-002` / `WC-004` — Density Spike is post-Awakening, exclusive to the Rexmar and Verehimu
  bloodlines (Kanja and Ozmund respectively); a micro-second, single-strike, no-mobility-cost
  trigger, "the Falling Star effect."
- `CC-010` — Kanja's Directorate/SBD designation is SBD-001 — the institutional classification
  system Captain explicitly sits *outside of* (see below).
- `MCD-230` — Kanja's Twenty-Two Victories (ages 18-30) break down as 10 Conventional Victories,
  6 Unwinnable Victories (each earning a Directorate-classified alias — Bane, the Blue-Collar
  Titan, the Iron Bastard, the Crow King, the Storm That Walks, the Scourge among them), and 6
  Operational/labor-logistics Campaigns. Captain is not generated by this mechanism at all — it
  has no single triggering battle and no Directorate classification.
- `CC-005` / `CC-006` — At age 30, Kanja's Long Mask persona begins and runs 284 years, ending
  with the Pi-Awakening between his 314th and 315th birthdays ("Day 0" = age 314).
- `MCD-246` — At age 30, Kanja surrendered the Trinity (Mafesto, Onyx of Oblivion, Obsidian
  Malice) to the vault at L9 in the Karkosa Complex under his father's peace deal with the
  Sovereign Trust, retaining only the Talisman of Mao, the Aegis-Talisman, and the Rexmar
  Machete for the 284-year Long Mask that followed. **This is directly relevant to a real
  contradiction flagged below.**
- `MCD-293` — Kanja's resting density escalates across the series (6,000x to 12,000x by Book 5),
  distinct from his Spike ceiling; not alias-specific but the general baseline underlying every
  combat showcase in this track too.

**Captain-specific:**
- `MCD-395` — Captain is explicitly **not** a Directorate classification. It originates as
  Garren Hask's own private name for Kanja, first spoken the morning after Iron Shallows
  (Rebellion era, age 21-22, `MCD-242`'s naval campaigns) — "a title of responsibility and trust
  rather than fear," tied to Kanja's habit of personally checking every casualty-list name. The
  name spreads through the crew quietly from there.
- `MCD-397` — Callum Breck independently arrives at the same name six weeks after his own
  already-locked first post-silence utterance (`CC-119`), deliberately choosing "Captain" over
  the "Trench Monarch" alias he himself coined (`CC-118`) — reasoning that "a monarch rules over
  what happens to his people, a captain answers for it." The name reaches general crew use from
  two independent sources converging on the same conclusion.
- `VB-061` — A crew-collective quote (not attributed to Kanja himself, matching the
  institutional-vs-self-given distinction): "The Directorate names what scares them. We named the
  man who feeds the crew before he feeds himself. That name's ours. They don't get to touch it."
- **Era span.** The alias's first three waves (`MCD-395`-`397`, `MCD-428`-`430`, `MCD-461`-`463`)
  are explicitly set within the Rebellion (ages 21-22, naval campaigns/`MCD-242`). From wave 4
  onward the material's own internal dating moves to "the Rebellion's close" (`MCD-1004`) and then
  explicitly past it: `MCD-1056` is dated roughly five years after the Trinity's surrender.
  Everything from wave 20 through wave 34 (`MCD-1056`-`1522`) is peacetime/post-war, and multiple
  entries span real elapsed decades and generations (Garren Hask's grandnephew, `MCD-508`; his
  great-grandniece, `MCD-597`; the Crow King lineage's fourth generation hearing of his death (`MCD-1486`);
  Hask's own death of old age, `MCD-1422`). **A real contradiction with `MCD-246` (the Trinity
  sealed at L9 for the entire 284-year Long Mask, ages 30-314) was found and fixed across Batches
  314 and 321: a number of post-surrender entries originally described "full Trinity combat
  showcases" naming Mafesto, Obsidian Malice, and Onyx of Oblivion by name. Every one of them
  (`MCD-1058`, `MCD-1091`, `MCD-1367`, `MCD-1370`, `MCD-1374`, `MCD-1377`, `MCD-1381`, `MCD-1386`,
  `MCD-1421`, `MCD-1515`, `MCD-1518`, `MCD-1521`) has been corrected to use Kanja's actual
  Long-Mask-era kit instead (the Forge-Coat, Ironfall Boots, Ironhand Gauntlets, Sovereign Eyes,
  Smoke System, and the Rexmar Machete, `ARS-344` through `ARS-356`, plus his own trained
  swordsmanship and instinctive Rexmar-Mar tactical sense) -- no beat, outcome, or line of dialogue
  changed, only the gear performing each action. The charter's own founding timestamp was also
  loosened (from "three years old" to "near enough a decade old" at `MCD-1376`) so this kit, which
  isn't built until ages 33-50, reliably falls inside its own existence window across the whole
  later run. This era-span question is now resolved, not open.**

### Relationships

**The ensemble cast (the widest and warmest of any alias track):**
- **Garren Hask** (`CC-115`/`116`) — dock-smith, the crew's ledger-keeper, first to call Kanja by
  his bare name. Names the alias itself (`MCD-395`). Recurring across the whole run: catches
  Kanja's paternalism over withheld intelligence (`MCD-918`); keeps a private notebook about the
  institution's future (`MCD-920`), brings it to the council (`MCD-1090`); is caught quietly
  overpaying Kanja a "command differential" and corrects it into an equal-risk-equal-share policy
  (`MCD-997`); his heart fails (`MCD-1089`), stabilized by Sera; hands his ledger to a
  now-grown deckhand he never named (`MCD-1384`); his grandnephew (`MCD-508`) and later
  great-grandniece (`MCD-597`) both join the crew's third/later generations; dies of old age at
  his own ledger table (`MCD-1422`); memorialized on the Pier Nine wall he never asked for
  (`MCD-1423`), closing the mortality arc opened at `MCD-920`.
- **Callum Breck** (`CC-117`/`118`/`119`) — coined "the Trench Monarch," then deliberately
  adopts "Captain" instead (`MCD-397`). His daughter Sera (below) recurs independently. Teaches
  his own silent-signal shore-watch craft to a trainee, dramatizing its origin for the first time
  (`MCD-1378`); keeps a private annual Nev Torr remembrance, eventually inviting Kanja
  (`MCD-611`); becomes the crew's **second rotating council chair**, succeeding Corren Halst
  (`MCD-1514`), tying his acceptance explicitly back to both `MCD-397` and `CC-118`; commands
  Kanja under his own tactical authority for the first time in the sub-series (`MCD-1515`),
  directly testing the private fear his silence arc (`CC-119`) still carries.
- **Efa Gol** (`CC-130`/`131`) — the most heavily-recurring figure in the whole track (25+
  appearances). Gives the sub-series' synthesizing reflection on the name across every alias
  (`MCD-560`); finds Kanja's private grief after he holds composure all day (`MCD-609`); her own
  aging becomes concretely real, refusing retirement but accepting help (`MCD-1373`); initiates
  an unprompted, unresolved conversation with Kanja about the mortality gap between his lifespan
  and the crew's (`MCD-1387`); raises the moral distinction between the crew's own memorial wall
  and an enemy's (`MCD-1522`).
- **Pell Ostra** (`CC-132`/`133`) — pinned under a collapsing structure; the Trinity's full
  resources spent on rescuing her alone rather than a battle (`MCD-429`); spends eleven months
  deliberately corrupting Kanja's own read on her demolition timing to land a bilge-water prank —
  the run's first pure comedy entry (`MCD-996`).
- **Corren Halst** — proven as an independent commander (`MCD-608`); proposed as a future
  council-chair successor and asks to be asked again in five years (`MCD-1090`); on schedule,
  argues the succession question itself was wrong and proposes a **rotating, three-year
  term-limited council-chair seat with Kanja explicitly barred from ever holding it** (`MCD-1379`
  /`MCD-1380`); becomes its first holder, leads the structure's first real combat test
  (`MCD-1381`), and steps down on principle at his term's actual end, declining a second term
  (`MCD-1514`).
- **Danne Sok** — introduces his wife and daughter to Kanja, establishing that crew families form
  their own independent impression of him (`MCD-591`). His **daughter is deliberately kept
  unnamed** across every appearance: followed from age six to sixteen when she enlists
  (`MCD-1002`); leads her first fully independent operation with neither Kanja nor Halst present
  (`MCD-1517`); her audit competence catches the crew's first purely self-interested (not
  coerced) betrayal, the relief clerk skimming grain at the hundredth Chronicle (`MCD-1520`).
- **Sera** (Callum Breck's daughter, `CC-117`) — at sixteen, tells Kanja directly she is
  choosing healer's training over crew membership, extending the charter's free-membership
  principle to someone who was never a member (`MCD-1057`); stabilizes Garren Hask's failing
  heart (`MCD-1089`); is offered a formal dispute-council seat (`MCD-1516`).
- **Orenn** — new minor, non-recurring: Sera's healer-mentor (`MCD-1057`).
- **Joran and Mira** — new named characters introduced together: Joran, a civilian father,
  drowns going back for his wife during a levee break the crew otherwise saves 31 people from;

  carves Garren Hask's name into the same wall she founded (`MCD-1423`); is offered a formal

  (`MCD-1516`); reflects on her still-new observer standing at the run's close (`MCD-1522`).
- **The unnamed deckhand** — taught to read using Hask's old ledgers (`MCD-1001`); becomes Efa

- **A captured Directorate officer** (unnamed) — asks to join the crew rather than accept parole,
  given the same slow-earned trust as any newcomer (`MCD-559`); a years-later check-in confirms
  he's fully, quietly integrated, having turned down a clean return to his old rank without
  telling anyone (`MCD-1382`).

**The institutional structure (unique to this alias among the eleven):**
- **The dispute council** — founded quietly sometime before `MCD-592` (its founding moment itself
  is never dramatized on the page; first shown already functioning). Given increasing formal
  weight across the run: it deadlocks 3-3 over civilian grief the charter didn't anticipate
  (`MCD-1371`); formally overrules Kanja's own proposed rescue operation 3-2, and he accepts it
  (`MCD-1000`); tests external governance claims into a liaison arrangement (`MCD-1376`).
- **The written charter** (`MCD-1056`, eight months after "the Trinity's surrender") — three
  founding principles: membership is chosen and free to leave at any time without owing
  explanation (extending the Maret Vos precedent, `MCD-593`); the council's authority stands
  independent of any war (`MCD-592`); Kanja sits as one voting seat among equals, no greater
  formal weight than anyone else. A fourth clause, on what happens once the founding generation
  who remembers why the crew started is gone, is deliberately left blank.
- **The rotating council-chair seat** (`MCD-1380`) — three-year terms, Kanja explicitly barred
  from ever holding it. First holder Corren Halst (`MCD-1381`); second holder Callum Breck
  (`MCD-1514`).
- **The fourth clause, finally written** (`MCD-1388`) — resolved not as a named heir but as
  standing structure itself: the rotating chair, a taught ledger lineage, the memorial wall, plus
  an explicit clause treating Kanja's own eventual absence like any other protected departure.
- **The involuntary-removal clause** (`MCD-1520`, the hundredth Captain Alias Chronicle) — the
  charter's first-ever expulsion mechanism, added only after its first purely voluntary,
  self-interested betrayal (a relief clerk skimming grain for profit): full restitution,
  immediate removal, and an explicit but unearned door left open to future trust.
- **The Pier Nine memorial wall** (`MCD-1372`) — founded by Mira, open to civilian and crew names
  alike; Tam Sullen's name (Efa Gol's Black Trench pair-partner, `CC-131`) added retroactively as
  its founding-era link; later attacked by unreconciled Trust holdouts trying to erase the crew's
  history rather than harm its people (`MCD-1521`); a final entry resolves whether an enemy
  soldier's name can share it by creating a distinct, adjacent marker instead of integrating it —
  preserving rather than flattening the moral distinction (`MCD-1522`).

### Abilities / gear

- **The Trinity in its crew-protective register (Rebellion-era waves 1-19 only).** Distinct in
  register from every other alias's showcases per the ledger's own framing (`MCD-396`): Mafesto's
  Kinetic Transfer System is shown absorbing/redirecting force *away from* civilians and crew
  rather than at an enemy (`MCD-396`, volley fire meant for eleven unarmored recruits); Obsidian
  Malice discharged to disable footing or scatter a boarding line rather than kill (`MCD-396`);
  Onyx of Oblivion's detection powers used for non-combat purposes. This register is confined to
  the Rebellion-era waves, before the age-30 surrender (`MCD-246`).
- **Coordinated command over solo heroics.** `MCD-461` — Kanja maps a strongpoint and directs
  three separate named subordinates (Efa Gol's decoys, Pell Ostra's demolition team, Callum
  Breck's assault element) through a three-vector plan, his own Trinity held mostly in reserve.
  `MCD-1515` inverts this further: Kanja fights *under* Callum Breck's tactical command as the
  newly-seated chair, the first time in the sub-series he isn't the one directing.
- **Kanja's own Long Mask-era kit (the seven-piece post-Mafesto gear system, `ARS-344` through
  `ARS-356` -- the Forge-Coat, Sovereign Eyes, Breath Collar, Ironhand Gauntlets, Ironfall Boots,
  Smoke System, Mend-Line -- plus the Rexmar Machete, his own trained swordsmanship, and his
  instinctive Rexmar-Mar tactical sense) runs throughout the post-"Trinity's surrender" waves**
  (`MCD-1058`, `MCD-1091`, `MCD-1367`, `MCD-1370`, `MCD-1374`, `MCD-1377`, `MCD-1381`, `MCD-1386`,
  `MCD-1421`, `MCD-1515`, `MCD-1518`, `MCD-1521`), consistent with the Trinity's own locked
  age-30-314 sealing (`MCD-246`) -- corrected across Batches 314 and 321 from an earlier draft
  that mistakenly used full-Trinity gear in these same entries. The deliberate-restraint beat in
  `MCD-1518` (Kanja choosing not to engage the Ironhand Gauntlets at all against a panicked crowd)
  stands on its own regardless of which gear system is in play.
- **New environmental registers for this alias specifically:** a high mountain pass defending a
  war-orphan caravan (`MCD-1367`); total blind darkness during a moonless storm, coordinated by
  Efa Gol on sound-count alone (`MCD-1374`); a crowded civic plaza during a food distribution
  (`MCD-1091`).

### Already-locked plot beats (book-level or Chronicle-level)

- **Origin, doubled.** Two independent namings converging on the same word: Garren Hask's private
  habit (`MCD-395`) and Callum Breck's deliberate choice (`MCD-397`), the latter explicitly tied
  to Kanja's refusal to let Nev Torr's death be assigned as Breck's fault.
- **The charter's full arc**, in order: founding (`MCD-1056`, three principles plus a blank
  fourth clause) → the blank clause tested by real grief with no precedent (`MCD-1371`/`1372`,
  producing the memorial wall) → a rotating, term-limited chair adopted specifically to resolve
  the succession question without naming an heir (`MCD-1379`/`1380`) → the fourth clause finally
  written as standing structure rather than a person (`MCD-1388`) → its first-ever involuntary-
  removal clause, added only after the sub-series' first purely voluntary betrayal, at the
  hundredth Captain Alias Chronicle (`MCD-1520`).
- **The founding-mortality arc**, in order: the promise (`MCD-920`, an aging Garren Hask's private
  notebook) → the near-fulfillment (`MCD-1089`, his heart failing, stabilized) → institutionalized
  (`MCD-1090`, the notebook reaches the council) → generational transmission underway (`MCD-1373`,
  `MCD-1384`) → his death (`MCD-1422`) → his memorial (`MCD-1423`), explicitly closing the arc
  opened at `MCD-920`.
- **"The war the name outlived"** (`MCD-1004`) — the dispute council directly asks, at the
  Rebellion's close, what the institution is for now that the war that birthed it is over; Kanja
  admits he doesn't know. Answered only much later and only partially, through demonstrated
  practice rather than a stated resolution: peacetime disaster relief (`MCD-1058`, `MCD-1383`),
  a paid Trust commission producing real internal friction (`MCD-1091`), and the charter's own
  eventual completion (`MCD-1388`).

### Reserved / unresolved threads

- **The Trinity/Long-Mask era-span contradiction, resolved (Batches 314 and 321).** A fraction of
  this alias's post-"Trinity's surrender" material (waves 20 onward) originally used the full
  Trinity in combat despite `MCD-246`/`CC-005` locking that gear as sealed at L9 for the entire
  284-year Long Mask (ages 30-314) that begins at exactly the point these entries are dated from.
  Every affected entry has since been rewritten to use Kanja's correct Long-Mask-era kit instead
  (the seven-piece post-Mafesto gear system, `ARS-344` through `ARS-356`, plus his own trained
  swordsmanship and instinctive Rexmar-Mar tactical sense), and the charter's own founding
  timestamp was loosened so that kit reliably falls inside its own ages-33-50 existence window.
  No longer open; left here only as a record of what the fix covered.
- **The mortality gap, Kanja's lifespan vs. his crew's.** Recurring and explicitly still open:
  first raised at `MCD-920`, revisited at `MCD-1373` (Efa Gol) and most directly at `MCD-1387`
  ("the sub-series' most direct confrontation of the theme... left unresolved rather than
  comforted away"). The charter's fourth clause (`MCD-1388`) addresses Kanja's own eventual
  absence institutionally but the emotional question — what it means for him to keep outliving
  everyone who built this with him — is never closed and reads as a deliberately standing, likely
  still-open thread for future entries.
- **Maret Vos still has no dedicated `CC-` dossier.** Corren Halst and Danne Sok received theirs
  at `CC-158`/`CC-159` (Batch 320, alongside the pronoun reconciliation applied to both -- he/him
  throughout, including nine files within this track corrected at Batch 321). Vos remains the one
  heavily-recurring figure in this ensemble without the same Hask/Breck/Maren-style dossier
  treatment Batch 48 gave those three.
- **Deliberately unnamed recurring figures**, consistent with established convention elsewhere in
  the ledger but worth flagging explicitly: Danne Sok's daughter (6 appearances, never named),
  the deckhand who inherits Hask's ledger (3 appearances), and Callum Breck's wife (`CC-117`,
  background only). Whether any of these should eventually be named is an open Game-Plan-stage
  question, not resolved here.
- **A numbering/labeling drift in the ledger's own Roman-numeral Chronicle titles, partly fixed.**
  Numerals X, XI, and XII were never used by any entry (the fourth wave's entries are labeled
  XIII-XV instead), while XVI, XVII, and XVIII were each used twice for two different, unrelated
  Chronicles (wave 5's `MCD-558`-`560` and wave 6's `MCD-591`-`593`). Batch 321 renumbered wave 5's
  three entries (`MCD-558`-`560`) to X, XI, and XII, closing that duplication using the unused
  numerals. The apparent second "XCII" was investigated and found not to be a real duplicate: the
  only other hit is `MCD-1486`, a Crow King Alias Chronicle that merely cross-references this
  track's own Chronicle XCII (`MCD-1422`) by name in its own prose, not a second Captain entry
  carrying the same numeral. The final entry (`MCD-1522`) self-confirms the correct total ("one
  hundred and two total Captain Alias Chronicles across thirty-four complete waves"), so no content
  is or was missing.
- **What happens to the "Captain"/Pier Nine institution after Kanja's own Pi-Awakening** (age
  314, `CC-006`, ending the Long Mask) is untouched by anything in this track — the run closes at
  wave 34 on a deliberately open, reflective note (`MCD-1390`, `MCD-1522`) well short of that
  point. Genuinely open for a future wave, not currently flagged in-ledger as reserved, but a
  natural next thread given how much institutional groundwork this track has already laid.
- **Narrator not yet explicitly confirmed for this alias.** `VB-020`/`021` establish Onyx of
  Oblivion as narrator for "Kanja chapters" generally, and `VB-026`'s progressive-handoff rule
  explicitly does not apply to the Phase 2 territory Annals — but it doesn't explicitly say
  whether it applies to the *Alias* Chronicle track either, and Captain's own material spans both
  the Rebellion (ages 21-22, where `VB-026`'s progressive handoff would technically apply if this
  counts as "Kanja-POV treatment of the Rebellion") and material explicitly decades into
  peacetime (steady-state Onyx per `VB-020`/`021`, if that's what governs). This is a Game-Plan-
  stage confirmation item, not resolved here, and applies identically to all eleven aliases, not
  just this one — no Alias Chronicle profile has done this confirmation yet since this is the
  first one drafted.

### Existing Chronicle corpus

102 Chronicles locked across 34 waves (`MCD-395`-`397`, `MCD-428`-`430`, `MCD-461`-`463`,
`MCD-506`-`508`, `MCD-558`-`560`, `MCD-591`-`620`, `MCD-918`-`920`, `MCD-996`-`1004`,
`MCD-1056`-`1058`, `MCD-1089`-`1091`, `MCD-1364`-`1390`, `MCD-1421`-`1423`, `MCD-1486`,
`MCD-1514`-`1522`) — the deepest, longest-running Alias Chronicle series by a wide margin, and the
one whose overall shape has changed the most from its own origin point. A synthesis of the
demonstrated arc, not a competing invention:

- **Waves 1-3 (9 entries, `MCD-395`-`463`):** origin and the alias's baseline combat register —
  crew-protective Trinity use, coordinated command, no solo heroics. Purely Rebellion-era
  (ages 21-22).
- **Wave 4 (`MCD-506`-`508`):** the first genuine no-clean-answer command dilemma, the first
  coerced (not voluntary) internal betrayal, and Garren Hask's grandnephew — the first
  generational-inheritance entry.
- **Wave 5 (`MCD-558`-`560`):** endurance leadership (declining a biological rest advantage),
  slow-earned trust extended to a former enemy, and Efa Gol's full synthesizing reflection across
  every alias — closing ten full alias waves project-wide.
- **Waves 6-15 (30 entries, `MCD-591`-`620`):** the single largest tonal shift in the whole track —
  the dispute council is founded, the crew's own institutional culture (quarrels settled without
  Kanja, families introduced, a self-correction rule after a wrong call, a genuine unconverted
  holdout, a first inversion where the crew cares for an exhausted Kanja, a decommissioned ship
  given a funeral, ordinary nursing labor, a settlement alliance negotiated by crew members while
  Kanja stays silent) becomes the dominant material, largely displacing combat entirely for long
  stretches.
- **Wave 16 (`MCD-918`-`920`):** paternalism directly confronted and corrected into a standing
  norm; the sub-series' first purely celebratory, conflict-free entry (a wedding); the mortality-
  gap theme's formal opening.
- **Waves 17-19 (9 entries, `MCD-996`-`1004`):** the run's first pure comedy (a year-long prank);
  Kanja's own economic treatment corrected by the crew; a full funeral/eulogy scene; the first
  direct-accountability scene with an outside victim; the crew's own institution formally
  overruling Kanja for the first time; a teaching-lineage founded; a multi-year relationship arc
  followed in one continuous entry; internal rivalry named and addressed; closing on the
  deliberately unresolved "what's this institution for, now the war's over" question.
- **Wave 20 (`MCD-1056`-`1058`):** the written charter (direct payoff to wave 19's open question);
  Sera's declining-to-join entry (the sub-series' first deliberate non-inheritance); and the first
  entry fought with Kanja's actual Long-Mask-era kit, roughly five years post-"Trinity's surrender"
  (see the era-span note above).
- **Wave 21 (`MCD-1089`-`1091`):** Garren Hask's heart scare and Corren Halst's five-year
  succession deferral; the crew's first paid Trust commission, producing genuine internal friction
  over serving the institution they fought.
- **Waves 22-30 (27 entries, `MCD-1364`-`1390`):** the longest sustained run — informal succession
  tests, bureaucratic patience, mercy over punishment for a con man, new environmental registers,
  pure humanitarian logistics, Maret Vos's second, more self-aware choice to stay, the sub-series'
  first genuine unresolved rescue failure (Joran's death, Mira's introduction), a deadlocked
  council resolved by Mira herself, the memorial wall founded, Efa Gol's aging made concrete, a
  blind-dark boarding rescue, Mira's own gradual belonging, external governance tested into a
  liaison arrangement, a rescue-engineering showcase with no enemy present, Callum Breck's craft
  passed down formally, the five-year promise answered on schedule with the rotating-chair fix,
  its first holder's first real test, a years-later officer check-in, a flood disaster mirroring
  Joran's own death, the ledger formally handed to the once-illiterate deckhand, a six-week
  self-sufficiency test, a predatory-labor-guild entry defended on the charter's own terms, Efa
  Gol's direct and unresolved mortality-gap conversation, the charter's fourth clause finally
  written, a full founding-crew reunion, and a deliberately open-ended solitary closer.
- **Wave 31 (`MCD-1421`-`1423`):** Mira's first active operational role; Garren Hask's death of
  old age; his memorial, explicitly closing the arc `MCD-920` opened.
- **Waves 32-34 (9 entries, `MCD-1514`-`1522`):** the rotating chair's first real succession test
  (Callum Breck becomes the second holder); Kanja fighting under another's command for the first
  time; Mira's council seat and the new observer-term precedent; Danne Sok's daughter's first
  fully independent command; the crew's protective role tested against its own protected people
  rather than an external enemy; Kanja's own first steps toward a personal memory-keeping practice;
  the hundredth Captain Alias Chronicle (the sub-series' first purely voluntary betrayal, forcing
