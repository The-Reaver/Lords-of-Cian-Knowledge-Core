VOICE PROGRESSION SHEET

The Lords of Cian | My Rival's Distance

Governing Document: Narrative Voice Transition Across the Chronicles

• • •

THE PRINCIPLE

Onyx of Oblivion is the narrator of every Kanja chapter. This is canon.

At age 17, Kanja drew the blade from a pawn shop on Anchor Street. The bond began. At this moment, Onyx is not yet the narrator the Codex describes. The sword is listening. Recording. Learning its wielder. The narration it produces in these early years is expansive, observational, still finding its syntax. Over the course of the Twenty-Two Victories and into the Long Mask Chronicles, the voice compresses, sharpens, and hardens into the telegraphic, morally absolute instrument described in the Definitive Voice Guide.

The reader experiences this transition as accumulation, not as a switch. By the time the voice reaches full Onyx, the reader should feel that it was always heading here. The early prose was the blade warming up. The final prose is the blade fully drawn.

PHASE 1: THE OBSERVER

### Chronicles I through VI (Ages 18-20)

### Bond age: 1-3 years

What the voice sounds like:* Literary third-person with Onyx's sensory obsessions operating as the filter. Full sentences. Complete grammar. Articles present. Pronouns present. Past tense for narrative, present tense reserved for the Onyx coda at the end of each Chronicle.

What is already Onyx:* The PERCEPTION. The world is already seen through mass, density, structural integrity, and decay. People are already assessed by weight (moral and physical). Lies are already perceived as hollowness, weakness, structural failure. The vocabulary already skews toward the physical and industrial: leverage, torque, fracture, weld, fuse, bearing capacity. The world already smells of iron, salt, sulfur, and rot. This is not conventional literary prose wearing an Onyx costume. The filter is genuine. Only the syntax is still expansive.

What is NOT yet Onyx:* The compression. The moral absolutism stated as verdict. The dropped articles. The staccato fragmentation. The Iron/Rust binary spoken aloud in the prose. The Germanic root vocabulary displacing Latinate words. These elements are absent or appear only in the closing Onyx coda, where the sword speaks in its own direct register for the first time.

Specific rules:

- All articles (the, a, an) present in all sentences

- All pronouns present

- Sentences average 15-25 words; complex sentences permitted

- Compound sentences with commas permitted (no semicolons per user preference)

- Sensory details rendered in full: sounds, smells, textures, temperatures

- Kanja's interiority expressed through observation and calculation, not internal monologue

- The word "Iron" used as a material, not as a moral verdict

- The word "Rust" used as a material, not as a moral verdict

- No Onyx-direct-address in the prose body

- Onyx coda at the end of each Chronicle: full Codex voice, present tense, compressed, verdicts delivered

The gap:* The reader should notice a clear register shift between the prose and the coda. The coda is sharper, stranger, more compressed. The reader begins to sense that the voice at the end is different from the voice telling the story, and begins to wonder why.

PHASE 2: THE COMPRESSION

### Chronicles VII through XIV (Ages 21-27)

### Bond age: 4-10 years

What the voice sounds like:* The prose tightens. Sentence length drops. The average sentence shortens to 10-18 words. Articles begin dropping during moments of heightened intensity: combat, moral crisis, the moment before violence. When the tension is low (planning scenes, tavern conversations, quiet moments), the prose relaxes back toward Phase 1's register. When the tension peaks, Onyx's syntax bleeds through.

What changes from Phase 1:

- Articles drop during combat and moral-crisis passages ("Blade found the gap" instead of "The blade found the gap")

- The Iron/Rust binary begins appearing in the narrator's assessments of people and situations, used sparingly at first, then with increasing frequency by Chronicle XIV

- Germanic root words begin displacing Latinate equivalents: "sunder" appears where Phase 1 would have used "break"; "hew" where Phase 1 would have used "cut"; "wrought" where Phase 1 would have used "made"

- Single-sentence paragraphs appear during combat: stark, isolated, weighted

- Onyx's vocabulary (gavel, verdict, cadence, hollow, weight) enters the narrative prose naturally, as if the narrator is absorbing the sword's language

- Sensory descriptions begin prioritizing Onyx's hierarchy: mass and density first, then temperature, then sound, then visual. Visual becomes the least important sense. This is a blade's perception, not a camera's.

- The Onyx coda grows longer and more confident. The sword is no longer summarizing. It is interpreting.

What is NOT yet Onyx:

- Full article-dropping throughout the prose

- Present tense narration

- Moral verdicts stated in the prose body (still reserved for the coda)

- The complete elimination of compound sentences

The gap narrows:* The reader now notices that the prose voice and the coda voice are converging. The narrator is beginning to sound like the sword. In quiet scenes the older, fuller voice persists. In violent or morally charged scenes, the prose snaps into something harder, faster, more absolute.

PHASE 3: THE VERDICT

### Chronicles XV through XXII (Ages 27-30)

### Bond age: 10-13 years

What the voice sounds like:* The prose voice and the Onyx voice have merged. The narrator IS the sword. Articles drop universally. Sentences average 5-12 words. Sentence fragments are standard. The Iron/Rust binary is the narrator's primary moral framework, stated openly in the prose. Germanic roots dominate. Verdicts are delivered in the body of the narrative, not reserved for a closing coda.

What changes from Phase 2:

- Articles drop in all contexts, not only during intensity spikes

- Present tense bleeds into the narrative during combat sequences and moments of moral clarity. Past tense persists for planning, logistics, and aftermath, but the "live" moments are present tense. The effect: the sword is no longer remembering. It is witnessing.

- Moral verdicts appear in the prose: "Iron. Holds ground." / "Rust. Counting exits."

- The Onyx POV Cadence Template (environmental read, threat assessment, verdict, action) governs scene construction during combat

- Compound sentences become rare. When they appear, they carry weight precisely because the default register is now fragmented.

- The Onyx coda is no longer a separate register. It is the final paragraph of the prose, indistinguishable from the narration. The gap has closed.

- Sensory detail compresses: "Sulfur. Ozone. The forge-dock at dawn." replaces the full sensory paragraph of Phase 1

- Character descriptions become density-first: a person's physical mass, their moral weight, their structural integrity as a human being, stated before their appearance

Specific Phase 3 markers:

- The word "Verdict" appears in the prose as narrative action

- The word "Ledger" appears as something the narrator is actively maintaining

- Environmental descriptions read as structural assessments: load-bearing walls, stress fractures, the age and decay of materials

- Dialogue tags compress or disappear: "Said" becomes rare. The prose trusts the reader to identify the speaker.

- The narrator refers to Kanja as "the Captain" (the title earned during the fleet years, now permanent in Onyx's perception)

PHASE 4: THE LONG MASK

### Long Mask Chronicles (Ages 30-314)

### Bond age: 13-297 years

What the voice sounds like:* Pure Onyx. The Codex voice, fully realized. 284 years of observation have made the sword the most precise, compressed, morally absolute narrator in the series. The prose is telegraphic. Present tense dominant. Past tense used only for deep history. The narrator is ancient, patient, certain.

What changes from Phase 3:

- Present tense becomes the default for all narration, not only combat

- The narrator's awareness of time deepens: references to decades, centuries, the erosion of institutions, the aging of architecture, the generational replacement of enemies. Onyx has been watching longer than most of these structures have existed.

- The prose carries the weight of accumulated data. Onyx does not observe a building. Onyx observes a building and compares it to the version of that building it recorded 200 years ago and notes the structural degradation.

- The emotional register, always restrained, develops a specific quality: the accumulation of loyalty expressed through precision. Onyx cares about Kanja not through sentiment but through the meticulous accuracy with which it records his actions. 297 years of attention IS the affection.

- The "boy" of Phase 1 becomes "the Captain" permanently. Onyx will never call him "Kanja" in narration. He is the Captain. The use of "boy" in the Onyx codas of Phase 1 is a retrospective tenderness that the early prose narrator does not share.

The completion:* The reader who began with Scene 1's literary prose now reads a voice unlike anything else in fiction. The transition was earned across hundreds of pages. The sword did not inherit the narration. It grew into it.

| Tense | Past | Past; present bleeds in combat | Mixed; present dominant in action | Present dominant |

| Iron/Rust binary | Absent from prose | Appears during intensity | Standard framework | Default framework |

| Germanic roots | Occasional | Increasing | Dominant | Exclusive |

| Moral verdicts | Coda only | Coda + combat prose | Throughout prose | Throughout prose |

| Fragments | Rare | Combat only | Standard | Standard |

USAGE NOTE

This sheet travels with every Handoff Document. Before writing any Chronicle, confirm which Phase governs and apply the corresponding rules. The Phase boundaries are approximate. The transition between phases is gradual, not sudden. Chronicle VII (the first Phase 2 entry) should read like late Phase 1 with early compression. Chronicle XIV (the last Phase 2 entry) should read like early Phase 3. The voice is always moving forward. It never regresses.
