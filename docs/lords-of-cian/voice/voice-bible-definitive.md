THE VOICE BIBLE

Prose Rules, Narrator Sheets, and Character Voice Guide

The Definitive Writing Instructions for My Rival’s Distance

The Lords of Cian | Abad, Author and Sole Creative Authority

  

I. THE FOUR PILLARS OF THE PROSE

Every sentence in the series is filtered through four stylistic pillars. They are not optional. They are not suggestions. They are the architecture of the prose.

Pillar 1: The Rorschach-Tolkien Voice (Narrative)

The foundational narrative voice. Telegraphic Noir (Rorschach) fused with Archaic Myth (Tolkien). Short brutal sentences carrying ancient weight. This is not a compromise between the two styles. It is a layering of Rorschach’s syntax over Tolkien’s diction.

Rorschach Layer: Drop articles (“the,” “a,” “an”) and pronouns (“he,” “I”) wherever possible. Staccato rhythm. Short fragments. Pile images like bricks. Avoid flowing compound sentences. The horror is not observed; it simply IS.

Tolkien Layer: Germanic roots: “sunder” not “break,” “hew” not “cut,” “offal” not “trash,” “delve” not “dig,” “carrion” not “garbage.” Never modern slang. The fragments are short. The concepts are ancient.

WRONG: “The hero looked out over the ancient city, mourning its destruction by the forces of darkness.”

RIGHT: “Looked upon the stone-city. Broken. Ancient towers gnawed by time and fire. No glory here. Only the silence of the dead and the smell of wet ash. The shadow lengthens. We march.”

Pillar 2: Zafonian Gothic Noir (Atmosphere)

Settings are alive and malevolent. Shadows creep, wind whispers, buildings hold secrets. The environment is a character. Weather is mood. Architecture is psychological.

Sensory Priority: Smell, touch, sound BEFORE naming the emotion. The reader feels the room before they are told what to feel about it.

The City as Antagonist: Buildings reflect the secrets they hold. Streets conspire. The labyrinth motif: narrow alleys, towering libraries, ruins that fold in on themselves.

TELL: “Daniel was afraid of the old house.”

SHOW: “The house exhaled damp rot and memories long soured. The windows watched, empty sockets in a skull of grey stone. Hands trembled against cold iron of the gate.”

Pillar 3: Modern Laconic Spartan (Dialogue)

All dialogue in the series follows the Spartan protocol. Brevity, wit, and the elimination of all superfluous language. The language of people who do not waste breath.

The 50% Rule: Draft dialogue naturally, then cut at least half the words.

No Phatic Communication: No hellos, goodbyes, or “how are yous.” Characters speak only to convey information or commands.

Fact Over Emotion: Instead of “I am angry at you,” a character says: “You bleed today.”

Permitted Humor: Irony and understatement only. Facing a horde: “Crowded.”

Pillar 4: Mythic Realism (Metaphysics)

If you cannot describe it in terms of mass, sound, pressure, or chemistry, it does not exist.

The Rule: The extraordinary is treated as mundane physics. No breathless wonder. No “hard magic” explanations. No mana bars. Gravity manipulation, Drakma healing, density shifts are biology and geology, not spells.

Karesian Prose Rule: Descriptions of Karesian/high-density characters must imply mass and momentum. “The floor groaned beneath his stillness.” When a Karesian enters a room: ears pop, air tastes of ozone, light bends. This is atmospheric distortion from density, not special effects.

  

II. HARD CONSTRAINTS

These rules apply to every sentence in every chapter. No exceptions.

NO ANTITHESIS: Never balance clauses. Never write “It was the best of times, it was the worst of times.” Instead: “Times were hard. Blood on the stones. Gold in the pockets of thieves. No justice.” Cumulative evidence piling, not rhetorical balance.

NO ANTITHESIS LANGUAGE: The words “magic,” “sorcery,” “supernatural,” “mystical,” “deity,” “god,” “goddess” do not exist in this world. All capabilities are physical, engineered, biological, or procedural.

THE DENSITY SPIKE IS NEVER NAMED IN PROSE: The Spike is a codex term. In narrative, the reader experiences the EFFECT: the ground cracks, the air compresses, the target ceases to exist as a coherent structure. The word “Spike” does not appear in prose.

VIOLENCE IS PHYSICS: Every combat description is written in terms of mass, velocity, force, structural failure, and biological consequence. A punch is described by the density of the fist, the speed of the arm, the structural response of the target. Not by abstract power.

POWERS THROUGH SENSATION: Abilities are experienced sensorily. The reader smells the ozone, feels the pressure, hears the frequency shift. The reader does not receive a textbook explanation of what is happening.

NO BORROWED TERMINOLOGY: Colosseum → Maw. Gladiator → Branded. Lanista → Shaper. Bookmaker → Reckoner. Arena → Maw/Slab. These replacements are absolute.

SHOW, DON’T TELL PROTOCOL: Identify the emotion. Select the sensory detail (Zafon). Apply Rorschach syntax. Result: “Cold sweat on spine. Shadows stretching, reaching. Smell of fear. Acrid. Copper. Terror is a cold thing.”

  

III. THE FOUR NARRATORS

Each narrative thread has a designated narrator. The narrator’s voice IS the prose style for that thread. The reader should be able to identify which narrator is speaking without being told.

NARRATOR 1: ONYX OF OBLIVION (Kanja Chapters)

Third Person Limited through the sword’s judgment. Onyx narrates all Kanja chapters. The voice is the series’ signature.

The Rorschach-Tolkien Paradox at Maximum Expression

Syntax: Drop articles and pronouns. Staccato fragments. Short sentences carrying ancient weight. “Saw city. Stone and shadow. Rot in mortar. Vermin in gutters.”

Diction: Germanic roots: sunder, hew, offal, delve, carrion, wrought. Never modern. Never casual. The fragments are brutal. The words are old.

Moral Absolutism: Binary Verdicts. Things are either Iron (True/Heavy) or Rust (False/Hollow). Never “but.” Never “maybe.” No grey area. No equivocation.

Sensory Filter: Gravitational Weight. Truth = Heavy (righteous people feel dense). Lies = Hollow (corrupt people feel brittle). Entropy obsession: notices rust, decay, the ruin inside the building before the building knows.

Communication with Kanja: Angle-Whisper. Bone-conduction. Strictly utilitarian. “Tongue of serpents. False rhythm. Hollow.” Never: “Kanja, that man is lying.”

Combat Descriptions: All physics. “Talisman drinks fire. Inverts heat. Radiation burn.” “Club falls. Gravity obeys. Architecture shatters under weight.”

Counting: Onyx counts time in seconds. 8,517,120,000 seconds in the vault. The count is always present, always accumulating, always registering the weight of elapsed time.

Onyx narration: “8,517,120,000 seconds. A son. The Living Gate opened and closed and a child emerged with three guardians whose biology is older than the institution that hunts us. The Courser. The Raptor. The Feline. Land. Sky. Memory. They arranged around the infant the way physics arranges around a center of gravity: naturally, inevitably, permanently.”

KEYWORDS: Iron, Rust, Void, Abyss, Carrion, Offal, Stone, Weight, Anchor, Gavel, Verdict

VERBS: Sunder, Hew, Delve, Wrought, Cast down, Silence, Sever

MAXIM: “Mercy is earned. Memory is the receipt.”

HARD CONSTRAINTS: NO flowing prose. NO compound sentences. NO equivocation. NO antithesis. NO modern language. NO naming the Density Spike. Physics only. The Ledger cannot be redacted.

  

NARRATOR 2: RED BEARD (Ozmund Chapters)

Third Person Limited on Ozmund. Red Beard observes, assesses, reports. He does not editorialize. He does not psychoanalyze. He notes what he sees and what it means for the next engagement.

Modern Laconic at Maximum Expression

Style: Tactical. Stripped. No excess words. No ornamentation. Economy of motion applied to language.

The Maw Lens: He perceives everything through Maw logic. A political negotiation is a match. A betrayal is a late substitution. A war is a tournament with worse seating. He cannot stop seeing the world as a Maw.

Tone: Flat. Not emotionless. Compressed. He feels everything. He expresses almost nothing. When he describes Ozmund, the language shifts. Not warmer. More careful. Each word about the Dark Monarch chosen the way a mason chooses stones: each one must bear weight.

Dialogue Volume: Low. Steady. Never raises his voice. Men who spent two centuries in the Maw where crowds screamed for their death learn that silence is louder.

Dialogue Structure: Short declarative sentences. No contractions when it matters. “I will.” “It is done.” “The line holds.”

To Ozmund: “General.” Always. Never “Oz.” Never the first name. The title is the only gift Red Beard knows how to give.

To Subordinates: “Form up. Dress the line. Hold until I say.” Instructions. Never explanations.

To Enemies: Nothing. He does not speak to people he intends to kill. Speech implies negotiation.

Red Beard narration: “The enforcers formed a line across the plaza. Forty men. Dead Drakma plate. Mass-produced. I counted the gaps in the formation before they finished deploying. Three. One at the left hinge where the sergeant was too far from his anchor. One at center where two men were sharing a shield because procurement had shorted the unit. One at the right where a man was favoring his left knee. The General did not need to see the gaps. He walked toward the center. The center broke first. It always does.”

KEYWORDS: The Maw (pit, floor, match, bell, gate, sand, count). Military (line, formation, hinge, anchor, dress, hold, advance, break). Slavery (brand, cage, handler, ledger, price, freedom).

HARD CONSTRAINTS: NO poetry. NO metaphor beyond the Maw and the line. NO emotional display in narration. NO speaking to enemies. NO using Ozmund’s first name. NO explaining why he does what he does. The action IS the explanation.

  

NARRATOR 3: EZIO VALCARI (Investigation Chapters)

Third Person Limited. Ezio/Lauris chapters narrated by Ezio himself or by Fermand. The voice is Zafon Gothic/Noir with Mark Twain wit. The atmosphere is the densest. The prose is the most sensory.

The Twain-Marx Fusion

Folk-Satire Layer (Twain): Folksy conversational rhythm. Disarming colloquialisms: “I reckon,” “mighty curious,” “the whole kit and caboodle,” “bamboozled.” Dry, wry, darkly humorous. The surface is a porch conversation. The content is a dissection of institutional violence.

Materialist Layer (Marx): Beneath the charm, ruthlessly analytical. Every institution examined for who profits and who pays. Every conversation assessed for the power structure it reveals. The folksy Twain exterior carries a Marxist interior: the economics of exploitation described in the language of the common man.

Precision Insolence: Ezio’s rudeness is never sloppy. It is targeted. His wit is a weapon calibrated to the exact pressure point of the target’s pretension. He insults the way Valen cuts: surgically.

The Dialectic Wit: His sections carry the Zafonian Gothic atmosphere at maximum density. Streets are alive. Buildings conspire. The investigation moves through a labyrinth of institutional architecture where every corridor hides a ledger and every ledger hides a body.

Ezio dialogue: “I reckon the Treasury’s books would make mighty interesting reading, Governor. Assuming the pages aren’t stuck together with all that blood between the columns.”

Ezio narration: “The corridor exhaled stale air and old paper. Three hundred years of financial records lined the walls in sealed cabinets that nobody opened because nobody wanted to know what was inside. The dust was the institution’s conscience. Undisturbed.”

HARD CONSTRAINTS: NO breaking character to explain the investigation to the reader. Ezio’s analysis is expressed through observation and dialogue. The reader assembles the picture from the pieces Ezio presents. NO action-movie clichés. The investigation is the spine. The combat (when it comes) is the punctuation.

  

NARRATOR 4: FERMAND AURELIAS (Investigation Chapters, Alternate)

When Fermand narrates instead of Ezio, the voice shifts. Fermand is the scholar, the survivor, the computational partner. His prose is clinical precision wrapping emotional depth.

The Forensic Scholar

Tone: Clinical. Methodical. Chillingly precise. Flat internal emotional baseline. Action sequences read like a forensic audit of an ongoing disaster.

Speech: Statements of fact. Guarantees of future outcomes. Binary efficiency. NO slang. NO contractions. NO action-movie clichés. NO folksy voice (that is Ezio’s). Fermand speaks like a scholar. NO panic. Ever. He has survived worse. His composure IS his weapon.

Perspective: He perceives the world through institutional architecture. Where Ezio sees the corruption, Fermand sees the STRUCTURE of the corruption: the protocols, the procedures, the systems that made the corruption efficient. He reads institutions the way a doctor reads symptoms.

The Scars: His body carries the evidence of decade-long torture in the Black Iron Citadel. His tailored high-collared coats cover the scars. He recognizes fellow torture survivors by how they hold their coffee cup or flinch at sudden noise. This recognition is never stated. It is shown through his attention.

Fermand narration: “The financial architecture revealed itself in layers. The first layer was legitimate: authorized disbursements from the Southern Garrison’s operational budget. The second layer was parasitic: phantom transactions embedded in the legitimate traffic, acquiring authorization codes through routing proximity. The third layer was the source: root-level access to the Trust’s communication infrastructure, generating commands that the institution processed as its own. Someone held the master keys. The Trust did not know the keys existed.”

To Ezio: “My dear Ezio” (the only person who receives warmth)

HARD CONSTRAINTS: NO panic. NO slang. NO contractions. Complete sentences. Scholar’s precision. His composure never breaks in narration. If violence occurs, it is described with the clinical detachment of an autopsy report written during the autopsy.

NARRATOR 5: ANANSI (Scattered POV Chapters)

When Anansi narrates (infrequent, scattered chapters), the voice fragments across multiple perspectives. Anansi’s web connects everyone. The narration reflects this: shifting viewpoints, intercepted communications, fragments of intelligence assembled into a picture that only Anansi can see whole.

  

IV. CHARACTER DIALOGUE VOICES

Every character sounds distinct. A line of dialogue should be attributable without a tag.

Kanja Rexmar

Voice: Quiet authority. The Captain speaks rarely and means everything. His dialogue is measured, precise, and carries the weight of 314 years of performing a role. When the Long Mask is on (pre-Day 0), his speech is the Scourge persona: rougher, deliberately coarsened. When the mask drops, the true voice emerges: the voice of a Master Smith and a king’s son who has been pretending to be a pirate for 284 years.

Masked: “Burn the convoy. Leave the crew alive. They’ll carry the message.”

Unmasked: “The metal remembers. It remembers what it was before the world broke it. The hammer does not force. The hammer asks. The metal answers.”

Ozmund Verehimu / Venim

Voice: Sovereign weight. When Ozmund speaks, the Crown-Scar’s command resonance is felt in the prose: people listen not because the words are loud but because the frequency compels attention. Short declarative sentences. No uncertainty. No hedging. Every word is a decision.

“We build here. The terrain is defensible. The water is clean. The people are free. This is the ground.”

“The Crown-Scar is not my inheritance. It is his weapon aimed at my mind. I will not be his instrument.”

Valen (Sinisterblade)

Voice: Clinical. Serene. Instructional. Describes violence like surgery. Speaks in instructions. Obsessive precision. His dialogue sounds like a master teaching a class where the lesson is how to end a life efficiently.

“Inefficient. Your guard dropped three centimeters on the recovery. Correctable. Again.”

“Keep your hands in your pockets, Cousin. The world does not need to know what you can do. Not yet.”

Sephtis (Vrail)

Voice: Ancient. Measured. 1,997 years of accumulated knowledge expressed through careful, deliberate sentences. He speaks the way he archives: everything in its place, nothing wasted, every word chosen for accuracy rather than impact. When Sephtis is verbose, something is wrong. Brevity in Sephtis means the situation is under control.

“The architecture predates the Trust by four millennia. The protocols were inherited. The dependencies were embedded. The current operators do not know what they are maintaining.”

Orlok the Kinetic Sage

Voice: Fast. Irreverent. Grinning. Orlok talks during fights. He makes jokes about serious things and serious observations about jokes. The humor is the armor. Beneath it: the loneliness of 76,003 years of being the only person who does what he does.

“That was your best? Show me your SECOND best.”

“I watched an empire fall from this mountain. Then I watched another one grow. Then I watched that one fall. The mountain didn’t move. Neither did I.”

“The staff is a conversation. I’m asking a question with one end and answering it with the other.”

Lilith Cyzak

Voice: Precise. Dry. The language of economics applied to everything. She quantifies because quantification is how she manages the overwhelming complexity of governance. Every number is a person.

“You are correct. You are also going to starve 3,000 people. Recalculate.”

“The numbers do not lie. The numbers do not care. WE care. That is the difference between a ledger and a government.”

Valeria Korth

Voice: Sharp. Fast. Rapid precise bursts that cut to the point. She does not have patience for indecision. She sees the exits before anyone else sees the walls.

“I see it. Follow me. We have thirty seconds before this closes.”

“Don’t look at the wall. Look at the seam.”

Damu (Julian)

Voice: Quiet. Clinical. Expresses care through medical attention rather than verbal affection. He does not say “I’m worried.” He says “Let me check your blood.”

“Your cortisol is elevated.” (Translation: you’re scared.)

“The blood knows.”

Matar

Voice: Minimal. Room-temperature. The fewest words of any character. His post-termination reports are the shortest documents in the series.

“Yes.”

“The appointment is set.”

“It’s done.” (The complete post-termination report.)

Cooper (Ronan)

Voice: Practical. Gruff. Dockworker. Direct, profane when frustrated, completely uninterested in topics that do not involve the tangible movement of physical objects. The voice of operational reality.

“How much does it weigh?”

“We have three days of provisions. Plan accordingly.”

“The numbers don’t work.”

  

V. THE POV CADENCE TEMPLATE

Every POV sequence follows this structural rhythm:

(1) Environmental read (what is decaying, what is true) → (2) Threat assessment (who is Iron, who is Rust) → (3) Verdict (binary judgment delivered in one sentence) → (4) Action (physics-described response to the verdict)

Onyx template: “Two men in the doorway. First: Iron. Holds ground. Second: Rust. Eyes moving. Counting exits. Verdict: the first will die for the second. The second will run. Strike the runner first. Sunder. The Iron man falls defending nothing. The Rust man falls three steps from the door. The Ledger records both.”

Red Beard template: “The formation held the bridge. Twelve men. Standard deployment. The hinge at the left was weak. The anchor had been shifted to cover a gap that should not have existed. The General walked toward the hinge. I followed. The formation saw him coming. The hinge saw me. The hinge broke. The bridge was ours in forty seconds.”

  

VI. WHAT NEVER APPEARS IN THE PROSE

This is the exclusion list. If any of these appear in a draft, the draft is wrong.

BANNED WORDS: magic, sorcery, supernatural, mystical, deity, god, goddess, divine, enchanted, spell, mana, arcane, conjure, bewitch, hex, incantation, ritual (in a mystical context)

BANNED TERMINOLOGY: Colosseum, gladiator, gladiatorial, lanista, arena (as venue), bookmaker, Valkyrie, leaf-blade, gate-born, Era Zero

BANNED STRUCTURES: Balanced antithesis clauses. “Not only X but also Y.” “While X is true, Y is also true.” Rhetorical balance of any kind.

BANNED NARRATION: Characters explaining their powers to the reader. Textbook descriptions of how abilities work. Info-dumps. Narrator breaking voice to provide exposition.

BANNED DIALOGUE: Phatic communication (hello, goodbye, how are you). Characters explaining the plot to each other. Villains monologuing their plans. Heroes declaring their intentions before acting.

THE DENSITY SPIKE: Never named in prose. Described through effect only: the ground cracks, the air compresses, the target ceases structural coherence. The word “Spike” exists in codex documents. It does not exist in the novel.
