# The First Student's Last Field Read

*Locked canon, Batch 261, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1291`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LXXII, wave 24, closing the wave. The first student steps back from active field use into a full-time teaching role. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The first student's hands had started shaking on cold mornings — not badly, not so anyone else would have noticed unless they'd watched those same hands verify a thousand readings over more years than either of them had bothered to count. He noticed. He'd been the one teaching those hands what stillness against iron was supposed to feel like.

"It's not the listening," the first student said, when Kanja asked directly rather than let it sit unspoken between them. "The listening's as clear as it's ever been. It's holding the rod steady long enough afterward, through the second verification pass, that's started costing me something it never used to."

"How long has this been happening?"

"Longer than I told you." He didn't apologize for the omission, only stated it. "I didn't want a misdiagnosis with my name on it to be the thing that finally proved I should have stepped back sooner. I'd rather choose the moment than have the moment choose it for me."

They took one last structure together, deliberately — a low-stakes footbridge the settlement council had already half-decided to replace regardless, chosen the same way Kanja had once chosen a supply-line gate for the second student's own first unsupervised test. The first student's read came back true, doubled verification clean both passes, the discharge as precisely controlled as it had ever been in a career that had started with a young engineer nearly turning the technique on a granary out of grief and resentment.

"Nothing wrong with that read," Kanja said, when it was done.

"Nothing wrong with it. That's exactly why I'm choosing now." The first student set the listening rod down for what both of them understood to be the last time it would leave his hand for a live field discharge. "I've got a decade of applied restraint and no illusions left about my own limits. The cohort taught me that teaching and doing aren't the same skill, and I think I've gotten better at the first one than I've stayed at the second. That's not a loss. That's just what happens to a hand, eventually, no matter how carefully it was trained."

"You could keep going. Slower. Fewer engagements."

"I could. I've thought about it more than you'd guess." He looked at the bridge they'd just correctly, cleanly brought down, one final clean number in a career built on nothing but honest ones. "But I taught a whole cohort that a wrong read costs more than an ungranted title. I'm not going to be the one who proves that only mattered when it was somebody else's hands shaking."

Kanja didn't argue him out of it. He'd learned, somewhere in the years since a young engineer had first confessed a temptation he hadn't needed to confess, that the doctrine's honesty had to apply to the man who first carried it forward as completely as to anyone he'd since taught. "Then you teach. Full time. No apology in it."

"None needed," the first student said. "I got to spend years being proof the doctrine could pass through hands that weren't yours. I'd rather spend what's left making sure it keeps passing through hands that aren't mine, either."
