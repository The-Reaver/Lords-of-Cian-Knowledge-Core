# The Night the Warehouse Burned Twice

*Locked canon, Batch 277, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1437`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XCVIII, wave 33.
Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench -- Onyx of Oblivion solo, Mafesto dormant and Obsidian Malice
undeployed (`MCD-232`). A detailed, battle-intense combat showcase combining fire and raid for the
first time in this alias's run, per standing craft instruction.*

---

The first fire was a decoy, small and set deliberately visible at the warehouse's eastern corner,
meant to pull every hand on-site toward buckets and a bottleneck door while the real attack came
through the loading gate on the far side. It worked exactly as designed for the first ninety
seconds. It stopped working the moment Kanja recognized the shape of the trap for what it was and
sent the crew toward the fire while he went, alone, toward the gate the smoke was meant to hide.

The second fire, set at the gate itself the instant the raiders realized they'd lost the element of
surprise, was the real one -- oil-fed, fast, and specifically intended to force a choice between
fighting through flame or losing the ledger room it guarded. Whisper of Shadows carried him through
smoke thick enough to blind an ordinary man, Onyx reading the raiders' positions by the sound and
heat-shadow of them rather than sight, closing the gap to the first attacker before the man had
finished shouting a warning to the others.

What followed was fought half-blind in a corridor turning to ash at both ends -- four raiders who'd
trained together and fought like it, coordinating a pincer that would have worked against anyone
reading the fight by eye alone. Cadence Ruin found the coordination's own rhythm inside three
exchanges and broke it apart from the inside, turning a disciplined four-man formation into four
separate men fighting alone within the length of a single corridor. Veil Piercer opened a gap in the
last man's guard as the ceiling beam above him began to give, ending the fight with just enough time
left to haul him clear before it came down.

The ledger room held. The decoy fire, unattended in the fight's aftermath, took longer to put out
than the real one had taken to survive, and Garren Hask spent the following two days re-copying
water-damaged pages from memory rather than mourning anything the flames had actually reached.

"You could have called us both ways at once," Corren Halst said, arriving too late to do anything
but help carry buckets from a fire that no longer mattered.

"I could have. Then neither fire gets fought properly, and the one that actually threatened
something burns unattended while we split ourselves thin." Kanja checked the seam where smoke had
found its way past his collar. "A decoy only works if you let it decoy you. I'd rather lose the page
count than the room."
