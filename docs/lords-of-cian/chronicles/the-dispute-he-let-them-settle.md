# The Dispute He Let Them Settle

*Locked canon, Batch 193, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-592`). Captain Alias Chronicle XVII, wave 6 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The argument had been building for a week before it finally broke open on deck, loud enough that
half the crew stopped what they were doing to watch: two of the newer hands, both good workers,
both convinced the other had claimed credit for a supply run that had nearly gone wrong and only
hadn't because of quick thinking neither would agree belonged to the other.

Every head on deck turned toward Kanja, the way they always did when a dispute threatened to curdle
into something worse. He didn't move toward them.

"Take it to the council," he said instead.

The council was new — six weeks old, five crew members chosen by vote specifically to hear
disputes that didn't need a captain's judgment, only a fair one. It had settled two minor
quarrels since its founding, both quietly, both without anyone feeling like they'd lost.

"You're the captain," one of the two arguing men said. "Shouldn't you decide?"

"I could. I'd probably decide fair. But then every time something like this happens, the answer is
always going to be me, and the day I'm not here — because I'm dead, or because I'm three thousand
miles away doing something that can't wait — this ship needs to already know how to settle its own
arguments without falling apart waiting for my verdict." He nodded toward the five council members,
already gathering. "They watched the whole supply run report get filed. They know the manifest
timing better than I do. Let them decide."

The council took an hour, heard both accounts, checked the manifest timestamps against the harbor
log, and ruled that both men had acted well and the credit belonged to both equally — a verdict
neither had expected and both, grudgingly, accepted faster than they would have accepted the same
ruling from Kanja himself.

"You could have ended that in five minutes," Garren Hask said afterward, closing his own ledger
for the night.

"I could have. But every minute I spend being the only person this crew trusts to be fair is a
minute this ship stays exactly as strong as I am, and no stronger. That's not what I want to build
here."
