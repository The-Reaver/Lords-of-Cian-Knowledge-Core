# What Danne Sok Never Told Anyone

*Locked canon, Batch 181, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-530`). Bane Alias Chronicle XV, closing the fifth wave.
Rebellion era, age 19, shortly after the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone
material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters beyond the already-locked
Danne Sok.*

---

Danne Sok had been freed from captivity by Kanja's own hand before the Black Trench, one of the three
earliest crew members — alongside Corren Halst and Maret Vos — who'd fought beside him before Maw-9
ever added the rest. In all the time since, through every story that grew up around the Bane name, he
had never once told anyone what he actually remembered about the boy who'd freed him, before any
alias existed to carry the weight of it.

"Everyone talks about him like the fear came first," he told a newer recruit, the two of them alone
during a quiet watch, the kind of conversation that only happened when nobody official was listening.
"It didn't. I was the one he freed. I watched his hands shake for an hour afterward, once we were
somewhere safe enough that shaking wasn't a liability anymore. Nobody talks about that part. I don't
know that anybody besides me ever saw it."

"Why haven't you told that story before?"

"Because the fear works better without it, and because it was never mine to spend on making a point."
Danne Sok considered the question carefully, weighing years of silence against the moment finally
asking to be broken. "I've watched this crew build something real out of that fear — protection, a
name enemies think twice about testing. I didn't want my own memory of a scared boy with shaking hands
to undercut something that was actually saving lives. So I kept it. I think I've kept it long enough,
now, that it doesn't feel like protecting him anymore. It just feels like erasing something true."

"What changed?"

"Watching him, still, all this time later, still doing the work of it — still choosing mercy when
cruelty would be easier, still building the reputation instead of just wearing it. I don't think a man
who's actually become the thing the stories describe still shakes afterward, the way he did that
first night. I think that's exactly the part worth remembering, even if it never makes it into the
version everyone else repeats."

He never told the story publicly, choosing instead to let it live only in the quiet watches where it
had first been shared — a private counterweight to the legend, kept by the one man who'd witnessed
the boy underneath it before there was any alias to protect.
