# The Crowd That Turned on Itself

*Locked canon, Batch 286, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1518`). Captain Alias Chronicle XCVIII, wave 33. A detailed Trinity showcase with a genuinely new adversary for this alias: not raiders or an institution, but the crew's own protected people, panicked past reason by a real grain shortage.*

---

The shortfall itself wasn't anyone's fault worth naming — a blight season, a delayed convoy, arithmetic that simply didn't come out even — but arithmetic had never once stopped a crowd from needing somewhere to put its fear. By the third morning of thinned rations at the distribution yard, the line three hundred deep had stopped behaving like a line at all.

Efa Gol read it before it broke, the way she read every discrepancy that mattered: not the shouting at the front, which was ordinary enough, but the dozen or so bodies working the crowd's edges with too much purpose for people who were supposedly just waiting their turn. She flagged it to Kanja with four words and no more. "Some of them aren't hungry."

He came without armor drawn, without Obsidian Malice so much as primed, because the surest way to turn three hundred frightened people into a genuine crush was to give them something worse than hunger to be frightened of. Onyx of Oblivion's Veil Piercer did the work instead, reading the difference between desperation and intent the way it read any battlefield's true shape — a mother shielding a child was not the same shape as a man positioning himself against the strongest section of fencing, and the power sorted eleven of the second kind out of three hundred of the first without a single wrong flag.

The moment the crush actually started — a surge from the rear, the fence groaning, two children nearly under it — Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System redirected the collective shove sideways along the fence line itself rather than through it, the accumulated weight of panic bleeding off into the ground the way it had bled off cannon-fire and collapsing trusses before, except this time the force it was catching came from people it was protecting, not attacking. Nobody watching ever saw a weapon. They saw the fence simply hold, and a path clear itself where the worst of the crush had been.

Pell Ostra pulled the eleven opportunists out one at a time from the yard's edges while the crowd's attention was on the line straightening itself back out, quiet enough that most of the yard never registered anything had happened beyond a scare that passed. Kanja worked the actual shortage the ordinary way afterward — standing at the front of the line himself for two hours, handing out what there was, letting people see with their own eyes that nothing was being kept back from anyone.

"You could've ended that in four seconds with the coat on," Efa Gol said afterward, not quite a question.

"I could have," he agreed. "And every person in that yard would have gone home telling their children they nearly died at a grain line. Some fights aren't won by finishing them fast. They're won by making sure nobody there ever has to describe it as a fight at all."
