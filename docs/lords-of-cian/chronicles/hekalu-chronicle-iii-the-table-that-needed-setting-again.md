# Hekalu Chronicle III: The Table That Needed Setting Again

*Locked canon, Batch 178, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-525`). Homage-era Detroit, Hekalu/Adom. Territory
Chronicle — Adom as protagonist, Kanja an unnamed background guest with no command, credit, or
resolution authorship. New standalone material. No new named characters.*

---

The rival cooperative owner who'd once returned unprompted with a joint wage-floor proposal, years
into the mutual-obligation network the table had bound him into, began quietly missing the network's
shared commitments — not out of any betrayal, but through the ordinary erosion of years and distance,
his own cooperative's daily concerns crowding out an obligation that had once felt immediate and
personal.

Adom noticed the drift before anyone else did.

"You haven't sat at this table in over a year," he said, seeking the man out directly rather than let
the obligation quietly lapse unaddressed. "I don't think that's a betrayal. I think it's just what
happens to any bond that isn't tended, no matter how real it was when it formed."

"I didn't realize I'd drifted that far." The man seemed genuinely surprised, the erosion having crept
up on him without any single moment marking its progress. "I still believe in the network. I just
haven't made the time the way I once did."

"Belief without renewed connection fades the same way any relationship does, whether or not the person
believing it notices happening." Adom didn't treat the drift as a failure on either side, simply an
honest fact about how the binding actually worked. "The table doesn't hold on its own, indefinitely,
once someone's sat at it. It needs returning to, the same way any real obligation does. I should have
sought you out sooner, before the gap grew this wide."

They sat together again that evening, the same unforced choice that had bound him the first time,
years before — and the connection, Adom noted, felt genuinely renewed rather than merely reactivated,
warmer and more immediate than it had been in longer than either of them had tracked.

"I didn't know the binding could fade," the man admitted afterward, something in him settled by the
renewal. "I assumed, once it took, it simply held."

"I assumed the same thing, for longer than I probably should have. I think I've learned tonight that
it holds as long as it's tended, and quietly weakens when it isn't — not unlike anything else worth
keeping." Adom watched the man rejoin the network's own gathering, welcomed back without ceremony.
"I'd rather know that now than lose people to distance I could have closed simply by asking them back
to the table."

An unnamed man near the table's edge, present for the renewal, left once the evening's gathering had
settled into its old, familiar rhythm.
