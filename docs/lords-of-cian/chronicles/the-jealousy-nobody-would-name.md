# The Jealousy Nobody Would Name

*Locked canon, Batch 222, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1003`). Captain Alias Chronicle LVI, wave 19. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

It had been building for weeks before anyone said it aloud, and when it finally surfaced it wasn't in
a confrontation but in a clipped, overcorrected silence — a junior crew member, three years aboard and
solid at every task handed to him, going quiet whenever Callum Breck's name came up, which by then was
often, since Breck had just been given command of a forward element on an operation the junior hand
had personally requested and been passed over for.

Kanja noticed it before anyone explained it to him, the way he noticed most things aboard the ship he
didn't announce noticing. He found the junior hand alone on watch two nights later.

"You've been passed over for something you wanted," he said, without preamble. "And you've decided
the reason is that Breck's more trusted than you are."

"Isn't he?"

"He's trusted with different things than you are, for reasons that have nothing to do with how this
crew ranks people against each other, because it doesn't. Breck went four months without speaking
after the Black Trench and came back and used what little he had left to save a fleet at Ghost
Harbor. That's not a favor I did him. That's a debt this crew owes him that has nothing to do with
you."

"I know his story. Everyone knows his story. I don't have a story like that."

"No. You have a different one — three years of never once missing a watch, never once needing to be
told twice, never once making this crew spend a moment worrying whether you'd hold. That's not a
smaller story. It's a quieter one, and quiet doesn't mean it isn't counted." Kanja sat down on the
rail beside him rather than standing over him. "You didn't get the forward element because the
operation needed someone who's already run three like it under fire. Not because I trust him more
than I trust you. If I didn't trust you, you wouldn't be standing this watch alone with me right now
without a second set of eyes checking your read."

The junior hand didn't have a ready answer for that, and Kanja didn't fill the silence for him.

"I'm not going to pretend the favoritism you're worried about doesn't exist anywhere on any crew,"
Kanja said eventually. "It exists plenty of places. I've seen what it does to people who serve under
it. I built this one on purpose so it wouldn't happen here, and that only works if I say so out loud
when I see it starting to fester instead of letting it sit and rot quietly the way you've been letting
it sit in you for a month."

"I didn't think you'd noticed."

"I notice most things I don't announce."

He didn't offer the forward command as a consolation — the junior hand hadn't earned it yet, and
they both knew it — but he did, before the watch ended, lay out plainly what would, in fact, put him
in line for the next one, and meant every word of it as instruction rather than apology. The
resentment didn't vanish overnight. It did, slowly, over the following months, stop being something
the junior hand needed to hide.
