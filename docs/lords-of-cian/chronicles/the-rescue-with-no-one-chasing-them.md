# The Rescue With No One Chasing Them

*Locked canon, Batch 260, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1260`). Crow King Alias Chronicle LXVIII, wave 23. A
mountain landslide traps villagers with no adversary anywhere in the story; the craft's coordination
discipline is used purely for search-and-rescue. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The slide came down in the night without warning, the way they always did, burying a third of a
mountain settlement's grain stores and two of its houses under rubble too unstable to dig by torch
alone. Nobody sent for the crew. They were three valleys over on unrelated business when a rider
reached them, and Kanja turned the whole party toward the mountain before the rider had finished the
sentence.

There was no enemy in this. No patrol to fool, no sensor grid to fill with false readings, no
Directorate reporting network to seed with a forged schedule. Just unstable rock, four missing
people, and a narrow window before a second slope let go.

What the crew brought wasn't the Hymn-Engine's deception at all -- it was the coordination
underneath it, the same discipline that let three hundred voices interleave three false patterns
without stepping on each other, turned toward something with no lie in it anywhere. Search teams on
four sides of the slide needed to move in exact, non-overlapping rhythm so vibration from one team's
digging didn't collapse what another team was still shoring up. The fourth generation ran it like a
ledger -- who was digging where, how long each shift could safely last, when to signal a full stop
so the whole slope could be listened to for settling before anyone went back in.

Three of the four were found alive. The fourth, an older woman, was recovered after the window had
already closed, and nothing in the discipline changed that.

"There's no clever piece to this one," the third generation said afterward, sitting with the settlement's
few survivors rather than with the crew. "No trick, no legend. Just people moving in an order that
kept the ones underneath alive as long as the rock allowed."

"Maybe that's the plainest version of it there is," Kanja said. "The part we always say is
underneath the songs. Here it's not underneath anything. It's just what it is, with nothing dressed
up around it."
