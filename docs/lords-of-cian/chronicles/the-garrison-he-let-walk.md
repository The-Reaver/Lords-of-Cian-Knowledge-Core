# The Garrison He Let Walk

*Locked canon, Batch 254, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1102`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXXII, wave 24, closing wave
24. A garrison released under Bane's own amnesty ethic re-arms and ambushes an unrelated Rebellion
column weeks later -- the first entry to show a direct, specific cost of the mercy doctrine at
strategic scale.*

---

The garrison at Kessic Overwatch had surrendered clean six weeks earlier — no tricks, no staged
wounded, an honest capitulation Bane had accepted on the same terms he always offered: lay down arms,
walk free, never take them up again against the rebellion. Most who took that deal kept it. Some
didn't have anywhere else to go and drifted back into whatever work had fed them before the war.

The garrison's former captain went back to his commission inside three weeks. He led the ambush that
hit a Rebellion supply column forty miles east, one Bane's own people had never touched, killing six
before the column's escort drove them off.

Bane heard it secondhand, three days after it happened, from a runner who hadn't known the ambush
commander's name meant anything to him specifically.

He didn't call a council. He didn't announce a change in policy. He found Corren Halst at the edge of
camp that night and said it plainly, the way he said most things that actually cost him something.

"Six people are dead who wouldn't be if I'd held that garrison instead of releasing it."

"You couldn't have known he'd break the terms."

"That's not the same as not owing the cost. I knew when I started doing this that some of the people
I let walk would use it to hurt someone else eventually. I did it anyway because the alternative was
holding or killing people who hadn't done anything to earn either, on the chance that one of them
might. I still think that's right. I'm not going to pretend six people paid nothing for me thinking
it."

"You're not going to stop offering the terms."

"No." Bane looked out at the dark beyond the fire line, where the news had come from. "The math
doesn't change because it cost something real this time. It was always going to, eventually. I just
hadn't had to sit with it yet."

Halst didn't argue him out of the doctrine, and didn't try to talk him into feeling better about it
either. He sat with him instead, until the fire burned down, and neither of them said anything else
about it that night.
