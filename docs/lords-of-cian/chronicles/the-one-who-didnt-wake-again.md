# The One Who Didn't Wake Again

*Locked canon, Batch 200, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-804`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle XIX, wave 7 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The raid on the fever-hold had gone as clean as any in years — a locked barge moored in a brackish
inlet, its guards down before half of them understood the smoke rolling across the deck wasn't
natural, forty-one captives freed into open air inside eleven minutes.

Forty of them walked off the barge under their own power. The forty-first, an old man who'd been
chained in the hold's worst corner for longer than anyone still aboard could say, didn't wake when
the shackles came off his wrists.

He carried the man ashore himself, gently, the Ironhand Gauntlets' plated strength doing nothing here
but making sure the grip stayed careful rather than merely strong. A healer among the freed did what
could be done, which by her own quiet admission wasn't much — infection, exhaustion, a body that had
simply run out of road before rescue arrived to give it more.

The old man died two hours later, on dry ground, surrounded by people who were free because of a
night he'd lived just long enough to see the start of and not long enough to finish.

"You got here in time," the healer said, watching him sit beside the body afterward, unmoving. "Eleven
minutes from first alarm to open shackles. Nobody does better than that."

"He was in that hold for years before I ever heard the barge existed. Eleven minutes measures the
wrong thing." He didn't look up from the old man's face. "The rescue isn't the whole debt. It's only
the part I can reach. Everything that happened before I got here — that's debt too, and it doesn't
go away just because the shackles finally came off."

"So what do you do with that?"

"Nothing that fixes it. I carry it, same as I carry the ones who walk off the barge standing up." He
finally stood, settling the old man's hands across his chest with the same careful attention he'd
carried him ashore with. "The forty who lived don't make this one not matter. And this one doesn't
make the forty matter less. Both things are just true, at the same time, and I don't get to pick
which one I feel tonight."

The barge burned at dawn, per custom, and the forty freed walked inland together, carrying between
them a name for the forty-first that none of the crew ever recorded, because it had never belonged to
the legend to begin with — only to the people who'd actually known him, and would keep it themselves.
