# What Grew From the Page

*Locked canon, Batch 271, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1409`). Crow King Alias Chronicle XCI, wave 31, opening
it. A trader's secondhand story reveals that the doctrine page lost during a raid years ago has
survived, passed hand to hand into a distant river town, and seeded a practice built on its
philosophy alone. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The trader hadn't come looking for the lineage at all. He'd come looking for the fourth generation
specifically, having heard through three other people that a man who cared about odd stories
involving pages and numbers might pay to hear one, and he told it plainly, expecting nothing more
than the coin: a river town two weeks upstream had, for going on six years now, a loose group of
people who settled disputes by a method they'd built themselves out of a single half-burned page
someone found wedged behind a granary wall. Nobody there knew where the page had come from. Nobody
there had ever heard the name Crow King. They just called what they did *listening properly*, and
they'd gotten a local reputation for it.

The fourth generation went very still.

"Listen before you act. Restraint over cleverness." He said it out loud, once, the way he might read
it off the page itself, because he still remembered writing those exact words down years before a
raid took the only copy out of his hands. "That's not a coincidence. That's the actual sentence."

He brought it to the apprentice and the third generation together rather than deciding what it meant
alone -- the same habit the fourth generation himself had learned from watching how the runner's-son
question got handled the year before, passed upward instead of settled in a corner.

"You always said the page didn't matter because it never held technique," the third generation said,
turning the trader's account over slowly. "You were right about that. It didn't need to. It held the
part that actually spreads."

"Then we go and see what they built," the apprentice said. "Quietly. Not to correct them. To find out
if we even should."

Kanja, when it reached him, asked only one question: whether the trader had said the town was better
off for it, or worse. The trader hadn't known to answer that, because nobody had thought to ask him.
So that became the actual reason for the trip -- not security, not correction, just the plain need to
know what six years of an idea, left completely alone, had turned into.
