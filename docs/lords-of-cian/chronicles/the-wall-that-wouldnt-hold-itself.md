# The Wall That Wouldn't Hold Itself

*Locked canon, Batch 206, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-900`). The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XLVI, wave 16. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The storm that hit the Warrow Bend embankment had no name and no enemy behind it. It came in off the
lowland flats two hours before dawn, dropped more rain in a single night than the canal district had
seen in three seasons, and by first light the retaining wall holding back the embankment's upper
terrace had given way in two places at once — not collapsed outright, but sagging, weeping mud
through cracks that widened visibly while men watched them.

Forty families lived on the terrace above it. Nobody had sent for the Trench Monarch. Corren Halst
heard about the wall from a fish-seller passing through at dawn and told Kanja before either of them
had eaten anything.

There was no tally to correct here, no owner to negotiate with, no rumor to trace back to its source.
Just mud, failing stone, and a clock nobody could see running out somewhere behind the wall's slow
weeping.

"We're not going to out-think this one," Kanja said, already stripping down to work clothes. "No
document's going to hold that wall up. It's going to be exactly what it looks like — hands, timber,
and however many hours it takes."

They worked through the morning and into the afternoon without any plan beyond the next hour's most
urgent problem. Danne Sok and Maret Vos hauled salvage timber from a collapsed shed two streets over
and wedged it into the worst of the cracking, buying time rather than solving anything. Kanja worked
the deepest breach himself, waist-deep in the collapsing footing, packing stone and clay by hand into
gaps that reopened almost as fast as he closed them. Onyx stayed sheathed the entire day. There was
nothing on that embankment a blade could have done that a shovel couldn't do better.

By the second night the worst of it had been shored, not solved — a temporary brace that would need
real masonry within the week, but one that would hold through the next storm if it came before repairs
were finished. Nobody along the terrace had lost their home. Two families had lost most of what they'd
stored on the lower shelves, and there was nothing anyone could do about that now beyond helping them
salvage what the water hadn't already taken.

Kanja sat on the wet stone afterward, hands split and raw in a way three years of Onyx-work had never
left them, and said little for a long while.

"Feels strange, not having anyone to be right about," Corren Halst said eventually, sitting down
beside him. "No owner refusing to pay. No rumor to run down. Just — a wall, and whether it held."

"That's most of what actually matters, I think, when you get past the parts people fight over." Kanja
looked up at the terrace, lamplight coming back on in windows that had gone dark the night before.
"The tally sheets, the negotiating, the reputation — all of it's in service of this. Roofs staying up.
People not losing what little they've built. I'd rather spend a day like this than a dozen duels, if
I'm honest. Nobody argues with a wall. It just does what it does, and you either meet it or you don't."

He didn't sleep much that night either, not from any threat but from the simple, unglamorous exhaustion
of having used his body for something no reputation could shortcut. The masons arrived within the
week and finished what the crew's two days of hands and timber had only bought time for. Nobody wrote
a pamphlet about the Warrow Bend embankment. Garren Hask logged it anyway, in the same plain hand he
used for every wage correction: *No adversary. No dispute. A wall, and the hours it took to hold it.
Recorded because it happened, not because anyone will ask.*
