# The Storm They Read Too Late

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XLVII, wave 16. Rebellion era, immediately following "The Calm That Was Made, Not Found." Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

By the time the wrong swell reached the supply hulls, there was no time left to retreat them to
shelter. Kanja read the water itself rather than any chart — a trick of watching wave-face angle and
gull flight he had picked up from Sephtis's own decades of teaching, not a forecast but a habit of
seeing — and understood the storm's true arrival was already less than an hour out, not the six the
false window had promised.

He didn't try to outrun it. He turned the escort into it.

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System banked the first real gust the way it had never been asked to bank a
false-calm's gentle chop, drawing hard kinetic load off the lead supply hull's overstressed rigging and
redistributing it through Kanja's own frame in three successive transfers, each one buying the crew
above him another minute to strike sail properly instead of losing it to the wind. Obsidian Malice
discharged twice in quick succession against the worst of the cross-swell striking the second hull's
exposed beam, not to attack anything but to physically blunt the wave's face at the moment of impact —
a use of the blade's raw kinetic output Onyx had never had cause to demonstrate against weather
itself rather than an enemy. Onyx's Veil Piercer, meanwhile, cut through the driving spray and dark to
find the third hull's snapped tow-line before it could foul the fourth's rudder, Cadence Ruin timing
the cut to the exact moment the strain went slack enough to sever cleanly.

Two supply hulls took real damage. None were lost. No crew died.

"You read a storm you had no forecast for," the escort's senior officer said afterward, still soaked
through, "faster than she could have called it fresh."

"I read a storm I could see," Kanja said. "That's not the same gift as hers, and it's not a
replacement for it — I was reading what was already on top of us. She reads what's still two days
away. We nearly lost these ships because someone made her trust a lie. What saved them was seeing the
truth with my own eyes once the lie ran out of time to hide behind."
