# The Blade Meant for Him

*Locked canon, Batch 153, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-482`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle X, first
entry in the fourth wave. Rebellion era, age 19, the Furnace District Strike. Not a territory
Chronicle. New standalone material. Kept deliberately unarmed, per this alias's established ethos,
tested here at its most personal. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

A man paid by the district administration to end the strike by ending its organizer found Kanja
alone at the tally table on the third night, blade drawn before anyone nearby had time to react —
the first threat of the entire strike aimed directly at Kanja himself rather than at the workers
around him.

Kanja didn't reach for a weapon he'd deliberately chosen not to carry.

He caught the man's wrist instead, turning the blade aside with the same unhurried precision he
brought to a tally sheet, disarming him without a single blow landing on either side — over almost as
quickly as it began, workers only fully registering the danger once it had already passed.

"Kill me and the strike doesn't stop," Kanja told the man, still holding his wrist, voice level
despite everything. "It gets worse for whoever sent you. A martyred organizer is a harder thing to
argue against than a living one asking politely for what's owed. Whoever's paying you hasn't thought
this through very far."

The man, disarmed and expecting violence in return, got none. "What happens to me now?"

"Nothing, if you walk away and tell whoever sent you that it didn't work." Kanja released his wrist,
the blade set aside rather than kept as a trophy. "I built this entire method on the idea that force
doesn't actually solve what it claims to solve. I'm not going to abandon that the one time force gets
pointed at me instead of someone else. That would make everything I've told these workers a lie the
moment it actually cost me something."

The workers who'd watched it happen were harder to convince than Kanja himself. "You should have let
someone deal with him properly," one said afterward, still shaken. "He came to kill you."

"He came because someone with money decided killing me was cheaper than paying what the tally sheets
say is owed. Responding to that with more violence doesn't disprove the cheapness of the calculation.
It confirms it." Kanja returned to the tally table, the work resuming exactly where the interruption
had left it. "The whole point was never that this method is safe. It's that it's right, even when it
costs something. Tonight it nearly cost me everything. I'd make the same choice again."
