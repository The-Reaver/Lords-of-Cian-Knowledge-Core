# The Ledger's Second Generation

*Locked canon, Batch 214, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-931`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle LVI, wave 19. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Word reached the crew secondhand, the way most of what mattered eventually did by that point in the
campaign: a district three weeks' travel from the nearest one they'd ever set foot in had just
finished training its own auditors, in full.

It took Ezio a day to work out where the method had actually come from, because none of it traced
back to him directly. It traced to the quarry district — the dozen he'd trained himself, the ones
who'd taken the ledger into their own keeping over a year earlier — who had, in the months since,
traveled to a neighboring district on their own initiative and taught what they knew to a fresh cohort
there, the way Ezio had once taught them.

"They didn't send for us," Ezio said, reading the account a traveling scribe had carried back. "They
didn't send for anyone who trained me, either. It's two links removed from anything either of us ever
touched directly."

"Is the method sound, that far out?"

"That's what I wanted to know first too." He'd checked, cross-referencing the new district's own
published tallies against production records he could verify independently. "It's sound. A few of
their conventions have drifted from ours — they mark uncertain testimony differently than we do — but
the corrective logic underneath all of it is exactly what I taught the quarry dozen. They didn't just
pass along a set of instructions. They understood it well enough to teach it themselves and let it
keep working once it left their hands too."

Kanja read the account twice before he set it down.

"The district He Never Visited built something from an incomplete account of what we do. This is
different. This is people we trained, training people we've never met, who built something whole
enough to train a third district neither of us will probably ever see."

"That's what you wanted, though. You said it yourself at the quarry — the point was nobody needing
you there anymore."

"I know what I said. It's different watching it actually happen twice removed from anything either of
us did with our own hands." He didn't say it as a complaint. "There's no telling how many more links
this goes before it stops being anything either of us taught, directly or otherwise, and just becomes
something districts do because it works."

"Is that a problem?"

"No. It's the only version of this that outlasts either of us needing to be in the room."
