# What Burned Loud Enough to Hear

*Locked canon, Batch 282, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1484`). Crow King Alias Chronicle C, wave 34, opening it.
A detailed Long-Mask-era gear showcase inside a burning warehouse, where fire's own chaos, not
silence, is the obstacle. Not a territory Annals entry. Corrected Batch 321, 2026-10-02: an earlier

Piercer, Soulbound Edge) despite this Chronicle's explicit anchor to "the fourth generation," whose

The vault at Hollowmere Keep had taught Kanja what total silence could hide. The granary fire taught
him the opposite lesson: that a building screaming with its own noise could hide just as much, in the
other direction.

Twelve hostages were chained in the granary's lower storeroom when the arson took, set deliberately by
the men holding them to cover an evacuation of their own before anyone thought to look for prisoners
left behind. By the time Kanja reached the outer wall, the whole structure was a wall of sound: timber
cracking overhead, grain sacks igniting in bursts that popped like far-off siege-shot, a roof beam
groaning somewhere above that told him, more clearly than any guard ever could, exactly how much time
the building intended to give him.

He had learned to read a room in total silence before. He had never had to read one actively trying to
drown him out. Kanja pushed past the noise floor the way a listener learns to hear one voice inside a
crowded hall -- not by finding quiet, because there wasn't any, but by learning the fire's own rhythm
well enough to hear past it: which cracks were structural and which were only grain sacks, which pop
was a beam failing and which was simply heat finding fuel. Within the storeroom itself, the same
instinctive Rexmar-Mar sense for pressure and intent that had carried him through every engagement
long before any gear existed to help it picked out four guards by the changed pressure of bodies
moving against smoke-thick air, their voices lost entirely under the roar but their positions as
legible as they'd ever been in silence.

The first guard's own charge met nothing but the Forge-Coat's grounding weave and the flat of the
Rexmar Machete, turned sideways through a support post already weakened by the fire -- plain leverage
and trained footwork, timed so the strike never risked bringing the ceiling down early. A single blow
from the Ironhand Gauntlets, aimed by the same pressure-reading rather than sight, dropped the second
guard before he found the door he was reaching for. The third and fourth broke and ran for the same
exit Kanja needed for the hostages, and lost that argument in under four seconds -- the Rexmar Machete
and the same instinct that had carried him through worse closing the gap between intention and
consequence with nothing wasted and no time spent on anything but the twelve chained lives the fire
was actively trying to take from underneath everyone in the room.

They cleared the storeroom with the roof beam still holding, barely, and carried the last hostage
through the outer wall's gap seconds before it gave. Outside, coughing smoke rather than words, Kanja
found he'd needed no deception at all in that room -- only the same underlying discipline the craft
had always run on, applied to noise instead of its absence.

"Silence hides things," he told the fourth generation later, once his voice came back enough to use.
"So does noise, if there's enough of it. I always thought those were opposite problems. Tonight I
learned they're the same problem wearing two different coats."
