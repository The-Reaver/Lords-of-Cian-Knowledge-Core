# What Burned Loud Enough to Hear

*Locked canon, Batch 282, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1484`). Crow King Alias Chronicle C, wave 34, opening it.
A detailed full-Trinity combat showcase inside a burning warehouse, where fire's own chaos, not
silence, is the obstacle. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The vault at Hollowmere Keep had taught Kanja what total silence could hide. The granary fire taught
him the opposite lesson: that a building screaming with its own noise could hide just as much, in the
other direction.

Twelve hostages were chained in the granary's lower storeroom when the arson took, set deliberately by
the men holding them to cover an evacuation of their own before anyone thought to look for prisoners
left behind. By the time Kanja reached the outer wall, the whole structure was a wall of sound: timber
cracking overhead, grain sacks igniting in bursts that popped like far-off gunfire, a roof beam groaning
somewhere above that told him, more clearly than any guard ever could, exactly how much time the
building intended to give him.

Cadence Ruin had mapped silent vaults before. It had never had to map a room actively trying to drown
it out. Kanja pushed past the noise floor the way a listener learns to hear one voice inside a crowded
hall -- not by finding quiet, because there wasn't any, but by learning the fire's own rhythm well
enough to hear past it: which cracks were structural and which were only grain sacks, which pop was a
beam failing and which was simply heat finding fuel. Within the storeroom itself, Veil Piercer read
four guards by the changed pressure of bodies moving against smoke-thick air, their voices lost entirely
under the roar but their positions as legible as they'd ever been in silence.

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System took the first guard whole, redirecting his own charge through a
support post already weakened by the fire rather than risking a strike that might bring the ceiling
down early. Obsidian Malice discharged once through the smoke, aimed by pressure rather than sight,
dropping the second before he found the door he was reaching for. The third and fourth broke and ran
for the same exit Kanja needed for the hostages, and lost that argument in under four seconds --
Onyx of Oblivion's Soulbound Edge closing the gap between intention and consequence with nothing wasted
and no time spent on anything but the twelve chained lives the fire was actively trying to take from
underneath everyone in the room.

They cleared the storeroom with the roof beam still holding, barely, and carried the last hostage
through the outer wall's gap seconds before it gave. Outside, coughing smoke rather than words, Kanja
found he'd needed no deception at all in that room -- only the same underlying discipline the craft
had always run on, applied to noise instead of its absence.

"Silence hides things," he told the fourth generation later, once his voice came back enough to use.
"So does noise, if there's enough of it. I always thought those were opposite problems. Tonight I
learned they're the same problem wearing two different coats."
