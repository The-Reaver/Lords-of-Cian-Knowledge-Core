# The Bid He Refused to Win Cheaply

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XXXVII, first
entry in the thirteenth wave. Rebellion era, age 20, in the weeks immediately following the Sewer
War of Killane's resolution. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No
new named characters.*

---

Killane's surface authorities, once the siege ended and the district needed its damaged
infrastructure rebuilt, opened the rebuilding contracts to open bid — and the lowest bid, submitted
by a contractor everyone underground already knew for cutting corners, was poised to win on price
alone.

Kanja submitted a competing bid he knew would lose on cost.

"You're bidding thirty percent higher than the lowest offer," the district clerk said, reviewing
the submission with open skepticism. "Why would the authority choose you over that?"

"Because the lowest bid uses green timber priced as seasoned stock, understaffs the shoring crews by
half of what the job actually needs, and skips the settling period any honest rebuild of that
gallery requires before it's safe to load." Kanja laid out his own materials accounting beside the
competing bid's public figures, the discrepancy plain to anyone willing to read both. "I'm not
asking the authority to pay more because I want the contract. I'm asking them to understand that the
cheap bid is cheap because it's planning to fail in exactly the way that section already failed
once, and somebody's going to be standing under it when it does."

"That's a serious accusation to make about a competitor."

"It's not an accusation. It's arithmetic. Weigh his stated materials cost against what seasoned
timber actually costs at current rates, and the numbers don't add up unless he's substituting
something cheaper than what he's charging for." Kanja didn't press further, letting the figures
speak for themselves. "I'd rather lose this contract fairly on an honest higher price than win it by
telling the same lie he's telling, and I'd rather the district know exactly what it's actually
choosing between before it decides."

The authority awarded the contract to a third bidder entirely, one whose figures matched Kanja's own
honest accounting almost to the coin — a result that cost him the work directly but left Killane's
rebuilt gallery standing on materials that would actually hold, a trade he considered, without
hesitation, the better outcome of the two available to him.
