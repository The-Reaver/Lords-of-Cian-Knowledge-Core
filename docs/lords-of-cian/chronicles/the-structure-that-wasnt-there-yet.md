# The Structure That Wasn't There Yet

The span bridge was three days from completion, its final tensioning cables strung but not yet
weight-tested, its scaffolding still bearing most of the load the finished structure was meant to
carry on its own — and the Trust column intending to cross it the moment it opened represented the
last reinforcement standing between the rebellion and a besieged supply line.

Reading an unfinished structure was nothing like reading a completed one.

"The tension's distributed wrong," Kanja said, the Aegis-Talisman's lens tracing a resonance pattern
that shifted meaningfully even as he held it in place. "It's not settled yet. Half the load's still on
the scaffolding, not the cables. If I read this like a finished bridge, I'll target the wrong thing
entirely."

He spent longer diagnosing it than he had any completed structure in recent memory, tracking not just
where the tension currently sat but where the engineers' own construction schedule meant it was
heading — the scaffolding due to come down within the day, the cables due to take on the full load
the moment it did, the whole structure a moving target rather than a fixed one.

The discharge, when it came, didn't target the bridge as it currently stood. It targeted the
scaffolding supports specifically, timed for a window when the cables had taken on enough real load
to fail under the sudden redistribution but the structure hadn't yet been certified stable enough for
the crossing the column was counting on.

The bridge came down three days early, the same three days the engineers would have spent finishing
it, and the column found their reinforcement route ended before it had ever technically existed as a
finished structure at all.

"You read a bridge that wasn't finished being built," the rebel engineer accompanying him said,
studying the collapsed span with open admiration. "I didn't think the doctrine worked on something
still becoming what it was going to be."

"Everything's still becoming something, if you look closely enough. A finished structure's just one
that's stopped changing while I'm reading it. This one hadn't. I had to read where it was going to be,
not just where it currently sat, and act before the two caught up with each other."
