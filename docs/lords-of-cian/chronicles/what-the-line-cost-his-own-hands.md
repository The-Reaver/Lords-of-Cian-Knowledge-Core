# What the Line Cost His Own Hands

*Locked canon, Batch 152, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-479`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle X, first entry in
the fourth wave. Rebellion era, age 18, before the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. New
standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

A stuck dredge cart came free wrong, the sudden give of it catching Kanja's own hand between the cart
frame and a loading stone before he could clear it — a crushing injury bad enough that the site's own
field medic ordered him off the line for a full week, no exceptions, if he wanted the hand to heal
straight.

He was back at the line in three days, hand bound tight, working one-handed at half his usual pace.

"You're not supposed to be here," the medic said, more exasperated than surprised. "I told you a
week. Nothing about a crushed hand heals faster because you decide it should."

"I know exactly what you told me. I'm not here to argue the medicine." Kanja kept working, awkward
and visibly slower, refusing every offer from nearby workers to simply take the shift for him. "I told
people I intended to dig for whatever I ended up wearing, the same as everyone else on this line. If
I let an injury be the reason I stop showing up the first time it costs me something, that was never
really true to begin with. It was just a nice thing to say until it got difficult."

"That's not strength. That's just stubbornness dressed up as principle." A fellow worker, watching
him struggle through a task that would have taken him half the time with both hands, said it plainly,
without cruelty. "Nobody would think less of you for actually resting. The whole point of everyone
digging together is that no single person has to carry it alone when they're hurt."

Kanja stopped, turning that over with more weight than the observation probably intended. "I think
you might be right. I think I've been treating the digging itself as the whole point, when the actual
point was always what it built — trust, something earned alongside everyone else. Insisting on
working through an injury nobody expects me to work through isn't earning anything. It's just proving
a point nobody needed proven."

He took the rest of the week off after that, genuinely, the line covering his section without
resentment or ceremony — and returned at full strength once the hand had actually healed, a small
correction that cost him nothing but the pride of having to admit a fellow worker's read was more
accurate than his own.
