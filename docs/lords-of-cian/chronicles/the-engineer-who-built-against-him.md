# The Engineer Who Built Against Him

*Locked canon, Batch 195, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-668`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XXXIII, wave 11 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The Trust's own fortification engineer had spent the first three weeks of the siege designing
countermeasures specifically against Kanja's tunneling tactics — reinforced chokepoints, collapse
traps, listening posts tuned to catch exactly the kind of quiet movement the crew relied on. He was
good at it. Three of his designs had nearly worked.

Kanja found him not through combat, but through a captured set of his own draft sketches,
annotated in a hand that revealed something the finished designs didn't: genuine respect for the
problem he'd been assigned to defeat, buried under orders he clearly took less personally than his
craftsmanship.

They met when a fourth trap, better designed than the first three, nearly closed on the crew's
extraction route — and failed only because Kanja read the engineer's own signature caution in its
construction, a built-in fail-safe the man had apparently been unable to bring himself to omit even
from a trap meant to kill the people it caught.

"You left yourself an escape route inside your own trap," Kanja said, once the ambush had resolved
without casualties on either side, the engineer himself taken rather than killed. "Why build a kill
trap with a flaw in it that a careful eye could find?"

The engineer didn't answer immediately, studying the man he'd spent three weeks designing against.
"I build things that work. Every design I've made for this siege works exactly as intended, except
that one detail, every time, in the same place. I told myself it was oversight. I don't think it
was."

"I think you're an engineer first and a soldier second, and some part of you kept building an exit
you'd want to use yourself if you were the one caught in your own work." Kanja studied the captured
sketches, genuine appreciation in it despite everything the man had built against him. "That's not
weakness. That's the same thing I've built this whole war on — caring more about honest craft than
about who it's technically serving."

The engineer worked the crew's own tunnels for the remainder of the siege, not as a prisoner but as
a defector who'd found, in the enemy's own methods, a version of the integrity his orders had never
actually required of him — his countermeasure designs turned, from that point forward, into some of
the crew's most effective defenses against the very tactics they'd once been built to defeat.
