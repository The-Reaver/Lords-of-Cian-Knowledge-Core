# The Column He Didn't Lead

*Locked canon, Batch 196, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-684`). Bane Alias Chronicle XIX, wave 7 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Efa Gol had run diversion forces under Bane's command a dozen times since the Black Trench, always
with him somewhere in reach — a signal away, a ridge over, close enough that the operation's real
weight could shift to him if the diversion stopped being enough. The column bound for the Ostrell
rail depot was the first time he told her, plainly, that he wasn't coming.

"I have somewhere else I need to be that day. You have this." He didn't soften it into a suggestion.
"Full authority. If it goes wrong, it's yours to fix, not mine to fix from a ridge you can signal.
I won't be in reach."

She'd led pieces of operations before — the crowd flow at Ash-Wharf, decoy columns that drew fire
so a real strike could land elsewhere — but always as the visible half of a plan with him holding
the other half in reserve. This was the whole plan, hers, with nobody's authority behind her
decisions but her own.

The depot didn't go the way the intelligence had promised. A shift change moved the garrison's
strongest company onto the exact ground her diversion was meant to cross, forty minutes before she'd
planned to move, no time to signal anyone who wasn't there to receive it anyway. She adjusted alone
— split the column, sent half wide around the shift-change company entirely, held the other half
back a full hour past the plan's own timing until the garrison settled into its new positions and
stopped watching as closely. The depot came down clean, six hours late and nothing like the plan
she'd started with, with no one to answer to in the field but her own judgment.

"You could have been there," she said afterward, when he finally rejoined the column two days later,
somewhere else entirely resolved in his absence. "Six hours is a long time to carry alone."

"I could have been there. I decided you needed to carry it alone more than I needed to be
available." Bane looked over her adjusted plan, the shift-change workaround, the hour's deliberate
delay — none of it anything he'd have told her to do, all of it exactly the kind of decision the
depot had needed. "You didn't need me hovering a ridge away to make good decisions. You needed to
find out you already could without me there to catch it if you didn't. Now you know. So do I."

She didn't answer that right away. When she did, it wasn't about the depot at all.

"Tam would have said the same thing to me. About not needing to be caught."

"I know. It's part of why I picked today."
