# What Pell Ostra Cleared

*Locked canon, Batch 199, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-776`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle XXI, wave 7 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The Trust had scuttled eleven hulks across the mouth of a fishing harbor rather than let it keep
feeding a rebellion it couldn't otherwise starve — old vessels stripped and sunk deliberately,
strangling a channel that three villages depended on for every boat that went out and every catch
that came back.

Pell Ostra spent a full day just listening to the wreck field before she touched anything, the same
way she'd once talked her way through the charges at the Black Trench and the Scrip-Forge accelerant
at Ash-Wharf — reading which hulks had settled stable and which were still shifting, which sat close
enough together that clearing one wrong would collapse two more on top of the gap she'd just opened.

"Eleven hulks, four of them load-bearing on the others," she told Kanja, chalk marks already covering
her forearm in a private shorthand nobody else fully understood. "I clear those four in the wrong
order, the whole field settles tighter instead of opening. I clear them right, the other seven
practically move themselves."

She worked for three days with charges sized individually to each hulk's own particular weight and
lean, never once repeating the same approach twice, muttering to the wreckage in the low continuous
patter that had unsettled every new recruit who'd ever watched her work and reassured every veteran who
understood it was simply how she thought out loud.

The fourth charge went wrong in exactly the way she'd warned it might — a hulk she'd read as stable
shifting half a second early, timber grinding against timber loud enough to carry across the whole
harbor — and she was already moving before anyone else registered the danger, redirecting the blast's
angle with a second smaller charge placed in the four seconds she had to spare, turning a collapse into
a controlled settle instead.

"That's the part nobody sees in the reports," Kanja said afterward, watching her check her own work
with the flat, unsentimental attention she gave everything. "Everyone remembers the explosion. Nobody
remembers the four seconds where you fixed the mistake before it became a disaster."

"The mistake's the interesting part. Anyone can set a charge that works when the wreck cooperates."
She was already moving to the next hulk, chalk marks accumulating. "The harbor doesn't care how good
my plan was if I can't fix it live when the wreck doesn't cooperate."

By the end of the third day the channel ran clear for the first time in six months, and the three
villages' boats went out the following morning past eleven fewer obstacles and one demolitions
specialist already packing for the next harbor that needed the same patient, unglamorous work.
