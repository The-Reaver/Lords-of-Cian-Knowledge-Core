# The Overseer Who Doubted the Boy

*Locked canon, Batch 129, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-435`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle VIII. Rebellion
era, age 18, before the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated
in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The Warehouse Twelve site overseer had heard every version of the Trench Monarch story by the time
the boy himself finally walked onto her section of the line — the dredge-worker who'd apparently
talked a foreman into fair rotations, the one the tally workers trusted enough to hand their own
under-counted numbers to without fear of the count being used against them later. She'd believed
none of it. Reputations built themselves out of desperate men wanting something to believe in, in
her long experience, and desperate men made poor witnesses to anything but their own hope.

"You're younger than the stories make you sound," she said, watching him work a jammed cart free
without asking anyone to do it for him. "And you're doing manual labor instead of making speeches,
which is either the most convincing thing I've seen from someone building a reputation, or the most
naive."

"Believe whichever one the evidence supports. I'm not asking you to take my word for anything." He
didn't stop working while he spoke. "Watch the rotations for a month. Check whether the numbers the
tally workers report match what actually moves through this section. If they don't, you'll have your
answer, and I won't waste your time arguing otherwise."

She watched for six weeks instead of one, out of a stubbornness that matched his own, checking every
figure against her own independent count. The numbers matched, every time, down to figures small
enough that no one organizing a con would have bothered getting them exactly right.

"I still don't fully believe in reputations," she told him, at the end of the six weeks, with the
particular grudging respect of someone who'd genuinely tried to find the con and hadn't. "But I
believe in numbers that check out every single time for six weeks straight. That's not nothing. That
might be more than the stories were ever worth."

"Numbers that check out is all I ever wanted the reputation to actually mean." Kanja set the last
cart of his own shift moving down the line. "Everything else people decided to call it was theirs to
decide, not mine to manage."
