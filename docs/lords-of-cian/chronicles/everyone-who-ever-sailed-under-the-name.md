# Everyone Who Ever Sailed Under the Name

*Locked canon, Batch 193, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-618`). Captain Alias Chronicle XLIII, wave 15 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Garren Hask's descendants had kept the tradition of the ledgers going long after his own hand grew
too unsteady to write in them, and somewhere in the accumulated volumes — a project none of them had
set out to build on purpose, just years of habit compounding — sat a running tally nobody had
formally commissioned: every person who had ever served aboard a ship under the name Captain, going
back to the very first weeks after Iron Shallows.

The number, by the time someone finally sat down and counted it properly, ran into the thousands.

"That can't be right," Kanja said, when the figure was first read to him, genuinely startled by it in
a way few things managed anymore.

"It's right," Efa Gol said. She was old now, one of the very few left who remembered the earliest
years firsthand, and she'd insisted on being the one to deliver the number herself. "Every recruit
off every dredge site. Every hand who signed on for a season and stayed for a lifetime, or signed on
and left after a year, and everyone in between. Every ship named by a vote instead of a single man's
say-so. Every council that settled a dispute without you needing to. It adds up, across this many
years, to more people than you ever counted one at a time."

Pell Ostra, checking her own decades of maintenance logs against the same tally, added the harder
truth alongside it. "Not everyone on that list is still alive. You know that better than most of us.
But even the names that end early are still counted, because they were still part of what this
became, however briefly."

Kanja sat with the number a long while, the weight of it settling somewhere the crew rarely saw
displayed so openly. "I never set out to build something this size. I just wanted the people closest
to me to know I meant it, when I said I'd bring them home or be honest when I couldn't."

"That's exactly how something this size gets built," Garren Hask's own descendant said, closing the
ledger. "Not by setting out to build it. By meaning the small thing, over and over, for long enough
that it stops being small."
