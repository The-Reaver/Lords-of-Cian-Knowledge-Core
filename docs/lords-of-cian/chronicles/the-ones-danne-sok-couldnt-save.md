# The Ones Danne Sok Couldn't Save

*Locked canon, Batch 254, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1114`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXXXIV, wave 28, closing wave
28. A genuine failure entry for Danne Sok -- his own split-second call costs a captive's life, the
first real operational failure attributed directly to one of the founding crew.*

---

There were two doors, and Danne Sok had a half a second to choose which one the column's small breach
team went through first. He chose the left, the one closer to the sound of a struggle, the one his
training and every instinct built over eight years told him held the greater danger.

The captive behind the right door died before anyone reached it — not from anything Danne Sok did
directly, but from a guard who'd had exactly the extra seconds the wrong door bought him to finish
what he'd started.

Danne Sok didn't hide from it. He reported it himself, plainly, before anyone could ask, standing in
front of Bane with the specific stillness of a man who'd already run the choice a hundred times in
the walk back and hadn't found a version where he'd have known to choose differently with what he'd
actually had in the moment.

"I had a half second. I went with the door that sounded worse. It was the wrong door."

"You couldn't have known that from outside."

"That's not the same as it not being my call. Six people are alive because the raid worked. One is
dead because I picked left instead of right, and I don't get to fold that into the six just because
the six is the bigger number."

Bane didn't tell him he was blameless, the way half the column would have if they'd been the one
standing there instead. He asked him a harder question instead.

"What would you do differently, exactly, if I put you back in that hallway right now with the same
half second and the same information?"

Danne Sok thought about it honestly, the way the question demanded. "Nothing. I'd make the same call.
I had nothing that pointed to the other door."

"Then that's the only apology you actually have to give, and it's not to me. Say that to yourself as
many times as you need to until it's the thing you remember instead of just the door. The dead don't
get an apology that changes anything. They get you meaning it when you say you'd have chosen
differently if you'd had anything at all that told you to."
