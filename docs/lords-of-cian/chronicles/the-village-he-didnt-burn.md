# The Village He Didn't Burn

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Bane Alias Chronicle XXVI, second entry in the ninth
wave. Rebellion era, age 19, months after the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Marrow's Edge sat exactly between a Trust supply line and a rebellion foraging route, which meant
both sides had reason to want it and neither had reason to protect it, and by the time Bane's column
passed through it had already been picked over twice — once by Trust requisition, once by a
rebellion company less disciplined than his own — until the sixty-some residents left had nothing
between themselves and the coming winter but a granary too damaged to seal against weather and a
well fouled by a collapsed retaining wall.

Nobody was fighting there. There was nothing left worth fighting over. Bane stayed anyway, five
days, with a dozen of his own crew alongside him, doing the kind of labor no account of his career
would ever think to record.

He reset the well's retaining wall himself, stone by stone, alongside two of the village's own
builders who'd never expected the help and didn't quite know what to do with a man whose name
frightened garrisons kneeling in the mud beside them measuring courses. Pell Ostra rebuilt the
granary's roof using salvage timber and a controlled application of her own trade that had nothing
to do with anything explosive. The crew split wood, mended what could be mended, and left before the
first real snow with the village's winter genuinely survivable in a way it hadn't been five days
earlier.

"You could have sent us on ahead and stayed a day," the village's oldest builder said, watching the
column prepare to move again. "Five days is a long time for a man people say can't afford to be
found standing still anywhere."

"Five days is exactly how long the well and the granary needed. I don't measure it against what
people say I can't afford." Bane checked the reset stonework a final time, solid under his own
weight. "Nobody's going to write a story about a well. That's rather the point of doing it."

No garrison ever found them there, no ambush interrupted the work, no dramatic reversal justified
the five days as anything other than what it plainly was — labor, given because it was needed, to
people neither side had bothered to protect. The village endured the winter. The crew moved on to
work that would, eventually, get written down.
