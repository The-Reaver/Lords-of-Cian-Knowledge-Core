# The Titan's Own Hands

*Locked canon, Batch 109, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-376`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle III, closing
the wave. Rebellion era, during the Sewer War of Killane (`MCD-234`, age 20). Not a territory
Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters.*

---

The old tunnel engineer had been conscripted into the infiltration crew for one reason only: he
understood Killane's pre-modern sewer layer better than any map that still existed, having walked
it as an apprentice forty years before the current municipal system had been built over it. He had
not expected, on his third week underground with Kanja's people, to find himself handed a shoring
timber by the alias itself and told to hold it steady while it was set.

"I didn't sign on to work beside you," the engineer said, not unkindly, bracing the timber as
instructed. "I signed on to tell your people where the old tunnels go. I'm sixty-one years old. I
didn't expect the man they're calling a titan to be the one doing the actual digging."

"Somebody has to shore this section before the next charge goes off two tunnels over, or the whole
thing comes down on people who don't deserve to be buried under it." Kanja drove the timber's base
home with three careful, measured strikes, checking the angle after each one the way a man checks
work he intends to trust his own life to later. "I didn't get given hands that only know how to hold
a sword. I had a trade before I had a war. Some days it's the only part of me that still feels like
it's mine."

The engineer watched him work for a while in silence — not the practiced motion of someone
performing labor for an audience, but the genuine, unhurried competence of a man who had done this
exact task a hundred times before it ever mattered to anyone watching. "The soldiers up top think
you're something out of a bad dream," he said eventually. "Eight feet tall, unstoppable, whatever
story's traveled furthest by the time it reaches them. They're not wrong that you're dangerous. But
nobody up there's ever going to believe you spent an hour of a six-week siege setting shoring
timber."

"They don't need to believe it. You do, because you're the one standing under it when it's done."
Kanja checked the last brace, satisfied, and reached for the next length of timber. "The title's
never been about the fighting for me, not really, even when the fighting's what people remember. My
father raised me at a forge before he raised me for a war. I know what it means to build something
with your hands that outlasts you, and I know what it means to watch something you built get torn
down by somebody who never once had to lift the timber themselves. I'd rather be the kind of titan
who still remembers which one of those feels like work."

The engineer said nothing further, but the shoring held through the next three charges without a
single failure, and he found himself, for the rest of the six weeks, volunteering for shifts on
whichever crew Kanja happened to be working — not out of loyalty to a cause he'd been conscripted
into, but out of the specific, unglamorous respect one tradesman learns to have for another who
still shows up to do the boring, load-bearing part of the job nobody writes songs about.
