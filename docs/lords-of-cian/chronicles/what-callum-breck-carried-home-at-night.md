# What Callum Breck Carried Home at Night

*Locked canon, Batch 277, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1436`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XCVII, wave 33,
first entry. Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. A dedicated Callum Breck personal-life register,
distinct from his already-locked coining-the-name entry (`MCD-368`) and his later reflection
entries (`MCD-632`, `MCD-403`-adjacent) -- set before the silence arc (`CC-119`), his daughter Sera
still an infant.*

---

Nobody on the crew begrudged Callum Breck the nights he left early. He'd earned the leaving as
plainly as he'd earned the name he'd chalked onto Tavin Greer's own forehead without asking anyone's
permission -- a dockhand four years older than Kanja, with a wife who never once appeared in any
account of the war and an infant daughter who appeared in every account that mentioned him at all.

What the crew mostly didn't see was the walk home itself -- forty minutes each way, most of it
spent, by Breck's own later admission, rehearsing which parts of the day he'd tell his wife and
which parts he'd fold away rather than carry across his own threshold. She knew what he did. She had
never once asked him to stop doing it. What she'd asked, the one time she'd asked anything of the
work at all, was that he not bring the worst of it into the room where Sera slept -- not the fear,
not the blood he occasionally came home wearing on a sleeve he'd already tried to scrub clean at a
public trough three streets back.

Kanja learned this only because he walked the route with him once, called along on a night neither
of them expected trouble on and found none. Breck talked the whole way, not about the raid two days
past but about a knot in Sera's blanket-wrap he still hadn't learned to tie properly, about a name
he and his wife hadn't settled on for a next child neither of them had decided yet whether the war
made unwise to plan for. He asked Kanja nothing in return, and Kanja offered nothing unprompted --
understanding, without being told, that the walk wasn't an invitation to discuss the work at all,
but the one stretch of Breck's day built specifically to hold nothing but the rest of his life.

At the door, Breck's wife took one look at both of them and set a second plate without asking
whether either had eaten. Kanja stayed twenty minutes, said little, and left before Sera woke,
carrying back to Warehouse Twelve the plain, unremarkable fact that the man who'd given him a name
he hadn't asked for went home every night to a life that had nothing to do with it.
