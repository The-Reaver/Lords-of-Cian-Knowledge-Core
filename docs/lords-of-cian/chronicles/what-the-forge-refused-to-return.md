# What the Forge Refused to Return

*Locked canon, Batch 114, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-390`). Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle II. Rebellion
era, during the Rolling Foundry Campaign (`MCD-241`, age 27), aboard The Anvil. A detailed showcase
of the Trinity defending an active rebuild against a raiding force, using captured incendiary
equipment turned to new purpose. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The raiding detachment struck the eleventh coastal settlement on the Rolling Foundry's route at the
worst possible hour, halfway through re-forging a season's worth of salvaged Dead Drakma into tools
the settlement's own smiths would need for the winter — the forge fires lit, the ore-barge's cargo
hold open and exposed, sixty unarmed apprentices mid-lesson on a beach that had no defensive works
of any kind.

Kanja met the first wave at the waterline, Mafesto already fully online, and did not retreat toward
the forge to protect it. He fought forward, away from it, deliberately drawing the raiders' attention
seaward and away from sixty apprentices who had never held a weapon in their lives.

The raiders had come equipped with incendiary charges meant to finish what the earlier burnings had
started — Dead Drakma-based devices, standard Trust issue, designed to ignite anything flammable
within a tight radius. The first charge thrown landed close enough that Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer
System absorbed the blast's kinetic force directly rather than letting it reach the sand behind him,
converting the detonation's own violence into stored charge that fed straight into Obsidian Malice's
next strike — turning the raiders' own weapon, mid-throw, into the fuel for the strike that broke
their forward line.

A second and third charge followed, thrown wide in the confusion. Onyx caught the pattern before the
raiders themselves understood they'd fallen into one — Cadence Ruin reading three simultaneous
throws as one collapsible rhythm, the same gift that had once unmade a coordinated two-man strike
years earlier now unmaking an entire volley, each charge redirected by a precisely timed deflection
into open water rather than sand, detonating harmlessly offshore in a series of white plumes the
apprentices would describe, for years afterward, as the most beautiful thing they'd ever been
frightened by.

By the time the raiders broke and withdrew, not one had reached the forge, not one apprentice had
been harmed, and the season's tool-stock finished its re-forging on schedule the following morning as
if the raid had simply been an unscheduled interruption rather than an attempt to finish what two
burnings hadn't managed.

"You could have fought them at the forge line," the settlement's head smith said afterward,
surveying the undamaged cargo hold. "Would have been a shorter fight."

"Would have put the forge and everyone standing at it in the middle of it." Kanja checked Obsidian
Malice's housing, satisfied, the war club's charge already bleeding back toward zero. "I didn't come
here to win a fight close to the thing I was trying to protect. I came to make sure the fight never
got close to it at all. That's not always the shorter way. It's usually the right one."
