# The Hostage at the Gate

*Locked canon, Batch 276, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1427`). Bane Alias Chronicle XCVII, wave 33. First
hostage-crisis register for this alias: VB-060's "Already-Finished Negotiation" presence trait used
purely defensively, with no counter-threat offered, to protect held civilians rather than to win an
argument or break a siege.*

---

The message came by a runner released for the purpose: three hostages at the garrison's outer gate —
a shopkeeper, an old man too frail to have done anything to earn it, and a child no older than nine —
and an execution set for dawn unless Bane presented himself alone at the gate before it, unarmed, no
gear, nothing.

"It's a trap built around the one thing you won't refuse," Danne Sok said. "That's not a negotiation.
That's bait shaped like a moral obligation."

"It's still hostages," Bane said, and went.

He left the Trinity with the column entirely — Onyx sheathed and handed to Corren Halst, no blade at
his hip, nothing that could be read as a weapon from any distance. He walked to the gate in the grey
before sunrise with his hands open at his sides and stopped exactly where the officer holding the
hostages told him to stop, and then he did the one thing the officer's plan hadn't accounted for: he
said nothing threatening at all. No declaration. No demand. No promise of what would happen if the
executions went forward. Nothing to argue with, negotiate against, or brace for.

The officer had prepared for a confrontation. He got a man standing perfectly still, entirely
unarmed, offering him nothing to fight and nothing to fear directly — and the absence of an argument
left the officer holding the only weight in the exchange himself. Minutes passed. The child was crying
quietly. The old man had stopped looking at anyone. Bane didn't move, didn't speak, didn't so much as
shift his stance, and the longer the silence held the more visibly the officer's own resolve to give
the order eroded, because there was no version of this where killing an unarmed, silent, unresisting
man's hostages in front of him read as anything but exactly what it was.

"Say something," the officer finally demanded.

"I don't have anything to negotiate with you," Bane said, quiet, the first words either of them had
spoken since he'd stopped walking. "I already came. That was the whole trade you asked for. What
happens next was always going to be entirely yours to decide, not mine."

The officer held the blade at the child's shoulder for another full minute — long enough that Corren
Halst, watching from the tree line with everything in him wanting to move, didn't — and then lowered
it, and released all three, and gave no order Bane's way at all.

Bane walked the hostages back himself, unhurt, unarmed the whole way there and the whole way back.

(a declaration in "The Pivotal Piece," `MCD-365`; an officer's own hesitation in "The Trap Built From

through the absence of anything to react against, rather than through anything said or implied. Also
the first entry where Bane goes fully unarmed and ungeared by his own choice into a scenario he cannot
control the outcome of, distinct from the wager-duel stand-down of "The Duel That Would Cost Nothing
But Him" (`MCD-1425`), where the terms guaranteed a fair fight -- here nothing is guaranteed at all.
No new named characters -- the officer, shopkeeper, old man, and child are unnamed; Corren Halst and
Danne Sok reused. Opens wave 33.*
