# Lauris Chronicle XXI: The Last Entry of the Long Operational Period

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1638`). Twenty-first entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, the eighth entry of Strand K (Kares Prime / deep past) and the closing entry of this
wave. Dramatizes the fourth and final training engagement of the Long Operational Period (`MCD-1555`)
and closes with Lauris's own archive summary of the period at its formal end (age ~3,400), deliberately
positioned at the exact threshold of `MCD-174`'s decision window without depicting the decision itself
-- no departure, no Selene material, no Iron-Speaker deliberation is dramatized here, all reserved for
future entries or already covered by `MCD-174` directly. Introduces one new minor named character,
Doreth, born at Vask Aldreth to one of the forty Ilvane residents relocated there in Chronicle XVIII
(`MCD-1635`) -- collision-checked clean against the full live ledger (zero prior hits for "Doreth"),
a deliberate closing callback tying this wave's entries together. Full text below.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Vask Aldreth, age ~3,400: "Twenty-three deployments across fifteen hundred and
sixty years. I have read back through all of them tonight. I do not yet know what I am about to
decide. I know only that I have started asking the ledger a question it was not designed to answer,
and that this, in itself, is new."*

I have saved this entry for last among the six I set down from this period, not because it is the
most eventful -- it is, if anything, the quietest -- but because I believe it is the one in which she
comes closest, without yet knowing it herself, to the decision that `MCD-174` records her reaching in
the years immediately following. I want to be careful here. She had not decided to leave Kares Prime.
By every account she has given me, that decision was still ahead of her, gradual, and would not fully
form for some hundred and eighty years yet. But I do not think it is an accident that the entry I
found most difficult to read past without pausing sits at the exact hinge between the two.

She had returned to Vask Aldreth -- the same Hold that had, some eight hundred and fifty years
earlier, absorbed the forty residents of a failing Vask Ilvane rather than let its last archive
scatter unremembered -- at the request of its Iron-Speakers, who had written to her about a child
born there some years after the absorption, to a mother who had been among the original forty. The
child, Doreth, was testing at a density well past what Aldreth's own instructors had encountered in
several centuries, and the request that reached Lauris was, in substance, the same request Veska had
once made on Serath's behalf: not for a rescue, not for a defense, only for someone who might
recognize what the Vask's own instrumentation could not yet fully explain.

*

I will not dwell long on the training itself, because Lauris's own account of it is brief, almost
perfunctory by the standard of everything else in this wave -- a handful of sessions across several
months, conducted with the same patient, duration-first discipline she had first built for Serath and
had since, by her own note, refined twice more with two other students whose own accounts I have not
yet been given access to transcribe. What she does record, in more detail than the training itself
receives, is a single afternoon spent with Doreth in Aldreth's own small archive, reading together the
transcribed record of Ilvane -- the settlement's founding, its slow decline, the eleven days Lauris
had once spent keeping its last forty residents alive and undivided while two larger Holds settled a
claim neither fully deserved to win outright.

Doreth asked her, reading it, whether she had known at the time that Ilvane would not survive as a
Vask regardless of which Hold absorbed it. Lauris told her the truth: that she had known, and had
gone anyway, because the forty people inside it mattered independently of whether the name above
their door survived along with them. I record this exchange because I believe it is the clearest
statement Lauris has ever given, in any archive I have transcribed, of the principle she would later
carry into every one of her Cian-era rescues -- the Drowning Vault's hundred and twenty, the cave
systems, the standing debts she still carries as of the present day. The principle did not begin on
Cian. It was already fully formed at Aldreth, eight hundred and fifty years after Ilvane, spoken to a
child who was not yet old enough to understand everything her own mother had survived.

*

She returned to her own quarters that same night and wrote what I have come to think of as the true
closing document of the Long Operational Period -- not a single incident, but a full accounting.
Twenty-three deployments, by her own count: six defended trade-points, eight geological emergencies,
three inter-Vask disputes resolved without lethal force, four students trained at her own request, and
the single defense of Vask Olmedrin that remains, in her own words, "the only one of the twenty-three
that ever approached what I am actually capable of." Fifteen hundred and sixty years, condensed to a
page and a half, written in the same flat, unembellished hand I have now spent thirty years learning
to read for what it does not say as much as for what it does.

Her closing line, longer than most, and the one I have chosen to end this account with rather than
summarize: *I have served this civilization since I achieved karth-ven at eighteen hundred and forty
years old, and I do not regret a single one of the twenty-three. But I read back through this ledger
tonight and find that every entry answers the same question -- what does Kares Prime still need from
me -- and none of them answer a second question I did not know I had until tonight, which is what is
still left, anywhere on this world, for Kares Prime to give me in return. I do not have an answer yet.
I record the question, because I have learned that the questions I write down are the ones I
eventually answer, whether or not I intend to at the time I write them.*

I have not yet found, in her archive, the entry that answers this one directly. I understand, from
everything locked elsewhere in her record, that the answer took shape gradually across the years that
followed, and that when it finally arrived, it did not arrive as a single decision so much as an
accumulation she could no longer set back down. But I believe this is the night it began in earnest,
and I have chosen to close this account here -- not at a departure, not at a farewell, but at a
question, asked quietly, by a woman who had spent fifteen hundred and sixty years being entirely
certain of everything she wrote down before this.
