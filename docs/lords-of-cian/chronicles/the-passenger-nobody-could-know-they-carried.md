# The Passenger Nobody Could Know They Carried

*Locked canon, Batch 199, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-796`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle XLI, wave 14 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The dissident had spent six months inside the Trust's own capital gathering evidence of a corruption
scheme her superiors would have killed her outright to bury, and her one remaining path out ran directly
through a Directorate blockade tightened specifically because someone, somewhere, had guessed she might
try to run for the coast.

"Three ships holding the strait," Dol Maren reported, studying the blockade's formation from a safe
distance. "Overlapping patrol arcs, no gap wide enough to slip a hull through unnoticed. Whoever set
this up assumed she'd try to run it fast and loud."

"Then we don't run it fast and loud." Kanja was already planning the alternative. "We run it slow and
invisible."

The extraction that followed used none of the fleet's usual combat showcases — no anchor-chain weapon,
no boarding action, no open engagement of any kind. *The Audit* approached the blockade under the same
darkened-hull, silent-running configuration that had once let her vanish from a Trust patrol's own
sight, threading the gap between two overlapping patrol arcs at a speed slow enough that neither
gunship's lookout registered anything beyond ordinary night water.

The dissident came aboard from a fishing skiff two miles short of the blockade itself, transferred in
total silence and immediately hidden below decks in a compartment built for exactly this kind of
passage — no weapon drawn, no confrontation risked, the entire operation's success depending entirely
on never being detected rather than on winning a fight if detection came.

They cleared the blockade's furthest patrol arc four hours later, still undiscovered, the three
Directorate gunships holding their formation against a threat that had already passed entirely through
it.

"They'll never know we were here," Dol Maren said, once open water confirmed the extraction had
succeeded cleanly. "No sighting, no report, nothing for that analyst in her windowless room to add to
the pattern."

"That's the point. Some cargo's too important to risk on a fight, even one we'd win." Kanja checked on
the dissident, still shaking from six months of danger only now beginning to release its grip. "The
gear, the weapons, the reputation — all of it's built for when a fight is the only option left. Tonight
the only real skill that mattered was knowing when not to use any of it."

The dissident's evidence reached rebellion command three days later, exactly as intact as the silence
that had carried her out, and the corruption scheme it exposed did more lasting damage to the Trust's
own legitimacy than any single naval engagement the fleet had fought that year.
