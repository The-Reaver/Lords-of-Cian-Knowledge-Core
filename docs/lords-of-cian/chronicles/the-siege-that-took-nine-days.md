# The Siege That Took Nine Days

*Locked canon, Batch 181, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-529`). Bane Alias Chronicle XIV. Rebellion era, age 19,
shortly after the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material — a detailed,
sustained multi-day combat showcase per Abad's craft instruction, distinct from every prior single-
engagement Bane Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The fortified depot at Threshbend had no single weak point worth a decisive strike — triple-walled,
provisioned for months, its garrison drilled specifically against the fast, overwhelming engagements
Bane's reputation had made famous. Breaking it would take patience the reputation had rarely been
asked to demonstrate: not one battle, but nine days of sustained, deliberate pressure.

Kanja gave it exactly that.

The first three days, he did nothing the garrison could report as an attack at all — Mafesto's
sensors mapping every patrol rotation, every supply pattern, while Onyx of Oblivion stayed sheathed
and the depot's own men grew steadily more certain nothing was actually coming. The fourth day, a
single guard post went dark without explanation, its men disarmed and returned unharmed by morning
with no memory of how. The sixth day, two more. By the eighth day, the garrison's own discipline had
begun fraying under an assault that never fully arrived, officers reporting confusion rather than
casualties, unable to identify a pattern in what was actually happening to their own men.

On the ninth day, with the depot's morale already broken by eight days of unexplained attrition, Bane
came through the main gate in full view of everyone — Obsidian Malice discharging once against the
gate's own mechanism, Onyx of Oblivion clearing the courtyard in a sequence the garrison, already
half-convinced they were fighting something inevitable, barely resisted at all. The depot surrendered
within the hour, its commander later admitting the actual battle had ended days before Bane ever
appeared openly.

"Nine days for one gate," the crew's forward scout said afterward, genuinely startled by the
timeline. "You could have hit it hard on day one and taken losses getting through those walls."

"I could have. I'd have lost people doing it, and the depot would have fought to the last man rather
than surrender to a force it could see and measure." Kanja surveyed the depot, intact, its garrison
disarmed rather than destroyed. "Nine days of pressure it couldn't see cost this crew nothing and cost
that garrison its will to fight at all. I've got the patience for nine days when the alternative is
counting bodies afterward."
