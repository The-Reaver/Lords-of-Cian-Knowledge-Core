# The Officer Kanja Chose Not to Ruin

*Locked canon, Batch 260, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1263`). Crow King Alias Chronicle LXXI, wave 24. A
lineage member, not Kanja himself, is tempted toward personal vengeance-by-information against an
officer responsible for real harm. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The intelligence had come to them sideways, the way most of it did -- a false signal thrown to
extract one piece of information had returned three, and one of the three was a name: the officer
who had personally ordered the reprisal burning of a granary district, the kind of order that left
children hungry through a winter for reasons that had nothing to do with any military necessity.

The third generation held the intelligence for two days before she brought it to Kanja, and when she
did, she brought a plan alongside it -- not a tactical strike, but something crueler and entirely
within the craft's reach: a fabricated correspondence trail, false enough to destroy the officer's
standing, his family's standing, everything he had, without a single blade drawn.

"He earned it," she said, before Kanja could ask. "Everything in that trail would be a lie, but every
consequence of it landing would be true to what he actually did."

Kanja didn't answer immediately, and when he did, it wasn't a refusal. "Tell me what it buys us,
tactically. Not what it costs him. What it buys us."

She didn't have an answer that wasn't personal. "Nothing," she admitted. "He's not commanding
anything we need stopped right now. This wouldn't save anyone. It would just make him suffer the way
he made others suffer."

"Then it's not the craft," Kanja said. "It's revenge wearing the craft's clothes, and I understand
exactly why you want it -- I'd want it too, some days. But the day we start using this for what a man
deserves instead of what a plan requires, we've built something I don't think either of us actually
wants to carry."

She let the plan go, though not easily, and the intelligence was used instead for exactly what it
was tactically worth -- confirming a supply route, nothing more -- while the officer himself went
untouched, his real crime recorded, unpunished by the craft, and left instead for whatever justice
the war or its aftermath might eventually bring him.

"I still think he earned it," she said, days later.

"He might have," Kanja said. "That's not the same question as whether we're the ones who should be
the ones who take it from him."
