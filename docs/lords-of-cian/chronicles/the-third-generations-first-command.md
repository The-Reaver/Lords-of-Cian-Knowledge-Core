# The Third Generation's First Command

*Locked canon, Batch 221, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-994`). The Crow King Alias Chronicle LVI, wave 19. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Neither Kanja nor the apprentice was within two days' travel when the request came: a border
settlement, threatened by a Directorate requisition column, needed the same kind of evacuation the
craft had run dozens of times before, and needed it inside a window that left no time to wait for
either of them to arrive.

The third generation ran it alone.

She had led pieces of operations before — a relay position here, a single false signal there, always
within reach of someone who had been doing this longer than she had. This was the first time the
entire shape of a plan, and every decision inside it, belonged to her from the first signal to the
last.

She chose, against what her own teacher would likely have chosen in her place, to abandon the
elaborate multi-signal Braid the situation seemed to call for and run something closer to the plain
relay-chain method built months earlier in a downpour neither of them had personally weathered
together — not because conditions demanded it this time, but because she judged, correctly as it
turned out, that the column's officer was exactly the kind of methodical, procedure-bound reader who
would trust a single clean signal more than he'd trust three clever ones layered together.

It worked. The settlement emptied two hours ahead of the column's arrival, with nothing more elaborate
than one carefully chosen true-sounding report and the patience to let the officer talk himself into
believing it.

When word reached the apprentice, days later, she asked only one question. "Were you afraid, running
it with no one to check your judgment against?"

"The whole time," the third generation admitted. "I kept waiting for the moment I'd need you there to
tell me I'd read it wrong. It never came. I don't know if that means I read it right, or if it just
means I got lucky and won't always."

"It means you made a real decision and it held," the apprentice said. "That's the only test there's
ever been. I made mine alone once too, a long time before you were old enough to watch me do it, and
I spent the whole operation just as afraid as you did."

When Kanja heard the full account later, he asked only whether she'd have made the same choice again,
knowing what she now knew. She said she would, and that this time she wouldn't need to be afraid of
the answer.
