# The Rumor He Never Corrected

*Locked canon, Batch 196, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-681`). Bane Alias Chronicle XVI, wave 6 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The story reached Bane's camp three separate ways inside the same week, which was usually how he
knew a story had legs: a dredge-line worker repeating it as fact, a Directorate leaflet repeating it
as warning, and a rebellion recruiter repeating it as recruitment, all describing the same event —
a garrison outpost at the Kessic salt flats, forty Trust conscripts, every one of them dead by
morning, Bane's mark left carved into the gatepost.

He had never been within eleven days' travel of the Kessic salt flats.

"It's working for us," the recruiter said, when Bane found him and asked him plainly to stop
repeating it. Two dozen new volunteers had come in on the strength of that story alone, convinced
the tide had turned further than it had. "People believe it. People join because of it. Does it
matter that it isn't true?"

"Forty conscripts are dead at Kessic. That part's true — a fever took the garrison, the same fever
that's been moving through three other outposts this season. Nobody carved anything into that
gatepost. Somebody dying of a fever isn't a victory, and it isn't mine, and I won't let it be
counted as either." Bane's voice didn't rise. "You want people to believe fear works. I've built a
whole reputation on letting them believe exactly that, when it's true. This isn't true. If I let it
stand, the next time somebody says I killed forty men, and it's real, nobody outside that room will
be able to tell the difference between the lie and the fact. That's not a reputation. That's noise."

The recruiter argued it another quarter hour — the volunteers were real, whatever the story, and
real volunteers meant real hands on real work that needed doing. Bane didn't dispute any of it. He
simply asked, once more, plainly, for the correction to go out the same three ways the story had
come in, and waited until the recruiter agreed.

The correction traveled slower than the original story always did — corrections always did — and a
handful of the volunteers who'd joined believing the gatepost tale left again when they learned the
truth of it, disappointed to find the war smaller and less theatrical than the version that had
moved them. Most stayed anyway, for reasons that had nothing to do with a story that had never been
real.

"You could have let it ride," Efa Gol said afterward, not disagreeing with the choice, just naming
it. "It cost you volunteers to correct it."

"It would have cost me something worse to let it stand." Bane watched the last of the leaflets go
out, the true account this time, plainer and less frightening than the one it replaced. "The fear
only works if it's true. The moment it stops being true and I let it keep working anyway, I'm not
using the fear anymore. I'm just lying with someone else's death."
