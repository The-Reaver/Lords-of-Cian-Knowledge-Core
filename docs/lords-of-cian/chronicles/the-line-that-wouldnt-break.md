# The Line That Wouldn't Break

*Locked canon, Batch 152, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-480`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XI. Rebellion era,
age 18, before the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material — a detailed
solo-blade combat showcase per Abad's craft instruction, distinct from "What the Sword Remembers"
(`MCD-369`) in focusing on defending others under fire rather than a powers demonstration. Narrated
in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Hired enforcers hit the dredge line at shift change, when the workers were most exposed and least
armed, sent by a site owner who'd finally decided the organizing had cost him enough that violence
was worth the risk of exposure. Kanja was the only one on the line carrying anything sharper than a
work tool.

Onyx of Oblivion cleared its sheath in the same motion that put him between the enforcers and the
nearest cluster of unarmed workers, and what followed wasn't the clean, decisive powers showcase the
line had heard about from the Warehouse Twelve stories — it was close, chaotic, fought in a tight
space crowded with people who couldn't get clear fast enough, every strike calculated as much around
what stood behind an enforcer as the enforcer himself. Whisper of Shadows carried him between three
separate knots of workers in the span of seconds, not to strike faster but to physically be the
obstacle standing where an enforcer's blade would otherwise have reached someone with no way to
defend themselves.

Cadence Ruin broke the enforcers' coordinated advance into six men fighting without a plan, but even
disorganized, six armed men in a crowded space took real effort to hold — Kanja took a shallow cut
across one forearm shielding a worker who'd frozen rather than run, the wound the price of choosing
protection over the cleanest possible fighting angle.

The enforcers broke and fled once it became clear the line wasn't going to scatter and wasn't going
to lose, dragging their own wounded rather than press further against a single blade that had somehow
made an entire work crew feel defended rather than helpless.

"You could have fought that cleaner," the site's oldest worker said afterward, binding the cut on
Kanja's arm herself, refusing to let anyone else do it. "Faster, fewer risks to yourself, if you'd
been willing to let a few of us take our chances."

"I wasn't willing to let anyone take their chances if I could stand in the way of it instead." Kanja
watched her work, letting the correction happen without protest. "A clean fight that costs someone
else more than it costs me was never the fight I wanted to win."
