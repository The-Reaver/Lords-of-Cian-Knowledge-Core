# The Last Reading Before the Peace

*Locked canon, Batch 257, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1198`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle LXXXVII, wave 29, closing the wave. Kanja performs one final structural certification of the tunnel network as the Sewer War of Killane's ceasefire takes effect, closing the loop on MCD-234.*

---

Word of the ceasefire reached the tunnels before Kanja had finished the last section of survey he'd set himself to complete regardless of what happened above ground that day. The siege that had defined this entire alias since its first Chronicle was ending, formally, in hours -- and the network he'd spent the whole of it building, breaking, rebuilding, flooding, sealing, and teaching people to read for themselves needed one final honest accounting before it passed from a war footing into whatever peacetime use it would find next.

"You could stop," Corren Halst said, finding him still working a gallery most of the crew had already left to celebrate above ground. "It's over. Nobody's going to ask you to certify anything today."

"That's exactly why I'm doing it today." Kanja kept working, methodical, unhurried, checking supports he'd shored years back against the same tactile standard the old digger had taught him. "Every reading I've done down here happened because someone's life depended on the answer being right. This one doesn't. I want to know what that feels like, just once, before it's the only kind there is."

It took the better part of the day -- galleries built under fire, junctions held through attrition, the western section that had survived two decades of later stress without a single failure, all of it walked, tested, and signed off clean or flagged honestly where it wasn't. He found nothing that surprised him and one thing that moved him more than he expected: the eastern gallery where "What Even He Couldn't Save" had finally given up its fight against decades of decay was, at last check, fully stable in its sealed state, the collapse that had beaten him years back having settled exactly the way sound engineering predicted it would.

By the time he finished, the ceasefire had already taken hold above ground, cheering audible faintly even this deep. He signed the final certification not as a soldier closing out a siege but as a tradesman closing out a job, the same signature he'd have used on any ordinary contract.

"Network's sound," he told Halst, handing over the completed survey. "Every gallery. Every seam I could reach."

"You didn't have to prove that today of all days," Halst said.

"No," Kanja agreed, looking back down the length of tunnel that had carried this entire war on its back. "I wanted to know it was true. Not because anyone needed saving. Because it deserved to be checked one last time before it became just a place people lived, instead of a place people survived."
