# The Boy He Trained Like He Meant It

*Locked canon, Batch 242, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1063`). The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle LXII, wave 21.
Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench -- Mafesto remains dormant and Obsidian Malice undeployed until the
Black Trench (`MCD-232`), a solo-blade Onyx of Oblivion showcase set deliberately without its named
powers invoked. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Callum Breck brought the boy to him on a rest day, the way a man brings something he's afraid to
carry alone. Nev Torr was sixteen, all wrist and no weight behind it yet, and Breck had been
teaching him footwork in the hour before shift for three weeks running because the boy had asked
him to and because Breck, watching him, had started to be afraid of what would happen to him in a
real fight without it.

"He's fast," Breck said. "Faster than I was fixing to teach him for. I'm out of what I know."

Kanja looked at the boy instead of at Breck. "You want to learn from me, or you want him to stop
worrying about you?"

"Both," Nev Torr said, which Kanja decided he liked better than either answer alone would have
been.

He didn't draw Onyx to begin with — put a stevedore's practice cudgel in the boy's hand instead and
took up its match, and spent the first twenty minutes doing nothing but correcting how Nev Torr's
weight sat wrong on his back foot every time he committed to a strike, leaving him a half-second
behind his own arm. When the boy finally planted correctly and the strike landed clean and hard
enough to jar Kanja's forearm through the block, he said so out loud, plainly, no performance in it.

Only then did he draw the sword.

Not for a demonstration. He told the boy to come at him with everything he had, no restraint asked
for and none given back, and he meant it — Cadence Ruin stayed dark the whole exchange, no
intention-reading, no gifted half-beat of foreknowledge, just a Rex bloodline body that had already
fought sixteen men at once and a boy who hadn't fought anyone yet. He let the fight run honest.
Nev Torr came in low and fast on the third pass, a real opening if Kanja hadn't shifted his hips a
handspan before committing the parry, turning the boy's own forward momentum into the thing that
spun him half around and put him on the ground — not swept, not thrown, just out-angled by a body
that understood where weight wanted to go a fraction sooner than his did. Twice more the same
lesson landed a different way: once on a feint Nev Torr telegraphed with his shoulder before his
blade ever moved, once on a recovery he rushed instead of resetting his stance for.

He didn't let the boy win once, and didn't apologize for it either. "You'll thank me for this before
you thank me for anything else I ever do," he told him, pulling him back to his feet after the last
fall. "Every fighter who ever went easy on you in practice cost you something you'll only find out
you were missing the day it matters."

Breck watched the whole hour without saying much, and afterward, walking the boy back toward the
crew's tents, put a hand briefly on his shoulder in a way that had nothing to do with footwork.

Kanja stayed on the practice ground a while after they'd gone, running the same three corrections
through his own stance out of habit, the way Hask ran figures twice before he trusted them.
