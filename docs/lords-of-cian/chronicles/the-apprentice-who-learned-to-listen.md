# The Apprentice Who Learned to Listen

*Locked canon, Batch 134, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-451`). The Crow King Alias Chronicle IX, closing the
third wave. Rebellion era, age 23. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The young singer who'd carried three hundred interleaved voices during the Braid had asked, in the
weeks afterward, to be taught the craft properly rather than simply deployed as an instrument of it —
a request Kanja hadn't expected and took seriously enough to answer in person rather than delegate to
anyone else in the crew.

"Singing the signals is the smallest part of it," he told her, on the first of what became a series
of quiet lessons between operations. "Anyone with trained breath control can carry a resonance once
they're shown how. The harder part is knowing which lie is worth telling and which one costs more
than it buys — every false signal spends something, some patience or trust or attention that doesn't
come back once it's used. The craft isn't the voice. It's knowing when silence would actually serve
better than the cleverest lie you could sing."

"How do you know the difference?"

"Some days I don't, not certainly. I've been wrong before — early on, more than I'd like to admit."
He didn't soften that admission for her benefit. "The best I can offer is this: listen longer than
feels comfortable before you decide what story the moment actually needs. Most people who fail at
this craft fail because they reach for the clever trick before they've finished listening to what's
actually in front of them."

She spent the following months learning exactly that — sitting with silence in situations where her
first instinct was to sing, learning the specific discomfort of choosing not to deceive when
deception was available and easy. By the time she led her own first solo operation, months later, the
plan she built relied on a single true statement delivered plainly rather than any interleaved lie at
all, and it worked precisely because nobody expecting the Crow King's usual craft anticipated
something that simple.

"You didn't use the trick," Kanja said afterward, genuine approval in it.

"You taught me the trick was never the point. Listening was." She said it simply, without needing him
to confirm it further. "I think I finally understand what you meant."
