# The Agent Who Stopped Filing Reports

*Draft only — not yet locked, pending Abad's approval. Bane Alias Chronicle V. Rebellion era,
within the "Bane" window, a new undercover-surveillance angle distinct from any prior Bane
Chronicle. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose. No new named characters.*

---

The Directorate agent had worked six months to secure a place among the dockworkers Bane's crew
recruited from, and had filed detailed, useful reports every single week for the first four of them
— crew movements, supply patterns, the location of two minor safehouses, nothing that compromised
anyone irreplaceable, exactly the kind of steady, careful intelligence a good agent produces.

The reports stopped the week the agent watched Bane personally carry an injured recruit two miles
on his own back rather than delay the column for a cart that wasn't coming in time, arguing the
entire way with the crew's own field medic about whether the man's leg could survive the jostling,
and losing the argument, and doing it anyway because the alternative was leaving him.

"You're supposed to be gathering intelligence," the agent's handler said, at their increasingly
tense biweekly contact, "not writing me character studies."

"The character is the intelligence." The agent didn't look up from the crumpled page, three false
starts already discarded. "You want troop numbers, I'll give you troop numbers. But if you want to
know why this crew doesn't break the way every other rebel cell we've infiltrated eventually
breaks, it's not the numbers. It's that man does that — the two miles, the bad argument, the choice
that cost him four hours of march time he can't get back — every single time, not just when someone's
watching. I've been in enough units to know what it looks like when a leader's kindness is
performance. This isn't performance. I don't know what to do with a threat assessment that has to
say that out loud."

"Write the report anyway."

The agent tried, three more times over the following weeks, and produced three documents that
technically satisfied the format and said nothing the Directorate could actually use — because
every honest attempt to describe what made Bane dangerous kept circling back to the same
uncomfortable conclusion, that the danger and the decency were the same trait wearing two different
names depending which side of the ledger you were standing on. The final report, the one that
actually reached the archive, was two lines long: *Subject's operational effectiveness cannot be
separated from personal character. Recommend reassignment; this agent's continued proximity risks
compromised objectivity.*

The reassignment was granted within the week. What the file never recorded, because the agent never
told anyone, was that the request wasn't dishonesty covering for a compromised asset. It was the
first genuinely honest thing the agent had written in six months, and the only report in the whole
file that turned out, years later, to have correctly predicted anything that mattered.
