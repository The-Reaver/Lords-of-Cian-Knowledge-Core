# The Decoys Efa Gol Never Used

Efa Gol kept a private ledger nobody else aboard *The Audit* had ever asked to see — not the
operations she'd run, but the ones she'd planned and then, deliberately, chosen not to.

"You have a whole notebook of decoys you never used," Kanja said, finding her going through it during a
rare quiet stretch between engagements. "Why keep planning things you don't intend to do?"

"Because knowing what I'm capable of and knowing what I should actually do aren't the same skill, and
I'd rather practice telling them apart on paper than learn the difference the hard way at sea." She
turned a page — a plan for a false-flag operation elaborate enough to have drawn an entire Trust
squadron away from a convoy it should have been protecting, abandoned before it was ever proposed.
"This one would have worked. It also would have gotten that squadron's own sailors killed chasing
something that was never actually a threat, for no reason beyond convenience. So it stayed on paper."

Another page: a plan to fake the sinking of *The Audit* herself, elaborate enough to convince the
Trust the ghost fleet had finally been destroyed, buying months of unguarded movement before the truth
came out. "Tempting. Would have worked. Also would have crushed every settlement that's built hope on
us still being out here. Not worth what it would cost people who've never even met us."

"Ten Ships That Were One worked," Kanja said, remembering the decoy squadron that had protected a
merchant convoy months before. "What made that one different?"

"That one only cost effort. These—" she tapped the discarded pages "—cost trust, or lives, or hope
that isn't mine to spend just because I'm clever enough to think of a way to spend it." She closed the
notebook. "Anyone can build a trick that works. The actual skill is building ten tricks and only using
the one that doesn't leave a debt somebody else has to pay."

Kanja considered the notebook's weight, thick with plans that had never left the page. "How many of
these have you actually used, against how many you've written?"

"About one in six." She said it without pride and without apology, simply a fact about the work.
"Which sounds inefficient, until you remember the other five are the reason nobody's ever caught me
using the wrong trick at the wrong cost."
