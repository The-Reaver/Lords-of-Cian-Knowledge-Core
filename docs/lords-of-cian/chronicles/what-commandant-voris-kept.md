# What Commandant Voris Kept

*Locked canon, Batch 112, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-385`). Crow King Alias Chronicle III, closing the wave.
Rebellion era, years after the Night of the Crow King (`MCD-236`, age 23), from Commandant Voris's
own perspective -- already a locked named figure from that battle. Not a territory Chronicle. New
standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters beyond the
already-locked Commandant Voris.*

---

Voris kept the original scarecrow.

It was against regulation, technically — captured rebel materiel was supposed to be logged and
destroyed, not retained by the officer it had humiliated — but no one at the Directorate archive had
ever quite found the nerve to demand it back from a man who had built the rest of his career on
having survived being outmaneuvered by it. He kept it in his own quarters, coat and feather crown
both, propped in the corner where a lesser man might have hung a captured banner instead.

"You'd think I'd want it burned," he told the young officer sent to interview him for the Coalfell
inquiry, years after that second scarecrow had turned up on a different garrison's sensor housing
entirely. "Everyone assumes hatred. It's not that. I built a career studying one night I lost as
completely and as elegantly as it's possible to lose without a single shot being fired. That
scarecrow is the only honest teacher I've ever had. Everything since — the triple-redundant grid,
the patrol doctrine, everything the Directorate's built to try to stop the next one — it all traces
back to me, standing in an empty command platform at dawn, staring at a coat stuffed with marsh
grass, finally understanding what I'd actually been fighting."

"And what was that, sir?"

"Not a man. A method." Voris looked at the scarecrow the way another man might look at an old
photograph of someone he'd once loved and lost. "Everyone below my rank thinks the Crow King's gift
is escape. It isn't. Escape is just what the gift produces. The actual gift is that he studies a
system's own confidence in itself until he finds the exact seam where that confidence becomes a
blind spot, and then he doesn't break the system — he uses its own rules against it, so precisely
that afterward you can't even be angry, because every failure traces back to you following your own
procedures exactly as written. I've spent years trying to build something that can't be studied that
way. I don't think it's possible. I think the only real defense against him is becoming unpredictable
enough that there's no confidence left to exploit in the first place, and no army I've ever served
has been willing to pay that price."

The young officer wrote all of it down, dutifully, for a report that would eventually recommend
exactly the doctrine changes Voris had just called impossible to implement at scale — and the
scarecrow stayed in the corner of the commandant's quarters for the remainder of his career, kept
not as a trophy of defeat, but as the only teacher honest enough to show him precisely how he'd
lost.
