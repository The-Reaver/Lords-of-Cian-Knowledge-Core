# The Second Volume

*Locked canon, Batch 285, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1513`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle CII, wave 34, closing the wave. The written creed begun at `MCD-1345` fills its last page and a second volume opens -- a legacy entry closing wave 34 on continuity rather than resolution, deliberately leaving the doctrine's still-open frontier (`MCD-1420`) untouched.*

---

It had taken nine years to fill: the five founding principles, the honest record of every miss alongside every call that held, the overlap-window method born out of a disagreement with no tiebreaker, the small-craft addendum written by hand to close a gap the law hadn't meant to leave. The last blank page had a wrong storm's cold-current gap logged into it, in the third-generation student's own handwriting, dated the same season the glass-network engineer had finally agreed his towers should feed the school's readers rather than replace them.

There was no ceremony planned for closing it. The apprentice noticed first, running a finger down to the page's bottom edge and finding nothing left beneath it, and the small crowd that gathered around the chart table that evening -- the student, the rival fleet's dual-tradition sailor, the northern pilot, three newer students who'd never known a school without a creed already written -- assembled itself the same unplanned way the equinox council once had, nobody summoned, everyone simply present.

The second volume's first page was left blank on purpose for a full week before anyone would write on it, as if committing ink to it too quickly might close something that was supposed to stay open. It was the apprentice, in the end, who wrote the first line -- not a new principle, not a correction to the old five, but a single flagged entry carried forward deliberately unresolved: the Titan-class disturbance no blended tradition could yet chart, the frontier the fleet had held at anchor rather than sail into, copied word for word from the first volume's final honest gap into the second volume's first page.

Kanja read it standing at the student's shoulder, saying little, the way he'd said little at the first volume's founding and little again at nearly every quiet turn since. "Sephtis started this with a scrap of paper and a hunch about pressure," he said finally. "He'd have liked that the second book starts with something nobody's solved yet, instead of something everybody has."

"He'd have liked that it's a book at all," the student said. "He never once believed anyone would still be reading it this far past him."

Nobody wrote anything else on the page that night. It stayed open on the table until morning, the single flagged entry sitting alone at the top of ninety more blank pages, and the coast's weather went on being read, generation by generation, by whoever the craft chose next.
