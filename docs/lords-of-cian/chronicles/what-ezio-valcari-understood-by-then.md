# What Ezio Valcari Understood By Then

*Locked canon, Batch 130, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-439`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle IX, closing
the third wave. Rebellion era, age 19, the Furnace District Strike. Not a territory Chronicle. New
standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters beyond the
already-locked Ezio Valcari.*

---

By the fourth day, Ezio Valcari had spent as many hours on the tally sheets as anyone but Kanja
himself, and he'd started to understand something about the method that hadn't been obvious to him
when he'd first started quietly correcting frightened workers' under-claimed figures upward.

"You could have won this faster," he said, watching the final count come together, the numbers now
undeniable in a way no administrator could argue past. "A show of force on day one would have gotten
their attention just as fast, maybe faster."

"Faster isn't the same as lasting." Kanja didn't look up from the last figures. "A show of force gets
attention because people are afraid of what happens next. The moment the fear passes, so does the
attention, and the administrator goes right back to believing he can wait out the next demand the
same way he waited out this one. Numbers don't work like fear. Numbers stay true whether anyone's
afraid or not. That's slower to build and harder to argue, but once it's built, it doesn't go away
the way fear does."

"That's not how I would have done it, six months ago." Ezio set down his own portion of the tally.
"Six months ago I thought the only way to make someone listen was to make them scared enough not to
ignore you. I've spent four days watching a man listen because the numbers made ignoring him
impossible, not because he was scared of anything. I didn't think that actually worked until I
watched it happen."

"It doesn't always work. Some administrators will burn the ledger rather than honor what it says,
and when that happens, the numbers alone won't be enough — I told someone that plainly, two days
ago." Kanja finally looked up. "But when it does work, it leaves something behind that fear never
does. It leaves a precedent nobody can credibly deny happened. That's worth the extra time it takes
to build."

The strike resolved that evening exactly as the ledger had already made undeniable, and Ezio Valcari
filed his own copy of the tally sheets away — not as a record of what had been won, but as a method
he intended to carry into whatever came next.
