# The Numbers He Couldn't Prove

*Locked canon, Batch 183, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-534`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XIII, first
entry in the fifth wave. Rebellion era, age 19. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material —
the first genuine limit of the tally method in this alias's run. Kept deliberately unarmed, per the
established ethos. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The administrator at the dye-works district didn't argue with Kanja's tally sheets. He produced his
own — a parallel set of records, fabricated wholesale but plausible enough, with no independent
paper trail on either side to prove which account was genuine. For the first time, the method that had
settled every prior dispute through undeniable numbers ran into a dispute where the numbers
themselves were simply contested, with no clean way to prove either version true.

"Your figures and mine disagree completely," the administrator said, unbothered, confident in the
stalemate he'd manufactured. "Neither of us can prove the other's records false. I'd say that leaves
this exactly where it started."

Kanja didn't have an answer built from tally sheets this time. He spent the following days doing
something the method hadn't previously required — not counting, but listening, gathering worker
accounts of specific, dateable events (a payday that fell on a particular holiday, an injury on a
named date) that could be cross-checked against outside records the administrator's fabrication
hadn't accounted for.

It took longer than any prior dispute, and it worked imperfectly — enough inconsistencies surfaced in
the administrator's own fabricated records, cross-referenced against unrelated outside dates, to
undermine his account's credibility without fully proving Kanja's own figures beyond all doubt.

"I couldn't give you numbers as clean as I usually can," Kanja admitted to the workers afterward, the
settlement reached but the victory less total than the method's usual results. "I found enough holes
in his account to make it not credible anymore. I didn't find enough to make mine unquestionable. I
want you to understand that distinction, rather than assume every dispute resolves as cleanly as the
ones you've heard about."

"Does that mean the method doesn't always work?"

"It means the method depends on numbers that can actually be checked. When someone's willing to
simply fabricate a counter-account with nothing to check it against, the numbers alone aren't
always enough. I had to find another way this time, and it cost real time and certainty to do it." He
set the settlement's imperfect terms aside, honest about the gap. "I'd rather you know the method has
a real limit than believe it's infallible and be disappointed the one time it genuinely struggles."
