# The Debt He Paid Before Being Asked

*Locked canon, Batch 232, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1033`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle LIX, wave 20. An administrator raises every wage in the district to fair rates three days before Kanja arrives, and the crew has to build a way to tell a genuine reform from a performance staged to make the ledger unnecessary. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Word of the tally team's approach usually meant a district tightened up — records went missing, overseers grew suddenly deaf to complaints, testimony got quietly discouraged before anyone with a ledger ever arrived. This district did the opposite. Three days before the crew reached it, every posted wage rate went up to match the fairest figures known anywhere in the region, back pay was issued for the past month without anyone asking, and the administrator personally met them at the gate holding his own books open before Ezio had so much as unpacked a pen.

"There's nothing left to find," the administrator said. "I've already fixed it. Ask anyone."

Ezio wanted to believe him and said so, quietly, once they were alone — the raises were real, verifiable, already in workers' hands. Refusing to credit that, just because it arrived early and unrequested, risked punishing the one thing they were supposedly trying to produce everywhere else: an administrator who paid people fairly without being forced to.

Kanja agreed with all of it and still wouldn't call the case closed.

He had Ezio take testimony anyway, worded differently than usual — not what are you owed, but what changed, and when, and what it cost you to wait for it. The answers were consistent and, on their face, encouraging. But three of the oldest workers, unprompted, mentioned the same detail: the new rates had appeared the same week a supply contract with a Trust magistrate's office had come up for renewal, a contract the administrator badly needed and that the magistrate's staff was known to weigh against a district's labor record.

Kanja didn't accuse him of anything. He simply asked to come back in four months, after the contract would already be settled either way, and check the books again — the same rigor applied to every district, no exception made for one that had gotten ahead of the request.

The administrator agreed, visibly unhappy about it, and the crew returned as promised. The rates had held. Nothing had quietly slipped back down once the contract cleared. Whatever the original reason had been, the reform outlasted its own likely motive, and the second visit's ledger entry recorded exactly that — not that the administrator's intentions had been pure, which no one could prove either way, but that the outcome had survived the test of no longer needing to be performed.

"You could have just taken the win," Ezio said afterward, on the road out.

"I did take it. I just didn't take it early." Kanja didn't look back at the district behind them. "A raise that only has to last until a contract clears isn't a raise. It's a deposit. The only way to know which one you're holding is to still be there after there's no reason left to keep paying it."
