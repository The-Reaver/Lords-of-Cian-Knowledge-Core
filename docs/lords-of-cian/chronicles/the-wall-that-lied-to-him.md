# The Wall That Lied to Him

*Locked canon, Batch 188, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-550`). The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XIV. Rebellion
era, age 25. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose. No new named characters.*

---

A Directorate engineer, having studied the doctrine's own diagnostic method closely enough to
understand what it actually listened for, built a fortification wall with deliberately falsified
stress points woven into its construction — decoy tension signatures designed specifically to read as
genuine to anyone diagnosing the wall the way Kanja diagnosed every structure, a trap built not to
resist the doctrine but to deceive it outright.

Kanja caught the falseness before committing, but only barely.

"Something about this reading doesn't sit right," he told his own crew, studying the wall's apparent
resonance a second and third time rather than trust the first pass. "The tension signature's
consistent, almost too consistent — real structures carry small inconsistencies from imperfect
construction. This one reads like it was built specifically to read cleanly."

"Could you be wrong? It might just be well-built."

"It might be. That's exactly why I'm not committing yet." He spent an additional hour confirming the
suspicion, cross-referencing the wall's own construction history against what a genuinely well-built
structure of its age and type should show — and found the falsification, subtle enough that a single
confident pass would have missed it entirely, calibrated specifically to trigger a discharge that
would have accomplished nothing against the wall's actual, unrelated structural weakness.

"He built a lie into the stone itself," Kanja said, once the deception was confirmed. "Not to stop the
doctrine. To make it fail confidently, convinced it had succeeded, while the wall's real weakness went
untouched."

He found the wall's genuine weak point instead — an unremarkable, poorly reinforced section the
engineer's elaborate deception had drawn attention away from, precisely because it looked too
ordinary to warrant the falsification effort spent elsewhere. A single discharge there ended the
engagement the decoy had been built to prevent.

"He understood the doctrine well enough to lie to it," Kanja told the crew afterward. "That's the
first time anyone's tried that specifically. I don't think it'll be the last, now that it's been
tried once. I intend to verify every reading twice from here forward, not just the ones that feel
wrong."
