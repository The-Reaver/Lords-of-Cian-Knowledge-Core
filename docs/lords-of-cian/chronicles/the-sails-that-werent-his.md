# The Sails That Weren't His

*Locked canon, Batch 132, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-444`). The Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias
Chronicle VIII. Rebellion era, age 21, Ghost Harbor. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone
material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The merchant convoy had been raided under black sails flying no colors but the ones survivors
insisted matched the ghost fleet's own silhouette — three ships plundered, two crews left stranded
rather than rescued, in a manner nothing like anything the actual fleet had ever done in a year of
operating this coastline. Word of it reached Kanja within days, along with the unwelcome discovery
that an ordinary privateer had begun deliberately mimicking the ghost fleet's reputation to cover his
own plunder.

He found the man three weeks later, anchored in a hidden cove north of Ghost Harbor, black sails
identical to the fleet's own at a distance and nothing alike up close — cruder rigging, a hull that
had clearly never been built for the speed the real fleet's reputation depended on.

"Clever," the privateer said, not remotely apologetic, watching Kanja's actual flagship close the
distance. "Nobody questions a raid attributed to the ghost fleet too closely. Everyone's too afraid
of what asking might bring down on them. I've made a fair living off your reputation this past year."

"You've made a fair living off people believing I did things I've spent a year making sure I never
do. Two stranded crews, no rescue offered." Kanja didn't raise Obsidian Malice, didn't need to — the
privateer's own crew had already begun quietly putting distance between themselves and their captain,
unwilling to test a fight they plainly didn't believe they could win. "I don't particularly care what
you do under your own name. I care a great deal about what gets done under mine."

"So what happens now? You sink me for borrowed sails?"

"No. You return to every port your raids touched and confess exactly whose work it actually was,
publicly, in front of witnesses who'll carry the correction as far as the false story traveled. I'll
have my own crew confirm it independently in every one of those same ports within the month, so
nobody's left uncertain which account to believe." Kanja held the man's gaze. "The sails come down
today. What happens to you after the confession is between you and whichever of those ports still
wants something from you once the truth is out."

The confessions ran their course over the following weeks, the false attribution unwound port by
port — a slower, less satisfying resolution than sinking the impostor outright would have offered,
but one that left the actual reputation intact rather than muddied by a fight nobody watching could
have properly sourced.
