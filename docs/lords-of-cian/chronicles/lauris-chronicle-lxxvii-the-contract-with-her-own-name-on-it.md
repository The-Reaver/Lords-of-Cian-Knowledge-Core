# Lauris Chronicle LXXVII: The Contract With Her Own Name On It

*Locked canon, Batch 300, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1694`). Seventy-seventh entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, a Strand D entry giving full-scene treatment to Operation 10, previously only
summarized at `MCD-178`: Operations Director Iyellen Macresh, exposed for embezzlement after three
failed Internal Affairs attempts, terminated by Lauris herself at Iyellen's own private estate in
the Kesmara Eastern Hills, closing the Apprentice Contracts. Dramatizes directly, for the first
time, the mentorship-report line already locked at `MCD-177` ("She does not need me. She did not
need me from the second day. The institution should plan accordingly."), showing Vael Korr-Drennen
filing it at the exact close of this operation. Reuses Chronicle LXXVI's Operation 9 discrepancy as
a private, unconnected echo rather than its cause, per that entry's own stated sequence. No new
named characters. No contradictions with any already-locked rule.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Apprentice Contracts, Operation 10: "She recruited me. She signed the paper that
made me External Asset LL-001. I do not know whether the Directorate understood what it was asking
when it gave this contract to me specifically, or whether it simply did not think to ask."*

I have written a great many accounts, across Lauris's career, of contracts that tested her body.
Operation 10 tested nothing of the kind. It is, by every measure of pure physical difficulty, among
the least demanding contracts in the whole of her Directorate service. I record it here at greater
length than its physical demands would justify, because I believe it is the true close of her
Apprentice period in every sense that matters, and because I do not think she has ever, in the
considerable span of years since, entirely finished carrying it.

The Directorate's Internal Affairs division had, by the time the contract reached her, spent the
better part of a year attempting to substantiate what its own quiet audits increasingly suggested:
that Operations Director Iyellen Macresh -- the same officer who had personally recruited Lauris,
signed her onboarding papers, and stood as the institution's public face on the day External Asset
LL-001 first walked through the Eastern District's gates -- had spent the preceding decade diverting
Directorate transport funds through a chain of falsified manifests and countersigned shortfalls not
unlike, though considerably larger than, the small unbalanced column Lauris herself had quietly
noted one operation prior. Three Internal Affairs attempts to build a prosecutable case had failed,
each time, against Iyellen's own institutional standing and the considerable care with which she had
covered a decade of theft. The fourth attempt did not fail. It confirmed, beyond any procedural doubt,
theft on a scale the Directorate judged could not be answered with dismissal alone.

The contract that followed named no other qualified asset. It named Lauris Letitia, specifically, by
designation, for termination at Iyellen's own private estate in the Kesmara Eastern Hills.

*

I do not know, and Lauris has told me she does not know either, whether the Directorate assigned her
this contract because she was, by that point in her Apprentice period, its most reliable and least
sentimental available asset, or because some officer somewhere judged there to be a particular,
deliberate cruelty in making the recruiter answer to her own recruit. She has told me she has chosen,
across the years since, not to spend further effort determining which. Both possibilities, she says,
tell her the same thing about the institution she had signed herself into.

She rode to the estate alone, without escort, and was received -- this detail she has confirmed to
me directly, more than once, because she believes it matters -- not by guards or resistance, but by
Iyellen herself, standing in her own garden, who had evidently understood, by the fact of who had been
sent, exactly what the visit meant before a single word passed between them.

"You," Iyellen said. Not a question, by Lauris's account. A recognition.

"Yes," Lauris said.

I will not dramatize what is not mine to dramatize. Lauris's own report for Operation 10 is the
shortest and plainest of her entire Apprentice period, four lines closing a contract that, by any
other measure, ought to have demanded a great deal more of her than it apparently did. She has told
me only that it was clean, that Iyellen offered no resistance and asked for none of the mercy the
contract's own terms did not permit her to grant, and that she has never, in all the years since,
described the encounter to me as difficult in the way a physical contract is difficult.

She has described it, instead, only once, as the first time she understood that an institution
could ask something of her that had nothing whatsoever to do with strength.

*

Vael Korr-Drennen filed his mentorship report that same week, closing her Apprentice Contracts and
her formal supervision alongside them. I have read the report in full, and I reproduce here only its
final line, already known to those who have followed her career this far: *She does not need me. She
did not need me from the second day. The institution should plan accordingly.*

I asked Lauris once whether she read that report at the time it was filed. She had, she told me --
Vael had shown it to her directly, before submitting it, which she has told me was not standard
practice and which she has never fully explained to her own satisfaction. She remembers agreeing with
its assessment. She remembers, less comfortably, that the report was filed the same week she buried
her recruiter in the Kesmara Eastern Hills, and that no line in it, however accurate, made any
mention of what closing that particular contract had actually cost her to carry.

"He was right that I did not need him," she told me. "He was not right that I needed nothing at all."
