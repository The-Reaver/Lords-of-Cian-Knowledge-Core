# The Youngest Hand in the Yard

*Locked canon, Batch 198, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-744`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XIX, wave 7 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The sorting yard's youngest hand came to the ledger table last, after every adult in the line had
given testimony, and stood there long enough that Ezio finally looked up from the page.

"You're next," Ezio said, "if you want to be."

The boy was small enough that the sorting table he worked at had been built low, years ago, for
hands his size, and he looked at the two of them the way a child looks at adults deciding whether to
take him seriously. "The others said you might not write mine down. That I'm too young to count."

"How long have you worked this yard?" Kanja asked.

"Since I could carry a full basket without dropping it."

"Then you've been earning a wage this district owes you for exactly as long as anyone else standing
in that line, and your number goes in the same book, no smaller letters, no separate page." Kanja
nodded to Ezio, who was already writing. "What's owed you?"

The boy gave a figure — modest, careful, clearly rehearsed the way a child rehearses something he's
afraid will be laughed out of the room — and Ezio recorded it without comment, cross-checked it
against the sorting tallies the way he checked every entry, and found it accurate to the coin.

"That's all?" the boy asked, when it was done. "You're not going to ask why I'm working a yard
instead of somewhere else."

"Somewhere else isn't my business to arrange for you. What's owed you for the work you've already
done is." Kanja closed the ledger over the day's tally and looked at him properly for the first time.
"Is there something you want, past the money? You don't have to say."

The boy thought about it longer than the question seemed to warrant. "I want time to learn my
letters. Not coin for it — the district's already going to owe me more than my family's seen in a
year. Just time. An hour, twice a week, that nobody can take back from me to put me on a sorting
table instead."

Ezio wrote that down too, in its own line beneath the wage figure, unmonetized, exactly as asked —
the first entry in the whole campaign that wasn't a debt in coin at all, and the district
administration, faced with a documented account it couldn't argue down to a smaller number, granted
the hour without ever quite understanding why a child's two weekly hours mattered as much to the
final settlement as the wages did.

*Continuity notes (not narrative): the Industrial Myth's first entry centered on a child laborer,
handled with the same documentary rigor as any adult testimony rather than as a sympathetic spectacle
-- consistent with this world's established long baseline lifespans not changing how childhood itself
is treated at the labor-and-dignity level. Extends "The Youngest Hand" into a non-monetary demand
(time to learn to read) recorded alongside the wage figure, prefiguring the fuller non-monetary
register explored later in the wave. No new named characters. First entry in the Industrial Myth's
seventh wave.*
