# The Wounded He Chose to Carry

*Locked canon, Batch 128, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-431`). Bane Alias Chronicle VII, first entry in the third
wave. Rebellion era, age 19, shortly after the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. New
standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The Suppression Brigade detachment broke before Bane ever closed the distance, the way most of them
had started breaking since the Black Trench made his name mean something worse than a soldier. Six
men went down running rather than fighting — twisted ankles on the scree, one man's own panic driving
him into a ravine wall hard enough to crack ribs — and by the time the ground fell quiet, Bane was
the only combatant left standing on a field that had barely seen a blow struck.

He spent the next two hours carrying them out.

Not killing the fallen, not leaving them for their own retreating column to find or not find in time
— carrying them, one at a time, back toward the Directorate's own lines, setting each man down within
sight of his own patrol before returning for the next. The ribs-cracked one tried to draw a hidden
blade on him halfway through the carry. Bane simply held the man's wrist still until the fight went
out of him, said nothing, and kept walking.

"You could have let them die out there," the crew's forward scout said afterward, watching from the
ridge with open disbelief. "Nobody would have questioned it. Half the Directorate already thinks
you're the thing that doesn't leave survivors."

"That's exactly why I carried them." Bane didn't slow his pace back to the rally point. "The fear
only works if it's true. The moment it stops being true — the moment I actually am the thing they're
telling recruits I am — I've become the story instead of the man underneath it. I don't intend to
disappear into my own reputation before I've had the chance to decide what it's actually for."

Word of the six survivors reached the Directorate's own ranks within the week, filed as an anomaly
no officer could satisfactorily explain: their most feared asset, sparing men he had every operational
justification to leave. It did nothing to soften the fear. If anything, a monster with rules was
somehow worse than a monster without them — but it left something else behind too, quiet and
unspoken, in at least six men who now owed their lives to the thing they'd been trained to dread.
