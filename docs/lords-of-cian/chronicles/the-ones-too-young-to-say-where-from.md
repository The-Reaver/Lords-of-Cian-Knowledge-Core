# The Ones Too Young to Say Where From

*Locked canon, Batch 235, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1042`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LIX, wave 20. A
moral-complexity entry confronting the limit of the crew's reunion doctrine when the freed have no
memory to trace. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Most of the hold at the breeding-stock depot went the way holds usually went — chains off, names
taken where names could be given, a place on the manifest of the freed instead of the bought.

The back compartment was different. Nineteen children, the oldest maybe six, none of them able to say
what port they'd come from or whose hands had held them before the depot's. A few had been born in
the hold itself. Kanja had freed people before who couldn't say their own name from trauma or
language or fear. He had never freed a group for whom there was, in the strictest sense, no *before*
to return them to.

Efa Gol found him still standing in the back compartment an hour after the rest of the ship had gone
quiet, the children already fed and asleep in a borrowed cabin, and didn't pretend the problem was
smaller than it was.

"The ledger works because somebody remembers something," she said. "A route, a face, a name half-
right. What do you trace when there's nothing on the other end of the thread?"

"Nothing," he admitted. "We're not reuniting them with anything. We're deciding where they start."

They talked it through the rest of the night, and the answer they came to wasn't clean. The network
built over decades to trace the *already-lost* back to family would, for the first time, be asked to
do the opposite work — vetting the settlements the crew had already helped stand up, the ones with
enough freed adults and enough stability to take in children who'd remember this ship, if they
remembered anything at all, as the whole of where they came from. Efa Gol was blunt about what that
meant: some placements would be wrong. A network built for finding blood relatives had no proven
method for choosing good strangers, and they would not know for years, maybe decades, which of
tonight's decisions had been the right one.

"Say it plainly, so I don't let myself forget it," Kanja said. "We're guessing, and calling it care."

"We're guessing," she agreed, "with better information than anyone else who'd take them. That's not
nothing. It's just not what you're used to being able to promise people."

He didn't argue it further, and didn't dress it up as a victory in the account Garren Hask kept
afterward. The ledger's entry for that night recorded nineteen names invented on the spot, nineteen
placements chosen by best judgment, and no claim of a debt repaid — only one still owed, open-ended,
to people too young yet to know it existed.
