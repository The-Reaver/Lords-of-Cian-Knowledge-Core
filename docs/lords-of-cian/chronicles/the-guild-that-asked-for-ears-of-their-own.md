# The Guild That Asked for Ears of Their Own

*Locked canon, Batch 283, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1492`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XCIX, wave 33, closing the wave. The miners' guild from `MCD-1491` formally requests a standing doctrine-trained inspectorate, and the second student takes the post. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The guild council that summoned Kanja back to the mine three weeks later didn't want a rescue this time. They wanted to know how many more shafts across their holdings might already carry the same kind of shifted, unread timber that had nearly buried eleven men, and how they might find out before the next collapse came from weather or wear rather than sabotage.

"We can't ask you to live down every shaft we own," the guild master said, plainly enough that Kanja respected the honesty of the ask more than the ask itself. "But we've heard what your teaching's built elsewhere — a Trust academy naming the whole thing a discipline, a shipping consortium hiring your trained engineers at ordinary wages instead of trying to own the method outright. We'd like the same. Someone trained in the doctrine, standing, paid, checking our shafts before they decide to fail on their own schedule."

It wasn't the first institution to ask for something like it, and Kanja turned it over with the same question he always brought to an offer that smelled of money and permanence both: whether it asked for exclusivity, or only for access. The guild wanted access — inspectors on retainer, no claim on the method itself, no attempt to keep other guilds from hiring the same training elsewhere. That was a different shape of request than a licensing board or a royal corps, and it earned a different answer.

"I can't do the checking myself. I've got a doctrine to keep answering for in a dozen places at once, and mines aren't the only structures asking." He thought of the second student, whose hands had steadied years past the age Kanja himself had learned the craft, and who'd spent the last several seasons training apprentices of his own rather than working solo commissions. "But I know someone who's spent longer listening to strained things than I have left in me to spend on any one industry. Ask him."

The second student took the post within the month, the doctrine's first sector-specific standing inspectorate — not curriculum taught at a remove by others (`MCD-723`), not a shipping guild's individual hires (`MCD-1082`), but a doctrine-trained figure embedded permanently inside an entire industry's own working structure, walking every shaft on a schedule before any timber got the chance to shift unnoticed again.

"Feels strange," he admitted to Kanja, the night before his first full inspection tour began, "being the ears instead of training them."

"You've been both for years now. This is just the first time anyone's paid you honestly for it."
