# The One Who Faked the Sound

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XLI. Rebellion era,
age 25. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material — a bait/ambush entry resolved through
judgment rather than technical verification. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters.*

---

The distress signal reached the rebel column secondhand: a Trust garrison's own iron reinforcement,
supposedly groaning under structural failure loud enough to be heard from outside its walls, the kind
of sound that usually meant a structure already compromised and worth finishing off quickly before it
could be reinforced.

Something about the report didn't sit right before Kanja ever arrived to check it himself.

"Structures that are actually failing don't usually announce it this cleanly," he said, studying the
garrison from cover, the reported groaning audible even from this distance — too rhythmic, too evenly
spaced, nothing like the irregular protest a genuinely stressed structure produced. "That's not a
structure complaining. That's someone banging on metal, trying to sound like one."

It wasn't a technical read that caught it — the Aegis-Talisman's lens, held from a distance, actually
returned an ambiguous signature, plausible enough that a less patient diagnosis might have accepted
the bait at face value. What caught it was years of listening closely enough to know the difference
between a structure's own voice and a good impression of one.

He didn't approach. The ambush, built around the assumption that the sound alone would draw him into
range, waited through the night for an engagement that never arrived, its own patience finally running
out before Kanja's did.

"You never touched the structure," the rebel scout said afterward, once the abandoned ambush position
had been confirmed at dawn. "How were you certain, without the Talisman actually telling you clearly?"

"I wasn't certain the way I usually am. I was certain enough, based on years of knowing what real
structural failure actually sounds like versus what someone trying to imitate it thinks it sounds
like. That's not the doctrine giving me an answer. That's just experience, doing what experience is
supposed to do when the instrument itself can't fully settle the question."

"And if you'd been wrong?"

"Then I'd have waited a night for nothing, which costs less than walking into a trap built specifically
to catch someone who trusts a single instrument over everything else they've learned."
