# The Ones Who Never Learned His Name

*Locked canon, Batch 271, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1410`). Crow King Alias Chronicle XCII, wave 31. The
apprentice and the fourth generation travel to the river town and watch the page's practice in
action -- and quietly correct the one place it was about to fail dangerously. Not a territory
Chronicle.*

---

They found the group by the town's water-rights dispute, which had been running low and mean for
three seasons over an upstream mill's diversion channel. A dozen or so people had gathered at the
millpond to hear both sides out -- not a court, not appointed by anyone, just people the town had
started sending arguments to because they were known to sit with a disagreement a long time before
saying anything at all.

The apprentice and the fourth generation stood at the back of the crowd and watched people practice,
without knowing it, the exact discipline they'd both spent years learning under someone else's roof.
One of the group -- an older woman who did most of the actual listening, patient past the point most
people's patience ran out -- let both sides talk themselves in circles for the better part of an
hour before asking a single question that made the miller's own foreman admit, unprompted, that the
diversion had started for a reason that no longer existed. The dispute didn't end there, but it
cracked open honestly for the first time in three seasons.

It nearly went wrong once. A younger member of the group, impatient with how long the older woman was
letting it run, started to claim -- confidently, and falsely -- that he'd personally surveyed the
channel's original grade and knew for a fact whose fault the diversion was. It would have worked. It
also would have poisoned the one thing the whole practice actually depended on, the thing the page
itself had insisted on above every other line: never win by lying, only by listening long enough that
the truth becomes impossible to keep hidden.

The fourth generation stepped forward before he'd fully decided to. Not to reveal who he was --
he never did, then or after -- just to ask, mildly, whether the young man had actually walked the
channel himself, or heard about its grade from someone else. The question was gentle enough to read
as honest curiosity. The young man, caught, admitted he'd heard it secondhand and never checked. The
group's founder -- the older woman -- looked at the fourth generation for a long moment afterward, not
suspicious exactly, just aware that a stranger had just asked precisely the right question at
precisely the right time.

"You've done this before," she said.

"Once or twice," he said, and let it stay there.
