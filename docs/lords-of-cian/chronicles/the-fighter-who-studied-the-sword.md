# The Fighter Who Studied the Sword

*Locked canon, Batch 255, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1140`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle LXXXIII, wave 28.
Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench -- Onyx of Oblivion solo, Mafesto dormant and Obsidian Malice
undeployed (`MCD-232`). A genuine near-defeat by an opponent who studied Onyx's own reported
patterns.*

---

The mercenary the owners' coalition hired that spring had spent four months doing something no
prior opponent had bothered with: reading every account of the Trench Monarch's duels she could find,
secondhand and imprecise as most of them were, looking specifically for the shape of the sword's
gifts rather than the man wielding it. She couldn't have known the names Cadence Ruin or Veil Piercer.
She'd worked out their effects anyway, from pattern alone — a fighter who read timing better than
anyone should, who found gaps in defenses that shouldn't have existed, who somehow turned killing
blows aside by fractions no ordinary skill explained.

Her answer was to fight without any exploitable pattern of her own, borrowing the arrhythmic
duelist's old trick and adding to it something new: instead of simply refusing rhythm, she varied her
attacks' intent mid-strike, committing fully to one line and then, a fraction before contact,
redirecting with a second, fully independent line of force — two attacks compressed into the same
motion, so that whichever gap Onyx found in the first would already be wrong by the time it mattered.

It nearly worked. Kanja took a shallow cut along his forearm in the fight's first minute, the first
wound any duelist studying the sword's own patterns had managed to land, and understood within
seconds that neither Cadence Ruin's timing-read nor Veil Piercer's seam-finding was going to solve a
problem built specifically to defeat both at once.

What he did instead had no name yet. He stopped trying to read her intent before it arrived and
started, instead, tracking the redirection itself — the small, consistent tell in her wrist an instant
before the second line of force replaced the first, a tell that had nothing to do with any of Onyx's
named gifts and everything to do with four years of ordinary bladework training before he'd ever
bonded the sword at all. He met her second line rather than her first, three times in a row, until she
understood her own innovation had already been read and adjusted for the same way it had originally
been built to read and adjust for him.

She yielded rather than press a fight she now understood she couldn't win, and Kanja, breathing hard
for the first time in longer than he could remember a duel making him, didn't press the advantage
further than the yield required.

"She almost had you," Hask said, tending the shallow cut afterward.

"She had a plan built entirely around beating the sword. It worked, for about four seconds." Kanja
flexed the bandaged arm. "What she didn't plan for was fighting the part of me that isn't the sword at
all."
