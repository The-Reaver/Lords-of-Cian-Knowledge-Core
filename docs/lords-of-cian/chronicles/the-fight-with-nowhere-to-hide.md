# The Fight With Nowhere to Hide

The blockade runner's escort — two Trust cutters that arrived on the runner's distress signal —
found Kanja's boarding party in full daylight, flat calm water, and a visibility no fog or rain would
ever offer them again. Every trick Onyx's powers depended on driving weather to sell had nothing to
work with. No rain to vanish into. No pitching deck to turn into cover no training accounted for.

Onyx adjusted without complaint. Whisper of Shadows, denied a storm to hide inside, fell back on the
oldest version of itself — misdirection built from stillness and silence rather than weather, each
step timed to the creak of the cutter's own rigging so that a boarding party crossing open, sunlit
deck registered, to the men defending it, as motion at the edge of hearing rather than sight. Veil
Piercer needed no storm at all; Cadence Ruin even less.

Mafesto took the brunt of the daylight fight directly, no ambush to soften the opening exchange — the
Kinetic Transfer System converting six sailors' committed blows into stored charge across a boarding
skirmish fought entirely in the open, nothing concealed, nothing timed around weather that wasn't
coming. Obsidian Malice discharged twice in full view of both crews, a demonstration rather than a
surprise, and the second cutter's captain ordered his own crew to strike colors before it reached him
directly.

"We'd trained for the fog fight," the first cutter's second officer said afterward, disarmed, watching
the Trinity work in conditions that offered it none of its usual advantages. "Every drill assumed
you'd need weather to be dangerous in. Nobody drilled us for what happens when you're just as
dangerous without it."

"The gear doesn't need the storm," Kanja said. "The storm just makes some parts of the job easier.
Doesn't make the job itself possible or not."
