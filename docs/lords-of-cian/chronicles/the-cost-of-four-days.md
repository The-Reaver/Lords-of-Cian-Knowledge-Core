# The Cost of Four Days

*Locked canon, Batch 119, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-405`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle V. Rebellion
era, unarmed throughout. A harder entry, dramatizing the genuine cost of the alias's patient method
rather than only its successes. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

A foreman's daughter died of a treatable fever on the third day of the district survey, while Kanja
was still four hours from finishing testimony from the tannery workers on the district's far side,
and her father's grief found its way to Kanja's own tent before the sun was fully up the next
morning.

"You've been here three days asking questions," the foreman said, raw and unguarded in a way exhaustion
makes honest. "Three days of writing down what we're owed, while the medicine that could have saved
her sat locked behind a debt neither of us could pay, a debt your own ledger is going to prove was
never legitimate in the first place. If you'd moved faster — taken what was owed by force on the first
day instead of asking politely for three — she might still be here."

Kanja didn't offer the easy defense available to him, the true but hollow observation that the method's
documented outcomes across a dozen districts had freed more people from more debt than force ever had.
"I don't know that moving faster would have saved her," he said instead. "I don't know that it
wouldn't have, either. I don't get to know that, and neither do you, and I'm not going to pretend the
uncertainty makes this easier to sit with. What I know is that a fast, forced solution here would have
been unpayable everywhere else — every debt-holder in the region would have had six months to hide
their records before we ever reached them. The method that saves the most people, over the whole war,
costs some people time they didn't have. That's not a trade I get to feel good about. It's just the
one I've chosen to make, and your daughter is the proof of what it costs when I make it."

The foreman had no answer that made the grief smaller, and Kanja didn't offer him one. He stayed at
the man's side for an hour past when the day's work should have resumed, saying nothing further,
before returning — visibly changed, the crew would say later — to the tannery testimony that still
needed four more hours to complete.

The district's full debt exposure was documented and delivered within the week, the fastest
turnaround the method had yet produced, though no one who witnessed the change ever asked directly
whether that was cause or coincidence.
