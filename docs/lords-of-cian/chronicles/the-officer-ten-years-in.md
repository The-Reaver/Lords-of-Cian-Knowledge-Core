# The Officer Ten Years In

*Locked canon, Batch 264, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1382`). Captain Alias Chronicle LXXXII, wave 28. A years-later check-in on the captured Directorate officer who asked to join the crew, now fully integrated.*

---

He didn't go by any rank anymore, and hadn't for years, though the old habit of standing a fraction too straight when Kanja entered a room had never entirely left him. Ten years since he'd asked to join rather than accept parole, tested the same gradual, unhurried way any unproven recruit was tested, he was, by any honest measure, simply crew now — the kind of steady, unremarkable presence nobody thought to introduce by his old title anymore, because nobody aboard remembered to think of him that way anymore either.

It was Corren Halst who asked him, on a quiet watch, what had actually changed. Not the test itself, she meant — she'd run enough of those over the years to know how the process worked. What had changed in him.

He thought about it longer than the question seemed to need. "I spent most of my service believing the Trust's order was the only thing standing between people and chaos. I still believe order matters. I just don't believe anymore that it has to come from above, handed down to people who never got a vote in it. This crew has more order than half the garrisons I served in, and not one person here was ever ordered into staying."

"Would you go back, if the Trust offered you your old rank and a clean record?"

"They did offer, six years ago. I never told anyone that at the time — I didn't want it read as me still weighing the option." He looked out at the harbor rather than at her. "I turned it down without needing to think about it, which is how I knew the answer had actually changed and wasn't just me telling myself it had."

Halst logged nothing about the exchange in any official record — some things, she'd learned from Efa Gol, belonged to memory rather than ledger — but she told Kanja that evening anyway, because it felt like the kind of quiet proof he ought to hear directly rather than secondhand.

"Ten years is a long test," Kanja said. "Most tests don't need to run that long to mean something. This one did."
