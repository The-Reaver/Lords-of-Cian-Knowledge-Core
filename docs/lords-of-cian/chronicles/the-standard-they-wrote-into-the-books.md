# The Standard They Wrote Into the Books

*Locked canon, Batch 238, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1052`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LX, wave 20,
closing the wave. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign. Not a territory Chronicle.
Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The regional smiths' guild council didn't send a challenger this time, and didn't send a skeptic
either. They sent a formal delegation, three elders and a clerk with a bound ledger of their own, to
a forge terrace two settlements from the tour's end.

"We're not here to test you," the eldest said, before Kanja could ask. "We already did that, years
back, and lost the argument to our own stress-tested iron. We're here because our journeyman
examinations still don't have a name for what half our apprentices are now asking to be tested on."

The senior smith's own successor — the woman who'd taken independent command of a wildfire response
two settlements prior, forging firebreak tools while Kanja worked under her plan rather than his own
— stood at the clerk's shoulder through the whole reading, because it was her own testimony, given
to the council over the preceding month, that had brought them here.

The proposed standard was plain enough once the clerk read it aloud: an open-method rebuild-and-teach
technique, examinable alongside the guild's existing craft disciplines, judged not on speed or
flourish but on whether a journeyman could take an ordinary apprentice with no prior grounding and
bring them to competent, independent work inside a fixed span of weeks. No reference to any alias.
No requirement that a candidate ever meet Kanja at all.

"You could put your own name on it," the eldest offered. "Most who found a method this size would
want it."

"Put her name on it." Kanja nodded toward the senior smith's successor without hesitation. "She's
the one who took it somewhere I never did — command of her own, under her own judgment, no one
standing behind her telling her what to do with it. That's the actual test of whether a method
outlives the person who started it. I passed it on. She's the one who's proven it holds."

The successor didn't argue the honor away the way the senior smith once had. She'd earned her
standing plainly enough, by now, not to need to hand it back. "Write it as the Open-Forge Standard,"
she told the clerk instead. "Not after anyone. After what it does."

The clerk wrote it exactly that way, in a guild ledger that would outlast the campaign, the tour, and
every alias the Directorate had ever bothered classifying — an entry with no proper name in it at
all, examinable by any guild journeyman anywhere the council's charter reached, whether or not they'd
ever once stood near a forge Kanja himself had lit.

"Eighteen months of this," the senior smith said afterward, watching the delegation's wagon start
back down the coast road, "and the thing that actually outlasts you was never going to be a story
about you at all."

"Good," Kanja said. "It was never supposed to be."
