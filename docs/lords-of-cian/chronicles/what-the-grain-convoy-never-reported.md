# What the Grain Convoy Never Reported

*Locked canon, Batch 260, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1272`). Crow King Alias Chronicle LXXX, wave 27. Hoarded
grain is quietly redirected to a starving population, a purely economic-justice register with no
combat. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The drought had nothing to do with the war, and the grain shortfall that followed it would have
happened regardless of which side controlled the region -- but the warehouse holding what remained of
the district's reserve was Directorate-requisitioned, guarded, and being drawn down slowly for the
garrison's own use while the settlements around it went hungry through a season no one had planned
for.

There was no garrison to defeat here, no evacuation to cover, nothing that resembled a battle. The
fourth generation approached it the way he approached everything: as numbers. He spent nine days
studying the warehouse's own requisition ledgers, quietly copied a hand from the clerk's own
distinctive script, and inserted a slow, steady trickle of falsified distribution orders into the
existing paperwork -- grain reported consumed by the garrison that instead reached three starving
settlements through a chain of couriers who never knew what they were actually carrying or why.

It took four months to move enough grain to matter, a fraction at a time, never enough in any single
shipment to draw an audit. No one starved who would otherwise have starved. No one in the garrison
ever noticed their own stores running leaner than the ledgers claimed, because the ledgers, examined
on their own, were perfectly consistent -- the same discipline that had once starved a phantom
position of two convoys, now feeding real people instead of starving a fictional one.

"It's the same trick," the fourth generation said, when someone pointed out the mirror of it. "Just
aimed the other direction. I spent a long time thinking of it as a weapon. This is the first time I
built it as something closer to the opposite of one."

"Maybe it was never really a weapon at all," the apprentice said. "Maybe it's just leverage, and the
direction it points was always our choice, not the craft's."

but deliberately inverted, feeding a starving population rather than starving a phantom position.
Extends the established theme (Chronicle LXIX, `MCD-1261`'s framing of the craft's failures and
successes both being worth recording plainly) that the discipline's real value was never the
performance of cleverness but the restraint and patience underneath it. No new named characters. No new proper nouns -- the warehouse, district, and
settlements all stay unnamed.*
