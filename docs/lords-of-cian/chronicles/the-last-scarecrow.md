# The Last Scarecrow

*Locked canon, Batch 201, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-860`). The Crow King Alias Chronicle XLV, wave 15 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Word of the five-province operation reached Voris within days, filtered through five separate
regional commands that had each, independently and without coordinating with each other, concluded
their own losses were an isolated local failure — only the full picture, assembled slowly across
multiple reports that took over a week to reach a single desk, revealing that all five had happened
within the same six-hour window, the work of considerably more hands than the single man Voris had
spent five years studying.

He found, tucked into the last of the five regional reports to arrive, a description of a scarecrow
left at a river crossing two provinces from anywhere he'd ever personally served — coat, feather
crown, propped without ceremony against a ford-marker post.

He hadn't left the original in years. He didn't need to, anymore, to understand exactly what this
one meant.

"They're not doing this alone anymore," he told his own second, the report spread across his desk
alongside the scarecrow he still kept in the corner of his quarters, years past any operational use
for either. "Five provinces, one night, and I don't believe for a moment he was in five places at
once. He's built enough people who can do what he does that he doesn't need to be everywhere the
trick shows up anymore."

"Does that trouble you more, or less, than when it was just him?"

Voris considered the question with the same care he'd once given the very first scarecrow, years
before. "Less, I think, in a strange way. I spent years studying one man's method, hoping to find its
edges. A method that can be taught, carried, and carried again by people who never met me and never
will — that's not a man anymore. That's something closer to a discipline outliving whoever first
built it, the way any real craft eventually does. I don't think there's an edge left to find in
that. I think I finally understand what he actually built, all those years ago in the marsh, wasn't
a trick at all. It was something meant to keep going long after the man who started it stopped being
the only one who could."

He kept both scarecrows in the same corner, from that day forward — the first, and the last he
expected to ever personally receive, side by side, a career's worth of study resting quietly between
them.
