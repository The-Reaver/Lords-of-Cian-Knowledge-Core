# What the Charter Actually Said

*Locked canon, Batch 255, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1121`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle LXIV, wave 22.
Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. The registrar's Trust-charter offer (`MCD-1064`) comes back
answered, and the answer fails its own condition.*

---

The document that reached Warehouse Twelve six weeks after the registrar's visit was thick,
handsomely bound, and — Garren Hask found within an hour of reading it — quietly broken in exactly
the place Kanja had told the man not to break it.

"Verification stays answerable to the districts," Hask read aloud, tracing the clause with one
finger. "That part's here, word for word. Then it says the districts elect a 'certifying panel' to
oversee it. And then, three pages later, in a section about funding eligibility, it says the panel
has to include two Trust-appointed financial auditors before a district's figures qualify for
charter backing." He set the document down. "That's not oversight. That's the owners' figures
answering to the Trust again, just with an extra layer of paperwork between them and admitting it."

Kanja read the clause twice himself before he spoke. "It's not a trap. I don't think he meant it as
one."

"Doesn't matter what he meant. Matters what it does."

It did not appear to have been written in bad faith — the registrar's own covering letter was
apologetic in places, noting language the offices above him had inserted after it left his hands,
and asking, almost too honestly for a Trust document, whether the district side could live with it.
Kanja spent two days weighing the honest arithmetic Hask had already run once before: sixteen
trained verifiers against four hundred districts, and now a charter that could, in theory, multiply
that reach by a hundredfold, if the auditor clause didn't quietly hollow out the thing it claimed to
scale.

He did not send back a counteroffer. He sent back the charter itself, the auditor clause circled in
Hask's own hand, with a single line beneath it: *this is the clause. Fix this one and send it again.
Everything else can wait.*

No answer came before the season turned. Hask kept the returned copy in the same drawer as every
other unresolved figure the district was still owed, filed under nothing more dramatic than a
district and a date.

"You could have signed it," he said once, weeks later, not quite a question.

"I could have signed something with my name on it that let the Trust decide whose figures counted."
Kanja didn't look up from the tally sheet in front of him. "That's not a charter. That's a longer
way of losing."
