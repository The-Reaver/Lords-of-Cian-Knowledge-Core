# The District That Asked to Be Next

*Locked canon, Batch 277, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1440`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle CI, wave 34.
Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. A formal expansion request, testing the method's scalability
constructively and contrasting the failed distant imitation (`MCD-947`) and the successful
independently-carried replication (`MCD-1435`).*

---

The delegation arrived without weapons and without demands, three foremen from a river-trade
district well beyond the five the tally method had ever reached, come to ask for something no owner
or Trust officer had ever asked for outright: that Kanja bring the method to their own docks
himself, in person, the way he'd built it in his own five districts from the ground up.

He told them no before they'd finished the request, and watched the refusal land as though they'd
expected worse.

"I can't be five places at once, and I won't pretend the method needs me standing in the room to
work," he said, "which means if it only works with me in the room, it was never actually built
right in the first place." What he offered instead took the delegation by surprise more than the
refusal had: two trained clerks out of Warehouse Twelve's own classes, neither one a fighter, both
carrying nothing more than the verification discipline itself and a written account of exactly how
five districts had built cross-witnessed trust one dispute at a time. No chalked name traveled with
them. No reputation arrived ahead of them to do any of the work the discipline itself would have to
do on its own.

The foremen left unconvinced that a pair of clerks without him could accomplish what his own
presence had accomplished five times already. Garren Hask, watching them go, allowed that they had a
point worth taking seriously -- the districts that already trusted the method had learned to trust
it because they'd watched him refuse to lie to them under pressure, not because two strangers
arrived with a well-written pamphlet.

"Then the clerks will have to earn that the slow way, the same way I did," Kanja said. "I earned it
by being wrong in front of people and admitting it. I can't hand that part to anyone. I can only send
people willing to be wrong in front of strangers and admit it just as honestly."

Whether it would take was a question nobody at Warehouse Twelve could yet answer, and Kanja declined
to promise the foremen it would before the clerks had even packed.
