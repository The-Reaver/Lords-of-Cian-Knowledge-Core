# What He Taught Before They Ever Fought

*Locked canon, Batch 196, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-699`). Bane Alias Chronicle XXXIV, wave 12 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Six new recruits joined the column the same week, none of them older than seventeen, all of them
eager in the specific untested way of people who had heard the Bane name a dozen times and never
once held a blade in anger. He could have handed them to any of the crew's experienced hands for
training. He took the six himself instead, three mornings running, before any of them so much as
saw a real engagement.

"Nobody's fighting anyone today," he told them, the first morning, watching six faces fall at the
absence of anything resembling the stories that had brought them here. "Today we're talking about
what happens to your hands the first time you actually have to use what I'm about to teach you
against a person instead of a post. I'd rather you hear that from me now than learn it for the first
time in the middle of a battle where there's no time to process it."

He taught them stance before strikes, breathing before speed, the plain unglamorous mechanics of
staying alive long enough to matter rather than the dramatic technique the stories implied he must
be built from. One recruit, faster to frustration than the rest, asked on the second morning why
none of it looked like what people described — the mountain that moved, the fog nobody could track
him through.

"None of that helped me until I already knew how to stand." Bane corrected the recruit's grip
without any of the theatrical weight he carried into a parley. "The stories skip the part where I
was exactly this unremarkable once, learning exactly this unremarkable stance, because unremarkable
doesn't travel the way the rest of it does. I'm not teaching you to become a story. I'm teaching you
to still be alive in six months so you get the chance to become whatever you actually become."

By the third morning the frustration had mostly burned off into something steadier, six recruits
moving through the same basic sequence with visibly more discipline than they'd started with, still
a long way from anything that would make a story, exactly as intended.

"You could have had any of us teach this," Efa Gol said, watching the third morning's session end.
"You don't usually spend three mornings on six recruits who haven't even seen a fight yet."

"I know. I'd rather spend it here than find out later that one of them died because the first thing
anyone taught them was the part that sounds impressive instead of the part that keeps you breathing
long enough to matter." Bane watched the six head off to their first real assignment, steadier than
they'd arrived. "The mountain moving is a story for later. This is the part that actually decides
whether there is a later."
