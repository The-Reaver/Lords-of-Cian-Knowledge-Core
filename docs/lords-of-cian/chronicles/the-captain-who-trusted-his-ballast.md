# The Captain Who Trusted His Ballast

*Locked canon, Batch 192, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-569`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XXIV, wave 8 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The squadron's commander asked for one thing before he was taken into custody: a chance to say, to
whoever would eventually write the official account of the engagement, that his design hadn't failed.

"Write it down accurately," he said. "The hulls held. The gun decks stayed dry. Every problem I set
out to solve, I solved. My ships fought better in that storm than any fleet in this navy's history has
fought in one. I built exactly what I said I'd build."

"Then what happened," the officer taking his statement asked, "if the ships did everything you
promised?"

"I built a fleet that could survive the weather. I never once asked what my crews would stop doing
once they believed it." He was quiet for a moment, watching rain still beading on a gun-deck shutter
that had held, structurally, exactly as designed — sealed shut around a gun crew that had never fired
a shot. "I spent a year teaching them not to be afraid of the storm. I never taught them to still be
afraid of the man using it. Those turned out to be two different lessons, and I only ever taught the
first one."

He requested, and was granted, a formal note in the record distinguishing his ships' engineering
performance from his tactical defeat — insisting, correctly, that the two shouldn't be conflated in
whatever lesson the navy eventually drew from the loss.

"Tell the next one who tries this," he said, as he was led away, "that hardening the ship was never
the mistake. The mistake was thinking hardened meant safe. Those aren't the same word, and I paid a
year of work to learn they aren't."
