# What the Name Would Carry Without Him

*Locked canon, Batch 264, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1390`). Captain Alias Chronicle XC, wave 30, closing the wave and this run. A final, reflective closer looking toward the distant future, deliberately left open rather than resolved.*

---

Kanja walked Pier Nine alone after the gathering broke up, the way he sometimes did when a night had held more warmth than he quite knew how to carry all at once. The wall caught the last of the lamp light, names old and new cut into stone that would outlast every hand that had ever held a chisel to it. Somewhere behind him, the ledger's keeper was closing out the evening's last figures under Hask's watchful, proud correction. Somewhere further off, Corren Halst was already back at the small, unglamorous work of a chair who'd learned that leading mostly meant making sure the structure kept running quietly rather than needing to be seen doing it.

He thought, as he sometimes let himself think on nights like this one, about a future none of tonight's table would live to see — a version of Pier Nine generations past every name he could currently put to it, run by people who'd only ever know "Captain" as an old, half-legendary story rather than a man who'd once let himself be handed a basin and told to make himself useful in a healer's hall. He didn't know, yet, what that future looked like. He suspected, the way he suspected most true things about this particular alias, that he wasn't meant to know it in advance. That not knowing was, itself, closer to the point than any answer would have been.

"You built something that doesn't need you to keep existing," Efa Gol had told him once, years before, counting coin at a table much like the one behind him now. He understood it fully only now, watching the wall hold its names in the dark, the way understanding some things only ever arrived slowly, one funeral and one wedding and one ordinary evening at a time.

This account leaves him here — not at an ending, because nothing about this alias has ever pointed toward one, but at a pause worth marking: ninety Chronicles into a name given freely rather than imposed, the founding table gathered once more, the wall still standing, the ledger still counting, the crew still choosing this, one ordinary day after another, exactly the way they always had.
