# The Night They Came for the Blade

*Locked canon, Batch 215, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-935`). Bane Alias Chronicle LI, wave 17. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Every part of the plan depended on Bane being asleep, and for once, the plan was right about that
much. Three nights of hard marching had put him under fast and deep in a tent no different from
any other in the camp, Onyx of Oblivion racked within arm's reach the way it always was and Mafesto
and Obsidian Malice set aside within the same easy distance — close enough to matter, far enough
that a man sleeping heavily wouldn't wake reaching for them out of habit alone.

The two who came for the blade had studied the camp for a week from a ridge line half a mile out,
long enough to learn the watch rotation's one real gap — the fifteen minutes between the third
watch's return and the fourth's start, when the sentries changed and, for a few minutes, nobody
new had eyes on the tent line yet. They came through that gap on foot, unarmed except for the
climbing hooks meant to lift the blade out through the tent's rear seam without waking anyone at
all.

What they hadn't studied, because there was nothing to study from a ridge line, was Garren Hask's
own habit of walking the tent line himself most nights before he slept, ledger in hand, checking
supply counts against what the day's marching had used — not a guard duty, never assigned to him,
just a thing he'd done since before there was a column worth counting. He came around the tent's
rear corner with a lamp and a tally sheet and found two men flat against the canvas with a hook
already through the seam, close enough to Onyx's rack to touch it.

He didn't shout. He set the lamp down where its light fell full across both of them, said nothing
at all for three full seconds, and then said, in the same flat voice he used for a bad supply
count, that the camp had eleven hundred people in it who would all be awake in under a minute if he
raised his voice once.

Neither man tested whether he meant it. Bane woke to find the pair already bound, Hask's ledger
open to a fresh page recording the hour and what had nearly happened, and the blade exactly where
he'd left it.

"You could have shouted first," Bane said.

"I could have. Then I'd have had eleven hundred people awake and two men with nothing left to lose
standing next to your blade in the dark." Hask closed the ledger. "This way I had a lamp, a tally
sheet, and them not knowing yet whether I was alone."
