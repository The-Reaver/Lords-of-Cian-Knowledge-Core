# The Wave That Gave No Warning

*Locked canon, Batch 284, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1503`). Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle CI, wave 34.
Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241). A detailed, battle-intense Trinity
showcase built around a mass evacuation against a natural catastrophe with no enemy involved. Not a
territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

There was no patrol to catch, no sabotage to trace, and no fire to answer. The undersea tremor that
sent a wall of displaced ocean toward a low coastal forge settlement gave exactly one warning: the
tide pulling back from the shore twice as far and twice as fast as any low tide should, a full
quarter-hour before the water came back the other way.

Kanja had that quarter-hour and nothing else. Roughly nine hundred people lived within reach of the
coming water, most of them settlement families with no forge business at all, spread across a strip
of shoreline too long for any ordinary evacuation to clear in time. He didn't fight anything. He
moved people, and moved them faster than anyone watching believed the Trinity's gear was built to
allow.

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System, designed to ground and redirect force rather than generate it, was
turned instead toward carrying — Kanja running loaded carts and litters at a pace no ordinary team
could sustain, the system grounding the accumulated strain of each sprint back through his own frame
rather than letting it slow him. Obsidian Malice's discharge, shaped into narrow, controlled pulses
the way it had once cleared paths through a pilgrimage crowd, blew open two jammed cart-tracks and a
collapsed footbridge rail in the seconds it would have taken hands alone to clear them properly. Onyx
of Oblivion's Cadence Ruin, reading the settlement's own layout by rhythm rather than combat pattern
for the first time, located every straggler still inside collapsing structures faster than a
door-to-door search could have managed, and Whisper of Shadows carried the last of them clear with
less than two minutes of the quarter-hour left when the water finally arrived.

The wave took the shoreline's outer buildings whole and left the forge terrace itself half-flooded
and structurally sound. Every person accounted for on the settlement's own tally survived. Garren
Hask, cross-checking names against the pre-tremor census that same night by lantern light, found the
count matched exactly — the first time in the campaign's tour that a full catastrophe had produced no
gap in his own ledger at all.

"You didn't fight anything today," a settlement elder said afterward, watching floodwater recede
from the terrace steps. "I've heard what your name means everywhere else on this coast. I didn't
expect it to mean this."

"Most of what this floor does isn't a fight," Kanja said, still breathing hard from the last carry.
"It just usually looks like one because a fight's easier to tell a story about than a quarter-hour
spent moving people fast enough."
