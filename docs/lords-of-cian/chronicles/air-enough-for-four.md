# Air Enough for Four

*Locked canon, Batch 195, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-676`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XLI, wave 14 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The mine shaft's ventilation collapsed on an ordinary working morning, sealing four miners behind a
rockfall with no fire, no enemy, no war involved at all — only a slowly thickening air pocket and a
clock neither side of the collapse could see or stop.

Kanja was working a nearby section when the alarm reached him, and he didn't wait for the
settlement's own rescue crew to organize before starting.

"How much air do they have?" the settlement foreman asked, watching him assess the collapse's
geometry with the same unhurried reading he brought to every structural problem, despite the clock
plainly not affording unhurried time.

"Depends how calm they're staying in there and how large the pocket actually is behind this fall.
Could be four hours. Could be one." Kanja traced the collapse's shape, identifying the same kind of
secondary path he'd once found in a Directorate-sealed mine — not a known access point, but a
narrow fracture the collapse itself had opened, too small for a full rescue party but not for a
single determined effort. "I'm not going to guess. I'm going to move like it's the smaller number
and be glad if I'm wrong."

Mafesto's frame let him work the narrow fracture at a pace no ordinary miner could sustain, clearing
debris continuously rather than in the rest-limited bursts human endurance alone would require,
Obsidian Malice used sparingly and only where a discharge's controlled force could clear a stubborn
section without risking the fracture's own stability. There was no combat in any of it — nothing to
fight, no enemy to meet, only stone, air, and a clock he refused to trust as generous.

He broke through in just under two hours, the four miners conscious but disoriented, air thinner
than any of them had wanted to admit even to themselves.

"You didn't know if we had four hours or one," the settlement foreman said afterward, watching the
miners carried clear. "You moved like it was one the whole time."

"Because moving like it's one and being wrong costs nothing but a little wasted urgency. Moving like
it's four and being wrong costs four lives I'd never get back." Kanja checked the last miner's
breathing, satisfied it was steadying. "That's not really a hard choice, once you say it out loud
like that. I'd rather work myself past exhaustion for nothing than rest comfortably for something
that costs this much."

to the war — distinct from "What Stood While It Was Falling" (a combined combat/engineering

rather than a known deadline. No new named characters. Second entry in the fourteenth wave.*
