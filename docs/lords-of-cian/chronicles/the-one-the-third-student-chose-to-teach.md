# The One the Third Student Chose to Teach

*Locked canon, Batch 263, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1343`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LXX, wave 24, first entry in the wave. The third-generation student, now the school's senior forecaster, selects and begins training her own apprentice -- the fourth generation of Sephtis's craft.*

---

She had put it off longer than either the retired successor or Kanja had expected, and when the retired successor finally asked her why, the answer wasn't reluctance.

"I wanted to be sure I wasn't choosing someone who reminded me of me," she said. "That's the easy mistake. You look for the student who already thinks like you, and you call it good judgment instead of what it is."

The one she chose instead was a boy who asked more questions than he answered, who'd twice gotten a reading wrong in open training and both times said so before anyone else noticed, and who had, in three years at the school, never once tried to shortcut the ledger's discipline of logging a miss the same way as a hit.

"Why him," the retired successor asked, not a challenge, genuinely curious.

"Because he's already better at being wrong than I was at his age," the student said. "The rest of it I can teach. That part, I've watched people wash out over." She meant the boy who'd walked away years earlier, unable to bear being visibly wrong with lives on the line -- a memory the whole school still carried as its clearest lesson in what the craft actually cost.

The first lesson she gave him wasn't a chart or a pressure-reading. It was Sephtis's own original ledger, the running record now three generations deep, opened to a page from decades before the boy was born -- an honest miss, dated and explained in Sephtis's own hand, the kind of entry the school's whole tradition had been built to protect rather than hide.

"Read this first," she told him. "Before you learn a single thing about wind or pressure. I want you to understand what you're agreeing to before you agree to it."

Kanja, present as he so often was for this alias, without ever quite being asked, thought of every apprenticeship he'd witnessed across every alias he wore -- forge-work, blade-work, ledger-work, siege-craft -- and how rarely any of them opened with the teacher's own oldest failure instead of the craft's first success.

"That's not how most people start something," he said.

"Most people are afraid the apprentice will quit if they see the cost too early," the student said. "I'd rather he quit now than break later, in front of a fleet, the way it broke the last one who didn't know what he was signing up for."
