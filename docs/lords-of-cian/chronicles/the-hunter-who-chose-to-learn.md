# The Hunter Who Chose to Learn

*Locked canon, Batch 282, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1482`). Crow King Alias Chronicle XCVIII, wave 33. The
Directorate officer who once tried to deceive Kanja himself returns years later asking to be taught.
Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

He came alone and unarmed, which was the only reason the fourth generation let him get close enough
to speak before deciding what to do about him.

The lineage recognized him before he said a word: the same Directorate officer who had once built a
false signal specifically to turn the Hymn-Engine's own logic back on Kanja, caught only because the
fourth generation had cross-checked his sources' suspicious over-consistency one season before it
would have worked. He'd left Directorate service not long after that failure, he told them plainly,
because the case had never stopped bothering him -- not the loss, which he'd absorbed and moved past
years ago, but the shape of what had nearly worked. He hadn't come back to try again. He'd come to
ask how it was done.

"You spent a career hunting this," Kanja said. "Now you want to carry it."

"I spent a career hunting the wrong half of it," the officer said. "I built my whole method on
finding the lie. It never once occurred to me the actual craft was listening first. I got closer to
beating you by accident, doing it wrong, than I ever did on purpose, doing it the way I thought it
worked."

The third generation was the one who asked the question none of the others wanted to. "Why should we
believe a man who spent years trying to end this."

"You shouldn't," the officer said. "Not yet. Not on my word." He held out both hands, empty, and let
the silence sit there rather than filling it with anything that would have sounded like an argument.

They didn't teach him that day. Kanja set a single condition instead: a full year working alongside
the lineage on operations that cost him something real -- risk, labor, nothing kept back for himself
-- before anyone spoke of teaching him anything. The officer agreed without negotiating the terms
down, which the apprentice noted afterward was itself the first real evidence any of them had.

"We've turned away people who wanted this for the wrong reasons before we ever really tested them,"
she said. "We've never once had someone who wanted it *because* he'd been on the other side of losing
to it. That's not the same test. I don't know yet if it's an easier one or a harder one."
