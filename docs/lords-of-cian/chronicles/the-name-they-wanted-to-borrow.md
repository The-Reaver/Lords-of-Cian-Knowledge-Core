# The Name They Wanted to Borrow

*Locked canon, Batch 215, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-934`). Bane Alias Chronicle L, wave 17. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The delegation from Verrow's Landing walked two days to reach the column, and their request had
nothing to do with the Directorate at all. A separate problem had been working the coastal road
for a year — a loose confederation of raiders, opportunists rather than soldiers, who preyed on
exactly the kind of small, undefended settlement Verrow's Landing was. The delegation's elder
spoke plainly: they didn't need Bane to come. They needed permission to say he might.

"Paint the name on the granary door," she said. "Tell the next patrol that passes through that
we're under his protection. We don't need it to be true. We need them to believe it's true long
enough to go raid somewhere that isn't watched."

Bane sat with that longer than the delegation expected him to. Every use of the name until now
had been his own — a thing he chose to carry into a room himself, spent and paid for in his own
presence, win or lose. This was different: people two days' walk from any column he actually led,
invoking a threat he might never be positioned to make good on, against raiders who would have no
reason to check the claim before testing it.

"If I say yes," he said finally, "and they test it, and I'm three provinces away when they do — the
people who die finding out I wasn't coming die because I let you borrow something I couldn't
actually lend."

"They might not test it at all," the elder said. "That's the wager. You'd be betting on a story
doing work you don't have to do. We're the ones asking to make that bet, with our own granary."

He gave them a narrower yes than the one they'd asked for: they could say the column had passed
through and left warning, which was true, and let whatever grew from that grow on its own, but he
would not let anyone paint his name on anything he hadn't stood in front of himself. Halst thought
it was a distinction without a difference. Bane didn't argue the point, only repeated what mattered
to him about it — that whatever protection the name bought them, it would be bought by something
that had actually happened, not something invented to sound like him.

The raiders tested the coastal road twice more that season and skipped Verrow's Landing both
times, for reasons the elder was still turning over months later, unsure whether the true warning
had done it or the story that grew past it.
