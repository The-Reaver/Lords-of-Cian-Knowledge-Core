# What Maret Vos Chose Again

*Locked canon, Batch 264, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1369`). Captain Alias Chronicle LXIX, wave 23, closing the wave. Years after nearly walking, Maret Vos is offered an honorable way out and declines it.*

---

The offer came from Corren Halst, not Kanja, and she made a point of it being that way: Maret Vos had given the crew years now, ever since the night he'd almost walked and stayed instead on nothing but the promise that leaving would always be his to choose without owing anyone an explanation (`MCD-593`). He'd since become one of the Second Chance's steadiest hands, the kind of quietly reliable presence nobody thought to worry about anymore, which was exactly why Halst thought the offer was overdue.

"You've more than earned a clean exit if you want one," she told him, in front of no one, the way the original almost-leaving had been handled in front of no one either. "A trade, a settlement, land if you want it — whatever would actually let you build something that isn't this. Nobody would think less of you. I'd be the first one saying you'd earned it."

He thought about it for three full days, which surprised her more than an immediate answer either way would have. When he came back, it wasn't with gratitude or with the old defensiveness that had nearly driven him off the crew the first time — just a plain, considered answer.

"I know what leaving with dignity looks like now. I didn't, the first time — I thought staying or going was about pride, about not looking like I needed anyone. I know better now. And knowing that, I'm choosing to stay. Not because I still don't know how to leave. Because I've actually thought about it properly this time, and this is still what I want."

Halst didn't push further. She logged it, the way Efa Gol would have, one line in the crew's own private accounting: not a debt owed, not an obligation honored, just a free choice made twice by the same man for two entirely different reasons.

"That's the whole point of the charter," she told Kanja that night. "The first time he stayed because leaving felt like failing. This time he stayed because he actually looked at leaving and picked us anyway. Those aren't the same thing, and I wanted him to know the difference before either of us let it go unspoken any longer."
