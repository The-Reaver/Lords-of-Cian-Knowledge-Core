# The Doctrine They Taught Their Own Engineers

*Locked canon, Batch 197, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-723`). The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XXVIII, wave 10 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The general who had once filed a career-ending report admitting there had been no ground worth taking
found himself, a year later, invited to lecture at a Trust military academy — not as a disgraced
officer, as his superiors had once framed him, but as the closest thing the Directorate had to an
expert on a doctrine none of them had ever managed to counter.

He hadn't expected the reversal. He hadn't asked for it either.

"They spent a year deciding my failure was actually the most honest account anyone had produced," he
told Kanja, the two of them meeting under an informal parley flag neither fully trusted but both had
agreed was worth the risk. "Now cadets study my report as a case study. Structural vulnerability, not
terrain dependency. They've built half a semester's curriculum around a single admission I made
expecting it to end my career."

"What do you teach them, if not how to fight me?"

"How to think about tension-bearing structures the way you clearly already do. Not how to beat you —
nobody's found that yet, and I'm not confident they will. How to recognize what you're reading before
you read it, so at least the next generation of engineers builds with the vulnerability in mind
instead of discovering it mid-engagement, the way I did."

Kanja considered that for a long moment. "You're teaching them to see the world the way I see it."

"I'm teaching them to stop being surprised by it. That's not the same as beating it. I don't know that
anyone beats a doctrine built on genuinely understanding what holds something together. I know that an
institution which keeps discovering that fact for the first time, one broken column at a time,
deserves whatever it gets. I'd rather my own people stopped being that institution."

"That's a strange kind of respect to build a curriculum out of."

"It's the only kind I had left to offer, after the report. I might as well make it useful to someone."
