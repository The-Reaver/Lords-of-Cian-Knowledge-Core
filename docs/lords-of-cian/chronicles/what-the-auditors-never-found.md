# What the Auditors Never Found

*Locked canon, Batch 255, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1145`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle LXXXVIII, wave 30.
Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. A final Trust review of Warehouse Twelve's own books closes the
charter/sanctions thread, modestly.*

---

The review team that arrived at Warehouse Twelve that spring carried orders that made no secret of
their purpose: a full audit of the tally table's own record-keeping, ostensibly routine, actually the
last available pretext for an institution that had failed to gut the method through a compromised
charter and failed to strangle it through quiet sanctions to try a third approach — find something
wrong with the books themselves, and use it to discredit the whole enterprise at once.

Garren Hask received them without ceremony and without objection, opening every ledger he kept to
scrutiny he'd already survived once, at Canal House, under considerably more hostile questioning.

"You're not worried," the lead auditor observed, three days into a review that had so far found
nothing beyond the ordinary imprecision of hand-kept records.

"I've had people try to find something wrong with these books before. The books were built to survive
exactly this." Hask didn't look up from the figures he was cross-checking against the auditor's own
copy. "You're welcome to keep looking. I'd rather you look for as long as it takes than have you stop
early and claim you found something you didn't."

They looked for eleven days, the longest and most thorough examination the tally method had ever
been subjected to, cross-referencing years of entries against outside records, interviewing owners
and workers across every district the method had touched. What they found, in the end, was exactly
what Hask had told them they would find: honest figures, honestly kept, cross-witnessed the way the
method had always claimed. No hidden favoritism toward cooperating owners. No fabricated shortfalls
used to extract concessions. No trace of the coercion the earlier owners' petition had alleged and
failed to prove.

The review's formal closing report, when it finally reached Warehouse Twelve, offered no apology for
the charter's failed auditor clause and no acknowledgment that the licensing reviews had ever been
coordinated punishment. It simply stated, in the flattest bureaucratic language available, that the
tally-verification practice at Warehouse Twelve had been found, upon exhaustive review, to meet or
exceed the Trust's own standard for reliable dispute documentation — grudging, undramatic, and, Hask
noted, the closest thing to a formal vindication the method had ever received from the institution
that had spent two years trying to either own it or break it.

"That's not a victory parade," Kanja said, reading the closing line twice.

"No," Hask agreed, filing the report in the same drawer as the returned charter and the sanctions
list. "It's just true, and written down where somebody official had to sign their name to it. Some
days that's worth more than a parade."
