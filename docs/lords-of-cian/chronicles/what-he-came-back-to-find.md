# What He Came Back To Find

*Locked canon, Batch 263, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1363`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XC, wave 30, closing the wave. Kanja reflects with Efa Gol, bookending Chronicle LVII (`MCD-986`), on what thirty waves of this alias have actually taught him -- and on the strange, quiet honor of building something that finally didn't need him at all.*

---

Efa Gol found him on deck the evening after his return, the same quiet hour she'd found him in years earlier when this alias had first turned his thoughts private rather than tactical. "You've got the season's ledger with you," she said, nodding at the pages still under his arm. "That's not usually a thing you carry around after reading it once."

"I keep going back over the same page," he admitted. "Not because anything's wrong with it. Because nothing is. A whole season, and my name isn't on a single line of it."

She considered that, the way she considered most things, without rushing toward comfort or correction. "Bothers you?"

"No," he said, and meant it, though it had taken him longer than a single conversation to be sure. "That's what I keep sitting with. It doesn't bother me at all. I think it might be the thing I'm proudest of, out of every alias I've ever worn." He thought back across thirty waves now -- a duel decided by skill, a fleet saved from drowning, a false positive that cost real coin, a fourth generation reading a sky alone for the first time, a woman he'd watched teach and retire and die, a season that had simply happened without him needing to be anywhere near it.

"Every other name I carry exists because somebody, somewhere, needed the weight only I could bring," he said. "Bane. The Titan. The Scourge. Even Captain, in its own way -- they needed me specifically, at least once, or the story doesn't happen the way it happened. This one's different. This one was never really about me being there. It was about proving that a thing built well enough doesn't need any one person there forever, not even the one who helped protect it into existing."

"Sephtis knew that," Efa Gol said. "First time I ever heard him talk about training a successor, that was the whole reason he gave. Not that he was tired. That the doctrine would only really matter once it could outlive him."

"It outlived him a long time ago," Kanja said. "It's outlived his successor now too. It ran an entire season without outliving me, exactly, but without needing me either. I think that might be as close as this alias gets to finishing."

"Finishing's not the same as ending," Efa Gol said. "You know that better than most, carrying names as old as you do."

"No," he agreed. "It isn't. There'll be another season, another storm, another student some of these people haven't trained yet. This alias isn't done. I don't think it's supposed to be." He set the ledger down, finally, and looked out at water that had, this season, kept every promise the school's own readings had made about it. "But thirty waves in, I think I finally understand what it was always building toward. Not a fleet that trusts me. A fleet -- a coast, a council, a craft -- that learned to trust itself, because enough honest people spent enough years teaching each other how."

Efa Gol didn't answer that right away. She just sat with him a while, the way she always had, while the water stayed calm and the sky, for once, had nothing at all to say.
