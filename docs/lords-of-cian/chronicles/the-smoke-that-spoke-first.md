# The Smoke That Spoke First

*Locked canon, Batch 259, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1237`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LXXII, wave 24,
closing the wave. Age 225, V3 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose.*

---

The coastal town's own watch-captain fired the signal wrong the first time — too short a burst,
gone before it caught the wind properly — and had to strike a second flare from the same stolen
capsule to get the full column rising the way she'd been told it needed to. She wasn't crew. She'd
never met him. What she had was a single Signal-mode canister, one capsule through a directional
nozzle, visible to five hundred meters, salvaged eleven years earlier from a raid the black-sailed
fleet had run through this same stretch of coast and left behind along with the freed captives — and
a half-remembered instruction, passed down through the town's own watch rotation like a piece of
inherited equipment nobody had ever expected to actually use.

He was three miles out when the column caught his eye, recognized the exact shade and rise pattern
his own crew used for exactly one thing: *help needed, here, now*. Nobody aboard had planted that
signal. Nobody aboard had told this town how to read the code that far past a raid's own aftermath.
It had simply spread — passed from the people who'd been freed to the people who took them in, kept
as a piece of protective folklore in case the debt ever needed calling in.

The debt, that night, was a raiding party of a different kind entirely — not slavers, opportunists,
testing a coastal town's defenses now that its reputation for protection had made it a target worth
probing precisely because everyone assumed it was protected. The signal brought exactly what it was
built to summon. The raiders found out, badly and quickly, that the folklore hadn't been
exaggeration.

Afterward, the watch-captain tried to return the canister. He told her to keep it — it wasn't his
signal anymore, hadn't been for eleven years, and a code that worked for anyone who needed it badly
enough to learn it right was worth more spread than hoarded.
