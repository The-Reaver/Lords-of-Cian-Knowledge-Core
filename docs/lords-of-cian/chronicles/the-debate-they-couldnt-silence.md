# The Debate They Couldn't Silence

*Locked canon, Batch 278, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1444`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XCVI, wave 32, closing the wave. The method's first formal public debate, argued in front of a neutral audience rather than won through documentation or a tribunal ruling.*

---

The merchants' assembly hall had never hosted anything like it: a moderated forum, called at the request of three regional administrators who had grown tired of losing wage disputes to a ledger they couldn't out-argue in private, proposing instead to discredit the method in public, once, before an audience of neutral guild members who would judge the outcome themselves.

Their chosen advocate was a Trust-trained rhetorician named for his skill at exactly this kind of hall, and his argument, when it came, wasn't about any single figure in any single ledger. It was that an outside crew with no standing in these districts had no right to adjudicate anything at all — that the real offense wasn't inaccuracy, it was interference.

"Then let's not argue accuracy," Kanja said when his turn came, "since he hasn't challenged a single number we've ever written. Let's argue standing. Who does have the right to say what a man is owed for his own labor, if not the man himself, given an honest way to say it?"

"An outside ledger arriving uninvited," the rhetorician answered, "answering to no authority these districts elected."

"We don't ask you to answer to us. We ask the arithmetic to answer to itself." Kanja gestured to Ezio, seated with three volumes he hadn't opened once through the entire proceeding. "Every figure in every one of those books was checked against the district's own records, by the district's own workers, and posted for the district's own administrators to challenge if they found an error. Not one has, in three years. If standing means anything here, it belongs to whoever's willing to be checked and hasn't flinched from it yet."

The rhetorician pressed twice more — interference, precedent, the danger of setting an example other outsiders might follow with less care — and each time Kanja returned the argument to the same ground: not whether the crew had a right to arrive, but whether anyone in the hall could point to a figure that was wrong. No one did. The guild's own vote, taken by show of hands at the evening's close, wasn't unanimous, but it wasn't close either.

"You never raised your voice once," Ezio said afterward, closing the volume he'd never had to open.

"I didn't need the room. I needed the numbers to still be true when he ran out of other places to point." Kanja looked back at the emptying hall, unsettled less by the win than by how easily the argument could have gone differently in front of a less patient crowd. "That's the part that should worry us — not that we won today, but that the whole case rests on nobody ever finding the flaw. We'd better make sure there isn't one."
