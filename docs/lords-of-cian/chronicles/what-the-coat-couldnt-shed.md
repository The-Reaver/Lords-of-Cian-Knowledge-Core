# What the Coat Couldn't Shed

*Locked canon, Batch 262, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1322`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LXXVI, first
entry in the twenty-sixth wave. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241). A
gear-vulnerability entry showing real Forge-Coat damage and improvised field repair. Not a
territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The tear happened the ordinary way most real damage happened — not in a dramatic exchange but in
an unglamorous accident, a collapsing scaffold catching the Forge-Coat's shoulder seam against a
jagged support beam during an otherwise unremarkable rebuild, opening a gash in the Dark-Drakma
leather long enough that its liquid-shedding, light-absorptive treatment (MCD-292) simply stopped
functioning across the whole panel. Rain soaked straight through where it should have run off.
Torchlight caught the exposed weave where it should have vanished into it.

"There's no spare panel," the senior smith's successor said, turning the coat over in the
terrace's evening light, tracing the tear's edge with a professional's eye rather than a tailor's.
"Not out here. Whatever made the original treatment work took years to develop. We don't have
years."

Kanja had gone without the coat's full function before, briefly, in smaller ways — a scuffed boot,
a dulled gauntlet edge. He hadn't gone without a whole panel's worth of it, and the difference
showed the very next night, a routine patrol nearly catching sight of him at a range the coat
should have made impossible, torchlight glinting where matte black should have swallowed it whole.

The fix, when it came, wasn't a repair in any sense the Forge-Coat's original craft would have
recognized — the successor working from what she actually had on hand rather than what the ideal
treatment called for, a stopgap compound of rendered fat, ground charcoal, and a thin cut of
salvaged Dead Drakma dust worked into the torn leather by hand until it held a rough approximation
of the shedding surface, ugly and matte and nowhere near as effective as the original, but enough
to blunt the worst of what the tear had exposed.

"It's not what it was," she warned him, checking the seam under torchlight one more time before
calling it finished. "Maybe two-thirds of the original function, if that. It'll need real repair
the first chance we get somewhere that actually has the materials."

"Two-thirds is enough to not get seen by accident," Kanja said, testing the shoulder's give.
"That's the only promise I actually needed tonight."

He wore the patched coat for six weeks before a stop finally carried the right materials for a real
repair, and in that time learned something about the gear he'd built his whole persona's caution
around — that its silence and its shadow were a manufactured advantage, not an inherent one, and
that the man wearing it was never quite as invisible as the coat alone made him feel.
