# Lauris Chronicle XXVIII: Four Hours to Spare

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1645`). Twenty-eighth entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, a Strand D entry giving full-scene treatment to Operation 17, the Drowning Vault
breach clearance already referenced at `MCD-179` and `MCD-1547`, and by name in Chronicle IV
(`MCD-1564`, "alone against twenty-three engineered subjects in a facility a mile beneath the
sea"): her thirty-hour-window race against secondary-containment failure roughly 1,200 meters deep
in the Voskharen Trench, viable without pressurized equipment via her Ironstorm Blood's
oxygen-binding capacity. Dramatizes the controlled Phalanx-arrested descent, the fourteen-hour
engagement against the twenty-three released Karesian-derived constructs, and the eight-hour manual
reconstruction of the breached primary containment barrier, resealed with four hours to spare.
Closes on deliberate dramatic irony, consistent with `MCD-183`'s reserved Operation 25 reveal: she
believes she has contained a breach, not knowing the true scale of what her reseal preserved. No new
named characters.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Established Hunter designation LL-001-S-017: "Thirty hours were given. I used
twenty-six. I record the four hours remaining not because they were generous, but because I have
learned to distrust any margin I did not personally verify before trusting it."*

Operation 17 reached Lauris as a race rather than a hunt, which by the Established Hunter period had
become, by her own private reckoning, the rarer and in some ways the more honest kind of contract --
no opponent to out-think, no institution to manage, only a clock and a failing wall roughly twelve
hundred meters beneath the surface of the Voskharen Trench. A Directorate facility, submerged, had
suffered a primary containment breach; secondary containment, the Directorate's engineers estimated,
would fail within thirty hours unless the primary breach was resealed by then. No Directorate diving
gear could operate at that depth for any useful duration. Lauris's Ironstorm Blood, binding oxygen
directly into her own biology rather than requiring her to carry or refresh it, made the depth simply
another distance to cross rather than a barrier requiring specialized accommodation -- a distinction
I have come, across enough of these accounts, to recognize as the recurring shape of nearly every
contract the Directorate considered impossible and she considered merely long.

She descended by controlled Phalanx-arrested fall, the Integrated Variable Phalanx's Triad-Lock
configuration braking her descent against the water's own resistance rather than requiring a
separate mechanism, reaching the facility roughly four hours after entering the water. I have asked
her, more than once, what four hours of controlled falling through absolute darkness feels like, and
she has told me, each time, that she used the descent to plan rather than to feel anything about it
-- a habit I have come to recognize, by now, as characteristic of her rather than evasive.

The breach had released twenty-three subjects into the facility's outer chambers: combat-capable
constructs, Karesian-derived in substrate, their density running somewhere between four and six
thousand times baseline, trained -- she recognized this within the first several engagements -- in
disciplinary forms drawn unmistakably from deep Karesian archival access, consistent in method with
the guardian construct she had encountered some operations earlier at the Salt-Locked Archive. Twenty-
three opponents of this caliber, encountered across a facility she did not have time to survey
properly before engaging them, would by any ordinary account have constituted the entire substance
of a contract this size. She cleared them across roughly fourteen hours, methodical rather than
rushed even against a closing clock, because she has told me she considers haste against opponents
of unfamiliar training the single fastest way to convert a difficult contract into a fatal one.

*

What remained, with roughly twelve hours of the window still available, was the breach itself --
and here Operation 17 departs from nearly every other engagement in her Established Hunter years,
because what she did next was not combat at all. She rebuilt the wall. Manually, stone and
structural member set back into place by hand across a further eight hours, the same patient
argument-by-argument reconstruction I have since watched her apply to considerably smaller
problems, scaled here to a task the Directorate's own engineers had assumed would require a
specialized recovery team days to complete once the immediate crisis had passed. She resealed the
primary containment barrier with, by her own final count, four hours remaining in the original
thirty -- a margin she has told me she trusted only once she had personally verified the seal's
integrity herself, twice, rather than accept the Directorate's own instrumentation as sufficient.

She reported the operation as a clean success, which by every measure the contract had set, it
plainly was. Secondary containment held. The thirty-hour window closed with margin to spare. What
she did not know, could not have known, standing exhausted at the reconstructed wall with the
facility's outer chambers finally quiet behind her, was the true scale of what that reconstruction
had preserved rather than released -- a question her own report left entirely unasked, because
nothing in the operation's scope had given her reason to ask it.

She would not learn the answer for eight further operations. I do not record it here, because it is
not yet, in the account this entry is telling, hers to know -- but I have chosen to close this
entry acknowledging its shadow rather than pretending it is not there, because I think a reader who
already knows what Chronicle VIII records of her present-day maintenance visits deserves to
recognize this moment for what it quietly was: not the end of a debt, but its beginning, sealed
behind a wall she rebuilt with her own hands and believed, for a time she now recognizes as far too
long, to be simply a job well finished.

Her closing note, written that day, in the plain and satisfied register of a contract genuinely
well closed: *Thirty hours given. Twenty-six used. The wall holds. I have verified it myself.* I
have chosen not to append to it the knowledge she would not possess for years yet. She has asked me,
more than once, to let that entry stand exactly as she wrote it then.
