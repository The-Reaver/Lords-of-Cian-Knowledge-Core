# The Fire That Asked for No Enemy

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XLVIII, wave 16, closing the wave. Rebellion era, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (age 27), roughly sixteen months into the eighteen-month tour. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. The senior smith's successor (established "What the Senior Smith Passed Down," `MCD-887`) leads for the first time; sixty apprentices appear as a group. No new named characters.*

---

The smoke reached the settlement before the fire did, carried in on a wind that had held dry for six
weeks straight — no Trust raid behind it, no punitive order signed by anyone, just a lightning strike
on parched grassland two ridges over and a summer that had given the flames everything they needed to
travel.

The younger smith had the floor's tools sorted into firebreak use before Kanja had finished reading
the smoke's direction off the ridgeline. "Scythe blades, hoe iron, anything with an edge that can cut
a break through dry brush faster than hands alone. We won't out-forge a wildfire. We can out-cut the
ground in front of it."

"You're already running this," Kanja said, watching her direct two apprentices toward the bellows for
a rush batch of blades rather than wait for him to say so first.

"Somebody has to, and the fire's not waiting for either of us to decide who." She didn't look up from
the anvil. "You said once the floor judges the work, not the person doing it. Floor doesn't care whose
name is on the plan right now. It cares whether the break gets cut before the wind shifts again."

He fell in beside her rather than take the lead back, working the second anvil while she ran the
first, the sixty apprentices splitting without being told between blade-forging, brush-cutting, and a
bucket-relay run off the settlement's own well — no enemy to fight, no ambush to read, nothing to do
but move faster than the wind and hope the ground cooperated.

It held. The break cut wide enough, finished in time enough, that the fire turned along the line the
apprentices' cutting had given it rather than through the settlement or the standing grain beyond it.
By the time the wind finally shifted for good, the worst of it had already burned itself out against
bare cut ground with nothing left to carry it forward.

"First time all campaign we've fought something that wasn't trying to hurt anyone," the younger smith
said afterward, watching the last smoke thin over blackened grass that would green again by spring.
"No garrison behind it. No order signed anywhere. Just weather and bad luck."

"Doesn't change much of what we do about it, though, does it." Kanja set his tools down, watching the
apprentices coil the last of the relay line, tired and unhurt and entirely unbothered by having spent
the day fighting an enemy with no face at all. "Rebuild what's damaged. Move fast enough that the
damage doesn't get to decide what happens next. Whether it's a torch or a lightning strike, the method
doesn't ask who lit it."

"No. It really doesn't." She looked at the blackened break line, cut clean and holding, entirely her
own plan carried out start to finish. "Guess that's the actual proof it was never really about you
either. Fire doesn't know your name. Ground held anyway."
