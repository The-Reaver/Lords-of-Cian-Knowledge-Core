# The Officer Who Read Every Report

*Locked canon, Batch 199, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-789`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle XXXIV, wave 12 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The Directorate analyst had never commanded a ship in her life and had no intention of starting — her
entire contribution to the war effort was a windowless room, four years of accumulated sighting
reports, and a stubborn conviction that a pattern nobody else had found was still there to be found if
someone was patient enough to look properly.

She found it eleven months before anyone aboard *The Audit* learned she existed: a correlation between
every allowed sighting, every vanished engagement, every deliberately unresolved rumor, and the tides
governing the specific stretch of coast the fleet favored for its psychological operations. The ghost
fleet, she'd concluded, wasn't unpredictable. It was simply patient in ways no prior analyst had had
the discipline to track across enough data to see the shape of it.

Her report reached a Trust squadron commander who, for once, took an analyst's paperwork seriously
enough to act on it — positioning three ships not where the fleet had last been seen, but where her
pattern predicted it would choose to be seen next, weeks before the sighting she'd forecast actually
occurred.

It nearly worked. *The Audit* sailed into water that should have been empty and found three ships
waiting with a precision no prior pursuit had ever managed, close enough that only Dol Maren's own
last-second reading of an unfamiliar current bought the margin needed to avoid an engagement fought on
entirely the enemy's terms.

"Someone found the pattern," Kanja said afterward, studying the near-miss with genuine unease. "Not
luck. Not a lookout's guess. Somebody actually did the work of understanding *why* we do what we do,
not just where we've done it before."

The analyst's name never reached the fleet, and never would — she stayed in her windowless room,
refining her model with the new data the near-miss provided, patient in exactly the same register the
fleet itself had always relied on to stay ahead of everyone else.

"We have to change," Dol Maren said. "If she's found the tide correlation, she'll find the next
pattern too, given enough time."

"Then we stop giving her a pattern to find." Kanja was already reworking the fleet's own operational
rhythm, deliberately introducing the kind of inconsistency that would read, to anyone building a model,
as noise rather than signal. "She's the first person on their side who's actually earned the right to
call us predictable. That's worth taking seriously. It's not worth losing to."

Neither side ever learned the other's name, and the quiet contest between a windowless room and a
darkened deck continued for the rest of the war — the first opponent the ghost fleet had faced who
understood it not through legend or fear, but through simple, patient study.
