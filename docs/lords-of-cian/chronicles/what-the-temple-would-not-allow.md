# What the Temple Would Not Allow

*Locked canon, Batch 260, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1269`). Crow King Alias Chronicle LXXVII, wave 26. The
lineage self-imposes a limit against using the craft on neutral, sacred ground. Not a territory
Chronicle.*

---

The fastest route past the garrison ran directly through the grounds of a shrine both warring sides
still, by unspoken agreement, left alone -- a place war orphans from a dozen different villages had
been quietly gathering and burying their dead for three years, tended by an old caretaker who owed
allegiance to neither the Directorate nor the crew and had made that fact plain to both.

The plan was simple enough: a false signal thrown from inside the shrine's grounds would have given
the crew four uncontested minutes to move an evacuation past the garrison's own sightline. It would
have worked. The third generation had the pattern ready before anyone raised the question of whether
they should use it at all.

Kanja was the one who stopped it, though he made clear afterward it wasn't only his call. "This
ground stays outside what the craft touches," he said. "Not because it's more sacred than a farming
family's safety was, the time we got that wrong. Because both sides already agreed, without ever
saying it to each other directly, that this is the one place neither of them brings the war. If we're
the ones who break that first, we're the ones who taught both sides it was never really a truce, just
a habit waiting for someone to break it."

They found the slower route instead, adding most of a day to the crossing and costing them a
tactical window that never fully reopened. The evacuation still succeeded, later and harder-won.

The old caretaker, who never learned how close her grounds had come to being used and never learned
they'd been deliberately spared, kept tending the same graves the same way she always had, unaware
that a discipline built on exploiting exactly this kind of unguarded trust had, for once, chosen not
to.

*Continuity notes (not narrative): the first entry establishing a self-imposed institutional limit on
where the craft will be used -- a place, not a person -- distinct from "What the Third Generation
Refused" (Chronicle XLVII, `MCD-916`), which turned on a specific grieving individual's vulnerability
rather than a location held as neutral ground by mutual, unspoken agreement between both sides of the
conflict. No named religious institution is introduced; the shrine is deliberately left generic and
unnamed, consistent with this world's established lack of a single dominant mainline Cian religious
institution. No new named characters; the caretaker is unnamed and one-scene. No new proper nouns.*
