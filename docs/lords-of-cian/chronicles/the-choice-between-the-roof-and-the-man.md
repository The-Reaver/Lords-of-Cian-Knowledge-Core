# The Choice Between the Roof and the Man

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XXIV, closing
the eighth wave. Rebellion era, age 20, the Sewer War of Killane. Not a territory Chronicle.
Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Two emergencies broke at once, forty yards apart, and Kanja had time to fully answer only one of
them.

Behind him, a gallery ceiling — already weakened by three days of nearby charge work — had begun a
slow, audible groan that meant it would come down within minutes unless shored immediately,
threatening the nine people still working beneath it. Ahead of him, through a breach in the tunnel
wall, a single wounded courier lay pinned under a collapsed support beam, alive but fading, the only
person who carried that night's full extraction route memorized in his own head.

"Nine people or one," the crew chief said, already moving toward the ceiling, the arithmetic
obvious and brutal.

"Go shore it. Take everyone you can grab on the way." Kanja was already moving the other direction.
"I've got the courier."

"The math says the ceiling."

"The math's wrong about what it's actually counting." Kanja didn't slow down. "You can shore that
ceiling without me — you've watched me do it enough times to know the sequence, and I trust the
crew to follow it under you. Nobody else on this crew can free a man pinned under a support beam in
under two minutes without bringing the rest of the wall down on him doing it. If I go to the
ceiling, the courier's dead for certain and the route dies with him. If I go to the courier, the
ceiling might hold long enough on the sequence you already know. I'm not choosing one life over
nine. I'm choosing the place only I can actually change the outcome."

Obsidian Malice cleared the beam in a single controlled discharge, precise enough not to shift the
wall around it, and Kanja carried the courier clear with forty seconds to spare before the tunnel
mouth behind them sealed on its own settling weight — the same moment, forty yards back, the crew
chief's shoring sequence held the gallery ceiling exactly as taught, nine people clear and
unharmed.

"You got lucky," the crew chief said afterward, once both crises were counted and both had ended
without loss.

"Maybe. Or I knew what each of us could actually do and split the problem so neither of us had to
do the other's job." Kanja checked the courier's route was still legible through the blood on the
map he carried. "I don't ever want to be the only answer to every problem. That's not strength.
That's a crew that hasn't been taught enough yet."
