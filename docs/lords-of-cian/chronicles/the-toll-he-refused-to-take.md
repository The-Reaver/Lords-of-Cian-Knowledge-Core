# The Toll He Refused to Take

---

The convoy flew Trust colors and carried Trust cargo manifests, which was reason enough for half the
boarding crew to assume the order to intercept was coming before Kanja had finished reading the
signal flags himself.

It wasn't grain for a garrison or steel for a foundry. Six ships, all of it food and medicine, bound
for a string of Trust-administered coastal towns that had nothing to do with the war beyond the
misfortune of falling under the wrong flag when the fighting started.

"We could take all six," the boarding officer said, watching the convoy hold formation, unaware yet
how close it had come to a fight. "Feed our own people for a season. Nobody would question it — it's
Trust cargo, headed to a Trust port, escorted by a Trust patrol. That's a legitimate target by any
standard we've used all war."

"It's medicine for towns that didn't choose this war any more than we did." Kanja lowered the glass.
"Let it through."

"Sir—"

"I said let it through." He watched the confusion on the officer's face and answered it before it
became a real argument. "We've spent this whole war teaching the Trust that we're not the thing their
own propaganda says we are — raiders, monsters, whatever story lets them justify what they've done to
people like us. The day we start seizing medicine convoys because the flag on them makes it legally
convenient, we've proven their story right. I won't hand them that."

The convoy passed unmolested through waters the ghost fleet could have emptied it in without firing a
shot, its escort never learning how close the encounter had come, its captains filing a routine
crossing report that mentioned nothing more dramatic than favorable winds.

"That's food and medicine we needed," the officer said afterward, still visibly unconvinced. "For
people who'll never know we let it through, on the other side of a war they didn't start."

"They'll know eventually. Reputation travels slower than fear but it travels further, and it lasts
longer." Kanja watched the convoy's sails shrink toward the horizon. "Every ship we let pass because
letting it pass was right is a ship whose crew tells the story afterward. Enough of those stories, and
even the Trust starts having trouble convincing its own people we're the villains in this."

The officer said nothing further, but the story reached the coastal towns within the month regardless
— carried by sailors who'd expected to be robbed and weren't, told in taverns where the ghost fleet's
name had always meant fear before it started, slowly, meaning something else instead.
