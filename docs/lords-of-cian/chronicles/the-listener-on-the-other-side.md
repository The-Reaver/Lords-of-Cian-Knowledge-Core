# The Listener on the Other Side

*Locked canon, Batch 261, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1286`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LXVII, wave 23, first entry. Introduces the doctrine's first genuine peer opponent — a Trust-trained diagnostician using the real method against him. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The garrison bridge should have read clean. Kanja's first pass found the true load-bearing tension exactly where three prior engagements against similar spans had taught him to expect it, and doubled verification confirmed the same number twice, the way it always had since the tower at MCD-497. He was a breath from discharge when something in the timing snagged — not the bridge's tension, but the *rhythm* of it, a pattern that shifted a fraction of a second after his first pass, as though the structure itself had noticed being read and adjusted.

Structures didn't do that. People did.

He pulled the discharge and swept the span's far end instead, and found him: a Trust engineer crouched at the anchor post with his own palm flat against the stone, running the same diagnostic listening Kanja had spent a decade building — not a fake, not the crude mimicry the freelance mercenaries had once traded on his name for, but the real technique, correctly applied, deliberately timed to false-read the bridge's tension a half-beat after Kanja's own first pass so that the doubled verification would confirm a number the man himself had just quietly altered by shifting his own weight against a hidden brace.

"You've been listening to the schematics," Kanja said, not lowering his stance. "The ones the engineer brought over three years ago."

"I've been listening to *you*," the man said, not moving from the anchor post. "Every engagement, every report, every countermeasure that failed because whoever built it never actually understood what you were doing — they built to break the technique instead of learning it. I did the opposite. I learned it properly, from the ground up, the way you must have, the first time. It took me four years. I don't think anyone else in the corps has managed it yet."

"And you're using it to feed me a false read."

"I'm using it because it's the only language that could possibly work against you." He straightened, unhurried, his palm still resting light against the stone. "Force doesn't. Countermeasures don't, not for long. You beat every design built to defeat the doctrine because you understand it more deeply than the people building against it. I decided the only honest answer was to understand it that deeply myself, and then see what happens when two people who actually hear the truth disagree about what to do with it."

Kanja held the listening rod loose at his side, recalculating everything about the engagement now that the opponent across the bridge wasn't a structure at all. "What happens now, in your accounting?"

"Now we find out whether the doctrine only works because you're the only one who's ever really had it." The Trust diagnostician's expression didn't shift, no malice in it, something closer to genuine scholarly hunger. "I'd like to know the answer as much as you would."
