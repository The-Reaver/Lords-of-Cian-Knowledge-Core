# The Battle Fought Inside the Storm

*Locked canon, Batch 137, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-458`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle VII, first
entry in the third wave. Rebellion era, age 29, the Gale Straits. Not a territory Chronicle. New
standalone material — a detailed armor-and-weapon combat showcase per Abad's craft instruction.
Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Every prior engagement had used Sephtis's storm predictions as a tool for timing — arriving before a
storm broke, retreating ahead of one, using the threat of weather to shape an enemy's movements from
outside it. This engagement was different by design: Kanja took the fleet directly into the storm's
leading edge, fighting inside conditions no Directorate war-fleet doctrine anticipated anyone
choosing to fight in voluntarily.

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System read the storm's own violence the way it read every other hostile
force — wind shear, wave impact, the battering of debris-laden gusts all converting into stored
charge rather than exhausting him the way the same conditions exhausted every other crew on the
water. Obsidian Malice discharged against the lead Directorate cruiser's mainmast in a strike timed
to the exact moment a cresting wave already had the ship listing past its recovery point, the
combined force finishing what the storm alone had already begun.

Onyx of Oblivion moved between vessels in conditions that denied every ordinary sense — rain sheeting
hard enough to blind, wind loud enough to swallow any shouted order, waves breaking over decks faster
than footing could adjust — Whisper of Shadows carrying him ship to ship through gaps the storm itself
opened and closed within seconds, Veil Piercer finding boarding points no defender expected anyone
sane to attempt in these conditions at all. Cadence Ruin didn't need to break a coordinated defense;
the storm had already broken it, scattering the Directorate fleet's formation faster than any tactic
could have.

Three cruisers struck their colors before the storm's leading edge had fully passed. The rest fled
downwind, unable to hold formation against both the weather and an enemy who seemed, to every
frightened lookout still capable of reporting it, entirely unbothered by either.

"Nobody fights inside a storm like that one," the fleet's own navigator said afterward, still
soaked through, watching the scattered Directorate ships vanish into the weather they'd been trying
to escape. "It's not that you timed the storm. You made it part of the fight itself."

"Sephtis taught me to read the storm's timing. The storm itself taught me the rest." Kanja watched
the weather begin, finally, to ease. "An enemy fighting the sea and me at once has already lost half
the battle before I've thrown a single blow. I intend to keep using that as long as the sea keeps
offering it."
