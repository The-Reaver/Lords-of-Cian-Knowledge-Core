Halvar Ilven's hands had started shaking two winters ago, not badly, not in any way that showed
unless you'd watched them hold a blade steady for as long as Daba had, and by the time he finally
said something about it the whole cell already knew and had simply been waiting for him to catch up
to what they'd seen.

"I can still teach it," Halvar said, when Daba came to him about it directly, the way he came to
everyone about everything that mattered. "The shaking's in my grip, not my head. I know exactly what
I'd be handing off badly if I kept going."

"That's not why I'm here."

"I know why you're here." Halvar's mouth did something that in a younger man might have been a
smile. "You're here because you're the only person left in this network who was there the first
year, and you think that means you owe me the conversation yourself instead of sending someone."

It was true, and Daba didn't pretend otherwise. Halvar had been fifteen the day the Rookery burned --
one of the caregivers who'd lived not because he'd been braver than the ones who hadn't, he always
said, but because a stairwell had collapsed behind him instead of in front of him, an accident of
geometry he'd spent the rest of his life trying to earn the right to. He'd been with Daba since
before 1804 had a name, since before the doctrine had a shape, since before dispersed cells and
cross-training and the discipline of staying too small to be worth burning had been anything more
than a grief looking for a structure to live inside.

"Tobin's ready," Daba said. It wasn't a question either, not really, but he asked it the way a
question anyway, because some decisions deserved the shape of one even when the answer wasn't in
doubt.

"Tobin's been ready for two years. You know that better than I do -- you're the one who taught him
the forging work himself." Halvar's shaking hands found each other and stilled a little, clasped.
"What Tobin doesn't have is what I have. He wasn't there. He doesn't carry it the way the rest of us
carry it."

"No," Daba said. "He carries something else instead. He carries what we built instead of what we
survived. That's not a lesser thing. That's the entire point of building it."

He said it and understood, saying it, that he was speaking as much to himself as to Halvar -- to the
part of him that still measured every new hand in the network against the terrible arithmetic of who
had been there and who hadn't, as though the dead of the Rookery could only be honored by people who
remembered them directly rather than by everyone who came after and simply refused to let the
network they'd built come apart.

The ceremony, if it could be called that, was almost nothing. Halvar handed Tobin the cell's own
coded ledger -- not the forged armaments themselves, which belonged to no one person, but the record
of who owed what to whom across four settlements and two generations of debt Daba's own teaching had
let them quietly discharge -- and Tobin took it with both hands the way he'd been taught to take
anything that mattered, and that was the whole of it. No speeches. Nothing that would have survived
being overheard.

Afterward, walking the perimeter together one last time as Halvar's own patrol rather than Tobin's,
Daba asked him the only question he'd actually come to ask. "Do you regret any of it? The years,
the cost, what it took instead of what it gave you?"

Halvar considered this longer than Daba expected, his shaking hands loose at his sides, the two of
them old enough now, both of them, that the question could be asked plainly without either man
flinching from it.

"I regret that it had to exist at all," he said finally. "I have never once regretted what we made
it into." He looked at Daba directly, the closest thing to a farewell either of them would allow
themselves. "Neither will Tobin, thirty years from now, when it's his turn to say the same thing to
whoever he chooses."

Daba said nothing to that, because there was nothing to add to it that wouldn't have diminished it,
and the two of them finished the walk in a silence that had held between them since before either
of them had counted the years it had lasted.
