# The Widow's Son Returned

*Locked canon, Batch 257, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1185`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle LXXIV, wave 25. A generational-reunion payoff to "The Widow Who Wouldn't Thank Him" (MCD-677) -- her grown son returns years later asking to be trained.*

---

Kanja recognized the eyes before the name registered -- the same steady, unforgiving directness he remembered from a woman who'd refused every offer of reconciliation he'd made her, years back, after work he'd done had cost her husband. The young man standing at the gallery entrance had her exact look, aimed at him now instead of away.

"You don't know me," the young man said. "I was eleven when it happened. My mother wouldn't let you near either of us afterward."

"I remember her clearly," Kanja said. "I never expected to see you again."

He hadn't come for an apology or to relitigate a grief that wasn't his to resolve on anyone's behalf. He'd come to ask, plainly, to be trained in the same trade that had killed his father and that Kanja still practiced in his name -- not despite what happened, he said, but because of it. He wanted to understand exactly what the work demanded, precisely enough that no one could ever again tell him a death like his father's had been unavoidable without his being equipped to judge that claim himself.

"Your mother's answer to me was no, every time I offered anything," Kanja said carefully. "I won't build something with her son behind her back."

"She knows I'm here. She didn't stop me." The young man held his ground. "She still won't speak to you. That's hers to keep. This is mine to choose."

It took three separate conversations before Kanja agreed, each one testing whether the request was really about mastery or about something closer to revenge dressed up as ambition. It wasn't. The young man wanted competence, the specific kind that let a person read a support beam correctly before it failed rather than after, and he threw himself at the training with an intensity that had nothing performative in it.

Months in, shoring a gallery correctly on his own for the first time, he said the thing that had clearly been sitting behind everything since the day he arrived.

"I'm not doing this to forgive you," he said. "I'm doing it so nobody's son ever has to stand where I stood, not knowing if the thing that took his father could have been read and stopped in time."

"That's a better reason than forgiveness," Kanja said. "Forgiveness is between you and her. This is between you and the work."
