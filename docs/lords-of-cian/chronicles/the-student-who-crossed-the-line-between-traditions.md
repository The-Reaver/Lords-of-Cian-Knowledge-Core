# The Student Who Crossed the Line Between Traditions

*Locked canon, Batch 263, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1351`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LXXVIII, wave 26, closing the wave. The rival forecaster, having seen her own tradition's vulnerability exposed, allows the student to cross-train a promising sailor from her own fleet -- the first time the two weather traditions blend directly rather than merely coexisting.*

---

The rival forecaster raised it herself, once the fever passed and the raid's lesson had settled into something she could act on rather than simply survive. "There's a girl on my third ship," she said, at the next truce meeting, "quick with a chart, steadier than she has any right to be at her age. I've watched her for two years and told myself I was still looking for someone better. I wasn't. I was looking for someone who wouldn't remind me I'm not as young as I was when I started."

"That's the same mistake I almost made," the student said. "I looked for myself in a student, too, before I learned better."

"Will you take her, then? Not instead of me training her. Alongside it. I want her to have both traditions, not just mine, in case mine isn't enough on its own the way it wasn't enough during the raid."

It was, both women understood without needing to say it, a genuinely new kind of request -- not the informal mutual berth of the original truce, not even the shared combat of the raid, but two separate weather traditions deliberately teaching the same student at once, each unwilling to let the other's knowledge go untaught for pride's sake.

The student agreed, and the arrangement that followed had no precedent in either fleet's history: the rival sailor spending half her training at the school, learning Sephtis's ledger discipline and the northern pilot's ice-reading chapter alongside her own fleet's older, independent tradition, carrying both without either side asking her to choose.

"Two traditions, one student," the retired successor said, watching the arrangement begin. "Sephtis would have called that either the smartest thing this school ever did or the thing most likely to confuse someone into being wrong twice as often."

"Maybe both," the student said. "She'll have to work out which pieces from each fit the best, instead of either of us handing her one finished answer." She watched the rival sailor take her first lesson at the school's chart table, uncertain and careful in a way that reminded the student of herself, years earlier, in front of Sephtis's own hand-guided instrument.

Kanja, who had watched two fleets go from wary distance to shared combat to this, thought the truce had finally become something neither side had originally intended when they first agreed simply to give each other's ships a berth.

"It started as staying out of each other's way," he said.

"It became teaching each other how not to need to," the rival forecaster said. "I don't think either of us planned that. I think we just kept choosing the next honest thing, and this is where honest things led."
