# The Impersonator's Price

*Locked canon, Batch 276, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1428`). Bane Alias Chronicle XCVIII, wave 33. First
impersonation-for-profit entry: criminal opportunists in crude replica gear extort merchants under
Bane's name, distinct from the honest rumor of "The Rumor He Never Corrected" (`MCD-681`) and the
Directorate's own fabricated propaganda in "The Story They Wanted to Be True" (`MCD-1098`).*

---

Word reached the column through a trade caravan before Bane did: a band of five, wearing dark cloth
and a crudely painted curved blade meant to stand in for Onyx of Oblivion, had been working the
southern trade road for a month, stopping merchants and taking a "tax" — a fifth of every wagon's
goods — in Bane's own name, invoking a reputation none of the merchants had any way to verify or
refuse.

"They're not spreading a lie about you," Efa Gol said, reading the caravan master's account twice.
"They're spending your name like it's coin. That's a different theft."

This one, Bane decided, wasn't a rumor to correct or a story to let stand for what it did for morale.
It was a mechanism being robbed by people who understood exactly how much it was worth and none of
what it was for.

Finding them took four days of following the same road the caravans used, and confronting them took
considerably less: five men in stolen-looking dark cloth, none of them remotely dangerous once the
actual Trinity stood in front of them instead of the idea of it, folded the moment Onyx's Cadence Ruin
read the tremor running through all five at once. There was no fight worth the name.

What mattered was what came after. Bane didn't execute them and didn't quietly disappear them either.
He marched all five back to the same market town where they'd taken their last "tax," in front of the
same merchants they'd extorted, and made them return every good still in their possession and confess
— out loud, in the square, to the people they'd wronged — exactly what they'd done and in whose name
they'd done it.

"You could have just killed them," the caravan master said afterward, not disapproving, just
surprised. "Would've been faster."

"Faster isn't the point," Bane said. "If people only ever hear that using my name gets you killed,
they learn to fear the name. I need them to learn something truer than that: that the name doesn't
protect theft, it exposes it. That only works if they watch it happen instead of just hearing it did."

The five were left to the town's own justice, whatever that turned out to be. The market's trade
picked back up within the week, and for a long while after, no one on that road tried the trick again.
