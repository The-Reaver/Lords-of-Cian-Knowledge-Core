# The Smith They Said Couldn't Be One

*Locked canon, Batch 159, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-502`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XV, closing
the fourth wave. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign. Not a territory Chronicle. New
standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

A woman turned away from every conventional smithing guild within reach of the campaign's territory
— rejected not for any deficiency of skill, which every guild that tested her privately acknowledged,
but purely on the grounds that guild membership had never been extended to women within living memory
— arrived at The Anvil with nothing but her own tools and a request: let her work, and let her work be
judged on nothing but its own merit.

"I'm not here asking for charity," she said, plainly bracing for another version of the same
rejection. "I'm asking for the chance every guild in this territory has refused to give me. If my
work isn't good enough once you've actually seen it, tell me that honestly. Don't tell me it isn't
good enough before I've had the chance to show you."

"I've no intention of judging your work by anything but the work itself." Kanja set her up at a
station alongside the campaign's other smiths without ceremony, without singling her out further than
the moment already had. "The guilds that turned you away weren't protecting a standard. They were
protecting an exclusion they'd stopped examining a long time ago. I don't build anything here on
exclusions I haven't examined."

She worked three days before anyone else on the floor stopped watching her out of the corner of their
eye and started simply watching her work the way they'd watch anyone else's — competent, precise,
entirely unremarkable in exactly the way that mattered most.

"The guilds will hear about this eventually," the campaign's senior smith said, watching her complete
a casting that matched the floor's own best work without qualification. "Some of them won't like it."

"They're welcome to their opinion. I'm not running a guild here. I'm running a forge that needs
skilled hands, and I've got one more skilled pair than I had a week ago." Kanja reviewed her work
alongside the rest of the day's output, no distinction drawn between hers and anyone else's. "If the
guilds eventually decide their own exclusion cost them a smith this good, that's a cost of their own
making, not mine to solve for them."

She stayed through the remainder of the campaign, her name eventually spoken among the crew with the
same plain respect any skilled smith earned — no longer "the woman they said couldn't be one," simply
one more hand The Anvil had been fortunate to have.
