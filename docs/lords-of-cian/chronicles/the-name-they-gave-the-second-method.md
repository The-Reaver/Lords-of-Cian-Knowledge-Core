# The Name They Gave the Second Method

*Locked canon, Batch 263, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1357`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LXXXIV, wave 28, closing the wave. Sailors informally name a verification technique after the retired successor -- not her own name, which was never recorded, but a description of her role -- mirroring the strait named for Sephtis without repeating it.*

---

No strait bore her name, the way one had come to bear Sephtis's, generations after his own death -- there was no single place she'd made famous the way his early calls had made a particular passage famous among sailors who never knew the man behind the naming. Her legacy, when it settled, settled into something smaller and, the student privately thought, more exactly right for who she'd actually been.

It started on the dock, among captains who'd learned the storm-interval verification protocol from the written regulation and never known any of the people behind it. When a junior officer's reading disagreed with a senior one, and neither side would simply defer to rank, sailors along the coast had begun calling the practice of checking a disputed reading against a second, independent source "the second reader's watch" -- a nod, none of them fully realizing it, to the two-reader disagreement protocol the successor herself had helped establish decades earlier at `MCD-979`, when she and Sephtis had first disagreed and resolved it by sending a scout to verify rather than either of them simply overruling the other.

The student heard a dockworker use the phrase without knowing its origin, months after the burial, and said nothing to correct him. She wrote it into the school's own record instead, exactly as it had emerged on its own -- not a name given deliberately, the way the strait had been, but a phrase that had simply grown out of a habit she'd taught without ever teaching anyone whose habit it had first been.

"She'd have liked that better than a strait," the student told the fourth-generation apprentice, showing him the entry. "A strait is a place. This is a thing people do, whenever a reading's in doubt, whether they know where it came from or not."

"She doesn't get her name on it," the boy observed, not quite a complaint, more an honest noticing.

"No," the student agreed. "Same as she never asked for one on anything else she built. The method got named. She didn't. I think that's exactly how she'd have wanted it, if you'd asked her while she was here to answer." She closed the ledger over the new entry. "Sephtis got a strait because sailors needed something to point to on a map. She gets a habit, because what she actually left behind wasn't a place. It was a way of checking your own certainty before you let it cost someone something."

Kanja, reading the entry after it was written, thought of his own name-outlasted-by-the-work reflection from years earlier, and understood that this lineage had now proven the same point twice over, in two different registers -- once in a strait that would outlast anyone's memory of the man, and once in a habit that would outlast anyone's memory of the woman who'd built it.
