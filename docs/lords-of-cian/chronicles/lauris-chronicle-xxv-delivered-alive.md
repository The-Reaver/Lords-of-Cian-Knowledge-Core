# Lauris Chronicle XXV: Delivered Alive

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1642`). Twenty-fifth entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, a Strand D entry giving full-scene treatment to Operation 13, the Captain
Drenneth Acquisition (previously only summarized at `MCD-1546`): the underwater hull-climb boarding
of the naval defector's flagship, the *Iron Veth*, and the acquisition itself via the same
calibrated collarbone-fracturing strike used at Operation 4. Dramatizes the operation's true
weight -- Lauris's later, out-of-contract discovery that Drenneth died during Directorate
processing despite her delivering him alive -- as an early, concrete data point in the
institutional-doubt trajectory that Chronicle III's Operation 19 later completes. Coincidental
homonym noted per `MCD-1546`: unrelated to Drenneth Threnarr-Vask, her Sister-Hold archivist
mentor. No new named characters; Captain Drenneth already locked.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Established Hunter designation LL-001-S-013: "Delivered alive. That was the
term of the contract, and I met it exactly. What was done with him afterward was not a term of the
contract. I have since decided this distinction matters to me more than it appears to matter to the
Directorate."*

Operation 13 asked something of Lauris the Directorate rarely troubled itself to ask precisely:
that she bring a man back rather than simply end him. Captain Drenneth of the Voskharen Naval
Authority had, by the time the contract reached her, stolen three of the Directorate's own
classified vessel-control protocols, destroyed the Authority's primary armory in the taking, and
assassinated two senior officers who had tried to stop him. The Directorate wanted the protocols
recovered intact, which meant it wanted his flagship, the *Iron Veth*, undamaged, which meant it
wanted him acquired rather than sunk with the ship he commanded.

I record, before the engagement itself, a detail I think matters: Lauris spent something close to
three months locating the vessel, working through channels she has never fully described to me,
before she moved at all. This was not, by then, unusual for her -- but I mention it because it
contrasts so sharply with what followed, which she has always recounted to me as brief almost to
the point of anticlimax.

She boarded at night, during a refueling stop, via underwater hull-climb -- adapting a lattice-grip
vertical-ascent technique her instructors at Karth-Ven had developed for sheer cliff faces to the
considerably more forgiving surface of a wooden hull, fingers finding purchase in seams no sentry
above deck would think to watch. She found Drenneth in his quarters and ended the matter with the
same calibrated collarbone-fracturing strike she had used against Therik Voll at Operation 4 --
clean, precise, delivered before he had finished rising from his chair. Of the roughly eighteen
crew aboard, six were terminated during her exit and twelve disabled non-lethally through
Phalanx-supported strikes, a ratio she has told me she considers among the cleanest of her career
given the vessel's confined geography and the genuine hostility of a crew defending a captain they
had, whatever his crimes against the Directorate, chosen to follow. Drenneth himself she delivered
alive, exactly as specified. A Directorate recovery team retrieved the stolen protocols intact
within days.

By every measure the contract itself set, Operation 13 closed as cleanly as any she had completed.

What she learned afterward, outside the contract's own scope and, by her account, some considerable
time later, sits differently in her archive than the operation's clean closure would suggest.
Drenneth died during Directorate processing. She was never told how, or by whose authority, or
whether his death had been intended from the moment of his acquisition or arrived at only once he
was no longer useful to whatever the Directorate had wanted from him. She has recorded, in her own
entry on the matter, that she does not believe she was lied to in any technical sense -- no one
told her he would live, and no one told her he had died until she asked, by which point the asking
itself felt, to her, like an admission that she had expected something the institution had never
actually promised.

I have heard her describe her feeling about it only once, in words I set down exactly: *I delivered
him alive because the contract asked for alive, and because I do not believe a man's death should
happen somewhere I cannot account for it. The Directorate did not share that belief. It never told
me otherwise. It simply never told me it disagreed until the disagreement had already been acted
upon.* This is, I think, the earliest place in her archive where I can find her drawing a clear line
between her own procedural cleanliness and the institution's -- not yet the fully formed doubt that
Operation 19's eighty interviews would eventually give language to, but its recognizable
antecedent, six operations before Operation 19 would teach her the word for what she was noticing.

One further note, offered here because I believe a careful reader deserves it and because Lauris
herself has remarked on the coincidence more than once: Captain Drenneth of the *Iron Veth* shares
no relation whatsoever with Drenneth Threnarr-Vask, the senior Sister-Hold archivist who recorded
her own birth on Kares Prime. She has told me the coincidence struck her, on first hearing the
defector's name during the contract briefing, as faintly absurd -- one more small, indifferent
irony in a life that has offered her a great many larger ones.

Her closing note: *Delivered alive. I will keep meaning that exactly, in every contract I am ever
given again, regardless of what is done with the delivery afterward. It is the one term I control.*
