# Lauris Chronicle XVII: The Second Shaft

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1634`). Seventeenth entry in Lauris Letitia's own Chronicle
series, the fourth entry of Strand K (Kares Prime / deep past). Dramatizes one of the eight geological
emergency responses from the Long Operational Period (`MCD-1555`) in the pattern of the Vask Threnarr
mining rescue (`MCD-171`), set at Vask Karth-Ven itself -- a deliberate contrast with `MCD-171`'s
pre-karth-ven event at her birth-hold, showing the same capability applied, decades later, at the
Vask that had become her own. No new named characters. Full text below.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Vask Karth-Ven, age ~2,150: "The third gallery is unsound past the training
floor's own supports. I did not know this until it failed. I know it now, and I have recorded the
knowing so that whoever reads this after me does not have to learn it the same way."*

Karth-Ven, she has told me more than once, was never meant to be permanent. It was built as a
training center, carved into a mountain spine that had not been surveyed for habitation with the
same care the older cooperative-era Vasks received, and by the time it had become, in practical
terms, her home, its deeper galleries had been in continuous use for twenty-eight thousand years
without the full geological review a settlement of its age would ordinarily have earned. I record
this not as criticism of anyone living when the failure occurred, but because Lauris herself records
it that way, and I have learned to trust her account of causes over almost anyone else's.

The failure came without warning during what her archive calls an ordinary training rotation -- a
third gallery, deeper than the residence chambers, used for storing calibrated Drakma stock for the
central training floor's upkeep. A support fault propagated through roughly forty meters of ceiling
in under two minutes, trapping eleven Karth-Ven residents behind a collapse of stone and worked
metal, and killing, in the collapse's first seconds, two who had been nearest the fault line. Lauris
was on the training floor itself when it happened, some two hundred meters distant. She reached the
collapse site, by her own account, in under ninety seconds.

*

I want to record, as precisely as I am able, what she did next, because I believe this is one of the
entries in her archive where the difference between her age at Threnarr and her age at Karth-Ven
matters. At Threnarr, nine centuries earlier, she held a single unstable shaft for three hours in
sustained Triad-Lock, absorbing the accumulated pressure of a mining collapse through raw structural
endurance -- her first lethal combat came later that same year, but the rescue itself had been, by
her own later assessment, closer to pure attrition than technique. At Karth-Ven she did not hold the
collapse. She read it.

Her archive entry describes, in more technical detail than I am fully equipped to transcribe
faithfully, a structural assessment conducted in real time while already inside the collapse zone:
identifying which sections of the fallen gallery still bore load, which had already released it
entirely, and where a further eleven residents behind the blockage could survive a controlled
partial clearing rather than requiring the entire mass held indefinitely. She did not, in other
words, simply outlast the collapse the way she had outlasted the shaft at Threnarr. She solved it --
clearing a precise channel through roughly six meters of compacted stone and Drakma stock while
holding the surrounding mass in a partial Triad-Lock calibrated to bear only what the channel's own
removal would otherwise have released, a distinction she is careful, in her own writing, not to
understate. The nine residents behind the blockage reached open air within forty minutes. The
remaining approach -- extracting the two bodies nearest the fault line -- she conducted afterward,
alone, and does not describe in detail.

*

What I find most worth recording here is not the rescue itself but the entry's closing section,
which she wrote not as narrative but as what I can only call a report -- addressed, implicitly, to
no one in particular, since Karth-Ven's own Iron-Speakers would have received a formal accounting
through other channels. She catalogued, in full, the specific structural signature that had preceded
the failure: a particular resonance in the stone, audible at a frequency she notes most Karesian
hearing dismisses as background settling, present for at least several decades before the collapse
occurred. She recommended, in writing, that every deep gallery at Karth-Ven be surveyed for the same
signature, and that the survey be repeated at intervals rather than treated as a single completed
task.

I asked her once, transcribing this entry many centuries later, whether the survey had been
conducted. She said it had, for approximately four hundred years, and then had quietly lapsed as the
Vask's surviving population continued to shrink and the personnel required to conduct it were needed
elsewhere. She did not say this with any particular grief attached to it. She has told me, on other
occasions, that grief attached to every instance of a shrinking civilization failing to sustain its
own maintenance would have consumed the entirety of what remained of her attention, and she chose,
deliberately, to reserve that attention for the moments the maintenance actually failed rather than
for every quiet year in between when it merely could have.

The third gallery was resealed rather than reopened. Karth-Ven's training floor continued in use
directly above it for the remainder of her time on Kares Prime, calibrated, as she notes elsewhere,
to read her combat signature more precisely than any other Karesian's in its recorded history --
built, in the end, on stone she herself had once had to hold together with her own body so that
eleven people she trained beside could keep living in the rooms beneath it.
