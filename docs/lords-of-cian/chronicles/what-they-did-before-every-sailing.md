# What They Did Before Every Sailing

---

A new hand, three weeks aboard and still learning which of *The Audit*'s customs were operational
necessity and which were simply habit, finally worked up the nerve to ask why the whole crew gathered
on deck for a few silent minutes before every single sailing, storm or clear sky, routine patrol or
certain engagement, without exception.

"Nobody's ever explained it," she admitted to Garren Hask, who'd been counting supplies nearby and
seemed, of everyone aboard, the most likely to actually answer plainly. "You all just stop what you're
doing and stand still for a few minutes. Then you go back to work like nothing happened."

"Started after the Black Trench," he said, not looking up from his ledger. "Before that, nobody thought
to do it — you just sailed, because sailing was all you knew how to do and there wasn't time to think
about what you might not come back from. After, once we understood exactly what a battle could cost,
Efa Gol started it without asking anyone. Just stood on deck one morning before we shipped out and said
nothing for a few minutes. Rest of us didn't know what she was doing. Followed along anyway."

"What is she doing?"

"Counting who's here." Callum Breck had drifted close enough to answer that part himself, voice still
carrying the careful economy it had kept since Ghost Harbor. "Not out loud. Just — looking. Making sure
everyone who should be standing on this deck is standing on it, before you leave and can't be sure
anymore."

Dol Maren, checking the rigging nearby, added her own piece without being asked. "It's not superstition.
It's not asking anyone for luck. It's just — some of us know from the Black Trench exactly how fast a
full crew becomes an incomplete one. Few minutes of quiet before every sailing reminds you not to take
the full crew for granted while you've still got it."

The new hand watched, that morning, as the ritual played out exactly as described — no words, no
ceremony, just every hand aboard standing still on deck for a few minutes, looking at each other, before
the work resumed and *The Audit* put out to sea as if nothing unusual had happened at all.

"Every sailing?" she asked, once it was over. "Even the routine ones?"

"Especially the routine ones," Garren Hask said, closing his ledger and heading back to his count. "The
ones you don't expect to matter are exactly the ones that catch you unprepared for how much they did."
