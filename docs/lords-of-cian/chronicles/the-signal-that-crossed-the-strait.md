# The Signal That Crossed the Strait

*Locked canon, Batch 282, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1481`). Crow King Alias Chronicle XCVII, wave 33, opening
it. The Hymn-Engine is carried across open water for the first time, using sound reflected off cliff
and tide rather than terrain. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

No marsh, no highland, no stone street to fold an echo through -- just a strait a half-mile wide,
a labor island on the far side of it, and a patrol boat that crossed the gap on a fixed rotation the
apprentice had watched for three nights before she trusted the pattern enough to use it.

Sound didn't behave on open water the way it behaved anywhere else the craft had worked before. It
didn't fold back the way stone did, or vanish into the way marsh mud swallowed it whole. It carried
flat and far and clean across the surface, further than anyone expected, then died all at once past
a certain distance as if it had run out of water to travel on. The apprentice had spent three nights
finding exactly where that edge sat, and built the whole operation around standing just inside it.

She didn't sing across the strait. Singing would have carried true, and true was the one thing she
couldn't afford the island's own watch to hear. Instead she used the cliff face behind her own
position, striking a hollow length of pipe against stone in a pattern that bounced off the rock,
skipped flat across the water, and arrived at the island sounding for all the world like it had come
from a boat still a quarter-mile out -- the patrol's own return signal, three minutes early.

The island's watch relaxed the way a routine trains a person to relax. Three minutes was enough. The
fourth generation's own falsified transfer paperwork -- already circulating through the mainland
office by then, a method he'd built for exactly this kind of layered operation -- had the labor roll
short four names by the time the real patrol boat actually returned, and nobody on the island thought
to count twice against a signal they'd already answered once correctly that night.

"Water doesn't carry lies the way stone does," the apprentice said afterward, coiling the pipe back
into her pack. "It carries the truth of a sound further than it has any right to, and then it just
stops, all at once, like it changed its mind. You have to find that line before you can use it. I
didn't know that going in. I know it now."
