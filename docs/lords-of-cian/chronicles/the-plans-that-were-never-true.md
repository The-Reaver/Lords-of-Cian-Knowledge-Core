# The Plans That Were Never True

*Locked canon, Batch 195, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-669`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XXXIV, wave 12 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The captured blueprint looked authentic in every way that mattered to anyone who hadn't spent a
lifetime reading structural plans: proper municipal formatting, a plausible surveyor's stamp, a
route through an unmapped section of old cistern that promised the crew a shortcut past the
garrison's known defenses.

Kanja spread it across the planning table and found the lie within minutes.

"The elevation markings don't agree with themselves," he said, tracing two sections of the same
drawn passage. "Here, the grade drops six feet over this stretch. Here, forty feet further along the
same passage, it's marked level again, with nothing between them to account for the difference.
Real surveyors don't lose six feet of elevation and then just forget to draw it back in. Whoever
drew this has seen real blueprints before, enough to fake the format, but they've never actually
walked a passage and measured one."

"It could still be real. Surveys have errors."

"Surveys have errors in the numbers. This has an error in the shape of the thing, which means
whoever drew it was inventing a passage, not recording one." Kanja checked a second inconsistency,
then a third — a support column marked at a spacing no builder would ever actually use, a drainage
grade that ran uphill for no reason any real cistern would require. "This is bait. A route drawn to
look plausible enough to walk into, leading somewhere they want us cornered rather than somewhere
that actually exists the way it's drawn."

They sent a small decoy party along the fabricated route instead, watched from a safe distance, and
confirmed the ambush waiting exactly where the false elevation numbers should have raised suspicion
in anyone actually planning to use the passage as drawn.

"How many people would have walked straight into that without catching it?" the crew chief asked,
watching the ambush eventually disperse, its quarry never having arrived.

"Most people. It's a good fake, built by someone who understood exactly how convincing a forged
document can look to anyone who isn't reading it the way the thing it's imitating actually has to
work." Kanja folded the false blueprint away, one more addition to the crew's growing catalogue of
enemies who kept mistaking a convincing surface for the genuine thing underneath. "That's always
been the gap I live in. Not that I'm harder to trap. That the traps built on lies about the
craft itself keep running into someone who's spent a lifetime knowing what the craft actually looks
like."
