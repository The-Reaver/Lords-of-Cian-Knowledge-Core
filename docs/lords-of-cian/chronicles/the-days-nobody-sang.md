# The Days Nobody Sang

*Locked canon, Batch 260, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1275`). Crow King Alias Chronicle LXXXIII, wave 28. An
extended operational lull during relative peace raises the question of whether restraint itself was
always the craft's purest form. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

Four months passed without a single operation needing the craft at all -- a stretch of relative quiet
along the front none of them had planned for and none of them entirely trusted, but real all the same.
No garrison to evade, no convoy to extract, no false signal worth throwing because there was nothing,
for once, that needed one.

The fourth generation found the quiet harder to sit with than he expected. He kept the numeric
discipline sharp out of habit, practicing falsified ledgers against imaginary depots that didn't
exist, and admitted to the apprentice, on one of the quieter evenings, that he was starting to wonder
whether a craft that goes unused for months is still a craft at all, or just a set of skills slowly
going soft from disuse.

"I used to think the songs were the point," the apprentice said. "Then I thought the restraint was
the point. Now I wonder if not needing either of them, for a while, is the actual point, and we just
never get to see it because something's usually burning."

"That's a strange thing to hope for," the fourth generation said. "A craft whose best outcome is
nobody ever needing it."

"Most crafts that matter are like that, if you think about it long enough. Nobody wants to need a
bridge repaired. They just want the bridge to hold." She considered it further. "I don't think we
went soft these four months. I think we finally got to practice the part that's hardest to practice
on purpose -- staying ready without anything to be ready for."

The quiet held for another six weeks before it broke, and when it did, the craft came back exactly as
sharp as it had ever been -- proof, the apprentice said afterward, that the discipline underneath it
had never actually been at risk, only resting.
