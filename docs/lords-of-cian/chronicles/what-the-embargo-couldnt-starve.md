# What the Embargo Couldn't Starve

*Locked canon, Batch 136, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-456`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle VIII.
Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone
material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The Directorate's regional command had finally stopped trying to burn or raid the Rolling Foundry
Campaign out of existence and settled instead on something slower and, on paper, harder to counter
directly: a coordinated embargo on raw ore and coal shipments across every route the campaign's
forges depended on, calculated to starve the operation of material rather than destroy it by force.

Within two weeks, every conventional supply line into the campaign's territory had gone dry.

"They've finally found something fire doesn't answer," the Anvil's senior smith said, watching the
ore stores dwindle toward a hard stop. "You can metabolize a raid. You can't metabolize an empty
stockpile."

"I can't metabolize it the way I've metabolized everything else. That doesn't mean it can't be
metabolized at all." Kanja was already three days into a different kind of work by the time the smith
raised the concern — mapping every abandoned mine shaft, every scrap yard, every sunk vessel's
reclaimable ironwork within reach of the campaign's territory that the embargo hadn't accounted for
because nobody planning a conventional blockade had thought to account for material that wasn't
moving through conventional routes in the first place. Scrap crews began salvaging wrecked
Directorate war material left over from earlier engagements. Old mine workings, abandoned as
unprofitable a generation before the war, reopened under crews willing to work marginal ore rather
than wait for the embargo to lift.

It wasn't as much material as the conventional supply lines had provided. It was enough.

"You didn't break the embargo," the senior smith said, weeks later, watching the forges running at a
reduced but genuinely sustainable pace on salvage and marginal ore alone. "You just decided you didn't
need what it was denying you."

"That's exactly what metabolizing it means. I'm not always going to be able to answer force with more
force, or fire with more fire. Sometimes the only answer is refusing to accept that the thing being
taken away was ever the only way to keep going." Kanja checked the newest cast against its mold, the
metal's quality lower than the campaign's usual standard but sound enough to matter. "The embargo
wanted to starve us into stopping. We're still running. That's the whole answer it's going to get."
