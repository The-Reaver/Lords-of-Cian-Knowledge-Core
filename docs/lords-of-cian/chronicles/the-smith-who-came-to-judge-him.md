# The Smith Who Came to Judge Him

*Locked canon, Batch 213, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-921`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XLVI, wave 16. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The stranger arrived on foot, alone, carrying a satchel of his own tools rather than any letter of
introduction, and told the first apprentice who greeted him plainly that he had walked four weeks to
find out whether the stories were true.

"Which stories," the apprentice asked.

"That a man fights with fire wearing a war-name and somehow also forges better than smiths who've
never held a blade at all. I've heard that claim about a dozen men in my life. It's been a lie every
single time." He set his satchel down at the edge of the floor. "I want to see the hands, not the
reputation."

Kanja met him at the forge rather than turn him away, recognizing the particular hunger of a
craftsman who trusted nothing secondhand. "You want a contest."

"I want the truth, and a contest is the only honest way anyone's ever given it to me." The stranger
produced two identical warped ship-fittings, pulled from the same ruined batch, twisted the same way
in the same storm. "One each. Straighten it, temper it, and we'll see whose holds under a mallet
after."

They worked without speaking long enough that the apprentices stopped pretending not to watch. The
stranger's hands moved faster, fewer motions between heats, his fitting true within four passes.
Kanja's took six, unhurried, each reheat held a beat longer than seemed necessary to anyone who hadn't
spent a year learning exactly how long was necessary.

"You're slower," the stranger said, when both fittings lay finished side by side.

"I know."

They tested both under the mallet. The stranger's held for six strikes, then cracked on the seventh,
the speed that built it leaving a brittleness the eye alone had never caught. Kanja's took eleven
before it gave, the extra heats bought back as a temper that forgave a fault line no reheat could
fully close.

The stranger turned the cracked half over in his hand for a long moment. "I came four weeks expecting
to find a legend standing on a lie. Faster than everyone, stronger than everyone, doesn't need the
craft at all — that's more or less the rumor that reaches the places I work."

"It's not true."

"No. It's worse, in a way I didn't expect it to be." He set the broken half down beside the whole one.
"You're not faster than me. I'd have believed that and walked home satisfied. You're just willing to
spend longer getting it right, every single time, with no one keeping score but the work itself. I've
met smiths who could outpace you in an afternoon. I haven't met many who'd choose not to, on
principle, this many times running."

"Speed was never the part that mattered. I needed something that would still be standing long after
I'd moved on to the next site and stopped being anyone's problem to manage."

The stranger studied the true-set, eleven-strike fitting a while longer, then began unpacking the rest
of his tools onto the bench beside Kanja's, unasked. "Four weeks to prove a lie. Poor use of a walk,
now that I'm actually here." He nodded toward the apprentices still watching from the rail. "Might as
well make it worth something. Show me that reheat again. I don't believe I've ever bothered to hold
one that long in my life."

*Continuity notes (not narrative): the first entry to test the alias's craft directly against a peer
master smith under formal, judged conditions — no Trinity gear, no combat, no political stakes — a
register never used across the alias's prior fifteen waves. Resolves the reputation's own tension
(legend versus earned skill) through an honest technical result: slower, more durable work beating
faster, brittle work, confirming the method was never about supernatural speed. The stranger stays to
teach and learn in return rather than departing satisfied or humbled. No new named characters. First
entry in the Lord of Embers' sixteenth three-Chronicle wave.*
