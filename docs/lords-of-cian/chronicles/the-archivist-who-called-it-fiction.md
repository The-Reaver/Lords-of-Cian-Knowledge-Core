# The Archivist Who Called It Fiction

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle II, wave 10.
Rebellion era (age 19-21), unarmed and non-combat throughout, matching the alias's established
ethos. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The Sovereign Trust archivist's published circular called the Industrial Myth ledgers "a propaganda
exercise dressed in the language of arithmetic," and cited, as proof, the sheer improbability that an
unarmed rebel could compile documentation more thorough than the Trust's own district offices
maintained.

Ezio read the circular twice, found nothing in it he could disprove by argument, and wrote back not
to argue but to invite.

*You are welcome to the original testimony, worker by worker, and to the production records we
cross-checked them against, and to speak with any witness named in any entry without myself or Kanja
present for the conversation, so that no one can claim your findings were shaped by our presence in
the room. Bring whatever scribe or method you trust least of ours. Use it against us.*

The archivist came expecting to be refused, or stonewalled, or handed a carefully curated sample
instead of the whole account, and found instead a locked chest of the full four-district record
opened for him without condition, and Ezio's own working notes — corrections, margin doubts, the
handful of entries flagged as unresolved rather than smoothed over — handed across without being
asked for.

"You've left your own mistakes in here," the archivist said, three days into an audit he'd expected
to take one, turning up an early entry Ezio had flagged and never fully resolved. "A propagandist
edits those out."

"A propagandist does. I'm not interested in a document that looks perfect. I'm interested in one
that's true, and true documents have loose threads in them, because people's memories aren't
perfectly consistent and neither are ours when we're checking them." Ezio didn't ask what conclusion
the archivist meant to publish. "Write whatever your own audit finds. That was always the offer."

The archivist's eventual report, circulated weeks later under his own name and evident reluctance,
did not call the ledgers propaganda. It called them, in language considerably drier than his first
circular, "substantially corroborated by independent cross-examination," and quietly stopped short of
recommending any Trust action on what it confirmed — but the word "fiction" never appeared in his
writing about the Industrial Myth again.
