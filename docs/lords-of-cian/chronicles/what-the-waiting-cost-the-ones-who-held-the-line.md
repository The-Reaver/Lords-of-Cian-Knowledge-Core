# What the Waiting Cost the Ones Who Held the Line

*Locked canon, Batch 192, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-578`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XXXIII, wave 11 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The junior officer who had openly questioned the doctrine in the tenth week found Kanja on the deck of
the captured harbor's own flagship, several days after the battle, and asked to say something he
hadn't been able to say in front of the crew.

"I was wrong to doubt it," he said. "I want that on the record, not because you need my apology, but
because I think the crew needs to hear it from someone who doubted out loud, not just from you saying
it worked."

"You weren't wrong to doubt it," Kanja said. "You were wrong about the reason it hadn't worked yet.
Those are different mistakes. Doubting the method because it hadn't paid off in ten weeks is a fair
thing to feel. Assuming it had failed, instead of assuming it just hadn't been given its moment yet —
that's the part I'd ask you to sit with."

The officer stayed quiet for a while, watching the harbor's captured stores being inventoried below.

"Three months," he finally said. "Two men requested transfer. One nearly came to blows with a
watch-mate over it. What do I tell them now — that it was worth it?"

"Tell them it cost something real. Don't let anyone pretend the waiting was free just because the
ending was good. Three months of a crew's patience, held under strain, for a doctrine that asked them
to trust a forecast that had nothing to show them for weeks at a time — that's a real cost, and it
doesn't stop being real just because it paid off this time. Next time it might not. I'd rather my crew
go into the next long wait knowing exactly what it costs them than believing it's always this clean at
the end."
