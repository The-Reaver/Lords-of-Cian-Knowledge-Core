# The Old Woman's Crow King

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Crow King Alias Chronicle XVIII, wave 6, closing
entry. Rebellion era, roughly a year after the Night of the Crow King (`MCD-236`, age 23), told
from the perspective of an uninvolved market-town resident. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

By the time the story reached the woman who sold dried fish at the Coalgate market, it had grown a
crown made of living crows rather than gathered feathers, a voice that could summon storms out of a
clear sky, and a habit of walking through walls that no stonemason's echo trick had ever needed to
explain the eastern-quarter confusion at all.

"He didn't walk through anything," her grandson told her, home from an apprenticeship two towns
over where he'd actually spoken with someone who'd served alongside the crew that night. "It was
people singing in the right spots. Nineteen of them. That's the whole trick."

"That's not nearly as good a story."

"It's the true one."

She considered this while gutting the next fish, unbothered by the correction. "Maybe. But I've
been telling the crow-crown version to every traveler who passes through for a year now, and half
of them leave Coalgate a little more careful about crossing anyone they don't understand yet. If
the story keeps a few fools from doing something stupid to a man they can't outthink, I don't see
the harm in the crows being alive."

"Doesn't it bother you, telling something you know isn't true?"

"Bother me?" She weighed the question seriously before answering it. "I've told a hundred stories
in this market that weren't strictly true and never once claimed to be. Everyone who buys my fish
knows a fish tale when they hear one. The Crow King's story isn't a lie I'm telling instead of the
truth. It's a shape the truth takes once it's traveled far enough from the person who actually lived
it. Nineteen singers in a stone street is his story. Living crows and walking through walls — that's
ours now. I don't think he'd mind the difference much, so long as either version keeps people
honest."

Her grandson had no answer for that, and didn't try to correct her again.
