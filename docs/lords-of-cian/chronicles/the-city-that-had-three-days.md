# The City That Had Three Days

*Locked canon, Batch 263, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1352`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LXXIX, wave 27, first entry in the wave. A supermassive storm system threatens Ghost Harbor (formerly Ash Harbor, `MCD-241`, `GEO-006`) -- the doctrine used for full civilian evacuation at city scale for the first time.*

---

The reading gave them three days, which the student called generous the moment she'd finished making it -- most systems this size gave a city half that, if they gave any warning at all. Ghost Harbor had never faced a call like this one in the school's own working memory: not a fleet to reroute, not a village of a few hundred to warn, a full port city that had rebuilt itself twice since the battle that renamed it, home now to more people than any single reading had ever been asked to move at once.

"Three days to evacuate a city that's never evacuated anything," the harbor's own senior magistrate said, when the student laid the reading in front of her. "You understand what you're asking."

"I understand what happens if we don't," the student said. She'd brought the full ledger with her, unprompted, the way the school had learned to since the costly miss two waves back -- every honest failure alongside every hit, so that Ghost Harbor's own leadership could weigh the call on the school's whole record rather than a single confident number. "I'd rather hand you the book and let you decide with everything in it than ask you to trust one reading blind."

The magistrate read longer than the student expected, long enough that the tide had turned before she looked up again. "This is thorough," she said. "It's also the first time anyone's asked this city to move all of itself at once for weather instead of war."

Kanja, present at the harbor as an unnamed presence among the growing evacuation effort, understood something about the city's own hesitation that the reading alone couldn't address: Ghost Harbor's people remembered fire and siege, not storms, as the thing that emptied streets. Asking them to run from weather the way they'd once run from an attack meant asking the city to trust a kind of danger it had no inherited instinct for.

The magistrate made her decision before the first day closed. Not because the reading convinced her entirely -- she said as much, plainly -- but because the ledger's own honesty had, the same way it had begun winning back trust up and down the whole coast since the false call two waves earlier.

"Three days," she told her own city council. "We use every hour of it."

The student, watching the evacuation orders go out, thought of every smaller reading that had led here -- a fleet, a village, a school, a truce between two fleets -- each one teaching the doctrine something it needed before it could be trusted with a whole city's worth of lives at once.

"This is the biggest one," she said to Kanja, quietly, before the work of moving an entire harbor's population began in earnest.

"It's the biggest one so far," Kanja said. "That's not the same as the last one it'll ask of you."
