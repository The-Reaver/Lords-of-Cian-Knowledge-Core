# The Granary They Burned to Prove a Point

*Locked canon, Batch 254, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1107`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXXVII, wave 26. A purely
punitive Directorate reprisal Bane arrives too late to prevent or meaningfully answer -- the first
entry where his presence has zero effect on the triggering harm itself.*

---

There was no military reason for it. The garrison commander who'd lost the district's last engagement
burned the settlement's entire grain store overnight, three weeks of harvest, not to deny it to the
rebellion — the rebellion had never touched the district's supply lines — but because a losing
commander needed something to point to that looked like consequence, and the people who'd sheltered
under his garrison's nominal protection were the only thing left within his reach.

He'd withdrawn his company beyond any distance Bane's column could close before Bane even heard about
it. There was no raid to mount, no officer to hold to account, nothing to strike that would put the
grain back or undo the message it had been meant to send.

Bane didn't pretend otherwise to the settlement's own council when he arrived to find the granary
still smoking.

"I'm not going to tell you I can fix this. He's gone, and even if he weren't, nothing I could do to
him gives your winter back what he took from it."

"Then why come at all," one of the council asked, not unkindly, just tired past the point of
expecting an answer that mattered.

"Because what I can actually do is get a relief convoy here from the next district before your stores
run empty for good, and because someone should say out loud that this wasn't a cost of war. It was a
man punishing you for something you didn't do, because you were the only thing left he could reach."

He spent the next four days organizing exactly that — runners to the neighboring district, an
emergency rationing plan the settlement's own elders drafted with his crew's logistics help rather
than his own direction, a relief convoy that arrived on the sixth day with enough to carry the
settlement through to spring.

It didn't undo anything. Efa Gol said as much on the ride out, watching the burned granary shrink
behind them.

"Some of what we do doesn't get to be a win," Bane said. "This wasn't ever going to be one. The best
version of this was always just making sure it wasn't also the end of the story."
