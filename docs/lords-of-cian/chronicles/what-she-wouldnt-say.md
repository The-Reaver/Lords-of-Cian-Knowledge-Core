# What She Wouldn't Say

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Crow King Alias Chronicle XXIII, wave 8, second
entry. Rebellion era, roughly two years after the Night of the Crow King (`MCD-236`, age 23). Not a
territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters beyond the
already-locked apprentice singer, who remains unnamed matching established pattern.*

---

The apprentice was three months into carrying real operational responsibility when a routine courier
run went wrong — a checkpoint tightened without warning, papers that no longer matched the guard's
own updated manifest, and she was taken for questioning before she'd covered more than two streets
past the gate.

The Directorate officer who interrogated her over the following two days asked, eventually, past the
ordinary questions about names and routes, whether the reports of a chanting technique used against
garrison sensors were genuine, and whether she knew how it worked.

She said nothing at all.

Not a denial, not a deflection built to sound convincing — nothing, held for hours past the point
where silence itself became its own kind of pressure, until the officer gave up on that line of
questioning entirely and moved to simpler things she could safely lie about instead.

"Why didn't you just deny it?" Kanja asked her afterward, once the crew's own extraction had reached
her before any further harm came to it. "A denial would have been easier to sustain than nothing at
all."

"You taught me that every lie spends something, even a small one." She said it plainly, still
working through the exhaustion of it. "I didn't know what a denial would cost me two questions
later, once he'd caught me in the shape of it. Silence doesn't have a shape he can catch me in. It
just sits there until he gets tired of waiting on it."

"That's not something I taught you directly."

"No. You taught me to listen before I decide what a moment needs. I listened to two days of
questions and decided the thing they actually needed from me was nothing at all." She met his look
steadily, past the fatigue. "I think that's the same lesson. I just had to find my own way to it,
under worse conditions than you probably meant for me to learn it in."
