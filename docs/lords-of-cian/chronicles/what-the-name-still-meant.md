# What the Name Still Meant

*Locked canon, Batch 254, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1120`). Bane Alias Chronicle XC, wave 30, closing wave 30.
Closing entry for the run, framed as continuation rather than retirement -- direct counterpoint to
wave 15's "What the Name Cost Him to Set Down."*

---

Efa Gol had been the first to check on the man beneath the alias, back before it had a name worth
noticing, and she was the one who asked him now, ninety operations and however many years into it,
whether the name still fit.

"You asked yourself that once before," she said. "Told Hask the alias had done what it was for.
That was a long time ago. It's still here."

"It is." Bane didn't treat the question as one he hadn't already sat with. "I don't think I was wrong,
that night. I think I was measuring the wrong thing. I was asking whether Bane still described what I
needed to be, and the honest answer was, by then, mostly not. But that was never actually the
question that mattered."

"What was?"

"Whether there are still people out there who need that door specifically — the one that looks like
restraint instead of a title their own crew gave a man, the one that reaches someone who wouldn't
trust the Trench Monarch or the Blue-Collar Titan or any of the rest of it fast enough to matter. And
there are. There will be, for a while yet. The name doesn't have to be about what I need anymore. It's
about which face gets to the right person fastest, when the moment doesn't leave room for them to
learn to trust a different one first."

Efa Gol looked at him the way she had for years, seeing past whatever the moment's name happened to
be to the man who'd been under all of them from the start.

"So it doesn't end."

"Not because it means less than it did. Because there's still work only that door opens." Bane
looked out at the column settling in for the night, ninety operations of it behind them now, however
many more ahead. "The name still means what it always meant. I'm just done asking whether it's mine
to set down. It was never really that. It's theirs, for as long as they need it to be."
