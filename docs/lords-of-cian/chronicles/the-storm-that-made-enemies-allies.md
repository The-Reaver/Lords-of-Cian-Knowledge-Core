# The Storm That Made Enemies Allies

*Locked canon, Batch 190, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-555`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XVI, first
entry in the fifth wave. Rebellion era, age 29, the Gale Straits. Not a territory Chronicle. New
standalone material — a detailed, large-scale combat-and-survival showcase per Abad's craft
instruction. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Sephtis's prediction gave warning of a storm larger than any the doctrine had yet timed around — not
an advantage to exploit, but a shared danger about to catch both the rebel fleet and the Directorate
squadron it had been engaging in the same stretch of water, neither side able to disengage far enough
to escape it before it broke.

Kanja made the call no prior engagement had required: he signaled the Directorate squadron directly,
offering a truce for the storm's duration rather than press an advantage neither side could actually
survive exploiting.

The squadron's own commander, faced with a storm bearing down on both fleets equally, accepted. What
followed was neither battle nor rescue but something the Gale Straits had never seen before — rebel
and Directorate vessels working the same storm together, Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System helping
stabilize a Directorate ship's own failing rigging alongside the flagship's, Obsidian Malice clearing
debris threatening both fleets equally, the two forces surviving the night through cooperation neither
side would have chosen under any other circumstance.

Dawn found both fleets battered but intact, the storm passed, the truce's own terms never
specifying what happened once the shared danger ended.

"We could resume the engagement," the Directorate commander said, watching his own crew work
alongside rebels who'd been trying to sink them a day before. "Nothing in what we agreed to prevents
it."

"Nothing does. I'd rather not, if you're willing to extend the same restraint I am. We've just spent a
night keeping each other's crews alive. I don't know that either of us can un-know that by morning."
Kanja met the commander's own visible uncertainty without pressing either way. "The choice is yours as
much as mine. I'll honor whichever you make."

The commander withdrew his squadron without further engagement, the storm's shared danger having
bought, if not peace, at least one night's genuine humanity between enemies who'd have killed each
other gladly the morning before.
