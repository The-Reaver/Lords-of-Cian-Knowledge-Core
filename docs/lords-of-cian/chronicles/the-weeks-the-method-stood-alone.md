# The Weeks the Method Stood Alone

*Locked canon, Batch 262, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1328`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LXXXII,
first entry in the twenty-eighth wave. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign
(MCD-241). Kanja incapacitated by a serious, non-fatal injury, testing whether the campaign holds
without him at its center. Not a territory Annals entry. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.

It wasn't an enemy that put him down, in the end, but a structural failure he hadn't fully
diagnosed before trusting his own weight to it — a half-collapsed foundry roof he'd gone up to
brace alone rather than wait for a second pair of hands, a support beam giving way under a load
even Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System couldn't fully redirect in the half-second it had to work
with. He came down with the beam rather than under it, which the healers agreed afterward was the
only reason he was alive to be furious about the recovery time they gave him.

Three weeks, minimum, before the injury to his ribs and the deep bruising along one side would let
him do more than walk the terrace slowly, watching other hands do the work his own hands had done
for every prior week of the tour. It was the longest stretch since the Rolling Foundry Campaign
began that the Lord of Embers wasn't, in any functional sense, doing the thing his own alias was
named for.

The campaign did not stop. That was the part that surprised him most, watching it from a bench at
the terrace's edge rather than from the middle of the work — the senior smith's successor running
the floor the way she had every day since her own predecessor stepped back, apprentices teaching
newer apprentices without being told to, a rebuild in a nearby
district completed on schedule by a crew that had learned the method well enough to no longer need
him present for it to hold.

"You built something that doesn't actually need you standing in the middle of it," the successor
told him, on the second week, watching him try and fail to disguise his own frustration at being
useless. "That was always the point, wasn't it? A method that only works while you're personally
lifting the hammer isn't really a method. It's just you."

"I know that," he said. "Knowing it and sitting through three weeks of proving it are different
things."

"Then sit through it," she said, not unkindly. "It's the last test the method actually needed, and
you're the only one who could give it to it honestly."

He healed, eventually, faster than an ordinary man would have and slower than his own impatience
wanted, and returned to the floor in the third week to a terrace that had genuinely not needed him
to stop running at any point while he was gone — the clearest proof the whole campaign had ever
produced that what he'd built was bigger than the man who'd started it.
