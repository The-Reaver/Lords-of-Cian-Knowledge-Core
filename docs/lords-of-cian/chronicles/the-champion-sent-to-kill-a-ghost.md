# The Champion Sent to Kill a Ghost

*Locked canon, Batch 280, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1460`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
XCIV, wave 32, first entry in the wave. A detailed, battle-intense personal duel showcase against a
Directorate-trained champion sent to kill Kanja specifically, distinct from every prior fleet-scale or
institutional antagonist. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The challenge came the ordinary way a message reaches a ghost — left with a fisherman who'd been paid
to deliver it and told nothing else. A single islet, no witnesses beyond the fleet's own lookouts, one
man against one man, no ships involved. Signed with no name, only a rank: First Blade of the Coastal
Directorate.

"He's not lying about the training," Garren Hask said, reading the man's approach through a glass as
the skiff put in. "Walks like he's spent twenty years being told he's the best they've got."

"He's not wrong to think it," Kanja said, and went ashore alone, unarmored but for the Ironhand
Gauntlets and the sword at his hip, leaving the Trinity's larger weight anchored offshore. This was a
duel, and he intended to fight it as one.

The First Blade was fast in a way most men weren't — trained specifically, it turned out over the
first exchange, against exactly this: a swordsman who moved like Onyx of Oblivion's Cadence Ruin,
reading rhythm and breaking it. He didn't fight to a rhythm at all. Every strike came from a different
tempo than the one before it, denying Cadence Ruin anything to seize on, and for the first several
minutes it worked — three cuts landed shallow across Kanja's forearm before Onyx found the pattern
underneath the deliberate patternlessness: the man's breath, which no amount of training could make
arrhythmic on command.

Soulbound Edge caught the killing stroke a half-second before it would have landed, turning it wide.
Whisper of Shadows read the islet's own wind shift to warn of a second blade the man had kept sheathed
until desperation called for it. Kanja ended it standing over him, sword at his throat, both of them
breathing hard.

"Finish it," the First Blade said. "I lost. I know what that means."

"It means you go home and tell them the truth," Kanja said, sheathing the blade instead. "That's the
only thing I want from this. Not your death. Your honest word."

The man stared up at him for a long moment, more shaken by the mercy than he'd been by the loss, and
said nothing at all as Kanja turned and walked back down to the water.
