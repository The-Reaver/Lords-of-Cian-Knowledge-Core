# The Raid He Watched From the Rigging

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Scourge Alias Chronicle XLVII, wave 16. Long Mask era, age 265, V4 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Efa Gol's successor had commanded decoy operations and boarding parties for over eighty years by
then, always beside him, never once without him within reach. This time he told her, three days
before the raid, that she would run the whole of it herself.

"The whole of it," she repeated, in a tone that wasn't disbelief so much as a demand he confirm it
twice.

"The whole of it. I'll be aboard. I won't take the deck." He said it plainly, without ceremony, the
way he gave every order that mattered. "Two hundred years from now I won't be aboard at all. Better
we find out now, with me one shout away, whether this holds without my hands doing the holding."

She didn't argue the logic, though he could see her weighing whether to argue the timing instead, and
chose not to.

The transport she'd chosen to intercept was smaller than most of their targets, deliberately — his
one concession, not stated aloud as a concession. She read the approach correctly, held the crescent
formation at the range the doctrine called for, and took the boarding action across two decks without
a single order that departed from what he'd have given in her place.

The moment that mattered came at the hold itself, where the transport's overseer put a blade to a
captive's throat and offered the only bargain overseers ever offered — passage for himself, in
exchange for the girl living. He watched from the rigging, one hand already on the Ironhand Gauntlet's
release clasp, and made himself wait.

She didn't rush it. She lowered her own weapon first, spoke to the overseer in the same unhurried
register Kanja himself used in exactly this kind of standoff, and gave him the one thing he actually
wanted more than passage — being listened to, for six long minutes, until his grip loosened enough
that the girl could step back on her own.

Two hundred and nineteen captives were freed before dawn, the overseer taken alive, and not once had
he needed to come down from the rigging.

"You watched the whole thing," she said afterward, not quite a question.

"I did." He came down at last, the gear still fully charged, unused. "You didn't need me tonight.
That's not nothing to learn, at either of our ages."

"Would you have done it differently?"

"No," he said, and meant it. "That's the part I needed to see most."
