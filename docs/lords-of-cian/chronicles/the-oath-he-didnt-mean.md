# The Oath He Didn't Mean

*Locked canon, Batch 147, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-472`). Taifa Chronicle II. Homage-era Detroit,
Taifa/Osei. Territory Chronicle -- Osei as protagonist, Kanja an unnamed background guest with no
command, credit, or resolution authorship. New standalone material. No new named characters.*

---

A new recruit had sworn the oath the same way every sworn cell member swore it — the same words,
witnessed the same way — and Osei had accepted him into the claimed land's outer network without
suspicion, the man's stated commitment matching everything the oath was supposed to require.

He hadn't meant it. Not fully. He'd sworn it for the protection and standing it offered, treating the
words as a formality rather than a binding, and when a Directorate patrol cornered him weeks later and
offered him a way out in exchange for information, the temptation found ground the genuine oath-takers
never gave it.

Osei didn't feel him the way he felt the others.

"Something's wrong at the outer post," he told his own inner circle, the unease unmistakable but
strange in its specific absence — not the sharp, immediate arrival that had converged sworn hands from
three cities during the dawn raid, but nothing at all where a genuine sworn presence should have
registered. "I can't feel him. I've never once not been able to feel a sworn hand in danger before."

"Maybe he's simply out of range."

"No. Distance has never mattered before. This is different." Osei moved toward the outer post
personally, arriving in time to catch the betrayal mid-transaction rather than after it had already
cost the network everything it might have. The recruit hadn't yet handed over anything decisive; Osei's
own unease, sharpened rather than dulled by the strange absence, had gotten him there before real
damage was done.

"You swore the same words everyone else swears," Osei told the man afterward, more grieved than angry
about it. "I believed you meant them the way everyone else does."

"I meant to. I don't know when that stopped being true." The recruit didn't fully defend himself,
something like genuine shame in the admission. "I told myself I still believed it, even while I was
walking toward that patrol. I don't think I actually did, underneath. Maybe you felt that before I let
myself admit it."

"I didn't feel anything from you at all. That's the whole problem." Osei's voice carried real
sorrow rather than triumph in the correction. "Mere agreement isn't the oath. Admiration for the
cause isn't the oath. I've always known that. I don't think I understood, until tonight, how precisely
the gift itself already knew it too."

An unnamed man, present at the outer post when Osei arrived, said nothing throughout and was gone
before the matter concluded.
