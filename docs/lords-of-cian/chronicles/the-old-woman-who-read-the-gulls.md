# The Old Woman Who Read the Gulls

*Locked canon, Batch 192, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-572`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XXVII, wave 9 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The settlement's oldest resident, a woman who had spent sixty years reading gull flight patterns and
tide foam to know when to keep the boats in without ever once being wrong badly enough to lose a crew,
sought out the courier before he left with his next warning route.

"Tell your sky-reader I want to compare notes," she said. "Not to challenge him. I've done this
sixty years with gulls and foam and a bad knee that aches worse before weather turns. He's got charts
and instruments and whatever else he's built. I want to know if we're seeing the same thing two
different ways, or if one of us is just luckier than the other."

The courier carried the request back, and Sephtis — intrigued rather than dismissive, by the courier's
own account — sent back not a lecture but a genuine question of his own: which signs had she trusted
longest, and how far in advance had they ever reliably given her.

Her answer, delivered over several exchanged letters across the following months, matched his own
method's findings almost exactly at the two-to-three-day range, and diverged sharply beyond it — her
gulls and foam told her nothing reliable more than three days out, where his charts, built from
patterns over open water she'd never had reason to track, could see eleven.

"She's not behind me," Sephtis told Kanja, reading her latest letter. "She's found the same truth
from the opposite direction, close to shore instead of far out. If I ever build a proper network past
just training people to use my own charts, I want settlements like hers in it — not students copying
my method, but people who've already found pieces of it themselves and just never had the rest of the
picture."

She became, without any formal arrangement or title, the first coastal correspondent in what would
eventually grow into a network extending far past anything a single forecaster or his one trained
successor could watch alone.
