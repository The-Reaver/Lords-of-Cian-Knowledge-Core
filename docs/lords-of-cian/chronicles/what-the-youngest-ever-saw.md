# What the Youngest Ever Saw

*Locked canon, Batch 256, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1160`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle LXXVI, wave 26. The method's first case built on a young child bystander's testimony -- not a child laborer's own wage claim, but a witness to someone else's wrongdoing, handled with careful independent corroboration rather than relied on alone.*

---

She was seven, the daughter of a weaver on the second floor, and she hadn't been sent to testify by anyone. She'd simply told her mother what she'd seen three weeks running from the stairwell where she waited for the shift bell: the district administrator counting coin into a second purse behind the pay window before the public counting ever began, moving handfuls from the workers' wage strongbox into his own before the line even formed.

Ezio had never taken formal testimony from a child so young, and he was careful about it in a way he wasn't with adult witnesses — no leading questions, no pressing on details she hesitated over, breaks whenever her attention wandered the way a seven-year-old's naturally did. What she gave him wasn't a figure or a total. It was a description, specific and consistent across two separate tellings a day apart without her mother present for either: the purse's color, roughly how many handfuls, which mornings, and one detail neither Ezio nor Kanja had thought to ask about — that the administrator always did it while humming, as if it were nothing at all.

"I'm not building a finding on a seven-year-old's word alone," Ezio said, plainly, before either of them decided anything. "Not because I doubt her. Because a finding this size needs more than any one witness, child or grown."

What her account gave them was a place to look, not a verdict. Ezio cross-referenced the mornings she'd named against the district's own wage-strongbox weight records — a detail the administrator himself hadn't thought to falsify, since he'd never expected anyone counting ounces of coin against a shift calendar — and found a consistent, measurable shortfall on exactly the mornings the girl had described, matching her rough handful-counts closely enough that guesswork alone couldn't explain it. A second worker, once shown the pattern rather than asked to accuse anyone outright, confirmed having noticed the same purse without ever having said so aloud, afraid of not being believed.

The finding, when it closed, credited the girl's account honestly as the reason the investigation started, without making her testimony carry weight it hadn't been asked to carry alone. Kanja made a point of telling her so directly, crouched to her height in the stairwell where she'd first seen it, that she hadn't gotten anyone in trouble by talking — the numbers had done that, once they knew where to look, and the only thing her honesty had done was tell two grown men where the numbers were hiding.

"You could've built the case faster taking her word as enough," Ezio said afterward.

"Faster isn't the same as right. She did her part. Ours was making sure hers never had to carry more than it should."
