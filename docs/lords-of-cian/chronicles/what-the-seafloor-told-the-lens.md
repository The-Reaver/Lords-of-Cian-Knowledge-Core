# What the Seafloor Told the Lens

*Locked canon, Batch 280, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1462`); corrected Batch 321, 2026-10-02 (the Lodestone Lens
is a Book-2-onward Moonvault gift that doesn't exist in this Rebellion-era window -- swapped for
Kanja's own unaided Rex/Mar senses). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
XCVI, wave 32, closing the wave. A detailed-senses register: Kanja reads a shifting seafloor at
extreme range to save a convoy from a hazard no lookout could see coming. Not a territory Chronicle.
Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

There was no storm, no enemy sail, nothing a lookout could have raised an alarm over. The merchant
convoy running two days ahead of the fleet's own course was simply sailing toward water that hadn't
been dangerous a season ago and had quietly become so since — a shoal built up fast by an undersea
slide nobody living had charted, sitting exactly where Danne Sok's own maps still marked safe depth.

Kanja found it the way he found most things the Trinity's other senses couldn't reach: standing at the
deck rail, eyes shut, letting his own Rex/Mar bloodline senses reach past sight — reading the
seafloor's composition and contour across a distance that made Garren Hask whistle low the first time
he watched it work properly.

"How far out is that," Hask asked.

"Further than the convoy can see it," Kanja said, already calling for full sail. "Which is the whole
problem."

The Ledger closed the gap in six hours of hard running, catching the lead merchant vessel with barely
enough daylight left to redirect the whole convoy around water that would have opened three hulls
before nightfall. The convoy's own captain, when Kanja explained what had actually happened, didn't
believe the shoal was real until a longboat sounded it directly and came back with numbers that matched
his reading within a fathom.

"Your charts weren't wrong when they were drawn," Kanja told him. "The sea just doesn't hold still for
anyone, chart or ghost."

Danne Sok redrew that stretch of coastline personally once the fleet made port again, crediting the
correction to "a reading from the deck" without further explanation — the kind of thing, Garren Hask
noted in the ledger, that kept happening often enough by now that nobody on the crew thought to ask how
anymore.
