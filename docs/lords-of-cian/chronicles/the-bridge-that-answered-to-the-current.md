# The Bridge That Answered to the Current

*Locked canon, Batch 283, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1494`); corrected Batch 321, 2026-10-02 (the Trinity
gear reworded throughout to the Long-Mask-era kit, since wave 34 falls years past the Trinity's
age-30 surrender, `MCD-246`; the second student's pronoun corrected to she/her, the majority usage).
Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle CI, wave 34. A detailed, battle-intense combat showcase on a floating
pontoon crossing, the doctrine's first application to a structure whose baseline never stops moving.
Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

Every fixed structure the doctrine had ever read held a tension that, absent interference, stayed where it belonged — a bridge's cables strained the same amount at midnight as at noon, a hull's keel answered the same load asleep or underway. The pontoon crossing lashed together from a dozen hulls to carry a column over a river too wide and too fast to ford had never once held still in its life. Its whole structure breathed with the current, timber and rope answering the water's push and pull in a cycle that never repeated exactly the same way twice, and Kanja understood within the first minute of listening that the standard baseline — read once, hold it, watch for deviation — was going to get someone killed the moment he tried to apply it here.

"It's not lying to me," he told the second student, present as escort and now, three years into her own inspectorate post, a second set of trained hands Kanja was glad to have alongside him. "It's just never staying still long enough to tell me one thing twice."

The ambush came at full dark, exactly when the column was most committed to the crossing and least able to reverse it — archers from both banks, and beneath the arrows, a swimmer working a blade against one of the mooring lines holding the whole assembly against the current's pull. Cut cleanly and left undetected, that single line would let the current swing the entire crossing broadside and dump half the column into water moving faster than any of them could swim against, all without a sound the standard baseline would have flagged as wrong, since a bridge that was supposed to shift with the current gave the cutting act nowhere obvious to hide.

The Forge-Coat and Ironfall Boots absorbed the arrow volleys and fed the impact into the crossing's own structure rather than any soldier's shield. The Rexmar Machete held the column's own rhythm steady against panic while Kanja worked the read the only way that could possibly work on a structure this alive: not a single baseline, but a continuously updating one, recalculating what "normal" meant for this exact stretch of current every few seconds, the same discipline the storm-tested keel read had taught him years before, pushed now into real time under fire. He caught the cut mooring line's failing tension in the half-second before the current would have swung it wide, and the Ironhand Gauntlets went not into the enemy but into the crossing's own emergency lashing — driving a replacement tension home hard enough to hold the line through the swimmer's cut before the break could finish.

The column crossed intact, the swimmer taken alive by soldiers posted at the far bank, and the pontoon crossing swayed on, exactly as unsettled and exactly as sound as it had been before anyone tried to end it.
