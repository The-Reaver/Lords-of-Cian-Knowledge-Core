# The Coat He Almost Didn't Put Back On

*Locked canon, Batch 259, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1255`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle XC, wave 30, closing
the wave. Age 313, V4 gear, one year before the Long Mask's established close. Not a territory
Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The mission had gone badly enough, and cost enough of what little it had cost the crew in recent
years, that Kanja sat with the Forge-Coat folded across his knees afterward longer than he'd sat with
any piece of the loadout in decades, turning over a thought that had been arriving more often lately
without ever quite finishing itself: that two hundred and eighty-three years might already be enough.
That the coat could stay folded. That whatever remained to be done under this name could, perhaps,
be someone else's work to finish now.

Efa Gol found him like that and didn't tell him he was wrong, which was its own kind of answer. She
sat with him a while instead, saying nothing, until Garren Hask arrived with the count from the
night's operation — the actual number, freed and safe despite everything that had gone badly getting
them there — and set it down beside him without comment, the way he'd set down every count for two
hundred and seventy-eight years running.

Efa Gol's successor came last, with the one piece of information that hadn't reached him yet: a
route, already scouted, already confirmed, already timed for a window three weeks out that would
close again for another full year if it went unused now. Not urgent in the way a crisis was urgent.
Urgent in the quieter way of a chance that wouldn't come back around, for people who had no way of
knowing a chance existed to wait for.

He didn't decide that night. He sat with the coat a while longer, unfolded it once, refolded it once
more, and put it back where it had lived for decades rather than setting it aside for good. Not
because the thought that had brought him to that chair was wrong. Because there was, it turned out,
one more year of work still worth finishing before the question of whether two hundred and
eighty-three years was enough needed an answer at all.
