# The Boy Who Didn't Know His Name

*Locked canon, Batch 133, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-448`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle IX, closing the third
wave. Long Mask era, age ~210, generations into the persona's 284-year span. Not a territory
Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The cabin boy had been born on a ship the Scourge's own reputation had once freed, decades before
the boy himself existed, raised on a version of the story that had already drifted several
generations from anything resembling a precise account. He didn't know, when he was assigned to
serve the flagship's own quiet, unassuming quartermaster during a supply run, that the man pouring
his own tea belowdecks was the same figure the boy's grandmother still told stories about.

"They say he doesn't sleep," the boy said, not recognizing his audience, filling the silence the way
young crew often did around a stranger. "They say the smoke follows him even when there's no fire
burning. My gran says he pulled two hundred people out of a keep with his bare hands and didn't say a
single word the whole time doing it."

"Your gran sounds like she tells a good story." The quartermaster didn't correct the number, didn't
correct any of it. "Most of what gets said about him is more story than fact, by now. That tends to
happen to reputations that outlast the people who first earned them."

"You don't believe the stories?"

"I believe there's usually something true underneath even the most exaggerated version. I just don't
think the true part is ever quite as interesting as what people prefer to repeat." He set the boy's
own cup down beside his. "For what it's worth — whoever he actually is, under all those stories — I'd
guess he'd rather be remembered for the two hundred people who actually walked out of that keep than
for whether or not he slept the night before he did it."

The boy considered that for a long moment, the particular seriousness of someone encountering an idea
too large to fully hold yet. "I hadn't thought about it that way."

"Most people don't. The story's usually easier to carry than the person underneath it." The
quartermaster finished his own tea and rose to return to his duties, leaving the boy with a version
of the truth close enough to matter, distant enough to keep the older, deeper secret exactly where it
had always needed to stay.
