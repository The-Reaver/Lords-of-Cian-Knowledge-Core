# The Millrace They Tried to Choke

*Locked canon, Batch 273, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1416`). Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XCII, wave 31. A
detailed, battle-intense Trinity combat showcase in the alias's first flowing-water infrastructure
register: a Directorate sabotage team targets the millrace powering a valley's trip-hammer forges.
Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Callum Breck caught it first, the way he'd caught the fabricated reports two seasons back — not the
sabotage itself, which hadn't happened yet, but the shape of the men who'd come to do it. Three
strangers asking too many specific questions about the sluice gates upstream, none of them once
asking about the forges those gates fed. "They don't care about the hammers," he said. "They care
about the water that swings them."

By the time Kanja reached the millrace, the team had already wedged a deliberate log-jam across the
narrow channel above the wheel-house, choking the flow that drove three separate trip-hammer lines
at once. Cut the water, and every forge in the valley went silent without a single blow struck
against them directly — starvation dressed up as engineering.

He didn't fight the jam. He fought the men who'd built it, and the water became the terrain the
whole engagement was fought across. Obsidian Malice's discharge didn't blast the log-jam apart in one
crude pulse — it cut through the wedged timber in a sequence of narrow, resonant slices, precise
enough to free the channel without sending a wall of backed-up current crashing down on the
wheel-house below it. Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System, built to ground a blow rather than deliver
one, took the sudden surge of released current straight through his own frame and fed it back into
the wheel-house's own foundation timbers, bracing a structure never built to survive its water
arriving all at once.

The saboteurs themselves had scattered upstream the moment the channel started moving again, banking
on the noise of freed water covering their retreat. It didn't. Onyx of Oblivion's Cadence Ruin had
already learned the millrace's honest rhythm in the minutes before the jam broke — the steady, even
slap of current against the banks — and the wrongness of three men moving against that rhythm stood
out from a hundred yards off, over rushing water, the way a hoofbeat stands out from a heartbeat once
you've learned to listen for the difference. Whisper of Shadows closed the distance before they
reached the tree line; Veil Piercer ended it before any of the three could reach the second gate they
were counting on.

The trip-hammers were swinging again inside the hour, feeding on water that had never fully stopped
moving. Callum Breck, walking the freed channel afterward, found the thing that actually told him
who'd sent them — a maker's mark on the abandoned wedging tools, Directorate quartermaster issue,
nothing improvised about it. "They planned this one properly," he said. "Somebody finally understood
what actually keeps this valley running."

"Then they understood it a season too late," Kanja said, watching the wheel turn. "It was never just
the fire. It was never just me, either."
