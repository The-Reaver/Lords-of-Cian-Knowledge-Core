# Daba Chronicle X: Four Hands, One Body

*Locked canon, Batch 296, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1580`). Entry 10 of 50 in Daba's own Chronicle series,
launched under the Character Chronicle Gameplan's own protagonist-series model. Dramatizes the
cross-trained cell structure taking deliberate shape for the first time -- reconnaissance, demolitions,
medical support, and combat differentiating into named roles within the founding core rather than
remaining one undifferentiated fighting group, extending `MCD-1567`'s "dispersed cells cross-trained
across reconnaissance, demolitions, medical support, and combat" clause from stated design to its
founding moment. Reuses Daba, Sael, Nessa, Corvin, and Farro (`MCD-1576`-`1579`); introduces Ketra,
collision-checked clean. Abad's approval: "rate 50 Chronicles in batches using as many agents as

The sixth member joined them not because Daba went looking for her, but because Sootgate's success
made the smallest possible kind of noise -- not the noise of a raid gone wrong, but the quieter noise
of hungry families suddenly less hungry, which traveled, eventually, to a woman named Ketra who had
spent the two years since the fire teaching herself, alone and by trial, how Trust-issue locks and
seals were built well enough to know exactly how they failed.

Daba tested her the way he had tested all of them, though by now the test had a shape he no longer had
to invent from nothing each time -- he gave her something valuable and watched what she chose not to do
with it. She passed the way Sael had passed, by declining the opportunity in front of her in favor of
the harder, quieter work of proving she understood why declining mattered. He brought her in within the
week.

It was Ketra's arrival, more than any single conversation, that forced the six of them to admit
something Daba had suspected for a while and had not yet said aloud: that they had stopped being five
people who each did a little of everything, and had become, almost without deciding to, six people who
each did one thing better than the rest of them combined.

He raised it plainly, the way he raised most things, on an evening when all six of them were gathered
in the cellar for the first time since Ketra had joined. "We've been calling ourselves one thing," he
said. "I don't think that's true anymore, and I don't think it should be."

Sael understood first, because she had already been living the shape of it for weeks without a name for
it. "I don't fight," she said. "I've never fought. I watch, and I count, and I tell the rest of you what
I've counted. If someone hands me a blade in the middle of an operation, that means the operation has
already gone wrong."

"Same," Ketra said, surprising herself slightly with how readily the word came. "I open things and I
close things behind me. I'm not the one who decides what happens after a door's open. That's not my
skill and I don't want it to become my skill, because the day I start thinking it is, I'll get careless
about the part I'm actually good at."

Nessa spoke next, quieter, the way she always spoke, but no less certain. "I set bones. I stop bleeding.
I decide, sometimes, who can walk another mile and who can't, and that decision matters more than
anything a blade could do in the same hour. I don't want to be handed a weapon and asked to also be
the reason someone survives the week. Those two things pull a person in different directions, and I've
watched what happens to people who try to hold both at once."

Corvin was the last to speak, and the pause before he did told Daba something had shifted in him since
the argument in this same cellar weeks before. "I'm the one who goes through the door once it's open,"
he said. "That's what I'm for. I used to think that made me the important part. I don't think that
anymore. I think I'm one hand out of four, and the other three matter just as much as mine does, and
none of us is the whole body by ourselves."

Farro, quiet as ever, only nodded. His part had never needed much explaining -- the roads, the routes,
the reasons a wagon could move at an odd hour without drawing an eye -- but hearing the others name
their own pieces made him say it plainly too, for the first time. "I carry things. That's all. I'm not
ashamed of it being small. Small is the whole design."

Daba looked around the cellar at the six of them, each now sitting in a corner that had, without any of
them planning it, come to belong to a particular kind of work -- Sael nearest the stair where she could
watch the street, Ketra nearest the tools, Nessa nearest whatever meager stores of cloth and thread they
kept for wounds nobody hoped they'd need, Corvin nearest the door itself, Farro nearest the maps.

"That's the whole of it, then," he said. "Not one fist. Four hands, doing four different things, and no
one of us able to do what another does, which means no one of us can be taken and cost the rest of us
everything we know how to do. Lose Sael, and the group can still open a lock, still set a bone, still
carry a load. Lose any one hand, and the body keeps standing."

"And you?" Corvin asked. "What's your hand?"

Daba considered the question longer than any of the others had considered theirs, because it was the
one he had thought about least, having spent every hour since the fire thinking about everyone but
himself. "I hold the shape of it," he said finally. "I decide which four hands work together on which
night, and why, and I carry the reason none of you ever has to carry more than your own piece. That's
not a fifth skill. It's just the work of making sure the other four stay four, instead of becoming one
thing large enough to be found."

Nobody argued with that. In the quiet that followed, Ketra reached out and touched the chalk numbers
still faint on the cellar wall -- four digits, worn now at the edges where hands had brushed past them
on their way to their own corners -- and said nothing at all, because by now nothing needed saying.
