# The Man Who Wore the Name

*Locked canon, Batch 118, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-401`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle IV, first entry
in the second wave. Rebellion era, within the Trench Monarch's early window (age 18). Not a
territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters.*

---

The man calling himself the Trench Monarch's lieutenant had been extorting protection money from
three canal districts for a month before word of it reached Kanja's own crew, and by then the
imposter had built a genuine local following on the strength of a name he had no claim to.

"He's using your reputation to rob the exact people you built it protecting," Callum Breck said,
laying out what the crew had confirmed. "Says he answers to you directly. Says the money's going to
fund the district's defense. It's not going anywhere but his own pocket."

Kanja found the man three days later, holding court in a warehouse with a small armed following and
a canal-district audience that had, understandably, started to distrust the name that had once meant
safety.

"You've been telling people I sent you."

"I never said your name outright." The imposter didn't quite meet his eyes. "Just let people draw
their own conclusions. It's not illegal to let a crown speak for itself."

"It's not a crown. I didn't want it in the first place, and you've spent a month teaching four
hundred people it means extortion instead of protection." Kanja didn't raise his voice, didn't reach
for Onyx. "Here's what's going to happen. You're going to return every scrip you've taken, in front
of the same people you took it from. Then you're going to leave this district and never use my name
again for anything, because the next time I hear it's happened, I won't be interested in a
conversation."

The imposter's small following, sensing which way the confrontation was actually going, quietly
began drifting toward the warehouse's exits before he'd finished deciding whether to argue. He
returned the money within the hour, publicly, humiliated rather than harmed, and was gone from the
district by nightfall.

"You could have made an example of him," Breck said afterward, watching the last of the returned
scrip get redistributed to its original owners. "Would have sent a clearer message."

"The clearest message is that the name doesn't protect theft, even when a real threat is standing
right in front of the man wearing it falsely." Kanja watched the district residents count their
returned money, cautious but recovering. "I didn't ask for this reputation. I don't get to control
who tries to borrow it. But I can make sure that every time somebody tries, it costs them more than
it's worth, until nobody's tempted to try again."
