# What the Ledger Said About the Boy He Cut Free

*Locked canon, Batch 259, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1231`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LXVI, wave 22,
closing the wave. Age 160, V3 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose.*

---

Garren Hask's ledger had a line for the man from thirty-eight years back — the one he'd cut free as
a nine-year-old captive, the one who'd grown up to run a small trafficking operation of his own
before Kanja found him and, rather than kill or forgive him, handed him bodily to Hask's own
bookkeeping instead of a verdict. *Held for account*, the line had read for years. Not released. Not
condemned. A debt still being tallied, its final sum not yet reached.

Kanja asked to see the line again on a stopover that had nothing to do with the man at all — a
supply run, a coincidence of geography, an old habit of checking. Hask found it without needing to
search. The entry hadn't changed in eleven years. Not because nothing had happened, Hask said, but
because nothing had been reported. The man had walked free of the crew's own loose custody four
years after being handed over, and simply hadn't been seen or heard from since — no relapse into the
old trade that anyone had traced, no reform anyone could point to either, just an absence, the kind
a ledger has no honest way to close.

"So we don't know," Kanja said.

"We don't know," Hask agreed. "I could write *presumed reformed*. I could write *presumed
returned to it*. Either one would let me close the line and feel better about it. Neither one would
be true, and you taught me a long time ago what a false close costs a ledger that has to be trusted
later." He didn't write anything new. The line stayed exactly as it was — *held for account* — a
debt neither forgiven nor collected, thirty-eight years old and counting, honest in its own
unfinished shape.

Kanja looked at it a long moment longer than the visit required, then closed the book himself and
handed it back. Some things, he'd learned, freedom didn't redeem and time didn't resolve either. It
just kept them open, the way an honest account was supposed to.
