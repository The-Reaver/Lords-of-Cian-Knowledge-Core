# What Pell Ostra Wrote in the Margins

*Locked canon, Batch 256, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1171`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle LXXXVII, wave 29, closing the wave. Pell Ostra's private, inadmissible shadow-notes -- kept alongside the official ledger but never shown to Ezio -- prove useful when a later case needs context the official numbers can't supply.*

---

She'd kept the habit since her first season guarding the tally operations, long before either Kanja or Ezio knew it existed: a private notebook, separate from anything either of them would ever call evidence, where she wrote down what she noticed standing at the edge of every testimony tent that never made it onto Ezio's official page — who flinched at which question, who exchanged a glance with whom before answering, which administrator's confidence cracked half a second before his words did. None of it was fact in the way the ledger demanded fact. It was impression, kept for no purpose she could have named clearly even to herself.

It mattered, finally, eleven months into a case Ezio had never been able to fully close. A shortfall at a shipping district had been documented and paid two years earlier, the file marked resolved, the administrator responsible long since replaced. When a strikingly similar fraud surfaced at a different district under a different name, Ezio's own records offered nothing to connect the two — different owner, different paperwork, no shared thread the official ledger had any way to show.

Pell Ostra remembered something the ledger never had. She'd noted, in her own private shorthand two years earlier, an unfamiliar man standing at the edge of that first tent throughout the whole audit, never testifying, never named, watching Ezio's methods with an attentiveness she'd found strange enough to write down even though nothing about him had ever seemed threatening. She hadn't mentioned it at the time because there'd been nothing to mention — a man watching wasn't evidence of anything.

"You kept this the whole time," Ezio said, reading two-year-old margin notes he'd never known existed, "and never told me."

"There was nothing in it worth telling you. It wasn't proof of anything. It was just a thing I noticed." What it became, cross-referenced now against a rough physical description of the new district's own suspect, was a lead neither of them would otherwise have had: the same unnamed observer, present at both frauds two years and two districts apart, someone who had apparently studied the method's own vulnerabilities closely enough, unnoticed, to later replicate the fraud in a district far from the first.

It didn't close the second case on its own — Ezio still had to build the arithmetic properly, the way he always did, before anything went into the official finding. But it told them where to look for a pattern the numbers alone hadn't shown, and Kanja made a point, afterward, of asking Pell Ostra to keep doing exactly what she'd always done rather than start writing her impressions into anything official.

"The official book has to hold to what it can prove," he told her. "That doesn't mean what you notice isn't worth keeping somewhere. Just keep it where it is."
