# Jibaro Annals III: The Week Five Doors Refused

*Locked canon, Batch 169, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-516`). Homage-era Muungano, Jibaro/Omoba. Territory
Annals entry — Omoba as protagonist, Kanja an unnamed background guest with no command, credit, or
resolution authorship. New standalone material. No new named characters.*

*Corrected Batch 340, 2026-10-02: removed a tech-level anachronism ("rail depot"); reconciled the

---

Five vacant buildings across Jibaro, each left to rot by an institution that still claimed to serve the
people around it — a guild-owned textile mill, a disused city freight depot, three empty tenements a
housing charity had let decay while its board kept collecting its endowment — became the target of a
single coordinated week of occupation, Omoba testing whether "The
Occupation" could hold multiple sites at once rather than only the single buildings every prior use had
tested.

It held all five.

Five separate crews crossed the one-day threshold within hours of each other, each building becoming,
in its own turn, exactly as unreclaimable by force as the seminary had once become — the owning
bodies' own men, who arrived expecting an ordinary eviction finding instead doors, walls, and locks
that simply refused every attempt at forced entry, however many men or however much equipment they
brought against it.

"Five at once," his own second said, watching all five holds settle into the same permanent
unreclaimability within the same week. "I didn't know if it would spread that thin and still work."

"Neither did I, honestly, until we tried it. I suspect it doesn't care how many buildings are asking,
only whether each one genuinely qualifies — held past the threshold, owned by someone who'd be
ashamed to be seen forcing it." Omoba reviewed the week's full account, all five buildings now
permanently under the occupation's protection. "The morally mixed schoolhouse taught me the gift has
real limits. This week taught me scale isn't one of them, as long as every site actually meets the
same standard."

The five buildings became, within months, a functioning cooperative housing and workshop network —
Jibaro's largest coordinated redistribution of unused property in a single campaign, made possible
entirely by an ability that had once seemed, from its first single-building test, far more modest than
it actually was.

"Five doors, one week," the second reflected, watching the network take its first tenants. "That's not
a small thing to have proven."

"It's not. I'd still rather prove it slowly and correctly than quickly and carelessly. Five was
already close to more than I was confident testing at once." Omoba watched the new tenants settle in,
satisfied without claiming the outcome had ever been a certainty going in. "Next time, maybe six.
We'll find the actual edge of this eventually, one careful test at a time."

An unnamed man who had helped carry belongings into two of the five buildings during the transition
left the district once the network was fully settled.
