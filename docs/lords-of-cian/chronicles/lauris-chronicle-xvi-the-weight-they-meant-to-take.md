# Lauris Chronicle XVI: The Weight They Meant to Take

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1633`). Sixteenth entry in Lauris Letitia's own Chronicle
series, the third entry of Strand K (Kares Prime / deep past). Dramatizes one of the six defensive
operations against non-Karesian incursions at orbital trade-points from the Long Operational Period
(`MCD-1555`, age 1,841-~3,400), at an unnamed outer trade-point distinct from the Olmedrin point she
later departs through (`MCD-175`). No new named characters -- the smuggling crew stays deliberately
unnamed, matching the project's established convention for secondary figures (Chronicle II's opposing
delegation). Full text below.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Kares Prime, age ~1,900: "Six crates of processed Drakma, one archivist held at
a blade's edge, and a crew that had done the arithmetic on our numbers correctly and the arithmetic
on me not at all. I corrected the second error. I did not need to correct the first."*

I have transcribed a great many of Lauris's archive entries by now, and I have come to recognize a
particular flatness of tone she reserves for the entries she considers, in her own private
accounting, unremarkable. This is one of them. She wrote perhaps a dozen lines about an incident
that, read against everything else in her archive from the period, tells me a great deal about what
the decline actually cost the civilization she was born to protect -- not in bodies, but in the
simple, humiliating fact that outsiders had begun to notice.

The trade-points, by her lifetime, were already thin arrangements: a handful of Karesian factors
overseeing the exchange of Living Drakma micro-particulate and high-gravity-forged metalwork for
textiles and foodstuffs the surviving Vasks could no longer produce in sufficient quantity
themselves. Cooperative-era Kares Prime, she notes elsewhere in her archive, would have staffed such
a point with two hundred defenders as a matter of course, more out of ceremony than necessity. By
her lifetime it was staffed with six, and a crew of outside traders -- their origin never
established, their vessel never identified beyond a hull-marking her archive describes only as
"unfamiliar, deliberately unfamiliar" -- had evidently concluded that six was a number worth testing.

*

The incident, as she records it, took perhaps eleven minutes from the moment the crew produced
weapons to the moment she arrived. I want to be honest about the limits of what I can reconstruct
here: she was not present for the opening of it, having been summoned by a distress signal relayed
through two intermediary Vasks before it reached her at all, which is itself a measure of how thin
the surviving communication network had become. By the time she arrived at the trade-point's
receiving hall, the crew held one of the six factors -- an archivist, unarmed, responsible for
cataloguing the exchange rather than defending it -- with a blade at his throat, and had already
begun loading crated Drakma onto a transport sled bound for their vessel.

She has recorded that she did not announce herself. She simply crossed the hall, at the same
unhurried walk I have since watched her use in rooms considerably further from home, and that the
crew's leader made the same error every outsider who has ever underestimated her makes: he read her
frame, her stillness, her apparent lack of urgency, and adjusted his threat accordingly, tightening
his grip on the archivist rather than releasing him, on the theory that a small, calm woman crossing
an open floor represented a negotiation rather than a foregone conclusion.

She took the blade from his hand before he registered that her stillness had ended. She has recorded
the specific mechanism -- a contact strike to the wrist joint, calibrated precisely enough to open
his grip without breaking it, a distinction I have learned she considers important -- and the
archivist was clear of the blade before the crew's remaining members had fully processed that their
leader was now on the floor. What follows in her account is unremarkable by the standard of anything
I have transcribed from her later Directorate career: four more crew members disarmed inside a
further ninety seconds, none of them seriously injured, the crated Drakma left untouched where it
sat rather than pursued onto the departing vessel, since the archivist's safety, not the cargo,
had been the operation's actual objective.

*

What she chose to record afterward is the part I find genuinely instructive. She did not write about
the fight. She wrote, in the same entry, a single paragraph about the six defenders who had been
posted at the point before her arrival and had, by her own account, done everything correctly --
raised the alarm, protected the archivist as long as they were physically able, held their positions
even after two of them were wounded -- and had still not been enough, on their own, to prevent the
attempt from nearly succeeding. She does not name this a failing on their part. She names it, in
words I have copied exactly: *Six was once ceremony. It is now the whole of what we can spare. The
outsiders have begun to understand the difference, and I do not know how many more trade-points will
be tested before every one of us understands it as well.*

I have thought often, transcribing this entry, about how young she still was when she wrote it --
some sixty years past achieving karth-ven, already the civilization's default answer to a problem it
could not otherwise solve, and already, in this small unremarkable incident at a trade-point whose
name she did not think worth recording, beginning to understand something the Iron-Speakers
themselves would not fully articulate for another fifteen hundred years: that a civilization can be
saved from any single incursion by one sufficiently capable woman, and still, incursion by
incursion, be quietly running out of the numbers that make being saved worth the cost of the saving.

She stayed at the trade-point for three additional days, by her own request, training the six
surviving defenders in a compressed version of the same precision-strike discipline she had used on
the crew's leader. She notes, at the close of the entry, that she did this without being asked, and
without expectation that the Iron-Speakers would authorize her to do it again at every point that
needed it. She was, at that age, already beginning to understand that some debts could not be
repaid one incident at a time. It would be another fifteen hundred years before she stopped trying
to repay them that way regardless.
