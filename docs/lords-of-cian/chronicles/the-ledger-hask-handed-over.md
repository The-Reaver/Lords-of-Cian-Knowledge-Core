# The Ledger Hask Handed Over

*Locked canon, Batch 264, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1384`). Captain Alias Chronicle LXXXIV, wave 28, closing the wave. Garren Hask formally hands the crew's ledger to the deckhand Kanja once taught to read.*

---

Garren Hask had been threatening to hand the ledger over for years, the way he'd once threatened to stop drinking or stop working double watches, which was to say not very seriously at all, until the morning Sera's most recent checkup left him quieter than usual on the walk back to Pier Nine. He didn't announce it dramatically. He simply set the ledger — the real one, the original, ink-worn cover he'd carried since before the Audit even had a name — down in front of the deckhand who'd once needed Kanja's patient teaching to read it at all, and said it was time.

"You've been checking my numbers for two years now," Hask said. "Quietly, the way Efa Gol taught you to, so I wouldn't feel old about it. I noticed. I let it happen because I needed it to happen, and because watching you get good enough to catch my mistakes taught me something about what this ledger's actually for. It was never mine. I just kept it longest."

The deckhand — grown now, steady, unrecognizable as the same anxious young hand who'd once traced letters under Kanja's patient finger — took the ledger with both hands, the same way Hask had once taken it from whoever kept it before him, a lineage none of the newer crew had ever thought to ask about.

"I don't know everything you know," the deckhand said. "Thirty years of context in every line."

"Nobody ever does, the day they take it over. That's not a flaw in the handoff. That's just what a handoff is." Hask smiled, the first genuinely unguarded smile Kanja had seen from him since the health scare years before. "You'll learn the rest the way I did. By keeping it long enough that it becomes yours."

Kanja watched from across the pier, remembering a much younger deckhand sounding out Hask's own handwriting letter by letter, and understood — the way he'd understood watching Sera's hands in her own hall — that some things this crew built would keep outlasting him not because he'd built them to, but because he'd simply gotten out of the way long enough to let them.
