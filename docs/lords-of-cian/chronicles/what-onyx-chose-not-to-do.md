# What Onyx Chose Not to Do

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XLIV, wave 15.
Rebellion era, age 18, the final days before the Battle of the Black Trench. Solo-blade entry,
Mafesto dormant, Obsidian Malice undeployed. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The Compliance officer who came alone to the Coldrace road, three days after the last ordinary
correction, was not looking for a fight. He said so himself, both hands visibly empty, before Kanja
had drawn Onyx at all.

"I was sent to kill you," he said. "I'm telling you that plainly, because I've already decided I'm
not going to. I've watched what's happened on this canal for the better part of a year. I don't
believe anymore that the men giving me the order are the ones actually in the right."

It could have ended there, an easy mercy requiring nothing further. It didn't. Two more officers,
unaware of the first's change of heart, arrived minutes later to finish the assignment he'd
abandoned, and the fight that followed was real, fast, and entirely one-sided — Onyx finding both
attackers' openings within the first exchange, Cadence Ruin and Veil Piercer working in the sequence
they'd worked a hundred times before.

The killing stroke was there, at the end of it, against the second officer — clean, available, no
different from a dozen finished duels before it. Kanja didn't take it. He held the blade a half-inch
from a surrendering man's throat instead, watched the officer's own hands come up, empty, the fight
already decided beyond any need for the last inch to close.

"You could have finished it," the first officer said afterward, watching the second man walked off
under guard rather than left where he'd fallen.

"I could have. I didn't want to." Kanja cleaned Onyx with the same unhurried care he always used,
the blade no different for the restraint than it would have been for the killing stroke. "He'd
already lost. Finishing it past that point isn't justice. It's just violence with better manners
than usual. I'd rather he walk away and tell the story of a fight he lost and survived than not be
able to tell it at all."

He pressed two fingers to the officer's collar before releasing him, the same gesture he'd used
after the very first honorable defeat, months before — the Black Ledger marking not an erasure but an
honest account closed without excess.

"That's twice now I've watched you choose not to finish something you could have," the first officer
said, still turning over his own decision not to complete the assignment he'd come for. "I don't
fully understand what that costs you."

"Neither do I, some days. I don't know if restraint gets easier the longer I carry this, or harder.
I know it's the part of carrying it I trust the most — not the sword's gifts. The choice, every time,
about what I let it do." Kanja sheathed Onyx and looked toward the road home, days now from a battle
neither of them could yet name. "Whatever's coming, I want that to still be true afterward. I don't
know how much that's going to be tested. I suspect I'm about to find out."
