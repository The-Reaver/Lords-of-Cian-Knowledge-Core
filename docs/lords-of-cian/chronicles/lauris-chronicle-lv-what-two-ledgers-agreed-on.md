# Lauris Chronicle LV: What Two Ledgers Agreed On

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1672`). Fifty-fifth entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, Strand W (Witness / present-day, quiet register). A stakes-free entry pairing
Lauris with Garren Hask, the crew's established "counter" and ledger-keeper (`CC-115`) -- a
deliberate thematic echo of Chronicle V's "two archives" evening with Sephtis, distinct in content:
practical bookkeeping rather than ancient architecture, and a record-keeper who counts casualties
and cargo rather than centuries. No new named characters. Written under Abad's blanket
authorization: "lock it, continue uninterrupted, test and push to main."*

---

*Archive fragment, present-day: "Garren counts a thing until the number stops changing. I have
spent six thousand years learning to be patient with numbers that never stop changing. We spent an
evening comparing methods. Neither of us won the argument. I do not think either of us wanted to."*

Garren Hask keeps a ledger the way other men keep a wound — checked daily, never quite closed,
attended to whether or not attending to it changes anything. I have known him since he was a
dock-smith with thirty-one years on Dock-Row Six and a wife whose suspicions of Scrip fraud turned
out, in the end, to have been understated rather than exaggerated, and in all the years since I have
never once seen him let a figure stand unverified simply because verifying it again would be
tedious. He named both of the fleet's flagship-class vessels himself — *The Audit*, *The Receipt* —
and I have come to think the names were never a flourish. They were, plainly, the truest description
he had of what he does.

Lauris found him one evening bent over a cargo tally that would not reconcile, off by some figure
small enough that any other man might have written it down to spoilage and moved on. Garren does not
write things down to spoilage. He recounts.

"How long have you been at this?" she asked.

"Since before supper." He did not look up. "It will not reconcile itself faster for my sitting here
looking cross about it, but I have found no better use for the time than to keep looking cross about
it anyway."

She sat across from him without being asked, which I gather he took, correctly, as an offer rather
than an intrusion, and asked to see the figures herself. What followed was, I am told — I was not
present for the whole of it, having been sent early to fetch a second lamp — nearly three hours of
two people cross-checking a single cargo manifest with the specific, companionable stubbornness of
professionals who each recognize the other's discipline as their own, differently dressed.

I arrived back with the lamp in time to hear the shape of it, if not the whole. Garren counts a thing
the way a smith tests a weld — repeatedly, physically, distrusting anything he has not personally
confirmed twice. Lauris, by her own account to me afterward, does not so much recount as simply
refuse to advance past an uncertainty until the uncertainty resolves itself honestly, which she says
is a slower method but, in her experience, a more durable one. Neither approach, tested against the
same stubborn manifest, produced an answer faster than the other. Both, eventually, produced the
same answer: four crates of salted fish recorded twice under two different dock-hands' hands,
neither dishonest, both simply covering for each other's shift the week the tally was taken.

"Four crates," Garren said, when it was done, in the tone of a man laying down a tool he has been
gripping too hard for too long. "I would have sworn on my own name it was theft."

"You wanted it to be theft," Lauris said — not an accusation, I want to be clear, but the same
plain observation she extends to everyone, including herself. "Theft has a shape you already know
how to close. An honest mistake by two men covering for each other has no shape at all until you've
found it."

He laughed at that, briefly, the sound of a man caught out by someone who has read him correctly and
does not intend to hold it against him. "You sound like my wife."

"I will take that as the compliment I assume it was meant as."

"It was."

I set this account down not for the mistake itself, which was resolved within the hour and cost no
one anything but Garren's evening, but for what I watched pass between the two of them once it was.
Garren Hask has spent this entire war as the man who tallies what everyone else's decisions cost —
ninety-three of a hundred and twenty at the Black Trench, twelve thousand and six freed at Maw-9 —
and I do not think I have ever, before that evening, seen him sit across from someone who tallies
things for a different reason and finds the exercise not a burden but, plainly, satisfying. Lauris
told me afterward that she likes an honest number the way some people like an honest answer, and
that spending an evening with a man who feels precisely the same way about four crates of fish that
she has felt, at various points, about ledgers with considerably higher stakes, was as close to an
ordinary pleasure as her life affords her. I believe her. I have rarely seen either of them look
more at rest than they did closing that manifest, two record-keepers agreeing, at last, on a number
neither of them had wanted to be true.
