# What the Trust Took Back Quietly

*Locked canon, Batch 255, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1138`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle LXXXI, wave 27,
closing the wave. Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. The Trust answers the returned charter with
sanctions instead of correction.*

---

No revised charter ever came back. What came instead, three months after Kanja returned the document
with its auditor clause circled, was quieter and harder to fight directly: a list, informal and
unsigned but unmistakably coordinated, of owners across five districts abruptly finding their Trust
trade licenses under sudden, minor, endlessly renewable review — nothing so crude as an outright
revocation, just enough friction to make doing business with any owner who'd cooperated openly with
Warehouse Twelve's tally table quietly more expensive than doing business with one who hadn't.

Garren Hask found the pattern before anyone named it out loud. "Every owner on this list settled a
dispute through us in the last year. Every single one. That's not a coincidence, that's a punishment
list wearing a licensing review's clothes."

"They can't say that's what it is," Kanja said, reading the same pattern. "Which means we can't prove
it either. Not with figures."

It was, for the first time, a problem the tally method's core strength — proof, documentation, an
honest number an owner couldn't argue with — had no direct purchase on at all. The Trust wasn't
falsifying anything. Every individual review cited a real, if trivial, procedural irregularity;
stacked together across five districts and dozens of owners, the pattern was unmistakable to anyone
looking for it and legally untouchable to anyone trying to prove it in a room like Canal House.

What Kanja did instead wasn't a countermeasure aimed at the Trust at all. He made the cost of
cooperating with Warehouse Twelve smaller rather than trying to make the Trust's retaliation larger —
absorbing, through the crew's own resources and Dol Maren's engineering competence, whatever licensing
delays cost an owner in practical terms, quietly covering transport gaps and storage shortfalls until
each review resolved itself the slow, bureaucratic way reviews eventually did. It cost the crew real
resources across a hard season. It also meant not one of the five districts' cooperating owners backed
away from the tally table, which Kanja understood by then mattered considerably more than winning any
single argument about who was punishing whom.

"They wanted this to make owners afraid of working with us," Hask said, watching the last of the
season's reviews finally clear.

"It didn't." Kanja didn't sound satisfied so much as tired. "That doesn't mean it didn't cost us
anything. It cost plenty. It just didn't cost us the thing they were actually trying to take."
