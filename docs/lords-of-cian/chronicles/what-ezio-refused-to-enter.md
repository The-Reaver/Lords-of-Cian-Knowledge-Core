# What Ezio Refused to Enter

*Locked canon, Batch 214, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-925`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle L, wave 17. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

It was the fastest settlement in reach, and Ezio said so plainly, and Kanja said no anyway.

The mill administrator holding out against a documented wage debt had a second, unrelated fact
attached to his name — a worker had volunteered it unprompted, certain it would end the standoff
inside the hour. A private matter, a family scandal the administrator had spent years keeping quiet,
nothing to do with wages, nothing Ezio had gone looking for. It had simply arrived, offered like a
weapon someone assumed the crew would want to use.

"One line," Ezio said. "I don't even have to phrase it as a threat. He reads the ledger, sees it's in
there, and he opens his books by morning. You know that's true."

"I know it's true."

"Then why are we still sitting here at midnight instead of settled."

Kanja didn't answer right away. He'd turned the same question over himself since the worker left,
and hadn't liked where it kept landing any better the third time than the first.

"Because the day we put something in that book to make a man afraid instead of something to prove
he's wrong, it stops being a ledger. It's leverage with a cover story. And once it's that once, every
administrator we've ever settled with gets to wonder, forever, whether the real reason he opened his
books wasn't the numbers at all — whether it was something we knew about him that had nothing to do
with what he owed anyone."

"He's stalling a debt we can prove six ways without it."

"Then we prove it six ways. It'll take longer. It always takes longer doing it the way that survives
someone checking our work in twenty years instead of the way that wins tonight."

Ezio didn't concede it easily — he pushed back for the better part of an hour, arguing the private
fact wasn't even false, wasn't cruelty, was just true and useful, and refusing a useful true thing
on principle felt to him less like discipline and more like losing on purpose. It was the first real
disagreement between them the campaign had produced, and neither backed down from stating his own
position plainly.

"I'm not telling you you're wrong to think it'd work," Kanja said finally. "I'm telling you what goes
in this book is wages, hours, production counts, and testimony about all three. Nothing a person
would be ashamed to have read aloud in a room that has nothing to do with what they're owed. That's
the whole rule. It doesn't bend because bending it once would be easy."

They settled the district nine days later, on the numbers alone, the slow way. Ezio never raised the
private fact again, and neither of them mentioned, afterward, which one of them had turned out to be
right about how long it would take.

*Continuity notes (not narrative): the first genuine internal disagreement between Kanja and Ezio in
the whole run — not a correction of a worker's testimony (Ezio's established role) but a direct
dispute over what the ledger method itself is permitted to contain, deliberately left with Ezio
unpersuaded in the moment rather than immediately won over. Establishes a hard boundary on the method
explicitly: only wage/hour/production facts and testimony about them, never personal leverage, even
when leverage would be faster and even when the leveraging fact is true. Distinct from "The Man Who
Padded His Own Page" (Ezio correcting a worker's own inflated claim) and from every administrator-
side fabrication entry — this is about what the crew itself refuses to weaponize, not what it catches
others weaponizing. No new named characters; the mill administrator and the worker who volunteered
the fact are both unnamed and off-page. Second entry in the Industrial Myth's seventeenth wave.*
