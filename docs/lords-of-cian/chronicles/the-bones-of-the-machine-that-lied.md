# The Bones of the Machine That Lied

*Locked canon, Batch 272, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1412`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XCI, wave 31, first entry. Wreckage from a Crawler of the depot's unresolved design is recovered and studied, narrowing the mystery's mechanism without naming its author. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The Crawler that reached the scholar's academy six weeks later hadn't been Kanja's to bring down. An allied column had caught it on open ground, unaware of what it was, and put it out of action the plain way — massed fire through a weak axle joint no diagnostic read had ever been needed for. What made it worth the wagon ride to the academy wasn't how it had died. It was that its plating matched the depot's, tooth for tooth, down to the same shudder-and-hold failure Kanja still hadn't found a name for.

"Whoever built this one built the other," the scholar said, running a gloved hand along a seam of the disabling mechanism laid open on his workbench, the third-generation apprentice bent close beside him with a lens Kanja had never seen her carry into a field engagement. "I've had six weeks to think about how, and I still don't like the answer I keep arriving at."

"Say it anyway."

"It's not stolen work. Not the engineer's old corps refining something they took off a dead man, the way the four-alloy variant was. This is *built from the published papers* — my papers, the academy's, the ones we put into open circulation on the belief that an honest method survives being known." The scholar lifted a thin strip of dampening material, its weave unmistakably deliberate. "Someone read exactly what we wrote about how the discharge finds its target and engineered, from that honest description, a structure that lets the finding happen and denies it the part that matters. Not a countermeasure built by guesswork against a rumor. A countermeasure built by comprehension, against the truth."

The third-generation apprentice hadn't spoken yet, turning the strip over under her lens with the same careful, unhurried attention the second student had once taught her to bring to a first read. "That's not an old corps. An old corps wants the design back for themselves. This isn't hoarding the knowledge — it's using ours against us and giving nothing back. Whoever it is, they don't want to be caught teaching it. They want to be caught doing nothing at all."

Kanja turned the fragment in his own hands last, listening to it the way he'd have listened to a live structure, finding nothing in the dead metal but the same honest silence every disarmed thing eventually settled into. "That narrows it less than either of you wants it to. Half the world's read what the academy's published by now. Every settlement that ever asked for a cohort, every council that debated banning it outright, every rival who ever lost a Crawler and wanted to know why — any one of them had the paper in hand long enough to build this from it, if they were patient and precise enough."

"Then what do we do with knowing how, if we still don't know who?"

"We keep publishing." Kanja set the fragment down, and didn't reach for it again. "The scholar built this doctrine on the belief that an honest thing survives being known, and I'm not going to let one design I can't yet beat convince either of us that the answer was always to have kept it a secret instead. Whoever built this learned it fair, the same way every one of you did. That doesn't make them right. It doesn't make hiding the next paper make us any safer, either — it just makes the next Crawler someone else's to solve, the same way this one became yours to help me understand." He looked at the third-generation apprentice, still turning the strip over in her hands like a question she hadn't finished asking it. "Keep the fragment. Next time the depot design shows itself, I want more than a shudder to bring back to this workbench."
