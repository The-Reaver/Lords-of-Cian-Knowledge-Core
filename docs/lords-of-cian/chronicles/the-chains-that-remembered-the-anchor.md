# The Chains That Remembered the Anchor

*Locked canon, Batch 110, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-378`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias
Chronicle II. Rebellion era, a new naval engagement distinct from the Siege of the Ghost Harbor
(`MCD-235`, age 21) -- a detailed showcase of the magnetic-interference weapon improvised from
melted anchor chains, plus the Trinity in a boarding-action context. Not a territory Chronicle. New
standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The convoy escort's captain had been briefed, correctly, that *The Audit* carried some kind of
improvised weapon capable of disabling a hull's iron fittings at range — what he had not been
briefed, because nobody who'd survived to describe it in detail had ever seen it used twice the same
way, was that the device drew its power from the same anchor chains that had once held the ship
herself captive at Iron Shallows, melted down and reforged into a housing that read, to anyone who
didn't know better, as ordinary ballast.

Kanja activated it personally, standing at the housing's control brace as the escort's three
gunships closed to engagement range. The effect built slowly, a low resonant hum climbing through
the deck plates before anything visibly happened — and then every iron fitting on the nearest
gunship's hull answered at once: rigging clasps seizing mid-motion, a gun turret's traverse locking
hard enough to strain its own gears, the anchor windlass below decks screaming as its own chain
tried, uselessly, to obey a pull it had no way to satisfy.

"Boarding party," he said, and went over the rail himself before the paralyzed gunship's crew had
finished understanding what had immobilized their own equipment.

Mafesto took the drop without strain, the Kinetic Transfer System converting the fall's full force
into stored charge before his boots ever found the deck. Three defenders came at him in the
confusion, weapons still functional even where the hull's fittings weren't, and Onyx met the first
exchange with Whisper of Shadows doing to three men in close, unfamiliar quarters what it had once
done to a single attacker in a blind spot — dissolving whatever certainty they carried about where he
actually was in the half-second that mattered most. The second defender's strike found only the
space Kanja had already left. The third found Obsidian Malice instead, the war club's held charge
from the drop released in a single discharge that cleared the forward deck in one motion.

Belowdecks, the ship's own iron heart kept fighting itself the entire time — chains straining toward
a source they couldn't reach, a compass housing spinning uselessly, a crew trying to fire weapons
whose mountings had locked mid-traverse — until the gunship was disabled without a single hull
breach, its captain surrendering not to force but to the specific, disorienting horror of watching
his own ship's ironwork betray him for reasons no report would ever adequately explain.

"Where did you even find the anchor chain to build that thing?" the surrendering captain asked,
watching his own frozen rigging with the particular exhaustion of a man who has just learned
something about the world he cannot unlearn.

"Your side's own chain, captured a year ago at Iron Shallows." Kanja checked the housing's output,
satisfied, and began powering it down before it drew more than the engagement required. "Anchor
chain remembers what it was forged for — holding a ship still against a current that wants to carry
it away. I just gave it a different current to fight. It's not magic. It's the exact same iron, doing
the exact same job it's always done, pointed somewhere your captains never thought to check."
