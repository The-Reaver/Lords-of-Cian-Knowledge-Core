# The Trade He Offered Himself For

*Locked canon, Batch 193, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-605`). Captain Alias Chronicle XXX, wave 10 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Three of the crew had been taken in a failed shore raid, held at a garrison outpost as leverage for
information the Directorate wanted about the fleet's movements, and the negotiator sent to arrange
their return made the offer with obvious confidence that it would be refused: the three crew members
for one hostage of equal value, held under the same terms, for the length of the negotiation.

Kanja offered himself before anyone else could volunteer.

"You understand what you're proposing," the negotiator said, visibly startled. "You'd be unguarded,
inside a garrison, for however long this takes to resolve."

"I understand it completely. That's the point. You want leverage that actually means something to
this crew — take the person it means the most to lose, and let that be the proof this isn't a trick
on my end." He didn't wait for the crew's approval, though several tried to object before he cut
them off. "I'm not doing this because I don't trust the rest of you to negotiate this well without
me. I'm doing it because the fastest way to get three of our people out safely is to remove any
doubt about whether we're negotiating in good faith."

The exchange held for six tense hours — Garren Hask handling the actual terms from the crew's side
with the same unflinching precision he brought to every ledger, while Kanja sat, unguarded and
unresisting, inside a garrison built to hold him if it came to that.

It didn't come to that. The three crew members were returned at the agreed hour, and Kanja walked
out the same way he'd walked in, having never once tested whether the garrison actually could have
held him.

"You could have broken out any time," the negotiator said afterward, some genuine unease in it now.
"Why didn't you?"

"Because the whole value of the trade was that I gave my word I wouldn't, and a word that only holds
until it's inconvenient isn't worth offering in the first place. I'd rather three of my people trust
that I'll come back for them the honest way than have them wonder, every time, whether the story
only worked because I could have cheated."
