# The Engineer Who Fought Like One

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XXXV, second
entry in the twelfth wave. Rebellion era, age 20, the Sewer War of Killane. Not a territory

---

The Trust's combat engineer fought differently than any soldier Kanja had faced under this alias —
not with raw aggression, but with the same structural literacy Kanja himself relied on, reading the
tunnel's geometry for advantage before ever throwing a strike, choosing ground the way a builder
chooses a foundation rather than the way a soldier chooses a line of attack.

"You read stone the way I do," Kanja said, circling him in the narrow gallery both had chosen,
without quite agreeing to it, as the fight's ground. "That's rare in a soldier."

"I'm not a soldier first. I trained as a structural engineer for eleven years before the war made me
something else." The man's stance shifted with the tunnel's own contour, using a support column as
cover the way another fighter might use a shield. "You fight the same way. I've read the reports.
Half of what people call your Trinity's advantage is actually just you understanding the ground
better than whoever you're fighting on it."

"Then this should be a fair fight, for once." Kanja didn't call on Obsidian Malice yet, testing the
engineer's own read of the space first. "Most of what I face doesn't understand the ground at all.
It's rare I get someone who does."

The exchange that followed was unlike anything the alias had fought before — not a contest of raw
capability, but a duel of structural reading, each combatant using the gallery's own geometry
against the other, Onyx's blade finding openings the engineer's careful footwork nearly closed in
time, the engineer's own improvised leverage against a support column nearly turning Mafesto's own
balance against him in a way brute strength alone would never have managed.

It ended not in a killing blow but in a stalemate both recognized simultaneously — the engineer
pinned against the column he'd used as cover, Kanja's blade a half-inch from a strike neither of
them, in that moment, particularly wanted to finish.

"You could take this," the engineer said, breathing hard, no fear in it, only professional
acknowledgment.

"I could. I'd rather not, if there's another way through this that doesn't end with the first person
I've fought in this war who actually understands what I've spent it building." Kanja lowered the
blade. "Walk away. Tell them the tunnel held. It's not a lie — you very nearly proved it could."
