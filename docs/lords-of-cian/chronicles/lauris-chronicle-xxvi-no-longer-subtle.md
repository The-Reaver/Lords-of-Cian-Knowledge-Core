# Lauris Chronicle XXVI: No Longer Subtle

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1643`). Twenty-sixth entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, a Strand D entry giving full-scene treatment to Operation 14, the Vask of the
Hollow (previously only summarized at `MCD-1537`): her solo, eighteen-day clearance of a
fourteen-kilometer subterranean gallery system that had defeated six prior Directorate exploration
teams, terminating roughly 720 engineered hostile entities and reaching the private conclusion that
the pattern she had been noticing since Operation 3 was "no longer subtle." Closes with a shorter
epilogue on Operation 20's return roughly two years later -- the new ~320-subject population
generated by the same central node, and her decision to destroy the node itself rather than merely
reseal the site, the only revision to Directorate containment doctrine her career produced. No new
named characters.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Established Hunter designation LL-001-S-014: "Eighteen days. Seven hundred and
twenty subjects, by my own count, which I trust more than the Directorate's. I stopped, somewhere
around the ninth day, expecting this to be an unusual site. By the eighteenth I understood it was
simply the largest example of something ordinary."*

The Vask of the Hollow reached the Directorate's records as a single line of grim statistic before
Lauris ever set foot inside it: six exploration teams sent in, six failed to return in any
condition the Directorate cared to describe in detail, and a seventh reduced from twelve members to
two before its survivors managed to withdraw. Fourteen kilometers of subterranean gallery, by the
best surveying the Directorate possessed, though I have since learned from Lauris that even this
figure understated the site's true extent by a considerable margin.

She went in alone, which by Operation 14 had become, per the Directorate's own quietly escalating
practice, simply how she was sent -- this being, as her own later archive notes and as Vael
Korr-Drennen's retrospective report would eventually confirm in writing, the operation after which
the Directorate stopped assigning her a supervising mentor at all, judging the arrangement, by then,
a formality neither party required.

I will not attempt to render the eighteen days in the sequence Lauris herself experienced them,
because she has never given me that sequence in full -- what she has given me, across several
separate conversations, is closer to a catalogue than a narrative, and I think the catalogue is the
truer document. Seven hundred and twenty hostile entities, by her own careful count, encountered in
successive chambers across the gallery's full depth, their biology consistently combining
Karesian-derived density features with a Cian-born human substrate -- neither wholly one thing nor
the other, each one a fresh confirmation of a pattern she had first begun to suspect, in far
smaller and more isolated form, at Operation 3, and had watched recur with mounting, undeniable
frequency at every operation since.

It was somewhere within that eighteen-day span, by her own dating, that she stopped thinking of the
pattern as something she was noticing and began thinking of it as something that was, in her own
private phrase, no longer subtle. She has explained the distinction to me carefully, because she
considers it important: a subtle pattern is one a careful observer might reasonably miss. Seven
hundred and twenty engineered bodies in a single gallery system is not subtle. It is, by her
account, closer to an admission -- made by whoever or whatever was responsible, though not
necessarily made knowingly -- that the processing she had been quietly cataloguing since her
earliest contracts had reached a scale no honest report could any longer describe as an anomaly.

She cleared the gallery. She did not, at the time, destroy its central resonance node, judging a
perimeter reseal sufficient by the Directorate's own then-current containment doctrine, and
withdrew after eighteen days having lost, by her own account, nothing she considered worth
recording as a cost.

*

Roughly two years later, Operation 20 returned her to the same site. The perimeter seal had held,
technically, exactly as designed -- and had proven, in the same stroke, entirely insufficient. A
new population, some three hundred and twenty subjects by her count, had been generated from
within, by the same central node her earlier clearance had left intact and active beneath the
Directorate's own seal.

She cleared the new population in considerably less time than the original eighteen days had taken
her, her methodology by then refined past anything Operation 14's version of herself had possessed.
What she did afterward is the detail I consider the more significant of the two operations, and the
one her own archive treats with unusual weight for so brief an action: she located the central node
itself and destroyed it outright, rather than reseal the perimeter a second time and trust a
doctrine that had already, once, proven itself inadequate to a site of this scale.

It was, as far as either of us has been able to determine from her records, the only revision to
Directorate containment doctrine her entire contractor career produced -- central-node destruction
in place of perimeter sealing alone, a change she implemented unilaterally and reported afterward
rather than proposed and waited for approval on, judging, correctly as it turned out, that a second
regrowth was a risk the site's history no longer justified accepting. Fourteen smaller redundancy
nodes her Operation 20 clearance missed entirely would not be found and destroyed until Operation
36, years later still -- a detail she has told me she did not learn until long after, and one she
records without apparent surprise, having by then long since stopped expecting any single clearance
of hers to be the last word on a site of this kind.

Her closing note, written -- by her own dating -- at the close of Operation 20 rather than Operation
14, which I think is itself instructive: *The pattern was never subtle. I was simply slow to stop
calling it one.*
