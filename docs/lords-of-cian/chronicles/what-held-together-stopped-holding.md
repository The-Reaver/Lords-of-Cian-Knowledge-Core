# What Held Together Stopped Holding

*Locked canon, Batch 113, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-387`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle II. Rebellion era,
a new engagement combining the Aegis-Talisman's resonance mechanic (`MCD-238`) with the Trinity in
detailed, technical use. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The engineering corps that designed the Trust's newest Crawler variant had been told, explicitly,
to solve the structural vulnerability the Iron Bastard's Stand had exposed: every prior model shared
a single resonant frequency across its Dead Drakma fittings, which was why ninety minutes of
broadcast harmonic had shaken an entire column's equipment loose from the inside. The new variant
used four independent fitting alloys instead of one, engineered specifically so that no single
frequency could address more than a quarter of any vehicle's structural mass at once.

Kanja opened the engagement by listening rather than broadcasting.

The Aegis-Talisman's frequency-inversion lens, held steady against the lead Crawler's hull for the
first ninety seconds of contact, did not attack anything. It read — cataloguing the four alloys'
individual resonant signatures one at a time, the shield's inverted-lens function turning what should
have been a purely defensive tool into a diagnostic one, mapping the exact frequency each new fitting
would answer to before ever committing to a broadcast. Only once all four signatures were catalogued
did the actual harmonic begin, cycling through each alloy's answer in rapid sequence rather than
attempting the single uniform tone the engineers had specifically designed against.

Twelve Crawlers began shedding fittings within four minutes. Mafesto absorbed the column's answering
fire throughout, the Kinetic Transfer System converting round after round into stored charge that
fed, in turn, into Obsidian Malice's opening strike against the lead vehicle's already-loosened
forward plating — a single blow landing on armor that four independent alloys had each already been
individually betrayed by, the structural failure cascading through a hull that had never actually
needed to be pierced, only persuaded to stop agreeing with its own design specifications. Onyx cleared
the boarding crew that spilled from the collapsing Crawler's rear hatch, Cadence Ruin finding the same
coordinated-timing gap it had found against sixteen Compliance soldiers years and a lifetime earlier.

By the time the engagement ended, eleven of twelve Crawlers had shed enough structural fittings to be
combat-ineffective, and the engineering corps' four-alloy solution had produced, in practice, a
vulnerability with four separate points of entry instead of one.

"You made it worse," the corps' lead engineer would write, in a report that never reached publication.
"We built four locks to replace one, and gave him four keys to find instead of memorizing a single
one. The old design only had one weakness. Ours had four, and he read all of them before we'd
finished believing the first design flaw was fixed."
