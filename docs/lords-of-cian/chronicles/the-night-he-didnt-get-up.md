# The Night He Didn't Get Up

*Locked canon, Batch 214, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-928`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle LIII, wave 18. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The tanning district's testimony was scheduled to begin at first light, the way it always did, and
first light came and went with the tally table still folded on the cart and Kanja still asleep behind
it.

Pell Ostra found him an hour after the district had started gathering, curled against a crate with
his boots still on, breathing slow and even and entirely undisturbed by the growing sound of a hundred
people waiting for a man who wasn't coming. Ostra didn't wake him. He'd counted the weeks without
counting them — six districts running one into the next with barely a night between, a pace no one had
asked Kanja to keep and no one had managed to talk him out of either.

"He's not hurt," Ostra told the district's own organizer, who'd come looking, plainly unsure whether
to be worried or offended. "He's just tired. The kind of tired that catches up whether you've got a
book of dead workers waiting on you or not."

"People came a long way for this morning."

"They can come a little further into the afternoon. Or I can wake him now and you can watch him try
to hold a straight column of figures with his hands shaking from three hours of sleep. Your choice,
but I know which one gets your district an honest tally."

Kanja woke past midday, and reached for the ledger before he'd fully opened his eyes, and Ostra had to
tell him twice that the morning was already gone before it landed.

"I've never slept through a scheduled morning. Not once, not in two years."

"You have now. Nobody died of it. The line's still there — longer, if anything, once word got round
you'd actually stopped."

He apologized to the district plainly when he finally sat down at the table, offering no excuse
beyond the true one — that he'd pushed the pace past what he could actually sustain, and it had caught
him the same way it would have caught anyone. Several testifiers told him afterward it was the first
time the method's face had looked, to them, like an ordinary exhausted man rather than something set
apart from the work it documented.

"You could've told me to slow us down," he said to Ostra that evening, once the tally had run its full
length anyway.

"I did tell you. Four districts ago. You didn't hear it that time either."

"I'm hearing it now."

"We'll see how long that lasts."
