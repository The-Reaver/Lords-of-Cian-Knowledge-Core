# The Frost That Didn't Wait for Spring

*Locked canon, Batch 262, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1311`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LXV, wave
22. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241), a highland forge terrace during
an early, unseasonable freeze. A detailed, battle-intense Trinity combat showcase in the alias's
first deep-cold environmental register. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose.*

---

The frost came three weeks before anyone's almanac said it should, hard enough that the terrace's
quench troughs skinned over by midnight and the apprentices' breath hung in front of them like
smoke off the forges themselves. It was the Directorate's own idea of an opportunity: a patrol
column that knew the cold would slow hands before it slowed anything armored, timed to hit while
half the terrace's water was already turning to something you couldn't dip a blade in.

Kanja felt the difference in his own grip before he saw the column crest the ridge — Mafesto's
Kinetic Transfer System routing heat as readily as force when he willed it, but the cold fought
back in a way fire never had to. "It's not resistance," he said to the senior smith's successor,
flexing a hand that answered half a second slower than it should have. "It's a different math
entirely. Heat wants to leave a body. I have to convince it to stay before I can spend it on
anything else."

He solved it the way he'd solved every other terrain the campaign had thrown at him — by turning
the cold's own logic against the men counting on it. Obsidian Malice's discharge cycle ran hotter
than strictly necessary through the first exchange, not for damage but for the steam it threw,
blinding a front rank that had planned their approach around clear sightlines across open, frozen
ground. Onyx of Oblivion's Whisper of Shadows moved easier over ice than boots ever could, closing
gaps a patrol trained for a straight charge hadn't accounted for, while Cadence Ruin read the
particular brittle rhythm frozen ground made under a footfall to place every strike before a
counter could form.

The apprentices did their own part without being told to — banking the forges higher rather than
letting them bank down defensively, so that by the time the column broke and ran there was a wall
of working heat behind Kanja's line instead of cold iron waiting to fail anyone's hands. One
apprentice, younger than the rest, kept a coal shovel moving the entire fight, feeding three
forges at once because it was the only thing his frozen fingers could still be trusted to do
well, and nobody thought less of him for it.

"You fought the weather as much as the column," the successor said afterward, breath still
fogging, watching the quench troughs steam where they'd frozen an hour before.

"I fought the weather first," Kanja said. "The column just showed up hoping I wouldn't have time
to finish that fight before theirs started."
