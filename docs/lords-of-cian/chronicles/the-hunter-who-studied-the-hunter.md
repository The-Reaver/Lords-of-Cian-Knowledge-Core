# The Hunter Who Studied the Hunter

*Locked canon, Batch 260, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1265`). Crow King Alias Chronicle LXXIII, wave 25,
opening it. A Directorate officer builds a false signal specifically to deceive Kanja himself, the
first entry where the craft's own logic is turned on its creator. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

He'd studied the reports for years, the same way Voris's second had, but he'd drawn a different
conclusion from them: if the Crow King's whole method rested on reading a pursuer's own confidence in
its procedures, then the only real countermeasure was to stop being a procedure at all, and become,
instead, a lie built specifically for the shape of the man reading it.

He planted the false signal himself, days in advance, threading it through three independent sources
so it would arrive at Kanja's own crew looking exactly like the kind of corroborated intelligence the
Braid itself was built to fabricate -- a supposed weakness in a garrison's own rotation, verified,
it seemed, from three directions at once.

Kanja almost took it. That was the part that shook the crew afterward more than anything else --
not that the trap existed, but that for the length of one conversation, weighing three sources that
agreed with each other in exactly the pattern he'd trained a lifetime to trust, he was ready to move
on it.

It was the fourth generation who stopped him, not through any instinct of his own but through the
same discipline he'd built the phantom garrison from: he cross-checked the three sources' numbers
against each other with the same exactness he checked his own fabrications, and found them too
perfectly consistent -- the kind of agreement real, independently-gathered intelligence almost never
produced, because real sources disagreed on small things even when they agreed on the large ones.

"You built something that would have fooled me," Kanja said afterward, more shaken by it than he let
most people see. "Not the Directorate. Me, specifically. Somebody finally understood that the craft's
weakness was never in the trick. It was in trusting my own read of a pattern that looked exactly like
the ones I'd trained myself to recognize."

"That's what the four of us are for," the fourth generation said. "You taught us not to trust our own
first read alone. I don't see why that should stop applying to you."

The garrison the false signal was meant to protect held, quietly, without anyone outside the crew
ever learning how close the trap had come to working exactly as designed.
