# Lauris Chronicle XLII: The Dozen Unaccounted

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1659`). Forty-second entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, the fourth entry of Strand L. Advances the Sample K-403 thread (`MCD-1534`): one of
roughly a dozen scattered Karesian biological fragments recovered from Korren Highlands archaeological
sites, of which Lauris delivered one to Directorate processing during Operation 4 without disclosing
her own recognition of it. Deliberately an archival/informational debt rather than a facility visit,
distinct in kind from Chronicles IV and VIII. Reuses already-locked Directorate historian Vael
Korr-Drennen (`MCD-1541`) as the research contact rather than inventing a new named figure. No new
named characters. Abad's blanket authorization: "lock it, continue uninterrupted, test and push to
main."*

-----

*Archive fragment, present-day: "One fragment traced. One record marked destroyed. Eleven, perhaps
ten, unaccounted for still. I do not know yet whether destroyed means gone or only means someone did
not want it found."*

Lauris does not often ask me to accompany her to an archive rather than a battlefield, and I confess I
find these occasions, when they come, more instructive than most of the ones that involve the Spine of
Dagon. This account concerns one of them: a week spent almost entirely indoors, in the company of old
paper and an older woman, pursuing a debt that has sat, by Lauris's own reckoning, unresolved since
Operation 4 of her Apprentice Contracts — decades before I was born, let alone before I began keeping
her record.

The debt is this. Sample K-403, recovered from smuggler Therik Voll's stores during that contract and
delivered, as her instructions specified, to the Directorate's Coastal Containment Office, was a
fragment of Karesian biological material she recognized on sight and never disclosed that she
recognized. She would determine, years later, through her own accumulated research, that it had been
pulled from a Korren Highlands archaeological site roughly seven years before the contract reached
her — and that it was one of approximately a dozen such fragments known, across scattered records, to
have surfaced from sites of the same kind over a span of decades, each one handled by some different
office, logged under some different classification, never once cross-referenced against the others by
an institution that had no idea what it was actually holding.

She has never accounted for where the other eleven went. This entry concerns her first genuine attempt
to change that.

*

The contact who made the attempt possible is Vael Korr-Drennen, a retired Directorate historian whose
own retrospective assessment of Lauris's Established Hunter period — the phrase "economic peak," coined
to describe how thoroughly the institution had come to rely on her by Operation 22 — Sephtis had
encountered independently, years ago, cross-referencing his own archive against surviving Directorate
scholarship. Lauris had never met the woman. I gather the introduction, arranged through channels
Sephtis holds rather than any Lauris controls directly, was cordial, careful on both sides, and
entirely professional — two people who had each spent a portion of their lives studying the same
subject from opposite ends of it, meeting for the first time to compare notes neither had expected to
need.

Vael Korr-Drennen had, as it turned out, retained partial access to certain decommissioned Directorate
transfer registries — not through any irregularity, Lauris was careful to establish, but through the
ordinary courtesy the institution extends its own retired historians when the material in question no
longer carries active classification. Between the two of them, working across four days with records
that resisted being read in any order but the one the original clerks had chosen, they traced a single
fragment — not K-403 itself, but a second sample logged under a different site designation the same
decade — through three documented transfers: an initial containment intake, a reclassification to a
research annex whose charter Vael Korr-Drennen herself remembered being quietly dissolved, and a final
entry that read, in full, *material disposed of per standing protocol, see attached.* The attachment
did not survive whatever purge had claimed the annex's other records.

*

I asked Lauris, on the ride back, whether *disposed of* settled the question for that particular
fragment, even if the other eleven remained open. She did not answer quickly, which I have learned, by
now, generally means the honest answer is less comfortable than a quick one would have been.

"It settles nothing," she said. "It only tells me that someone, at some point, wanted the record to
say the material was gone. Whether it actually was gone, or whether someone found it more convenient
to write that it was, I cannot determine from a single line in a register three decades stale." She
considered the point a moment longer. "I have learned not to trust an institution's own account of its
own losses. I have less reason to trust it here than almost anywhere else I have tested it."

Vael Korr-Drennen, for her part, offered to continue the search on her own time, working through what
remains of the decommissioned registries at a pace her own access allows — an offer Lauris accepted
plainly, without the reflexive independence I have seen her extend toward almost every other
collaborator she has ever worked with. I think this says something about how much the debt has come to
weigh on her: eleven, perhaps ten, fragments of a dead world's biology, scattered somewhere across an
institution that has never once understood what it was holding, and Lauris — who has cleared facilities
alone that killed entire Directorate teams — apparently willing, for this particular ledger entry, to
let someone else help carry the search.

Her own closing note: *Eleven remain, unless the twelfth line I have not yet found says otherwise. I
will not close this account on the strength of a register that has already lied to me once about what
it kept and what it threw away.*
