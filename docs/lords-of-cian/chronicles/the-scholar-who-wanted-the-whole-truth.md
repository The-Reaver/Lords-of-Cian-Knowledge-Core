# The Scholar Who Wanted the Whole Truth

*Locked canon, Batch 260, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1274`). Crow King Alias Chronicle LXXXII, wave 28,
opening it. A historian seeks out the crew to document the craft's true history against the
exaggerated folk legend. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

She'd been collecting versions of the story for six years by the time she finally reached anyone who
had actually been there -- a crow the size of a horse, in one telling; a crown that let its wearer
hear every conversation within a day's walk, in another; a dead man's ghost wearing Kanja's shape, in
a third, told by someone two provinces away who swore an uncle had seen it happen. She wasn't
interested in correcting any of them for the sake of correction. She wanted, she said, to understand
how a true thing turns into six different false ones without anybody meaning to lie.

Corren Halst gave her the most time of anyone she found. He told her about the original marsh trick
without embellishment -- the real numbers, the real fear, the scarecrow that had been a plain,
practical decoy and nothing more than that before legend got hold of it.

"Why does the crown keep appearing in every version," she asked, "when nobody who was actually there
mentions one?"

"Because a king needs a crown," Corren Halst said. "The story needed him to be something bigger than
a scared young man inventing a trick on the spot to save three hundred lives, so people gave him a
crown he never wore, because the truth was smaller and stranger than the shape people wanted the
story to have."

She wrote all of it down -- the marsh, the mountain, the salt flat, the failures alongside the
successes, the four generations carrying it forward in shapes none of them had planned -- alongside
her own honest accounting of exactly how and why each detail had drifted in the retelling. It became,
over the following years, the closest thing to a true record the story would ever have outside the
crew's own memory of it.

"You could have just let the legend stand," Corren Halst told her, near the end. "Most people
prefer it."

"Legends outlive the people who lived them either way," she said. "I'd rather the truth had a chance
to outlive them too."
