# Taifa Chronicle I: Bound Without a Word

*Locked canon, Batch 89, 2026-09-09 (`MCD-352`). First entry in Taifa's own Chronicles, its
protagonist Osei (`PH2-053`), not a Kanja Chronicle. Kanja appears only as a guest, present but
granted no command, no intervention, and no resolution credit, matching the established convention
from Xaragua Chronicle I (`MCD-334`) through Kazi Chronicle I (`MCD-351`). Narration is close-third
on Osei throughout, not Onyx of Oblivion. Continues Detroit's own run of territory Chronicles. Slots
into no existing mainline battle — original homage-era material set in Taifa itself.*

---

Osei had sworn a hundred men and women into the oath by the time the test came, scattered across
three cities and one stretch of claimed land none of them could reach inside a week's hard travel,
and he had learned, by then, not to ask how the coordination actually happened. It simply happened.
A man in one city moved the exact hour a woman in another needed him to, with no message carried
between them, no signal either could have sent in time even if the roads had been faster than they
were.

The test, when it came, wasn't one Osei chose. A raid on the claimed land's outer post arrived at
dawn, faster than any rider could have reached the other sworn cells to warn them, faster than
anything but the oath itself could travel. Osei felt it the way a man feels weather turning before
the first drop falls — not information exactly, closer to a certainty arriving whole, that the
outer post needed every sworn hand it could gather, now, from wherever those hands happened to be
standing.

They came. Not summoned — nothing summoned them, not in any way a courier or a signal fire could
have managed in the time available — simply present, converging on the outer post from three
different directions at once, arriving within minutes of each other despite starting from towns that
shared no road and no warning between them.

The stranger had sworn nothing, and stood at the post's edge that morning the way he stood at most
things, present without claiming a stake in what unfolded. He watched the sworn arrive from three
directions and asked, once the raid had broken against numbers it hadn't planned for, the only
question that seemed worth asking.

"How did they know to come."

"They didn't know. They felt it, the same way I felt the post needed them before I'd sent word to a
single soul." Osei was still catching his breath, the fight only just finished. "It isn't magic, and
it isn't some trick of timing I can take credit for planning. Every one of them swore the same oath
I did, meaning what they meant when they said it. That's not nothing. Once a person swears something
like that and means it all the way through, the distance between them and the place the oath needs
them stops mattering the way distance usually matters."

"Everyone who swore came. What about the ones who didn't."

Osei's face did something complicated at that, something the stranger would not fully understand
until considerably later, from a source other than Osei himself. "My own brother Yaw swore it
alongside me, first day, no hesitation. Most who stand near this cause long enough eventually do.
But I've watched men stand right beside the oath for years, agreeing with every word of it, admiring
it, defending it in every room where it needed defending — and never once actually swear it
themselves. Those men feel nothing when the post needs them. Not because the gift doesn't reach that
far. Because they were never actually inside it to begin with. Agreement isn't the oath. Only the
oath is the oath."

"That seems like a narrow door for something this powerful."

"It's supposed to be narrow. If it worked on anyone who merely liked the idea, it wouldn't be worth
a thing — a hundred men who like an idea will scatter the first time the idea costs them something.
A hundred men who swore it and meant it stood a claimed post together this morning without a single
message passed between them." Osei looked out at the post, already being rebuilt by the same sworn
hands that had just defended it. "I'd rather have that hundred than ten thousand who only ever
agreed."

The stranger was gone before the post's repairs were finished, the way he always seemed to be. He
left nothing carved, no phrase repeated hand to hand — only, the following week, word reaching Osei
from a fourth city, one he had no sworn cell in at all, that a stranger matching no description
anyone could quite agree on had been seen helping clear rubble from a different claimed outpost
entirely, gone again before anyone thought to ask why.

Kanja is the unnamed guest, present at the claimed outer post before and during the raid, watching
rather than fighting, matching the Umoja/Yara/Areíto pure-witness end of the established range. The
scene puts Osei's signature ability ('Kin at a Distance,' `PH2-053`) directly on the page for the
first time: everyone who has sworn his oath acts in coordinated concert even scattered and entirely
out of contact, exactly matching the ability's stated mechanic. The ability's cost is dramatized
explicitly rather than left abstract: Osei states plainly that mere agreement or admiration for the
cause does nothing -- only those who have actually sworn the oath and meant it are reached, matching
PH2-053's stated limitation that the ability "cannot compel anyone who hasn't sworn it." Yaw
(PH2-053) is referenced consistently with his already-locked role as co-founder and sworn brother,
not separately detailed here. The RNA's real exiled first president remains backstory-only per
PH2-053's own precedent and is not referenced on-page. No new named characters are introduced; no
new proper nouns requiring a collision check. This Chronicle slots into no existing MCD- rule --
original homage-era material set in Taifa itself, continuing Detroit's own run of territory
Chronicles (Kazi already has one; Hekalu, Nyansa, and Kiti still do not).*
