# The Silence They Built to Blind Him

*Locked canon, Batch 215, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-933`). Bane Alias Chronicle XLIX, wave 17. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The intelligence had cost three informants and six weeks to confirm, and Maret Vos read it aloud
twice before anyone believed it: a Directorate engineering corps had spent a season lining an
entire depot's inner walls with quarried wool-felt and packed ash, layer on layer, floor to
ceiling, in a chamber built for exactly one purpose. Someone in the Directorate's own analysis
office had finally asked the right question about Bane — not what he could see, but what he
listened for — and had built a room that gave a man nothing to hear at all.

"They're not wrong," Bane said, turning the schematic in his hands. "Whatever's carrying this for
me doesn't need light. It needs something moving. Air, sound, weight settling somewhere. Take all
of that away and I'm exactly as blind in there as anyone else."

Halst wanted to burn the depot from outside and be done with arguing about it, and for two days
that was the plan, until word came that the depot held forty prisoners the Directorate meant to
move out through it rather than keep in it — moving them through the one structure built
specifically to strip Bane of the advantage that had freed every prisoner before them.

He went in anyway, and for the first eleven steps past the felted door it worked exactly as
designed. Onyx of Oblivion answered nothing — no rhythm to unmake, no bond to trace, no voice
beneath a lie, only a padded stillness so total that Bane's own breathing sounded wrong to him,
too loud in a room built to swallow everything else. He stopped where he stood and made himself
do what the room hadn't accounted for: not listen with whatever the blade lent him, but track the
plain, unglamorous way any tracker learns first — the give of the floor felt through his boots,
telling him where the ash-pack had been walked thin by regular traffic; the faint draft of
warmer air on the left side of his face, telling him where a door had opened recently enough to
still be losing its heat.

Two guards found him twelve steps later, exactly where the draft said a guard post would be, well
before either of them expected anyone to have found them at all.

The forty prisoners came out within the hour. Bane didn't pretend afterward that the room had
failed — it had done precisely what it was built to do, and for eleven steps it had cost him
everything he usually relied on.

"They built the one thing that actually worked against you," Vos said later, still turning the
schematic over as if it might explain itself further.

"They built the one thing that worked against the part of me they'd noticed," Bane said. "They
didn't know there was a part under that they hadn't."
