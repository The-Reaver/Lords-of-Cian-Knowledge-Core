# The Roof Before the Ledger

*Locked canon, Batch 257, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1188`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle LXXVII, wave 26. A proactive civilian-housing entry -- days of unglamorous resettlement construction for displaced families, with no crisis, no combat, and no rescue framing.*

---

Nobody was trapped. Nothing was collapsing. Four hundred families burned or shelled out of the districts above the siege lines needed somewhere dry to sleep before the coming season turned, and that need didn't announce itself with the urgency of a cave-in or the drama of an ambush -- it simply sat there, unglamorous and enormous, until someone did the work.

Kanja spent nine days on it without a single moment that looked like what people expected from him. No Trinity showcase. No rescue against a clock. Just foundation lines laid out with a cord and a level, timber measured and cut to spec, walls raised one course at a time alongside crews who'd never fought in the siege at all and had no particular reason to know who he was beyond another set of hands that showed up on time.

"You could be doing something that matters more," one of the carpenters said on the third day, meaning it as a compliment rather than a slight.

"This matters exactly as much as anything else I've done down here," Kanja said, setting a joist level and checking it twice before moving to the next. "A roof that doesn't leak this winter is worth as much as a rescue. It just doesn't look like one while it's happening."

There was a particular satisfaction in it he hadn't expected -- work with no enemy to outthink and no clock counting down, just the plain, cumulative arithmetic of enough hands doing enough honest labor that by the ninth day four hundred families had somewhere real to sleep instead of a tarp stretched over rubble. Garren Hask, tracking the material costs against the resistance's strained reserves, found the tally almost embarrassingly modest next to what a single rescue operation routinely cost in gear repair alone.

"Cheapest thing you've ever done that mattered this much," Hask said, looking over the final numbers.

"It's not cheap," Kanja said, looking out over a street of new roofs holding steady under the season's first real rain. "It's just quiet. Those aren't the same thing."
