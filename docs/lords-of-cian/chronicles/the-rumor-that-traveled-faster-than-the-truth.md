# The Rumor That Traveled Faster Than the Truth

*Locked canon, Batch 194, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-624`). The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XIX, wave 7 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The story reached the crew before any of them had heard it start: that the Trench Monarch had taken
a quiet payment from a site owner to look the other way on a debased shipment, that the whole
reputation for honest tallies was a performance covering a boy who could be bought like anyone else.

"It's not true," Danne Sok said, the first time it reached him, already reaching for the nearest
weapon out of pure reflex.

"I know it's not true. That's not the problem." Kanja kept him from moving. "The problem is that a
lie like this doesn't get disproven by anger. It gets disproven by numbers, the same as everything
else does, and I'd rather spend a week proving it wrong than an afternoon making it look like I had
something to hide by reacting to it with a fist."

He called for the full accounting instead — every site's tally for the month in question, laid out
publicly, cross-referenced against the independent clerks who'd verified the Silt Row settlement
weeks before, alongside a plain, unhurried account of exactly where the rumor's specific claim
diverged from anything that had actually happened.

"Whoever started this picked a shipment real enough to sound believable," Kanja said, addressing the
assembled crowd at the dredge site where the rumor had first taken root. "That's clever. It's also
checkable. Here's the shipment's real manifest, here's who signed for it, here's the tally from that
week showing exactly what left the district and exactly what it was worth. If I'd taken a payment to
look away from something, there'd be a gap somewhere in these numbers. There isn't one. You don't
have to trust my word on that. You can check it yourselves, the same as anyone's checked every
number I've ever given you."

It took four days for the rumor to lose momentum, not because anyone announced a victory over it,
but because the numbers simply held up under every attempt to find the gap the story required, and
a story that can't survive its own details being checked eventually stops being repeated.

"Whoever started it wanted you angry," Sok said afterward, still turning the thought over. "Wanted
you to answer it the way I almost did."

"I know. That's the part that worries me more than the rumor itself. If a lie can make me look
guilty just by making me look furious, then the reputation was never as solid as I thought it was —
it was solid as long as nobody found the right way to provoke me. I'd rather it be solid because the
numbers are true, no matter what I feel about someone trying to say they aren't."
