# Daba Chronicle XLV: The Watch That Has No End Yet

*Locked canon, Batch 296, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1615`). Entry 45 of 50 in Daba's own Chronicle series,
closing this block. Set centuries into what the Directorate would come to term Kanja's Long Mask, an
older Daba reflects on the network he has spent his life building against a threat `MCD-1569` says
he "cannot yet name" -- deliberately left unresolved, reserved for Book 1 per that rule's own
framing. New minor named character (Quill Vantry), collision-checked clean. Abad's approval: "rate

The girl who brought him his reports now had not been born when Halvar Ilven laid down the ledger,
and neither had her mother. Quill Vantry set the night's dispatches on the table the way every
courier before her had set dispatches on this same table, in this same room, in a settlement that
had changed its name four times across the centuries precisely so that no single record could ever
place it twice in the same location, and Daba read them the way he had read every report for longer
than any single ledger in the network could account for.

None of it was urgent. That was, increasingly, the shape of most nights now -- not because the world
had grown safer, but because 1804 had grown so thoroughly dispersed, so deeply cross-trained into
itself, that urgency rarely climbed all the way up to where he sat. A supply route rerouted around a
Trust patrol that had wandered somewhere it shouldn't have. A cell in the eastern reaches reporting a
clean season, nothing to flag. A rumor, three hands removed and probably worth nothing, of an old
Directorate file finally being quietly closed rather than reopened.

"You've read that last one four times," Quill said. She was young enough still to say things like
that to him directly, the way Perrin once had, the way every generation of courier eventually
learned they were permitted to.

"I'm not reading it for what it says." Daba set it down. His own hands, unlike Halvar's, had never
started shaking -- a small mercy of whatever it was in him that had let him outlast three generations
of the network he'd founded, unasked for and never fully understood, the same quiet stubbornness of
body that let some people simply keep going long after the world expected them to stop. "I'm reading
it for what it doesn't say. The Directorate has closed files before. They rarely close them because
the thing they were watching for stopped being real."

"You still think it's coming. Whatever it is."

"I've thought that since before you were born, and I thought it before your mother was born, and I
will most likely go on thinking it after both of you have handed this room to someone else." He said
it without weariness, which surprised even him a little, all these years in. "I built this network
wide enough and quiet enough to survive something I have never once been able to name. Not the Trust.
Not the Directorate, not really, though they've cost us plenty along the way. Something underneath
all of it, that none of our reports have ever quite managed to point at directly."

Quill was quiet for a while, the particular quiet of someone deciding whether a question was hers to
ask. "Does it frighten you? Not knowing what you're keeping all of this ready for?"

"It used to." Daba looked at the dispatches again, at four thousand small unremarkable facts that
added up, across the whole width of the network, to something considerably larger than any one of
them. "Now it mostly just keeps me honest. A man who knows exactly what he's preparing for stops
checking his work. I have never had that luxury, and I've come to think it's made every cell in this
network sharper for it, generation over generation, rather than weaker."

Somewhere beyond the reach of anything he could see from this room, he knew without needing a report
to confirm it, the man he had once taught to read a ravine's own weight against itself was still out
there under some name or another, still carrying forward whatever the two of them had first built
together on a hillside neither of them spoke of anymore. Their paths had not crossed in longer than
Quill had been alive. Daba had made his peace with that a lifetime ago -- had understood, the same
night he'd burned a dispatch that named no one, that the surest way to keep two things safe was to
let them grow entirely apart from each other, roots reaching toward the same unnamed weight from
opposite ends of the same dark.

He gathered the night's reports, filed the closed Directorate case where it belonged -- noted, not
trusted -- and told Quill to get what sleep she could before the next courier relay needed her. The
watch did not end tonight. It had not ended in longer than anyone currently serving in 1804 could
remember, and Daba, older now than any founder of the network had any right to expect to be, found he
no longer minded standing it. Whatever was coming would come on its own schedule, not his. Until
then, the work was simply to remain -- small, dispersed, unclaimed by any name louder than a number
-- ready.
