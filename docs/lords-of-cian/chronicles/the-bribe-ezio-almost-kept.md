# The Bribe Ezio Almost Kept

*Locked canon, Batch 278, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1446`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XCVIII, wave 33. The crew's internal-transparency discipline (MCD-1152) tested from Ezio's own side for the first time, after Kanja's earlier refusal of a personal bribe (MCD-1152).*

---

The offer came to Ezio directly, not to Kanja — a mill owner's steward, alone with him over the ledgers late one evening, sliding a folded note across the table with a sum on it large enough to cover two years of an apprentice auditor's keep, in exchange for one page of a case left un-cross-referenced.

"No one would ever know which figure you didn't check," the steward said. "One page. The rest stands as it is."

Ezio didn't take the note, and didn't refuse it outright either. He sat with it folded on the table for the length of a full breath longer than he'd meant to, doing the same arithmetic in his head he did with every other figure that crossed his desk — what the money could buy, set against what one unchecked page might quietly cost some worker down the ledger's own line. It was the pause, more than the offer, that unsettled him afterward.

He told Kanja that same night, before he'd decided anything, the note still folded in his coat.

"I didn't take it," Ezio said. "But I want you to know I thought about it longer than I should have."

"How long is longer than you should have?"

"Long enough that I noticed myself thinking about it instead of just refusing."

Kanja didn't treat it as a confession that needed absolving. "You told me before you decided. That's the part that matters more than how long you sat with it. A man who's never tempted by anything isn't proving he's honest — he's just never been tested. You were tested, and you still brought it to me instead of the page."

"Doesn't feel like something to be proud of."

"It's not pride I'm after. It's whether the next offer finds the same man who told me about this one." Kanja set the note between them, unopened past its first fold. "Take it back to him unclaimed. Not to prove anything to him. So you know exactly what it feels like to have already decided, the next time one of these lands on your table when I'm not in the room to hear about it."

Ezio returned the note the following morning, and the case's every page was cross-referenced in full, the one the steward had hoped to buy included. What lodged with Ezio longest wasn't the refusal. It was the honest half-second of wanting to keep it, and Kanja's quiet insistence that the half-second didn't need to be hidden to be forgiven.
