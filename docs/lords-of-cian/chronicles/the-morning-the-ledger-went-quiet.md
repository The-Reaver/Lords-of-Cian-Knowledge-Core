# The Morning the Ledger Went Quiet

*Locked canon, Batch 275, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1422`). Captain Alias Chronicle XCII, wave 31. Garren Hask dies of old age at his own ledger table, a quiet, deliberately unresolved-into-comfort payoff to the mortality thread running since MCD-920.*

---

He was found sitting upright, which was the detail everyone kept returning to afterward, as if it mattered — and it did, a little. The ledger's newest keeper, the same hand Kanja had once taught letter by letter over Hask's own old figures (`MCD-1001`), had come in at first light to start the day's accounts and found him already there, quill fallen from loose fingers, the last entry finished and correctly totaled, the line beneath it left blank the way it always was until the next day's business gave it something to hold.

There was nothing for Sera to do when she came, and she said so plainly, the way her training had taught her to say the hardest true things without softening them past use. His heart, the same one she'd stabilized years before in her own hall (`MCD-1089`), had simply finished the work it had been doing on borrowed time ever since. Old age, in the end, rather than any single failure worth naming.

Kanja arrived within the hour. He didn't sit. He stood at the table for a long while, looking at the last entry in a ledger he'd once watched this same man refuse to hand over to anyone he didn't trust completely, and understood — not for the first time, but for the first time with nothing left to prepare for — that the promise he'd made a lifetime of years ago, to become the one who remembered and carried the tradition forward once the founding generation was gone (`MCD-920`), had just stopped being something owed to the future and started being something owed right now.

Efa Gol came and stood beside him without a word for a long time before she finally spoke. "He'd tell you not to make this bigger than it is," she said. "He'd want the ledger open again by tomorrow, same as any other day."

"I know," Kanja said. "That's exactly what I'm afraid of doing right."

The ledger's keeper, still holding the fallen quill, asked the only question that mattered to the day's own business. "Do I close out yesterday's line, or does someone else?"

"You do," Kanja said. "That was always going to be you."
