# The Morning the Ground Lied to Everyone

*Locked canon, Batch 261, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1295`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LXXVI, wave 26, first entry. The doctrine's first citywide earthquake-triage application. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

No enemy had built anything to defeat this time. The ground itself had simply moved, and a full quarter of the port district stood in some state of compromise by the time Kanja reached it — leaning walls, cracked foundations, a market hall whose roof hadn't yet decided which way it meant to fall. Nothing here had been engineered against him. The earthquake didn't care what he could hear.

The problem wasn't discharge. It was arithmetic.

Forty-one structures needed reading before anyone could be let back inside a single one of them, and every hour spent verifying one carefully was an hour not spent on the other forty, while the district's own residents pressed at the cordon line wanting only to know whether their homes were still theirs to live in. Doubled verification, the standing protocol since the tower at MCD-497, had never been built for triage at this scale — it assumed time enough to be certain before committing to an answer, and today there wasn't time enough for that on every single structure without abandoning some of them to guesswork.

He made the call he'd never had to make before: a single fast pass on every structure first, sorting the district into three piles by ear alone — clearly stable, clearly failing, and genuinely uncertain — before spending the doctrine's real, doubled care only on the uncertain third. Twenty-six buildings cleared within the hour on a single confident read each. Nine condemned outright, their tension screaming wrong even at a sprinter's pace past them. Six left for the afternoon's slower, careful work, the only six that actually earned the full protocol's time.

"You're not doing this the way you usually do it," the second student said, matching his pace between buildings.

"I can't. Not today. The protocol I built assumes the danger stays where I found it while I take the time to be sure. Today the danger is time itself — every family standing at that cordon waiting to hear whether their home is safe is a cost too, even if it's not the kind of cost the doctrine was built to weigh." Kanja moved to the next wall, palm flat against cracked stone for barely two seconds before moving on. "I'm not abandoning the standard. I'm choosing where it actually needs spending today, instead of spending it evenly across every structure whether it needs it or not."

By dusk, the district had its answer — twenty-six homes reopened before nightfall, nine confirmed lost, six still under careful watch. No misdiagnosis in the fast pile. The six slow reads, when they finally came, confirmed every one of the fast pile's calls had held true.

"It worked," the second student said, watching the returning families move back through the cordon line.

"It worked today," Kanja said. "I don't know yet whether it works every time triage forces this choice on me. I know the ground doesn't care how careful my usual method is, and today I had to decide that careful and fast aren't always the same virtue wearing different clothes."
