# The Duel Fought With Borrowed Steel

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XXXVIII, wave 13.
Rebellion era, age 18, before the Black Trench -- solo-blade entry, Mafesto dormant, Obsidian Malice
undeployed. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters.*

---

The ambush at the depot's outer fence line happened faster than the retrieval party had planned for
— three men, close range, in the narrow space between two supply sheds where a full swing of any
blade risked striking the crates stacked on either side.

Onyx caught the first strike cleanly. It never got the chance to answer the second. A grasping hand,
not a blade, closed around the hilt mid-parry, and in the scramble that followed the sword went into
the mud between two combatants, neither able to reach it without exposing themselves to the third
attacker still circling free.

Kanja left it there. There wasn't time to retrieve it, and a stevedore's iron pry-bar, abandoned
against the nearest supply shed, was close enough to reach in the same half-second Onyx became
unreachable.

It was nothing like fighting with the sword. The pry-bar had no gift for reading rhythm, no edge fine
enough to find a seam in anything, no bond that would turn a killing blow aside by fractions of an
inch at the last possible instant. It was simply iron, heavy and blunt, and everything Kanja did with
it in the next ninety seconds came from three years of dredge-line work and nothing else — the same
ordinary strength that had won the fight against the net-caster at Undertide, applied now to
something considerably less forgiving than a fisherman's cast.

He took a glancing blow to the ribs that Onyx would have read and turned aside before it landed. He
answered it anyway, driving the pry-bar's flat edge into the second attacker's knee with a force that
had nothing elegant in it, and closed the fight with the third man through sheer refusal to be
outlasted rather than any technique a training manual would have recognized.

Onyx was still lying in the mud when the last attacker went down. Kanja retrieved it afterward,
cleaning it with the same care he always did, and stood for a moment in the narrow space between the
two sheds considering what had just happened.

"You fought that whole thing without the sword," Corren Halst said, arriving late enough to have
missed it and furious about the timing. "I didn't know you could."

"Neither did I, entirely, until I had to." Kanja sheathed Onyx, the blade clean again, no different
than it had been an hour before. "I think I'd started trusting it more than I trusted myself,
somewhere in the last few months. Today reminded me those aren't supposed to be the same trust. The
sword's a gift. It's not the reason I can fight. I'd rather know that for certain than find out the
hard way, someday, when there isn't a pry-bar close enough to reach."
