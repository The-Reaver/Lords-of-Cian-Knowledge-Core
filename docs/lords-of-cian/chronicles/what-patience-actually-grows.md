# What Patience Actually Grows

*Locked canon, Batch 144, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-469`). Atunbi Chronicle II. Homage-era Los Angeles,
Atunbi/Oluwole. Territory Chronicle -- Oluwole as protagonist, Kanja an unnamed background guest
with no command, credit, or resolution authorship. New standalone material. No new named characters.*

---

Where the dawn demolition strike had come fast and cost the garden a third of its ground before "Don't
Move, Improve" could do anything about it, the reclassification effort that followed years later came
the opposite way entirely — slow, bureaucratic, spread across seasons of zoning hearings and
incremental paperwork, exactly the kind of sustained pressure the fast strike had proven the ability
couldn't answer.

This time, the pace favored him completely.

Every hearing the reclassification effort scheduled, Oluwole answered not with argument alone but with
another season's visible, undeniable improvement to the ground in question — new plots cultivated,
water systems refined, yield documented and shared with the same board reviewing the land's
classification. Where the demolition crew had needed him to react in minutes he didn't have, the
reclassification board needed him to simply keep doing, over years, what he already did every day,
and every season handed him more evidence than the last.

"They're trying to prove this land is underused, abandoned, ready for something else," a fellow
gardener said, watching the third consecutive hearing conclude without a ruling in the developers'
favor. "And every season you just... keep making that argument harder to sustain."

"That's the whole shape of what I do. I don't have an answer for a threat that moves faster than
growing season allows — I proved that the hard way once already. But nothing about reclassification
paperwork moves faster than a growing season. It moves considerably slower, if anything." Oluwole
checked the latest yield figures against the prior year's, the trend line unmistakable. "Give this
gift patience instead of speed, and it doesn't just survive the fight. It wins it outright."

The reclassification effort withdrew in its fourth year, unable to sustain a claim of abandonment
against four consecutive years of expanding, documented cultivation — a victory as complete and
unambiguous as the dawn strike's outcome had been costly and partial.

"I used to think the gift's limit meant it was weak against anything determined enough," the fellow
gardener said afterward. "I don't think that anymore. I think it just needed the fight to happen on
its own terms instead of someone else's."

"Most gifts do, I'd imagine." Oluwole returned to the day's ordinary work, unhurried, already thinking
about next season. "Mine's just more honest than most about which terms those are."

An unnamed man who had watched several of the later hearings from the gallery, saying nothing
throughout, left the final one without being noticed.
