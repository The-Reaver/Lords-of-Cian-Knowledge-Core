# Aztlán Chronicle I: One Body, Many Hands

*Locked canon, Batch 84, 2026-09-09 (`MCD-347`). First entry in Aztlán's own Chronicles, its
protagonist Ollin (`PH2-023`), not a Kanja Chronicle. Kanja appears only as a guest, present but
granted no command, no intervention, and no resolution credit, matching the established convention
from Xaragua Chronicle I (`MCD-334`) through Sankofa Chronicle I (`MCD-346`). Narration is
close-third on Ollin throughout, not Onyx of Oblivion. Continues Los Angeles's own run of territory
Chronicles. Slots into no existing mainline battle — original homage-era material set in Aztlán
itself.*

---

Ollin had drilled the same forty people for six months before he ever asked them to hold a line for
real, and even then, on the morning it mattered, he still half expected the discipline to dissolve
the moment fear got a real vote in the matter. It didn't. That was the whole of what he'd built,
proven in the space of one bad hour: forty separate fears, standing shoulder to shoulder long
enough, stopped behaving like forty fears and started behaving like one thing that didn't have
room left over to be afraid.

The counter-crowd came at the walkout's flank exactly where the police line thinned, the way it
always did when a march grew larger than anyone official had planned for. Ollin's people didn't
break formation to meet it. They held, exactly as drilled, and the strange thing — the thing Ollin
would spend the rest of his life trying to explain to people who hadn't seen it — was how the line
moved. Not forty men making forty separate decisions half a second apart. One decision, arrived at
all at once, the way a single hand closes.

The stranger was inside the formation when it happened, having fallen in with the line an hour
earlier without asking anyone's leave, matching its rhythm the way water matches whatever shape it's
poured into. He held his ground when the line held. He stepped when the line stepped. Ollin noticed
him only the way he noticed anyone new inside a formation built entirely on trust already earned —
a small, precise attention, filed and set aside for later, because the counter-crowd wasn't giving
him room for anything else right now.

It lasted four minutes. It felt, to everyone standing in it, considerably longer, and considerably
shorter, both at once — the specific distortion of time that comes from a body doing exactly what
forty months of drilling had built it to do without asking permission from the mind riding along
inside it. The counter-crowd broke first. It always did, against a line that didn't waver in the
one place a line was supposed to.

"That's the part nobody believes when I describe it," Ollin said afterward, still catching his
breath, addressing the stranger without quite deciding to. "That it isn't willpower. Willpower runs
out. This doesn't, not for as long as the line holds, because it isn't forty separate wills anymore.
It's one."

"Looked that way from inside it too." The stranger didn't offer more than that, the way he rarely
did. "How long did it take to build."

"Six months of drilling nobody outside this line ever saw, so that four minutes could happen exactly
once, exactly the way it needed to." Ollin looked down the line at his own people, still catching
their own breath, still standing closer together than the moment strictly required, the way people
do when something has just been proven true in their bodies that words hadn't managed to prove
first. "I built this because nothing else I tried actually worked. Marches alone don't hold a line.
Speeches don't hold a line. This holds a line, because by the time it matters, it isn't a choice
anymore for anyone standing in it."

"That sounds like a gift with no edges."

Ollin didn't answer that right away. Something crossed his face that had no name he was willing to
put to it yet — would not, in fact, find the words for it until considerably later, on a night this
Chronicle does not cover. "Every gift has edges. I haven't found this one's yet. I intend to, eventually,
whether I go looking for it or not." He turned back toward the dispersing crowd, already thinking
past the morning toward whatever came next. "For now it's the only thing standing between people I
drilled myself and whatever comes at them next. That's enough to be worth the six months it cost to
build."

The stranger was gone before the formation broke for the day, the way he always seemed to be. He
left nothing carved, no phrase repeated hand to hand — only, the following week, one more body in
the line during drill, falling into the rhythm without being taught it, gone again before anyone
thought to ask his name.

*Continuity notes (not narrative): Ollin is the protagonist and viewpoint character throughout;
Kanja is the unnamed guest, embedded inside the drilled formation itself during the confrontation
and again during a later drill session, without taking command, credit, or narrative authorship of
the outcome, closer to Xaragua Chronicle I's and Borikén Chronicle I's more physically-involved end
of the established range. The scene puts Ollin's signature ability (`PH2-023`, "The Formation")
directly on the page for the first time: people he has personally organized and drilled, holding a
defensive line together, fight and move as a single, dramatically amplified force, exactly matching
the ability's stated mechanic. The ability's cost (fracturing explosively once real, ignored
internal grievance reaches its breaking point) is deliberately not shown or restaged here -- this
Chronicle is set before that already-locked backstory event (Iya's 1970 walkout over unaddressed
sexism inside the organization), and Ollin's own closing line ("I haven't found this one's edges
yet... whether I go looking for it or not") is a deliberate, unresolved forward reference to that
future rupture, matching the hook pattern already established in Areíto Chronicle I and elsewhere,
not a payoff. No new named characters are introduced; no new proper nouns requiring a collision
check. This Chronicle slots into no existing MCD- rule -- original homage-era material set in
Aztlán itself, continuing Los Angeles's own run of territory Chronicles (Sankofa already has one;
Atunbi, Ijoko, and Orin still do not).*
