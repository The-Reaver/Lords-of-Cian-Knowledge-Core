# The Man Who Wouldn't Claim His Own Debt

*Locked canon, Batch 278, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1442`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XCIV, wave 32, first entry. The worst-off-first discipline (MCD-371) run for the first time against a claimant's own pride rather than an administrator's staging.*

---

Renner Kall had fitted looms in the district for thirty-one years, longer than any other hand on the floor, and when his turn came to sit across from Ezio's ledger he gave a figure for his unpaid overtime that was, by Ezio's own later reckoning, less than half of what the attendance logs actually showed.

"Sixty days," Renner said. "Give or take. Not worth writing down twice over when there's boys here owed three times that and no thirty years of savings to fall back on."

Ezio didn't argue with him there at the table. He thanked him, marked the figure as given, and came back that evening with the yard's own attendance board and the materials-draw records that tracked exactly which loom had been running under whose hands on which night. The two accounts didn't match. Renner's true figure was closer to a hundred and forty days.

"You undercounted yourself," Ezio said, laying the two pages side by side. "Not by a little."

"I know what I said."

Kanja was the one who sat down across from him a second time, not to press the number but to ask why. Renner's answer, when it came, wasn't about the boys with less saved. It was that thirty-one years of being the man other hands measured themselves against didn't leave much room for being the one who needed the most looking-after — that writing the real number down felt like admitting the floor had finally worn him down the same as anyone else.

"It hasn't worn you down," Kanja said. "It's owed you a hundred and forty days regardless of what you can carry without complaining about it. The ledger isn't asking whether you can bear the debt. It's asking whether the debt is true."

Renner sat with that a long moment, looking at the two mismatched columns as if they belonged to two different men. "Write the true one, then. Not for me. So the boy at the next loom doesn't learn from watching me that the way to be respected here is to ask for less than what he's owed."

Ezio corrected the entry that night — the ledger's true figure, not the modest one — and the correction ran the opposite direction every prior deflated claim in the crew's experience had run: not fraud inflating a number upward, but pride quietly shrinking one down, and the same discipline catching it either way.

"The numbers don't flinch," Ezio said afterward, "whichever direction a man's pride pushes them."

"No," Kanja said. "They just have to be given the chance to hold still long enough for someone to read them honestly."
