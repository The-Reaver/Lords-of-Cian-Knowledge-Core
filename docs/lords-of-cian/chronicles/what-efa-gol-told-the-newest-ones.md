# What Efa Gol Told the Newest Ones

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Captain Alias Chronicle, wave 15. Long Mask era,
decades after the naval campaigns. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose. Features already-locked crew member Efa Gol, elderly; no new named characters (the new
recruits are deliberately left unnamed, distinct from the earlier "new hand's first night" entry).*

---

Efa Gol was one of the last of the original Warehouse Twelve generation still active aboard, and by
long-standing habit now, every fresh intake of recruits spent their first week under her particular
watch before being folded into the wider crew — not for training in any formal sense, but for
something she considered just as necessary.

"You'll hear a hundred stories about him before you've been here a month," she told the newest
group, six of them, barely a week aboard. "Some true, some grown taller in the telling the way
stories do. I'm not here to correct the stories. I'm here to tell you the one thing that actually
matters underneath all of them."

"What's that?" one of the recruits asked.

"That every single thing you've heard — the thirty feet he wouldn't give up, the fever he sat
through, the hostage trade, the order he reversed the second a junior hand proved him wrong — none
of it was ever about being impressive. I watched it happen, most of it, since the very first weeks.
It was never a performance. It was just what he does, whether or not anyone's writing it down
afterward."

"Is that why everyone calls him that instead of whatever the Directorate calls him?"

"That's exactly why. Their names are about what he does to enemies. Ours is about what he does for
us, every single day whether it's dramatic or not, whether anyone's watching or it's just an
ordinary afternoon hauling crates alongside Hask. You'll earn your own understanding of it in time.
I'm not asking you to take my word for it forever. I'm asking you to watch closely enough, the way I
did, to see it for yourselves."

Years later, long after Efa Gol herself had finally, gently, stepped back from active duty, more
than one of that intake would tell the same story to a newer generation still, in almost the same
words, without ever having been asked to keep the tradition going. It simply kept going, the way the
truest things on that crew always had — not commanded, not performed, just passed on because it was
worth passing on.
