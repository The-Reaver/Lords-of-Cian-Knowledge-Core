# The Flood That Came From Below

*Locked canon, Batch 131, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-440`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle VII, first
entry in the third wave. Rebellion era, age 20, the Sewer War of Killane. Not a territory Chronicle.
New standalone material — a detailed armor-and-weapon combat showcase per Abad's craft instruction.
Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The Directorate engineers sent to collapse the flooded sections of the Killane tunnel network hadn't
counted on the collapse itself being turned against them, and by the time Kanja reached the lower
gallery where forty tunnel workers had gone to ground rather than surface into a waiting patrol, the
water was already rising past their knees.

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System read the tunnel the way it had learned to read fog and darkness —
not by sight, which the flooded dark denied him entirely, but by pressure differentials transmitted
through the plating, mapping the collapsing sections and the rising water table both at once, faster
than any lantern could have shown it. He moved through the flooding gallery at a pace that should
have been impossible in water already at his thighs, each step converting the drag of the current
into stored charge rather than losing momentum to it.

The drainage gate the engineers had jammed shut to trap the flood in place gave way to a single
discharge from Obsidian Malice, the war club's charge concentrated into a blow that turned two years
of engineering work into an afterthought — water that had been rising for twenty minutes began
draining within seconds, the gallery's level dropping fast enough that the trapped workers could see
it happening in real time.

That left the engineers themselves, six of them positioned at the gallery's upper exit with orders to
ensure nothing trapped below came out alive. Onyx of Oblivion cleared the sheath in the same motion
that carried Kanja up the flooded stairwell, Whisper of Shadows closing the distance faster than
their own lanterns could track him, Veil Piercer finding the gaps in armor built for demolition work
rather than close combat. Four surrendered before a blow landed. The other two didn't get the choice
— Cadence Ruin disrupted whatever coordinated defense they'd planned into two men fighting alone, and
neither lasted past the opening exchange.

By the time the last of the water drained, all forty trapped workers had reached the surface, and
six Directorate engineers stood disarmed in a gallery they'd meant to be a tomb.

"You read that flood like you could see through the water itself," the tunnel crew's foreman said,
still catching his breath. "How."

"The same way I read everything down here. Not with my eyes. With the pressure, the weight, the way
the water and the stone both push back when something's wrong with them." Kanja set Obsidian Malice's
head against the tunnel floor, its cycle spent but the gallery secured. "Down here, sight's the least
reliable sense anyone has. I learned that the hard way, same as everyone working these tunnels does."
