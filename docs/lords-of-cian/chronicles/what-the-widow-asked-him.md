# What the Widow Asked Him

*Locked canon, Batch 197, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-728`). The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XXXIII, wave 11 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

She found him herself, rather than waiting for him to be brought to her, and Kanja let her, refusing
the crew's offer to stand between them.

"I don't want an apology," she said, before he could offer one. "Apologies don't put a father back in
a house with two children who keep asking when he's coming home. I want to understand what happened,
plainly, from the person who was actually there. Not a runner's secondhand account. You."

He told her plainly. The pillar, the reading, the level he hadn't known existed below it, the new
verification step it had cost her husband's life to teach him. He offered none of it as an excuse,
because he'd decided, before she ever arrived, that it wasn't one.

"You're not going to tell me it was necessary," she said, watching him carefully. "The garrison. The
siege it prevented."

"It was necessary. That doesn't make it acceptable that he died for it, and I'm not going to pretend
those two things cancel each other out just because one of them is true. Both stay true. I don't get
to resolve that by explaining the strategy to you."

She was quiet for a long moment, longer than he let himself fill with anything.

"My husband spent thirty years believing metal answered to the people patient enough to listen to
it. He'd have understood the doctrine better than most people who've asked me about it since. That
doesn't make me forgive what it cost. It just means I understand, better than I'd like to, exactly
what you were trying to do when it happened."

"I'm not asking you to forgive it."

"Good. Because I don't, not yet, and I don't know that I will. I came to understand, not to release
you from anything." She stood to leave. "Keep the new verification. Make sure it means something more
than a rule you follow because it's easier than remembering why you built it."

"It already does."
