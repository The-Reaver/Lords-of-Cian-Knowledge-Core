# The Building That Asked to Be Heard

*Locked canon, Batch 218, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-968`). The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LVII, wave 19. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The old foundry warehouse had stood empty for longer than Kanja had worn any alias at all, its iron
bracing rusted past any use, scheduled at last for demolition to clear ground the district needed for
something newer and more useful than a memory. Nothing about the job required the doctrine. A crew with
hammers and time could have brought it down without him.

He came anyway, on no one's request, the day before the demolition crew arrived.

"I don't need to read this one," he told Danne Sok, who'd come along more out of curiosity than any
operational need. "There's no weak seam to find, no enemy to answer, no one waiting on the other side
of it needing anything from me at all."

He put his palm against the bracing regardless, the way he had against a hundred structures with a
purpose behind the touch, and let the resonance return whatever an empty, dying building still had left
to say after years of no one asking.

It answered slower than anything living, the tension long since settled into a kind of patient,
unhurried decay that had nothing urgent left in it to diagnose. No failing seam demanding correction.
No hidden deception waiting to be caught. Just an old structure, worn down to the honest end of what it
had been built to carry, in no particular hurry about it.

"Every wall I've ever read has been asking something of me," he said, more to the building than to
Danne Sok. "Hold me up. Bring me down. Tell them I'm innocent. This one isn't asking anything. It's just
letting itself be heard, one last time, before there's nothing left to listen to."

He stayed with his hand against the iron longer than the reading required, the way a person might sit
a while after a conversation had already finished saying everything it needed to.

"You've spent this whole doctrine deciding what to do with what you hear," Danne Sok said eventually.
"What do you do with a building that isn't asking you to do anything at all?"

"Nothing," Kanja said, stepping back at last. "For once, nothing is the whole answer. I've read
structures to save people, to win battles, to prove my own innocence, to teach the next hands who'll
carry this after me. I don't think I'd ever once read one simply to say goodbye to it before it was
gone." He looked up at the warehouse a final time, already half a memory itself by morning. "I don't
know that the doctrine needed this stop. I think I did."

The demolition crew brought it down the next day, quick and unremarkable, exactly the way it should
have gone.
