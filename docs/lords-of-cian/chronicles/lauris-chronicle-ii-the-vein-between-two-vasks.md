# Lauris Chronicle II: The Vein Between Two Vasks

*Locked canon, Batch 293, 2026-09-17 (`MCD-1562`). Second entry in Lauris Letitia's own Chronicle
series, and the first of the "Strand K" (Kares Prime / deep-past) thread under the four-strand
pacing convention agreed this batch (`docs/lords-of-cian/character-chronicle-gameplan.md`).
Dramatizes one of the three inter-Vask security operations from the "Long Operational Period"
(`MCD-1555`, age 1,841-~3,400) -- resource-scarcity conflicts between Karesians resolved without
lethal force, her presence alone sufficient to redirect toward deliberation. Set at Vask Aldreth,
already a named location (Era D-E collision-check, Batch 291). No new named characters -- the
opposing delegation stays deliberately unnamed, matching the project's convention for secondary
figures who don't need individual identity. Full text below. Abad's approval: "lock."*

---

*Archive fragment, Lauris's hand: "Threnarr and Aldreth. A vein, not a war. I walked between them.
They stopped because I was already standing where the first blow would have to land. I did not
draw the Spine of Dagon. I did not need to."*

This is not mine to have witnessed. It is hers, recorded in the archive she keeps aboard the
Karkosa, in her own hand, and I set it down here exactly as I found it, because I believe there are
things about her that cannot be understood from what I have seen — only from what she chose, once,
long before I met her, to write down herself.

She was some six hundred years past achieving karth-ven, still young by Kares Prime's reckoning,
already known across the surviving Vasks as the woman who had held Olmedrin alone against four
hundred raiders. Vask Threnarr — her own birth-hold — and Vask Aldreth, its nearest neighbor across
a mountain saddle, had shared a forgeable ore-vein for eleven thousand years without incident. The
population that once worked both sides of it no longer existed in the numbers that made sharing
effortless. By the year of this account, Aldreth's smiths needed the vein's southern face six
months earlier than Threnarr's schedule allowed, and Threnarr's Iron-Speakers, reading their own
dwindling stores, refused to move the schedule for anyone.

I want to be precise about what this was, because the temptation is to call it a war and it was
never that. Two communities, each too small to survive losing the argument, each too honorable to
simply seize what it needed, standing at the edge of violence not from malice but from arithmetic.
Twelve armed representatives from each Vask met at the vein's boundary marker. Neither side had
drawn a weapon. Both sides had brought them.

Lauris arrived on foot, unarmored, the Spine of Dagon sheathed and slung rather than drawn, and
walked directly into the space between the two delegations — not beside either, not behind a
mediator's table, but standing in the exact geometric center of where the first exchange would have
to pass through her. She has recorded that she did not plan this. She recorded, later, that her
body simply understood before she did that the argument needed a body in it that neither side could
afford to strike and neither side wished to.

She did not speak for either delegation. She did not propose a solution. She asked, of both sides
in turn, one question: what does the vein cost you if you lose this argument, precisely, in numbers
you can defend. Threnarr's representative gave a number. Aldreth's gave a different one. She asked
them to explain the numbers to each other, directly, without her as intermediary — and then she
simply remained where she was, unmoving, while they did.

It took the better part of a day. No blow was thrown. By evening the two delegations had reached an
arrangement neither had proposed at the outset: Aldreth would take the southern face for four
months rather than six, in exchange for Threnarr's own claim on a smaller secondary deposit Aldreth
had never valued highly enough to defend. Lauris did not suggest this arrangement. She has
recorded, with what I read as genuine puzzlement rather than false modesty, that she does not know
whether her presence changed the arithmetic or only gave both sides time enough to do the
arithmetic they already knew.

Her archive entry for the day is four lines long. The last of them reads: *I held a place two armed
groups could not fill with each other. I do not know if this is a use of my body or a use of the
space my body takes up. I record both possibilities.*

I have read perhaps forty of her Kares Prime archive entries by now, transcribing them into what
will eventually be a fuller record than either of us expected to need. Across all forty, this is
the only one in which she expresses uncertainty about what she herself accomplished. Every combat
action, she records plainly, cause and effect, no ambiguity permitted. This one entry she leaves
open. I do not think that is an oversight. I think it is the truest thing in the whole page: some
debts get discharged by force, and she has never doubted her capacity for that kind. This one she
discharged by simply being present, immovable, and correct about where to stand — and forty
centuries later, on a different world entirely, I watch her do the exact same thing in rooms that
have nothing to do with ore.
