# The Fleet That Went Blind Together

*Locked canon, Batch 263, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1350`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LXXVII, wave 26. A detailed Long Mask-era gear combat showcase: with the rival forecaster incapacitated by illness during a genuine weather emergency, a third-party raider exploits the moment both fleets are effectively weather-blind at once. Corrected Batch 321, 2026-10-02: an earlier draft staged this as a full-Trinity showcase, despite this Chronicle's placement well within the Long Mask era of just over 283 years, after Kanja's already-locked age-30 surrender of the Trinity to its sealed vault (`MCD-246`); replaced with his correct Long-Mask-era kit (`ARS-344` through `ARS-356`, plus the Rexmar Machete). Also removes a writers'-room "two waves earlier" reference from the narrative prose.*

---

It was the worst possible timing, and the raiders who struck knew it -- the rival forecaster laid low by a fever that had nothing to do with weather at all, unable to give her own fleet a reading just as a genuine, fast-moving storm system closed on the strait both fleets shared under their truce. For the first time since the truce began, one side of it had no working forecaster of its own, and a squadron that had been watching the truce for exactly this kind of gap moved in under cover of the coming weather.

The student read the storm for both fleets without being asked, because the alternative was watching an ally go in blind, truce or no truce, a courtesy that went well past anything the truce itself required. "Ninety minutes to landfall," she called across to the rival flagship, "and someone's using it as cover. I have you both."

Kanja moved before the raiders had fully committed, and this fight, unlike the earlier truce-holdout engagement, was never in question about whose side anyone was on -- both fleets faced the same raiders together, coordinated for the first time not by rank or command but by a single shared reading. The Forge-Coat and Ironfall Boots took the lead raider's charge and redirected it into open water exactly as the storm's leading edge arrived, letting the weather itself finish disabling a ship Kanja had only needed to nudge off course. His own instinctive Rexmar-Mar tactical sense timed strikes of the Rexmar Machete into the storm's own gust-lulls against a second raider's rigging, the same technique the truce-holdout entry had shown, now deployed in genuine joint defense rather than unilateral restraint. The Smoke System carried Kanja between both fleets' outer lines, close enough to both flagships that neither side needed to guess whether he was protecting them equally. The Ironhand Gauntlets threw a wall of displaced water ahead of the third raider's bow, the storm's own building swell doing half the work a calm sea would have left entirely to the blow.

By the time the storm's own worst hour passed, three raiders were disabled and the fourth had fled into weather no sane captain followed anyone into. The rival forecaster, fever-weak but conscious, had been carried on deck to watch her own fleet's safety confirmed by a reading she hadn't made herself.

"You covered for me," she said to the student afterward, not quite comfortable with the debt.

"I covered for both fleets," the student said. "You'd have done the same, and we both know it, because you already have -- you read weather for me once without being asked, before either of us had a truce to call it under." She didn't press the succession question again. She didn't need to. The rival forecaster's own fleet had seen, plainly, what a gap in her tradition actually cost.
