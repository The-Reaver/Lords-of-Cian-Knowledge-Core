# The Walk No One Heard

*Locked canon, Batch 195, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-658`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XXIII, wave 8 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The crew needed to know how many soldiers actually occupied the reserve garrison built into
Killane's old cistern network, and the only way to know for certain was to walk through it — not
fight through it, not sabotage it, simply walk its length, count, and leave without a single person
inside ever knowing anyone had been there at all.

Kanja went alone.

Onyx of Oblivion's Whisper of Shadows carried him past four separate watch positions without a
single head turning, footsteps finding the cistern's own worn stone in the exact places where
centuries of foot traffic had already softened any sound a step might make. He counted as he went —
provisions stacked by a quartermaster's tally he read upside-down through an open doorway, cots
arranged in a pattern that told him more about unit strength than any guard would have volunteered
under questioning, a reinforced section of wall that hadn't been there on the crew's last survey
and told him exactly where the garrison expected an attack to come from, if one ever did.

He passed within arm's reach of a sleeping sentry twice, once on the way in and once on the way
out, and left the cistern network the same way he'd entered it — through a drainage grate nobody
had thought to watch, because nobody had ever needed to think about a threat that never announced
itself with so much as a single displaced stone.

"Sixty-two men, not the ninety we estimated," he reported back, sketching the cistern's layout from
memory, precise down to the reinforced wall. "They're expecting an attack from the eastern
approach. They're not expecting anyone to have already walked the whole garrison and left without
a fight."

"No confrontation at all?" the crew chief asked, checking the sketch against their own outdated
survey.

"None. That was the entire point." Kanja set the sketch down, satisfied with its accuracy. "A fight
tells them we were there. A walk they never noticed tells us everything about them instead. I'd
rather know exactly what I'm facing than prove I could beat it and still walk in blind."

The garrison's own after-action logs, recovered weeks later, showed no record of any incursion at
all — the closest anyone came to noticing was a sentry's half-remembered dream about a draft from
a grate that shouldn't have been open, dismissed by morning as nothing worth reporting.
