# The Rival Who Called Him a Setback

*Locked canon, Batch 259, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1233`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LXVIII, wave 23. Age
190, V3 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The reformers found him first, which had never happened before — usually it was the other way
around, a rumor chasing a sighting chasing a wake of freed captives he'd already left behind. Three
of them, legal advocates who had spent eleven years building a bill through the Trust's own
legislature to outlaw debt-bondage transfer on the mainland routes, cornered him on a supply dock
with no weapons and no fear of him at all, only a grievance they'd clearly been rehearsing for
years.

Every raid he ran, their leader said, handed the bill's opponents a fresh argument: *see what
lawlessness tolerance for this creates, see why the routes need tighter control instead of looser
law*. Three years of hard-won legislative ground had been lost in the six months since his last
visible raid in their region, not because the bill was wrong, but because a black-sailed ship
burning a depot to the waterline made for a louder story than a committee vote did, and louder
stories moved votes the wrong direction.

He didn't argue the math. He'd never claimed the reputation was cost-free, only that its cost fell
on people he'd chosen to accept it falling on, not on people who hadn't chosen anything. What he
offered instead wasn't retreat — it was a narrower target. He'd leave the operations sitting in
genuine legal ambiguity, the ones the bill was actually written to close, to the reformers' own slow
work, and spend his raids only on the ones already flatly illegal under existing Trust law, the
ones no committee vote could make worse by being interrupted.

It didn't resolve anything between them. The advocate left still convinced the whole approach was
reckless; Kanja left still convinced that people currently in chains couldn't wait for a bill's
timeline regardless of whose argument it strengthened. Both kept doing exactly what they'd been
doing before the conversation, only now aimed a half-degree apart instead of squarely at each
other's feet.
