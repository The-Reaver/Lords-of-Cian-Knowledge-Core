# The Hunt for One Man

*Locked canon, Batch 182, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-532`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XIV. Rebellion
era, age 18, before the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material — a
detailed, sustained pursuit combat showcase per Abad's craft instruction. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

A hired killer, dispatched specifically to remove the dredge line's most effective organizer before
the man could testify in a wage dispute, moved through the district for two days ahead of Kanja's own
awareness of the threat — professional, patient, unwilling to strike until certain of a clean
opportunity. By the time Kanja learned of the contract, the killer had already begun closing in.

What followed was neither a single blow nor a siege, but a running pursuit across the district's own
tangled dockside geography — Onyx of Oblivion the only weapon available this early in the Rebellion,
Whisper of Shadows carrying Kanja rooftop to rooftop tracking a professional who knew exactly how to
disappear into crowds and shadow. Twice the killer nearly reached his target before Kanja closed the
gap; twice he vanished again into the district's own maze rather than risk a confrontation he could
see coming.

The chase ran the better part of a full night before it ended — not in the open, but in a narrow
alley the killer had chosen specifically because it offered no room for Onyx's usual reach. He'd
misjudged the confinement as an advantage. Veil Piercer found the gap in his guard regardless, ending
the contract without a killing blow, the man disarmed and bound rather than left dead in the street.

"Two days he had before we even knew," the organizer said afterward, shaken by how close the contract
had actually come to succeeding. "I didn't know any of this was happening until it was already over."

"That was rather the point. A contract like this only works if the target never sees it coming until
it's too late to matter." Kanja handed the bound killer over to the crew's own custody rather than the
authorities, unwilling to trust a system that had, in effect, permitted the contract in the first
place. "I'd rather you never have to know how close it came. I only tell you now because you deserve
to understand why the testimony matters enough that someone paid to stop it."

The organizer testified within the week, the wage dispute resolving in the workers' favor — the
targeted killing prevented, the actual purpose behind it achieved anyway.
