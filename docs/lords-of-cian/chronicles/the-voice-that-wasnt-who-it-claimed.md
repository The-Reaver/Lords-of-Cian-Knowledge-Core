# The Voice That Wasn't Who It Claimed

It was Pell Ostra who noticed it first — small inconsistencies in a supply requisition that
shouldn't have existed, timed just slightly wrong against the crew's actual movements, the kind of
pattern only someone who audited manifests for a living would catch. Someone aboard was passing
information outward, and had been for at least two months.

The crew's instinct, when Kanja laid it out plainly, was suspicion turned toward the newest hands —
the ones who hadn't yet earned the years of proof everyone else carried. He stopped that instinct
before it could take hold.

"That's exactly the assumption whoever's doing this wants us to make," he said. "Turn the newest
people against each other, let paranoia do the work of an actual investigation, and the real source
gets to keep operating while we tear ourselves apart looking in the wrong place."

The real source, when Pell Ostra's audit finally traced it, was someone who'd sailed with the crew
for four years — trusted, well-liked, present at more than one moment the crew had assumed was
private. He hadn't done it for money. He'd done it under threat, a family member held by a Trust
officer who'd promised harm if the reports stopped.

Kanja didn't have him executed, or even confined the way the man clearly expected.

"You should have told me," he said, simply, once the truth was out. "Not because I'd have handled
it perfectly. Because carrying that alone for four years, believing you had to choose between us and
someone you loved, was never a choice you needed to make by yourself."

They moved on the officer holding the man's family within the week, and got them clear. The man
stayed aboard, on lighter duty and closer watch for a time, not as punishment but as the slow,
deliberate rebuilding of trust the whole crew understood had to be earned back rather than simply
declared restored.

"Most captains would have killed him," Efa Gol observed, watching the man work weeks later, clearly
more at ease than he had been in years.

"Most captains would have made an example instead of understanding a person. I've never wanted to
be most captains."
