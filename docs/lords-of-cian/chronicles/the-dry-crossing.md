# The Dry Crossing

*Locked canon, Batch 254, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1103`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXXIII, wave 25. New
desert/heat survival-environment register -- the column crosses a waterless salt basin under a hard
deadline, with heat and thirst doing what no enemy in the run has managed.*

---

The basin had no name on any map Bane's people carried, just a blank pale stretch between two ridges
that the local guide called, without elaboration, the place nothing lived. Thirty-one miles of
cracked salt flat with no water anywhere along it, and a two-day window to cross it before the
column's own supply ran out on the far side regardless of what they did.

No enemy pursued them across it. None needed to. By the second day, the heat alone had put four
people on litters and left the rest moving in the kind of silence that came from having nothing left
to spend on talking.

Bane gave up his own ration without telling anyone he was doing it — poured half of what remained in
his own skin into a litter-bound woman's cup when he checked on her, the way he checked on every
litter twice a day, and said nothing about it before moving on to the next one.

Callum Breck saw it. He didn't say anything either — hadn't said much of anything, still, since long
before this crossing — but the next morning the ration count that got called out to the column had
quietly shifted a half-measure smaller for Bane specifically, folded into the general distribution
without explanation, corrected by someone who'd noticed and adjusted for it without being asked.

Bane caught it on the third distribution, understood exactly what had happened, and said nothing
about that either. It was the kind of exchange that didn't need words to be complete.

They crossed the last mile into shade and the first standing water in two days with the column
intact, no deaths, four people carried the whole distance by others who'd had less to spare than the
ones being carried.

"Nothing to fight out there," Efa Gol said, once they'd made camp on the far side. "Just distance and
heat."

"Sometimes that's worse," Bane said. "You can plan around an enemy. You can't argue with how far the
water actually is."
