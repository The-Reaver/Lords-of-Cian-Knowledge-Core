# The Storm That Nearly Took the Anvil

*Locked canon, Batch 262, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1314`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LXVIII, wave
23. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241), open water between coastal
settlements. A detailed, battle-intense Trinity combat showcase combining a genuine sea storm with
an opportunistic Directorate naval strike. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose.*

---

The storm wasn't sent by anyone. That was the part the Directorate captain had gambled on when he
brought his cutter in behind it, using the swell and the driving rain as cover a warship alone
never could have bought him — that a converted ore-barge running forges below deck would be too
busy fighting the sea to notice one more hostile shape in it until it was already alongside.

The Anvil was never built for open water in weather like this. Kanja felt the whole deck answer
wrong underfoot, a mobile forge-ship's low, heavy stability fighting a swell it hadn't been
designed against, and for the first time since the Directorate raid on the ship itself (MCD-423)
the actual vessel, not just what it carried, was the thing genuinely at risk. He split his own
attention the way the fight demanded: Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System grounded through the deck
itself, bracing a listing forge-mount before it could tear loose and take three apprentices with
it, while Onyx of Oblivion's senses cut through rain and spray thick enough to hide a hull length
away, finding the cutter's shape against the dark before its own lookouts found the Anvil.

"They're not here for a fair fight," he said, feeling the cutter's angle. "They're here to use the
storm to do what a straight approach couldn't."

Obsidian Malice answered that gamble directly — a discharge timed not at the enemy hull first but
at the Anvil's own straining forge-mount, a controlled pulse that fused a cracking joint solid
before the swell could finish the job the storm had started, buying the deck crew the seconds they
needed to lash down what the sea was trying to take. Only then did the fight turn outward: Veil
Piercer finding the cutter's own storm-battered seams, Whisper of Shadows crossing black water
between the two hulls faster than any boarding line could have carried a man, and a short, brutal
exchange on a deck that was pitching under both crews equally, the storm showing no more mercy to
the attackers than to the ship they'd come to sink.

The cutter didn't survive the swell once its crew stopped fighting it to fight the Anvil instead.
Kanja pulled six of its own sailors out of the water before the sea finished what the Directorate
captain's gamble had started, hauling them up onto a deck that was still, by any honest measure,
losing its own fight with the storm.

"You'd have let the weather kill us and called it clean," he told the captain, dragged up soaked
and furious at his own rescue. "I don't get that kind of exemption. Neither do you, tonight."
