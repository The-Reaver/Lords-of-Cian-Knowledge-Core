# When the Coat Didn't Answer

*Locked canon, Batch 200, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-810`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle XXV, wave 9 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The consortium's newest hire called it a lash rather than a weapon, a coiled length of raw Living
Drakma wired into a hand-cranked charge box, and nobody on either side of the fight that followed
fully understood what it would do until it did it.

The first strike caught the Forge-Coat's shoulder plating dead-on, and instead of the familiar
grounding hum that meant impact converted safely into the weave's own structure, something else
happened — a bright, wrong current that skipped past the grounding pathway entirely and found bare
skin beneath, dropping him to one knee with a jolt that had nothing in common with any blade or bolt
the coat had ever answered for cleanly in over a hundred years of service.

"It's not built for that," he said aloud, mostly to himself, cataloguing the failure with the same
flat attention he gave anything worth understanding quickly. "Whatever that current is, the weave
reads it as something it isn't."

The lash-wielder pressed the advantage, confident now, closing for a second strike he clearly
believed would finish what the first had started.

There was no time to diagnose a coat that had simply, for the first time in its service, failed to
do its one job. So he did the only thing that had ever worked before there was a coat at all — he
came up inside the lash's reach where its length couldn't be brought to bear, and met the second
strike not with armor but with the Rexmar Machete, plain steel against a weapon that had no answer
for a blade that simply refused to be where the current expected it.

Three exchanges, no theatrics, no smoke — the kind of fight the earliest freed captives back at
Pirate Dawn had once described as the part the stories always left out, the part that was just a man
who'd held a sword his whole life. The lash hit the deck disarmed, its charge box cracked and
useless, before its wielder understood the coat's failure had bought him nothing but a few seconds
of false confidence.

Efa Gol's successor found him afterward, examining the coat's scorched shoulder seam with the same
flat attention he'd given the failure itself. "It's never done that before."

"It's never met that before." He set the coat aside, unrepaired, for the crew's own smiths to study
properly rather than patch in the field. "A hundred years of gear teaches you to trust it. One
afternoon teaches you it's still just built things, with built things' limits. I'd rather learn that
now, from a lash, than later, from something worse."
