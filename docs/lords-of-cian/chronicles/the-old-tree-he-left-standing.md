# The Old Tree He Left Standing

*Locked canon, Batch 261, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1285`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LXVI, wave 22, closing the wave. A quiet closer applying the living-structure distinction to an ordinary civilian tree, with the opposite outcome of the condemned foundry elegy. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The old growth stood at the edge of a settlement that wanted it gone — not out of malice, but because its roots had begun lifting the corner of a granary foundation, and the council saw a structural hazard where it had once seen shade. They'd sent for Kanja the way they'd have sent for any engineer: to confirm the tree was a danger, so the felling could proceed without an argument about it afterward.

He put his palm to the bark the way he had on the fig-root crossing, slower now, more certain of what he was listening for. The tree's own tension breathed exactly the way the bridge's had — rising and falling on a cycle with no relation to the granary's weight above it, a living thing answering for itself rather than a structure straining under a load it hadn't chosen.

"The roots are lifting the foundation," the council elder said, watching him work. "That's not in dispute. We just want to know how much time we have before it's genuinely dangerous rather than just inconvenient."

"The roots aren't lifting the foundation because they're failing. They're lifting it because they're growing, the same way they've grown for longer than this granary's stood here. The tension I'm reading isn't strain. It's just — persistence." Kanja stepped back from the trunk. "If you want an honest structural answer: the granary's foundation is the thing under stress, not the tree. Shim the corner, redirect the two roots doing the actual lifting six feet either way, and you'll have another century out of both of them."

"And if we'd rather just be rid of the argument?"

"Then you'd be felling something that isn't the problem to solve one that's cheaper to fix twice than to undo once." He didn't press it further than that — the choice was theirs to make, not his to enforce with the doctrine or without it. "I've spent most of this doctrine's life reading things that were built to fail eventually, one way or another, and deciding when to help that along. This is the first time in a long while the honest answer was just: leave it standing."

The council chose the shim. The granary's corner settled true within the season, and the tree kept growing exactly as it had before anyone had asked it a question it had no way to answer except by continuing to do what it had always done.

"You didn't discharge anything," the second student said, watching him pack the listening rod away unused. "First time in a long while you've come to a structure and left it exactly as you found it."

"Most of what I do ends with something coming down. It's easy to forget the doctrine was never built to be a hammer looking for a nail — it was built to tell the truth about a thing, and sometimes the truth is just that it's fine, and the argument around it was the only thing that needed fixing." Kanja looked back once at the old growth as they left. "I've buried enough honest structures that didn't deserve it. I'll take the ones I get to leave standing."
