# The Last Ledger Line

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Scourge Alias Chronicle, wave 10. Long Mask era,
age 300, V4 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters.*

---

Garren Hask had kept the crew's ledgers since before the Scourge existed as a name for anyone to
carry, and at an age that had long since stopped meaning anything ordinary by any shorter-lived
people's arithmetic, he asked for one evening alone with the full run of them — every raid, every
liberation, every tally recorded across nearly three centuries of the persona's service — to do
something he'd apparently been putting off for longer than he cared to admit.

"A final count," he said, when he finally showed the finished page. "Not final as in anything's
ending. Final as in I wanted one number that held the whole of it, instead of scattered across two
hundred separate entries a man would have to spend a week reading to actually understand."

The number, carefully totaled and re-checked twice by Hask's own exacting hand, sat at the bottom of
the page in ink dark enough to have been pressed with real intention: two hundred and eleven thousand,
four hundred and six individual human beings, freed across every engagement the Scourge had ever
carried out, from a captain's honor blade at Pirate Dawn's dawn to a reef no chart had ever recorded.

"You counted every one."

"I counted every one I had a record of. There'll be some the raids themselves never gave me a clean
number for — chaos doesn't always leave a countable line behind it. So it's a floor, not a ceiling.
The real number's higher." Hask said it the way he said everything in the ledgers, precise and
unsentimental, but something in how carefully he'd set the page down suggested the number had cost
him more attention than the dry recitation let on. "I wanted you to see it as one figure, once, before
I got too old to trust my own count anymore."

"Why now?"

"Because I've started noticing I have to check my own addition twice where I used to just know it was
right the first time." Hask didn't dress that up any more than Efa Gol once had, decades before, about
her own half-second. "I'm not telling you I'm finished. I'm telling you the ledger deserved one true
total while the man keeping it still trusted his own hand enough to be sure of it."

He held the page a long while, two hundred and eleven thousand four hundred and six pressed into a
single line that had taken nearly three centuries and would never, either of them understood, be the
last line the work itself required.

"Keep counting as long as you trust your hand," he said finally, handing it back. "After that, teach
someone else to. The number was never the part that mattered most. Somebody still needs to be the one
who cares enough to keep it honest."
