# The Line He Drew Inside His Own Column

*Locked canon, Batch 209, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-909`). Bane Alias Chronicle XLVI, wave 16. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Garren Hask brought it to him before breakfast, which was how Bane knew it was serious — Hask
never interrupted a meal for anything he thought could wait until after one.

A recruit barely three weeks into the column, one of the salt-flat conscripts who had walked away
from the Directorate line rather than fight, had put a captured quartermaster's clerk on his knees
the night before and beaten him for the location of a supply cache the man had already given up
freely, twenty minutes earlier, to someone else. Two witnesses. No dispute about what happened, only
about what it meant.

"He says the man was Directorate," Hask said. "Says that makes it different."

"It doesn't." Bane set down the bread he hadn't started eating. "Bring them both. The recruit and
the clerk."

He heard it standing, not seated the way he judged the freed captives' own disputes — this wasn't
a facilitation, and he didn't want it read as one. The recruit's account changed twice in the
telling, tightening each time toward something closer to an excuse. The clerk, still frightened,
said only that it had happened and that he didn't understand why, since he'd already told them
everything.

"You want to know why the column doesn't get raided in its sleep," Bane said, to the recruit and
to the dozen others who had gathered without being summoned, "when we camp within a day's march of
three garrisons that outnumber us. It isn't the reputation alone. It's that every person we've ever
taken from the other side of a fight has told the next one what actually happened to them. That's
the whole of it. That's the entire mechanism. The day someone tells a different story is the day it
stops working, for all of us, not just for you."

"He's Directorate," the recruit said again, quieter now.

"He was. Right up until the moment he stopped being a threat to anyone, which was twenty minutes
before you touched him." Bane looked at him for a long moment. "I'm not going to kill you for this.
I considered it, and it would be the wrong lesson — it would say the punishment for cruelty is more
cruelty, delivered by someone with better aim. What I am going to do is send you back the way you
came, without the column's protection and without anyone here vouching for you to the next column
that'll ask. You broke something that isn't mine to forgive on the clerk's behalf alone. It belongs
to everyone who's ever trusted the column because of what it doesn't do."

The recruit left before midday, walking the same salt-flat road he'd walked in on, and nobody
stopped him and nobody wished him well. Hask wrote the incident down in full, including the
recruit's name — the only entry in his ledger that season recording someone the column had chosen
not to keep.

"Some will say it's soft," Hask said afterward. "Sending him off instead of ending it."

"Some will say worse things about the alternative." Bane picked the bread back up, finally, cold by
then. "The name only means anything as long as it's true. I'd rather lose a recruit than lose that."
