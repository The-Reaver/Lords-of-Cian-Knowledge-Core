# The Settlement That Was Never There

The report reached The Anvil the way every burning report reached it: a rider, a district name, a
column of smoke described secondhand by someone who claimed to have seen it from the ridge road.
Kanja had the crew turning toward it within the hour, the way he always did, the way the Trust had
learned to expect and had stopped being able to prevent.

It was Callum Breck who noticed the shape of it was wrong before anyone reached the district at
all. "This is the fourth burning report out of the same stretch of coast in six weeks," he said,
laying the four riders' accounts side by side against the campaign's own route log. "Different
districts, same general road, and every single one's brought us a day's hard march off our actual
schedule and back again. We've rebuilt nothing out of any of the first three. Because there wasn't
anything to rebuild when we got there."

Kanja read the pattern the same way once it was laid out for him — not a burning campaign at all,
but four decoys, each just credible enough to pull the crew a day off its real line, walking them
closer each time to a stretch of coast road with good ambush ground and no settlement worth
defending anywhere near it.

"So we don't answer the fourth," Breck said.

"We answer it. Just not the way they're counting on." Kanja turned the route log over, tracing the
ambush ground the pattern was clearly walking them toward. "They've spent six weeks teaching us
their signal. Time we taught them we'd learned it."

The crew approached the fourth reported district on the ambush road itself rather than the
predictable relief route, Trinity engaged before the first false report had promised any target
to defend at all. The detachment waiting in the coastal scrub, set for a tired crew arriving from
the wrong direction expecting a burning settlement that had never existed, found instead a column
that knew exactly what ground it was standing on and exactly why. It broke in minutes, with nothing
behind it to salvage and nothing ahead of it to burn.

"Four fake reports and a week of marching to bait one ambush," Breck said afterward, watching the
detachment's survivors withdraw. "That's a patient campaign against a man they know can't ignore a
burning report."

"It is. And it worked, for six weeks — got exactly the marches it wanted out of me, every single
time." Kanja looked back down the coast road the crew had walked four times for nothing before this
one paid off. "Doesn't change what happens the next time a rider comes in with smoke on the
horizon. I still go. I just go a little slower on the fifth report than I did on the first, and
check the ground before I check the district."
