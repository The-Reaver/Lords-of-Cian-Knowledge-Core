# The Name They Never Needed

*Locked canon, Batch 271, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1411`). Crow King Alias Chronicle XCIII, wave 31, closing
it. Kanja, the apprentice, the third generation, and the fourth generation weigh what they found at
the river town, and decide to leave it exactly as it is. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

"So what do we do about it," the third generation asked, once the apprentice and the fourth
generation had finished telling it, "a practice out there wearing our discipline's face and none of
our name?"

"Nothing," Kanja said, before anyone else could answer. "That was never a question that needed us to
decide it. It already answered itself six years ago, the day that page ended up behind a granary wall
instead of in a fire."

"They're not as fast as we are," the fourth generation said. "Not as exact. The old woman doesn't
know a tenth of what any of us knows about reading a room under real pressure. But she got the actual
thing right -- the part underneath the trick, not the trick itself. Maybe more right than some of the
scouts we trained ourselves, honestly."

"That's because we didn't train her," the third generation said. "We couldn't have. She built it
herself, out of six words on half a page, the slow way, the only way that ever actually holds." She
looked at the apprentice. "That's the same thing you did, before any of us existed to teach you
anything."

The apprentice hadn't said much the whole conversation, turning something over. "I keep thinking
about what you told the fourth generation once," she said to Kanja, "when he asked what happens if
someone finally does ask to be the fifth. You said you didn't know what the second would look like
either, until she was already there." A small, dry smile. "I don't think anyone's going to ask this
group to be a fifth generation of anything. I don't think they'd want it. They already are whatever
this is. They just don't call it what we call it."

"Good," Kanja said. "Let them keep not calling it that. I never needed the name to survive me. I only
ever needed the part underneath it to. It just did, two towns and one lost page ahead of me finding
out."

Nobody suggested going back to correct anything else, or to formalize what the river town had built,
or to fold it into anything. They let it stand exactly as it was -- imperfect, unaffiliated, entirely
its own -- and rode home with the quiet, settled feeling of having found proof, by complete accident,
of something Kanja had said as a guess only one season before and none of them had been sure yet
whether to believe.
