# The Three Voices in One Hand

---

Every prior showcase had used the Trinity in sequence — the Aegis-Talisman diagnosing, Mafesto
absorbing, Obsidian Malice striking, Onyx clearing — each piece doing its own work in its own moment.
Against the fortified relay station's reinforced iron shell, ringed by three concentric defensive
rings each built from a different alloy, Kanja tried something he hadn't attempted before: listening
through all three weapons at once, rather than one lens followed by the others in turn.

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System, absorbing the station's answering fire in real time, carried its
own faint resonance data in how the impacts transferred — a second, cruder listening channel running
parallel to the Talisman's precise reading. Obsidian Malice, held ready rather than yet discharged,
picked up harmonic bleed through its own housing from the outer ring's vibration the moment the
Talisman's broadcast began. Onyx, blade held still against the ground rather than in motion, felt the
station's own foundation trembling through the earth itself.

Three separate signals, three separate instruments, one structure — and for the first time, Kanja
read all three simultaneously rather than building the picture one layer at a time.

The composite picture assembled faster than any single-instrument diagnosis ever had: outer ring,
middle ring, and the station's true iron core, all mapped within the span of a single sustained
listening pass instead of three sequential ones. The discharge that followed took all three rings down
in one continuous cascade, each layer's failure feeding directly into the next without the pause a
sequential approach would have required between them.

"That's new," Onyx said afterward, the ground beneath the collapsed relay station still settling.

"It's not new. It's what I should have been doing years ago, if I'd trusted that every piece of this
Trinity was already listening the whole time, whether I was asking it to or not. I just hadn't thought
to ask all three at once before today."
