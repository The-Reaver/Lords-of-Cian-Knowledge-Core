# Lauris Chronicle XXXVI: The Two She Already Moved

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1653`). Thirty-sixth entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, part of the wave-one push of Strand L. Continues the Operation 38 facility family
(`MCD-191`) from a different angle than Chronicle XXXV (`MCD-1652`): the two of the three withheld
facilities she has already cleared and relocated to concealment, matching the K-Theta cave-system
precedent at `MCD-190`. Deepens rather than resolves -- a maintenance visit surfaces three natural
deaths among the relocated population since her last count, establishing that "cleared" has never
meant "resolved" for these subjects either, only relocated to a slower and gentler kind of waiting.
No new named characters; the facility stays uncoded, matching `MCD-191`'s and Chronicle IV's own
practice. Narrated by Fermand Aurelias.*

---

*Archive fragment, present-day: "Forty-one, the first year I counted them. Thirty-eight, now. Eleven
years. Three deaths, none by violence, none by my failure to arrive in time. I do not know what to
call a loss I did not cause and could not have prevented. I am recording it as a loss regardless."*

Lauris keeps two ledgers of relocated subjects with the same quiet discipline she keeps every other
account in her archive, and I had assumed, until this visit, that the two facilities she cleared
from Operation 38's withheld three sat closer to resolved than the third she has left untouched --
cleared, relocated, counted, and therefore, in whatever rough accounting closes an account of this
kind, further along than a door she has not yet opened. I no longer believe the distance between
them is as large as I assumed.

She goes to both sites on a rotation she has never fully explained to me, staggered enough that no
outside observer could predict when she will arrive at either. I accompanied her to the nearer of
the two this time, an inland holding she reached across two days of unremarkable travel, and I want
to record plainly that nothing about the approach suggested crisis. The concealment held. The
subjects, insofar as I could observe from the periphery she permitted me, appeared undisturbed in
whatever suspended, sheltered existence her original clearance secured for them. This is not, I want
to be clear, an account of a debt suddenly gone wrong.

It is an account of a debt that has continued to age in ways she had not fully priced into her own
arithmetic. She counted, as she always does, and the number did not match her last visit's record.
Thirty-eight against a prior forty-one. She confirmed, through the same careful indirect means she
uses at every site of this kind, that the discrepancy was not violence, not discovery, not
Directorate interference of any description. It was age. Three of the relocated subjects, whose
biology carries enough Karesian-derived material to slow their aging considerably but not, it now
appears, to arrest it entirely, had simply died in the intervening years -- one of what her
subsequent inquiry suggested was likely a degenerative condition present since before her original
clearance, two of causes she could not determine with the access available to her.

"I rescued forty-one people from a Directorate processing queue," she told me, on the walk back to
where I had waited. "I did not rescue them from time. I do not think I ever told myself that
distinction clearly enough, until now."

I asked her, because I thought the question worth asking even knowing she might decline to answer
it, whether the deaths changed anything about what she intends to do with either facility's
population in the longer term. She considered this longer than she considers most questions I put to
her. "No," she said finally. "It changes how urgently I should be thinking about it. I have been
treating both sites as stable in a way that lets the larger question -- what actually becomes of
these people, beyond continued concealment -- wait indefinitely. Thirty-eight against forty-one is
not an emergency. It is a reminder that indefinite is not the same as safe."

She recorded the three deaths individually in her archive, by number rather than name, in the same
structural form she uses for every entry of this kind -- a detail I note only because I have come to
understand, across the length of this chronicle, that the absence of names in these entries is never
carelessness. She does not have names to give the majority of the subjects she protects across her
accumulated facilities. She has told me before that she considers this one of the debt's own costs,
distinct from the debt itself: that the people she has spent centuries protecting are, in her own
archive, mostly numbers, and that this is a fact she records without excusing.

She did not visit the second of the two facilities on this occasion; her rotation places it several
months out yet, and she declined my suggestion that the news from the first site justified moving the
schedule forward. "One site's numbers do not tell me the other site's numbers," she said. "I will
know what I know when I go. Rushing the visit changes nothing about what I find when I arrive."

Her closing note, entered before she closed the archive: *Two of three cleared. Thirty-eight and
however many the second site holds, when I next count them. Cleared was never the same word as
finished. I do not think I will stop needing to relearn that distinction.*
