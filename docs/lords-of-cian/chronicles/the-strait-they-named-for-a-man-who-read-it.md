# The Strait They Named for a Man Who Read It

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LIV, wave 18, closing the wave. Long Mask era, generations after Sephtis's death. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. Names Sephtis's successor; no new named characters.*

---

No one had voted on it. No institution had declared it. Sailors had simply started calling the narrow
stretch of water off the harbor where Sephtis had spent his last years by his name, the way sailors
name things that matter to them long before anyone official gets around to writing the name down — the
strait where the old man used to sit on the rocks and watch the water for the pleasure of it, decades
after he'd stopped needing to watch it for anyone's survival.

His successor, herself an old woman now, heard the name used by a young crew member who had no idea
she was the one it would have meant the most to.

"Do you know whose name that is?" she asked him.

He didn't. Almost no one aboard did anymore. She was, by then, the oldest living person who had known
him at all, and even she found she had to work to remember the exact sound of his voice rather than
the shape of everything he'd taught her, which had long since become indistinguishable from her own
thinking.

She didn't correct the young sailor's ignorance. She let the name stand exactly as it had grown, an
ordinary piece of local custom rather than a monument, which she understood — the way Sephtis himself
would have understood it — was the more honest kind of remembering.

That evening she sat with her own student, the third generation of the craft since Sephtis had first
taught it to one person on an open rail with nothing behind him but his own certainty, and taught her
what she had once been taught: not the mathematics first, but the harder part underneath it.

"The sky doesn't care whose name is on the strait," she told her. "It never has. What it cares about
is whether the person reading it is willing to be wrong in front of people who need them to be right.
Everything else — the name, the reputation, the school, all of it — is just what's left over once
enough people have done that long enough for it to be trusted."

Her student wrote nothing down. She only listened, the same way, decades earlier, a much younger
woman had once listened to an old man on a rail who never meant to start anything larger than teaching
one person to read the water honestly.
