# The Storm That Wrote Its Own Signature

*Locked canon, Batch 218, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-961`). The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle L, wave 17. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The watchtower's iron-braced roof needed reading before dawn, the garrison inside it scheduled to be
relieved by an assault Kanja meant to end before it began — and the lightning storm rolling in off the
ridge line had other plans for the ironwork than he did.

He reached the Aegis-Talisman's frequency toward the bracing on the first close strike and the return
came back wrong in a way none of the doctrine's prior failures had — not muffled, not deadened, not
faked, but overwritten, a static charge climbing the ironwork ahead of each strike and imposing a false
tension signature of its own across the entire structure.

"That's not the roof lying to me," he said, watching a second strike land a ridge away and the false
signature spike again in answer. "That's the storm writing over it before I can even ask."

He tried working through it, isolating the charge and reading past it the way he'd learned to read
past falsified signatures before. It didn't hold — the storm's own signature wasn't a fixed
deception he could catalogue once and account for. It changed with every strike, a moving target with
no consistent shape to subtract.

"I can out-think a wall built to lie to me," he said, lowering the reading. "I don't think I can
out-think weather."

He didn't force it. He pulled the assault team back from the tower entirely, held the line at a
distance through the worst of the storm's hour, and let the ironwork go unread rather than commit a
discharge against a signature he couldn't trust.

"You had the tower," one of the fighters said afterward, once the sky had cleared and the read came
back clean and simple in the storm's absence. "Why not push through?"

"Because pushing through a reading I don't trust isn't discipline, it's guessing with more confidence
than I've earned," Kanja said. "I've learned to read through ice, through water, through a wall built
on purpose to fool me. I hadn't yet learned there's a kind of noise that isn't there to deceive me at
all — it's just bigger than the both of us, and the only honest answer to it is to wait."

The tower came down clean an hour after the storm passed, the same read he could have forced through
uncertainty hours earlier, arrived at instead through patience the doctrine had never before required
of him against an opponent that wasn't even trying to stop him.
