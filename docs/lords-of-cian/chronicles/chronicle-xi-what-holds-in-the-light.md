A promise made in shadow is not a promise. It is a debt waiting for the dark to collect it.

The Second Century Mark was held in the open, by Trust custom -- a dock hall with its roof stripped
to rafters years ago and never rebuilt, so that whatever was said inside it was said also to the
gulls and the weather and anyone standing on the harbor road with nothing better to do than
listen. Governor Maren Tallis had chosen the venue herself. Onyx would come to understand, before
the day was through, that she had chosen it thinking openness was a formality to survive rather
than a condition that could bind her.

Two hundred years had cost Kanja most of what a younger man mistakes for strength and left him
everything a older one learns to call it instead. He did not stand at his full height anymore
without meaning to. He stood at it today.

The terms were mostly settled before either party sat -- twelve years, taxation ceded to
recognition, the Scourge's economy acknowledged in writing as a thing the Trust could no longer
afford to pretend didn't outgrow it. Tallis read the public draft aloud, level-voiced, competent.
Then she set it down and said, quieter, meant for fewer ears than the hall technically held:
"There will be a second document. Unsigned. Understood between us alone, in case either side needs
room to breathe without the other calling it betrayal."

"No," a voice said, from the hall's far side, before Kanja could answer for himself.

Not loud. Certain -- the specific certainty of a woman who has watched every institution built to
own her compliance fail one at a time and stopped being surprised by the failing.

She had not been invited. Nobody in the hall could later agree on when she'd entered, only that
she was standing in it now, in the light that came down through the stripped rafters, where every
eye in the room that cared to look could find her.

Tallis did not know her. Onyx marked that Tallis tried anyway -- the particular attempt of a
powerful woman assessing whether an unknown one is a threat worth naming. "This isn't your
table."

"It's everyone's table. That's what a dock hall with no roof means." She did not raise her voice
to carry it. She didn't need to; the hall did that work for her. "You want a second document
nobody signs, so that whichever of you breaks the first one gets to say the other one understood.
I've sat across from that exact sentence my whole life, wearing a different face every time. It
never once meant what it said it meant."

"And what do you propose instead."

"One document. Everything either of you actually intends, said once, out loud, where the gulls
can hear it same as I can." A pause, Modern Laconic, cut to the bone. "If it can't survive being
said in the light, it isn't a truce. It's a plan to betray each other later and call it patience."

Tallis's jaw did the thing jaws do when a room has just been rearranged without her permission.
"You understand I could have you removed."

"You could try it in front of a hundred witnesses and a harbor road full of dockhands who'll tell
the story different from however you write it down." No threat in the words. A fact, stated the
way a woman states the load rating of a floor she's tested with her own weight more times than she
can count. "Or you could bribe me quietly after, somewhere with no roof stripped off it and no one
watching. I'd tell you to try that one too, except you'd find out the hard way it doesn't work
either. Money's never been the thing that moves me. Neither has a threat, so long as somebody's
looking."

Kanja had not spoken yet. Iron does not fill silence that is already doing its own work. He watched
the way a Governor two hundred years his political senior found, for the first time in the
negotiation, a party in the room she could not read the angle on -- and watched something in
Tallis's posture change, not surrender, calculation resetting itself around a variable she hadn't
priced.

"Say it once, then," Tallis said, finally. "Everything. In the light."

She did. Onyx has heard many things called witnessing in its long service and marks this apart
from all of them: not a scribe's transcription, not a Directorate's classified record, but a
woman standing in stripped rafter-light making both sides say, aloud, in front of a room that would
remember, exactly what they meant and exactly what they'd accept losing if the other side didn't
hold up their half. Twelve years. Taxation ceded. Recognition, real and public, not a courtesy. No
second document. Nothing said in this hall today that either party could later claim had meant
something private and different.

When it was done, it was done in a way that Onyx understood -- without yet having the words for it,
would not have them for a long time yet -- could not quietly come apart later. Not because either
side had grown suddenly honest. Because the shape of what they'd agreed no longer had a dark corner
left in it for either of them to hide a second meaning.

The truce held its full twelve years, exactly as recorded. The Governor's Shackle was named in that
same hall before the day ended, the Talisman answering to a weight it had carried without a name
until Kanja spoke of it aloud for the first time in a public room -- a hundred and fourteen years,
by the reckoning read into the record that afternoon, until whatever waited at the end of that
count came due.

The woman was gone before the formal signing, the way she always seemed to be. She left nothing
carved, no phrase passed hand to hand this time. She left a filing -- a single line entered into
the Trust's own dock-hall ledger under a seal none of Tallis's scribes recognized and none could
later trace to any registered office, recording only that the accord had been *witnessed, in full,
by one present.* No name attached. No office claimed. Just the fact of it, on the record, where
the dark could never quietly get to it.

Kanja looked at the entry a long while before the hall emptied.

He did not ask who had filed it. Onyx did not ask either.

Some doors, once opened, do not need remembering who opened them. They only need to stay open.

"The Door That Stays Open" is the mechanism behind the unidentified ledger filing that closes the scene

this note deliberately leaves unglossed as in-world subtext for why later Long Mask campaigns (the

publicly witnessed accords over deception -- a pattern, not a new locked fact. No existing locked

dock-hall texture and the near-miss second-document maneuver are new detail, not a revision. This is
the third hook left for a future Chronicle to draw together, alongside Chronicle IX's carved word and
Chronicle X's folk phrase -- this time an artifact (the unidentified ledger seal) rather than a spoken
trace, continuing the series' escalating pattern as more homage-era comrades accumulate.*
