# Lauris Chronicle LIX: What She Says to Things That Can't Answer

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1676`). Fifty-ninth entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, Strand W (Witness / present-day, quiet register), closing this second Strand W
push. A stakes-free entry pairing Lauris with Pell Ostra, whose established habit of addressing her
materials directly rather than the people around her (`CC-132`) is read here as a genuine kinship
with Lauris's own private relationship to her own body and density. No new named characters.
Written under Abad's blanket authorization: "lock it, continue uninterrupted, test and push to
main."*

---

*Archive fragment, present-day: "Pell Ostra asked a compound to cooperate with her today, the way
she asks everything to cooperate with her, as though it might actually decline. I told her I
understand the habit better than she may think. She asked what I say to my own density when I need
it to hold. I told her the truth: please, mostly. She said that was the correct answer."*

I have described Pell Ostra elsewhere in this account by the trait that most immediately
distinguishes her from every other person in this crew's story: she does not command her materials.
She addresses them — a charge, an acid, a compound not yet mixed — by what she is asking them to do,
the way a colleague requests cooperation rather than demands obedience, and I have never, in years
of watching her work, heard her voice change register between speaking to a person and speaking to a
beaker.

I did not expect, before the afternoon I am about to set down, that this habit would be the thing
that brought her and Lauris closest together of anyone I have paired her with in this series. I
understand it now, having watched the two of them work side by side over a set of compounds Pell was
testing for stability, in a way I do not think I understood it before.

"You talk to it," Lauris said, watching her measure a reagent with the particular care of a woman
who has, by her own account, lost track of how many fingers this exact compound has taken from
careless people over the years. "The compound. You spoke to it just now."

"I did."

"Why?"

Pell did not look up from her measuring, which I have come to understand is not rudeness in her but
simply the total, unbroken attention she extends to anything volatile enough to deserve it. "Because
it isn't obedient. Nothing I work with is obedient. It does what its own nature does, whether I ask
politely or not, and the only real choice I have is whether I understand that nature well enough to
ask for the right thing. Ordering it does nothing. Understanding it and asking correctly does
everything."

Lauris was quiet for a moment in the particular way I have learned to recognize as her turning
something over rather than failing to find a response. "I do something similar," she said finally.
"With myself."

Pell looked up at that, genuinely interested — I have rarely seen her attention leave a compound
mid-measurement for anything, but she set the reagent down with real care and gave Lauris her full
regard. "Go on."

"My density doesn't obey me either. Not the way people assume it does. It saturates, past a certain
point, in a direction that surprises even Sephtis some days. I don't command it. I ask it — the same
way you're describing. Please hold. Please don't announce me yet. Please let this be gentle instead
of total, this once. It listens more often than not. It has never once listened because I ordered
it to."

"Please, mostly," Pell said, half to herself, as though testing the phrase against her own private
vocabulary and finding it fit. "That's the correct answer."

They spent the better part of the afternoon that followed comparing notes in a register I do not
believe either of them had ever had occasion to compare with anyone else — two women whose most
formidable capacities are, by their own separate accounts, not obedient servants but partners of a
kind, requiring a specific, attentive courtesy rather than command. Pell showed her, at one point, the
precise phrasing she used on an unstable compound that had nearly taken her hand three years prior —
not a warning, not a threat, simply a plain, respectful request stated aloud before she touched it
again — and Lauris listened to it with the close, genuine attention I have watched her bring to very
few things in this account that were not, in some sense, a fight.

"Do you think it actually hears you?" Lauris asked, near the end of it.

"I don't know," Pell said. "I know it goes wrong less often when I ask than when I don't. That's
enough of an answer for me."

"That's enough of an answer for me too," Lauris said, and I watched something pass between the two of
them that I do not think either one would call friendship exactly, not yet, but which read, from
where I sat, like the particular, quiet recognition of two people who have each spent years being
misunderstood as commanding something that, in truth, they have only ever learned to ask.
