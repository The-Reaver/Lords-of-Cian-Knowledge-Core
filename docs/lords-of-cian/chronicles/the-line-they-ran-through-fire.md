# The Line They Ran Through Fire

*Locked canon, Batch 217, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-956`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle LIV, wave 18. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The besieged port had been cut off from the sea for eleven weeks by the time word of it reached Danne
Sok's charts — a Trust blockade drawn tight around a fishing city that had refused, early in the
Rebellion, to hand its granaries over to a garrison requisition, and had been starved for the refusal
ever since. No warship stood between the port and the open sea. Six converted merchant hulls did,
spread in an overlapping picket line wide enough that nothing had slipped past it in a month.

"It's not a fight we win by being stronger," Dol Maren said, laying the picket's spacing out against
Danne Sok's chart. "Six hulls in that formation can put a boat in the water fast enough to intercept
anything that tries to run it slow. We don't out-fight a picket like that. We out-run it, or we don't
get through at all."

Efa Gol's answer was three decoy hulls, run wide and loud a full night ahead of the real approach,
loaded with nothing but noise and lantern-signals timed to draw the picket's attention toward the
formation's western edge — a variation on tricks she'd built before, spent deliberately here rather
than held back, because a starving port was worth the cost of a plan she might otherwise have saved.

The real run went through the gap it opened, *The Ledger* riding low and dark with the season's grain
stores packed into every hold that could hold them, Mafesto's reading keeping her threaded between two
picket hulls with less than a hundred yards of clearance on either side. One picket hull caught the
movement anyway and gave chase, close enough that Obsidian Malice's discharge was the only thing that
disabled her rigging before she could signal the rest of the line.

They didn't fight the picket. They didn't need to — by the time the alarm reached the rest of the
formation, *The Ledger* was already inside the harbor mouth, grain stores unloading into hands that
hadn't eaten a full meal in longer than anyone aboard wanted to ask about, and the picket's remaining
five hulls held their formation rather than risk the same fate chasing a target they'd already lost.

"They'll tighten it after this," Garren Hask said, watching the unloading from the rail. "Next run
through won't be this clean."

"It won't," Kanja agreed. "Which means we do it again before they finish tightening it, not after." He
watched a line of children carrying sacks too large for them toward the granary, passed hand to hand by
adults too weak to carry a full one alone. "This isn't the kind of fight anyone writes a ballad about
either. It's just grain, and a port that gets to eat another month because we ran a line through fire
to bring it."

The port held through the winter. The picket, reinforced within the week per Garren Hask's own
prediction, never caught a second run cleanly enough to stop it either.
