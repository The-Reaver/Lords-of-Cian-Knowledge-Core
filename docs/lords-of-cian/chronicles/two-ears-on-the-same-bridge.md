# Two Ears on the Same Bridge

*Locked canon, Batch 261, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1287`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LXVIII, wave 23. A detailed diagnostic duel between Kanja and the Trust diagnostician, decided by restraint rather than raw skill. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

They read the same bridge from opposite ends for eleven minutes before either of them touched a weapon, and it was the strangest engagement of the doctrine's whole run — no army behind either man, no siege timetable forcing a discharge, just two people who could genuinely hear the same structure trading false signatures back and forth like a conversation neither wanted to be the first to end honestly.

The Trust diagnostician fed him three more false reads before Kanja caught the pattern underneath the deception — each false signature was itself perfectly, technically correct as a piece of diagnostic listening, just deliberately mis-timed to arrive a half-beat after Kanja's own verification pass, exploiting the exact gap doubled verification was built to close. It wasn't a flaw in the man's understanding. It was a flaw in trusting that a second confirming pass, taken by the same listener under the same conditions, could ever catch a deception coming from someone who understood the method as completely as the person being deceived.

Kanja broke the pattern by refusing to verify at all — pulling his hand back from the stone entirely and simply watching the other man's stance instead, the small, unconscious shift of weight that preceded every false feed. Whisper of Shadows closed the bridge's length in under two seconds once Kanja stopped trying to out-listen him and started reading the *listener* instead of the structure. Obsidian Malice never discharged. Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System grounded a single defensive strike meant to buy distance, redirecting it harmlessly into the bridge's own stonework rather than through either man.

The engagement ended with Kanja's forearm across the Trust diagnostician's throat and neither man having damaged so much as a single stone of the bridge they'd spent eleven minutes lying to each other about.

"You didn't win by hearing better," the man said, once Kanja had let him back to his feet. "I've spent four years making sure no one could out-hear me on a structure. You won by deciding not to play the game I built."

"I learned that lesson a long time before I learned yours," Kanja said. "A student of mine came close to using this doctrine on a granary out of anger once, and what stopped him wasn't better hearing. It was knowing when *not* to trust his own certainty. You built a trap that only works against someone who trusts the read over the person giving it to him. I've spent years training myself out of exactly that."

"Then I built the wrong trap."

"You built a very good trap. You just built it for a version of me that stopped existing a long time before you ever studied him." Kanja released his grip fully, stepping back rather than pressing an advantage that had already resolved. "What happens to you now is your own decision, same as it's always been for everyone who's ever come at this doctrine honestly instead of with a countermeasure. I'm not interested in ending you over a bridge nobody actually needed to fall."
