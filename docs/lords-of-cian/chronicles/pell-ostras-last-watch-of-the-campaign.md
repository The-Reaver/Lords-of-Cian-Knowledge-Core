# Pell Ostra's Last Watch of the Campaign

*Locked canon, Batch 198, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-770`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XLV, wave 15 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Pell Ostra kept the last watch outside the ledger office the night before the crew moved into the
Furnace District itself, and he spent most of it turning over what, exactly, he'd spent the better
part of two years actually guarding.

It had never been Kanja's body, not really — not on this campaign. He'd stood outside a hundred tent
flaps across a dozen districts, armed with nothing more than his own stillness, and in all that time
no blade had ever been drawn against the man inside, no blow had ever needed blocking. What he'd
guarded, he understood now, watching the lamp still burning inside where Ezio and Kanja were reviewing
the district ledgers one final time before morning, was something with no shape a weapon could
protect.

He thought about the captain who'd once pointed forty armed men at an unarmed man holding a book, and
lowered his weapons instead of using them. He thought about the moneylender's scribe who'd put his
pen down rather than keep lying with it. He thought about the widow who'd wanted blood and been given
a documented account instead, and never come back to see what it accomplished, and the impostor who
was never found, never punished, made irrelevant instead by a book anyone could check. None of it had
needed him standing there with a blade. All of it had needed someone standing there at all — visible,
unmoving, making it plain that nothing inside that tent was hiding behind force, so that no worker
waiting in line ever had to wonder if speaking honestly would cost them something worse than silence.

"You've been quiet all night," Kanja said, stepping out into the dark once the ledgers were finally
closed for the day.

"Just thinking about what I've actually been doing out here." Pell looked at the tent, then at the
furnace glow already visible on the horizon, the district that would carry the name into legend
waiting for them at first light. "I used to think I was standing guard in case somebody needed
stopping. Two years and nobody needed stopping. What they needed was somebody standing here long
enough that they stopped being afraid to talk."

"That's the whole job. It always was."

"I know that now." Pell settled back against the post he'd leaned on most nights that season. "Took
me longer to understand than it should have. Go get some rest. Tomorrow's going to need you rested
more than it needs me awake, and I've got one more watch left in me before this stretch of it's
done."
