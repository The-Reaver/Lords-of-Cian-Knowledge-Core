# What He Chose to Burn

*Locked canon, Batch 111, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-380`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle I. Rebellion era,
the morning after the Ash-Wharf Massacre (`MCD-235`, age 22) -- the unplanned, emergent birth of
the Scourge persona. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The coat was still smoking when the evacuation's last count came in: 4,073 civilians clear of the
bombardment zone, one Trust cruiser sunk, two beached and burning on the breakwater, and the man who
had stood in the center of his own detonated stockpile to make it happen was still standing,
technically unharmed, in a garment that had not survived the night nearly so well.

Efa Gol found him at the harbor's edge an hour after the last flames along the wharf had guttered
out, still wearing the ruined coat, watching the wreckage settle into the water the way a man
watches a debt he isn't sure yet how to categorize.

"You should let someone look at that," she said, nodding at the coat's charred shoulder, more habit
than real concern — she'd already checked him for burns twice and found nothing worse than singed
hair. "It's done. You don't have to keep wearing it."

"I know." He didn't take it off. "I keep thinking about what it looked like, from the water, standing
in the middle of eight hundred tons going up at once and not moving. I didn't plan that. I didn't
choose the image. It just happened, because it was the only way to get the timing right, and now
every person who watched it happen is going to carry that picture around for the rest of their
lives whether I want them to or not."

"Is that a problem?"

"I don't know yet." He finally looked away from the water, toward the evacuated crowd still huddled
along the safer stretch of shoreline, four thousand people who were alive because of a decision made
in less than a second and executed in a burning coat he hadn't chosen to burn on purpose. "Bane was
the Directorate's name for me. The Trench Monarch was Breck's. This — whatever this becomes — nobody
gave me. I gave it to myself, by accident, standing still while the world tried to decide whether
I'd live or die doing it. That's a different kind of name to carry. The others were given. This one's
earned, and I didn't even mean to earn it."

Gol didn't have an answer for that, and didn't pretend to. She stood beside him instead, watching the
same wreckage, until the coat had cooled enough that he finally let her help him out of it — not out
of ceremony, just the practical reality that a ruined coat could not keep doing the job a whole one
would need to do for however many more years this war demanded of him.

"Whatever it becomes," she said eventually, "it's going to scare people who deserve to be scared, and
it's going to protect four thousand people who are walking home tonight because you didn't move.
I'll take that trade, name or no name."

"So will I." He let the coat go, finally, into the fire crew's hands for whatever salvage could still
be made of it. "I just don't think either of us gets to decide, from here, what it ends up costing
before it's done."
