# What Hands That Couldn't Lift a Hammer Still Built

*Locked canon, Batch 262, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1315`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LXIX, wave
23, closing the wave. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241). A meritocracy
entry extended to a settlement's disabled and elderly population, distinct from every prior
merit-based-inclusion entry. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The settlement's headman apologized for it before Kanja had even finished walking the terrace —
nearly a third of the adults too old or too injured for forge work, a bad harbor accident two
winters back having taken hands and legs the campaign's usual offer of training couldn't do much
for. "We've got more people who want to help than we have work that fits what's left of them," he
said, as if it were a confession rather than an arithmetic problem.

Kanja had heard some version of this complaint from a dozen headmen across the tour, always
phrased like an apology for a shortfall that wasn't anyone's fault. He didn't answer it the way
he'd answered the others — by finding some corner of physical labor light enough to fit around an
injury. He answered it by asking, plainly, what each of the third actually knew how to do.

It turned out to be considerable. A woman who'd lost the use of one arm in the harbor accident had
kept every settlement's trade ledger from memory for a decade before that and could still do it
faster than anyone with two working hands and a slate. A man too old to swing a hammer had spent
forty years reading weather off the coastline before there was ever a forge to protect from it, and
his read on an incoming front proved more reliable than the tide charts the crew had been using.
Two boys who'd never walk without support turned out to have the patience for the terrace's most
exacting inventory work, counting Dead Drakma stock to a precision the busier apprentices kept
getting wrong.

"None of this fits the shape of what we usually call forge work," the headman said, watching the
ledger-keeper correct a supply tally three names above where he'd started, "but it's not nothing."

"It was never really about the hammer," Kanja said. "The hammer's just what happened to be the
first tool I knew well enough to teach anyone else. The actual rule underneath it is simpler — find
what someone's genuinely good at, and build a place for that, instead of deciding ahead of time
what the place needs to look like."

The terrace ran better within the week, not worse for the accommodation — supply counts tighter,
weather called further ahead, records straighter than any prior stop's — and the third of the
settlement that had come expecting an apology instead found itself, for the first time in two
winters, simply necessary.
