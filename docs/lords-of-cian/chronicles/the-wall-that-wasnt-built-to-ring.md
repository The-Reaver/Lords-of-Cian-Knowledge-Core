# The Wall That Wasn't Built to Ring

*Locked canon, Batch 135, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-453`). The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle VIII.
Rebellion era, age 25. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The Directorate engineer assigned to the border outpost had studied the Iron Bastard's every prior
engagement closely enough to identify the pattern underlying all of them: metal, tension-bearing
structure, something that could hold and release a resonant frequency once diagnosed correctly. His
solution was to build a fortification that had none of that at all — a packed-earth and loose-rubble
berm, reinforced with timber laid in deliberately irregular, non-load-bearing patterns, nothing in
its construction capable of carrying or releasing a resonance the way metal or taut cable could.

Kanja spent nearly an hour against the berm's outer face before he stopped trying.

Mafesto's sensors found nothing to diagnose — no tension architecture, no structural resonance
waiting to be identified, just inert packed earth that absorbed every attempt at a discharge the way
sand absorbed a blow, dissipating rather than transmitting. Obsidian Malice's charge, built for
exactly the kind of structural failure the doctrine specialized in creating, found no failure point
the berm's construction offered a foothold for.

"You built something the doctrine can't read," Kanja said finally, to the watching engineer on the
berm's far side, close enough now to be heard plainly. "That's a real answer. I don't have a
resonance for something that isn't holding tension in the first place."

"Then I assume you're retreating."

"No. I'm switching to the method every siege before this doctrine ever existed used against exactly
this kind of wall." He set Obsidian Malice aside, and the crew he'd held back specifically in case
the resonance approach failed began the slower, older work instead — sappers digging beneath the
berm's foundation, timber shoring set in the tunnel the way generations of siegecraft had done it
long before any Aegis-Talisman existed to make it faster. Six hours later, a section of the berm gave
way from below, undermined rather than resonated, and the outpost fell to the same patient method
that had taken fortifications apart for centuries.

"You beat something my doctrine genuinely couldn't touch," Kanja told the engineer afterward, without
false modesty about what had actually happened. "That's worth remembering. Not every wall rings. I
still need the old methods for the ones that don't."
