# Lauris Chronicle XXVII: What Brokenwall Was Never Told

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1644`). Twenty-seventh entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, a Strand D entry giving full-scene treatment to Operation 16, the Brokenwall mass
civilian density-anomaly outbreak (previously only summarized at `MCD-1538`): her twenty-two-hour
sustained precision-strike clearance of roughly two hundred simultaneously afflicted civilians, her
private discovery of the planted Ionic Rite-derived resonance node responsible, and her decision to
rebury rather than destroy it. Deliberately leaves the parallel, later Velaris outbreak (Operation
22, where she destroys the equivalent node outright) unnamed and undramatized, since Operation 22
falls outside this entry's 1-20 operation range and is reserved for separate treatment. No new named
characters.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Established Hunter designation LL-001-S-016: "Two hundred afflicted. None of
them chose this. I did not have the luxury of my usual climb toward a single opponent's peak. I had
two hundred separate peaks, all at once, and twenty-two hours to reach each one before it reached
someone who had not agreed to be part of this."*

Operation 16 differs from nearly everything that came before it in Lauris's Established Hunter
period in a single, decisive respect: it was not, in any sense the Directorate's usual contracts
recognized, a hunt. Brokenwall, a modest coastal city of no particular strategic weight, reported
roughly two hundred of its own residents simultaneously and inexplicably afflicted with density
anomalies within the span of a single night -- ordinary citizens, by every account, waking or
collapsing mid-task with capability and instability neither they nor anyone around them had any
framework to survive.

The Directorate's standing response to an outbreak of this scale called for containment measures
Lauris has described to me, without much softening, as a euphemism for a city's worth of
casualties. She proposed a different arithmetic, and the Directorate, by then accustomed to
granting her latitude no other contractor received, allowed it.

What followed was, by her own account, the most sustained single stretch of continuous engagement
of her Established Hunter years -- not the extended combat-progression climb that characterized her
usual contracts, where her own density rose across a single prolonged fight against a single
escalating opponent, but something closer to its inverse: two hundred separate, discrete
engagements, each afflicted individual approached, assessed, and either stabilized or, where
stabilization proved impossible, ended with as much precision and as little collateral harm as
twenty-two continuous hours of unbroken concentration could provide. She has told me she does not
consider Operation 16 among her more difficult contracts in the sense her later Branded-tier
engagements would come to define difficulty. She considers it, instead, among the most demanding in
a sense the Directorate's own metrics never learned to measure: the sustained, granular attention
two hundred separate lives required, each one distinct from the last, none of them permitted the
luxury of her full, escalating capability the way a single opponent would have been.

She preserved, by the close of those twenty-two hours, the large majority of Brokenwall's
unaffected population -- a figure her own report to the Directorate recorded plainly, and one the
Directorate, satisfied with the outcome, did not press further to understand.

*

What the Directorate's report does not contain, because Lauris did not put it there, is what she
found afterward, in the quiet that follows any operation once the visible crisis has ended and only
the question of cause remains. Traced back through the pattern of the afflictions -- their
distribution, their simultaneity, a coordination no natural anomaly cluster could plausibly produce
-- she located a small resonance node, Ionic Rite-derived in construction, hidden beneath the
paving stones of the city's central plaza. Deliberately planted. Deliberately activated, from
outside, to trigger precisely the outbreak she had just spent twenty-two hours containing.

She did not report it. She has explained her reasoning to me more than once, and I record it here
as faithfully as I am able: by Operation 16, she no longer trusted that the Directorate possessed
either the understanding or the discipline to be handed a device of this kind without, at minimum,
studying it in ways she considered likely to produce a second Brokenwall somewhere else, and at
worst, weaponizing it deliberately. She reburied the node rather than destroy it outright, a choice
she has since described to me as the more cautious of two imperfect options rather than the better
one -- she did not yet trust her own judgment enough, this early in the pattern's unfolding, to
commit an act of destruction against evidence she could not yet fully interpret. The Directorate
filed Brokenwall as Cause Unknown and moved on. It has never learned otherwise from her.

She has told me she does not know, to this day, whether the deliberate hand behind Brokenwall's
activation was Anu Un Ra's own apparatus reasserting some fragment of intentional control, or a
separate actor entirely, working with tools drawn from the same tradition for reasons of their own.
A second such outbreak would come, years later, in a different city -- but that day, and the choice
it would ask of her a second time, was still ahead of her when she rode out of Brokenwall having
told its citizens, and no one else, the truth of what had nearly killed two hundred of them.

Her closing note: *I saved the city and lied to the institution that sent me to save it, by
omission rather than by word, which I have never found to be a meaningfully different thing. I
would make the same choice again. I have made it again since.*
