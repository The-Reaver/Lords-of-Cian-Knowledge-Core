# The Engineer Who Stayed

*Locked canon, Batch 257, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1190`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle LXXIX, wave 27, opening the wave. A long-term-integration payoff to "The Engineer Who Built Against Him" (MCD-668) -- the defected Trust engineer's standing is tested by his former colleagues.*

---

The Trust fortification engineer who'd defected after recognizing Kanja's own craftsmanship in the traps built against him had been with the crew eight months now, embedded deep enough that most workers had stopped thinking of him as anything but another engineer. His old colleagues hadn't forgotten, and a delegation from his former command found the tunnels through channels that shouldn't have existed, carrying an offer that was really a threat dressed as mercy -- amnesty, reinstatement, forgiveness for a lapse in judgment, if he walked back out with them today.

"They're not here for me," the engineer told Kanja plainly, before the delegation even reached the gallery. "They're here to see if you'll let them take me, or if I've actually become something you'd fight to keep."

It wasn't an idle test. Kanja had backed plenty of people's standing before -- a stonemason against her own guild's prejudice, an honest reheat against a skeptical master -- but none of those had come with an armed delegation prepared to escalate if the answer displeased them. He met the delegation himself, without theater, and let the engineer speak for his own choice rather than speaking for him.

"I didn't defect because I lost a fight," the engineer told his former commander directly. "I defected because the work here doesn't ask me to build traps for people who never did anything but try to survive under siege. That's not a lapse. That's the first honest professional judgment I've made in years."

The commander pressed, reminding him what he was walking away from -- rank, resources, a reputation he'd spent a career building. The engineer didn't flinch, and Kanja didn't step in to answer for him, standing close enough to make his own position unmistakable without ever taking the argument out of the engineer's hands.

The delegation left without him. Kanja waited until they'd cleared the gallery before he spoke.

"You didn't need me to fight for you back there."

"No," the engineer said, some of the tension only now leaving his shoulders. "I needed you to trust I could fight for myself, and just be standing close enough that they understood what happened next if they didn't take no for an answer."

"That's a fair division of labor," Kanja said. "I can live with that one."
