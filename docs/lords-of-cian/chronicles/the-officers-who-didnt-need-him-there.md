# The Officers Who Didn't Need Him There

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Captain Alias Chronicle, wave 11. Rebellion era. Not
a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters beyond
already-locked crew.*

---

Kanja spent the better part of a year, alongside every other demand on his time, deliberately
training a rotating handful of the crew's most trusted hands — Corren Halst, Danne Sok, and two
others without a Trinity function or any density advantage to lean on — in the specific work of
commanding an engagement without him present at all.

"You already know tactics," Corren Halst said, midway through the training, half a complaint. "Why
does it need to be formal? We've been reading your calls for years."

"Reading my calls isn't the same as making your own with the same confidence I'd have making them.
I want a version of this crew's judgment that doesn't need to check with me first, because the day's
coming when I genuinely can't be everywhere this crew needs to be, and I'd rather that day find you
ready than find you frozen waiting on an order I can't give in time."

The test came sooner than expected — a coastal raid that split the crew across two fronts, Kanja
committed to one engagement while the other, smaller and less urgent-seeming at the outset, escalated
faster than anyone predicted. Corren Halst took command of the second front without waiting for
instructions, made three calls in under ten minutes that matched, almost exactly, what Kanja later
said he'd have ordered himself, and got every person under him out clean.

"You didn't need me at all," Kanja said afterward, genuine satisfaction in it rather than anything
resembling being upstaged.

"We needed you to teach us how not to need you, for exactly this kind of day. That's different."
Corren Halst didn't downplay it, but didn't overstate it either — the plain confidence of someone
who'd earned a thing and knew it. "Doesn't mean we don't still want you here most days. Just means
this crew doesn't fall apart the one day you can't be."

"That's the whole point. A crew that only works with me standing over it isn't a crew. It's an
extension of me, and I don't want that. I want people who can carry this the way I've tried to
carry it — because they choose to, not because there's no other option."
