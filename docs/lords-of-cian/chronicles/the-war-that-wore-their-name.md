# The War That Wore Their Name

*Locked canon, Batch 269, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1405`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
XCIII, wave 31, closing the wave. The fleet's reputation is invoked as propaganda by both sides of a
distant conflict the fleet has no part in; Kanja travels to publicly refuse both endorsements. Not a
territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Word reached the fleet the way word always did — through Hask's own network of correspondents, traders,
and freed captives who kept writing back long after they'd been let go. This word was stranger than
most: a civil conflict three regions removed from any water the fleet had ever sailed, between a
sitting island council and a breakaway faction contesting it, and both sides, independently, claiming
the Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea's blessing.

The council's pamphlets said the ghost fleet had quietly supplied them for their lawful defense of
order. The breakaway faction's own criers said the opposite — that the ghost fleet's whole doctrine of
freeing the chained and answering only to conscience stood behind their rebellion against an unjust
council. Neither claim had a shred of truth in it. Neither side had ever exchanged a single word with
anyone aboard The Audit, The Receipt, or The Ledger.

"They're not lying to hurt us," Efa Gol said, reading the third and most recent pamphlet aloud on The
Ledger's deck. "They're lying because the name's become useful enough to steal. That might be worse."

Kanja crossed three regions of open water he had never sailed before, arriving not with the Trinity
raised or a fleet behind him, but alone, unannounced, to stand in the island council's own public
square where both pamphlets had circulated freely for months.

"I want to be plain, because I understand both of you have built something on my name that isn't
true," he said, to a crowd that had gathered within the hour of word spreading that the ghost himself
had actually come. "I have never supplied your council. I have never blessed your rebellion. I don't
know your grievances well enough to judge which of you is right, and I won't pretend otherwise just
because it would be convenient for either of you to hear it."

"Then why come at all?" the council's own representative asked, plainly unsettled that the endorsement
she'd relied on for months was being pulled out from under her in public.

"Because a name people trust is worth something real, and letting it get spent on a fight I know
nothing about cheapens it for everyone who actually earned that trust honestly," Kanja said. "If you
want my name in this, earn it the way everyone else has — show me who's actually been chained and who's
actually done the chaining. Until then, it isn't yours to use, either of you."

He left the same day, without taking a side, without resolving the conflict itself — that was never his
to resolve — and without a single new pamphlet printed under his name in the weeks that followed. Garren
Hask logged it not as a victory or a rescue, but as its own category: the first time the reputation had
to be publicly reclaimed rather than built, tested, or spent.
