# Before the Marsh

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Crow King Alias Chronicle XXXI, wave 11, first
entry. Rebellion era, a flashback set before the Night of the Crow King (`MCD-236`, age 23),
recounted years later among early crew already locked in canon (Corren Halst, Danne Sok, Maret Vos,
all present since the Battle of the Black Trench per `MCD-234`). Not a territory Chronicle.
Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Long before three hundred fighters vanished out of the Voskharen Wetlands and a coat stuffed with
marsh grass earned a name that would follow him for the rest of the war, the first attempt at
anything resembling the trick had involved four people, a single false campfire, and a failure
embarrassing enough that none of them spoke of it again until years afterward, once the Crow King's
reputation had grown large enough to make the memory funny instead of merely humiliating.

"You want to tell it, or should I?" Danne Sok asked, years later, the four of them — Kanja included
— sharing a fire considerably less improvised than the one the story concerned.

"You tell it. You were the one who nearly gave the whole thing away laughing."

The plan had been simple enough in theory: a false camp, voices pitched to suggest more people than
were actually present, meant to draw a small patrol away from a real position two ridgelines over.
What none of them had accounted for was how badly four untrained voices, none of them holding a
note steady for more than a few seconds at a time, actually sounded when strung together — closer to
an argument than a camp, and Danne Sok's own attempt to hold a low register had cracked so
completely that Corren Halst had needed to bite down on his own sleeve to keep from laughing loud
enough to blow the whole operation regardless of how it sounded.

"The patrol walked right past anyway," Maret Vos said, still amused by it after all these years.
"Not because we fooled them. I think they just assumed anyone that bad at hiding wasn't worth
investigating."

"That's not entirely wrong," Kanja admitted, no defensiveness in it at all. "It took the Wetlands,
and a great deal more preparation than four people arguing badly around a fire, before the idea
actually became anything worth a name. I don't think it's an accident that the version that worked
came after failing badly enough, early enough, that I actually had to think through what was wrong
with it instead of getting lucky a second time."

"So we get credit for the failure that built the thing everyone else remembers," Corren Halst said,
satisfied with that arrangement.

"You get exactly that much credit. I wouldn't take it away from any of you."
