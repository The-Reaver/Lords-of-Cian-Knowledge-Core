# The Rotation Corren Halst Finally Took

*Locked canon, Batch 279, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1459`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle CII, wave 34, closing the wave. A graceful institutional handoff extended to one of Kanja's own founding crew for the first time, mirroring his own declined permanent council seat.*

---

The leg had been giving her trouble since long before anyone else noticed, and by the time Corren Halst finally said something about it, she'd already worked out exactly how she wanted the conversation to go.

"It's not the fighting that's the problem," she told Kanja, testing her weight against the old dock injury that flared worse every winter now. "It's the ladders. Front-line tunnel work means ladders, and ladders mean a leg that doesn't always answer when I need it to."

"How long have you been managing around it instead of telling anyone?"

"Longer than I should have. I kept telling myself it wasn't bad enough yet to matter." She held his gaze rather than looking away from the admission. "It's bad enough now. I'm not asking you to fix it. I'm telling you because pretending otherwise gets someone hurt on a rotation I shouldn't still be running."

It would have been easy, and not without precedent elsewhere in the crew's history, to treat this as a problem requiring reassurance -- to insist she could still manage, to quietly redesign rotations around her rather than name the change plainly. Kanja didn't reach for that instinct.

"Then you're off front-line rotation," he said. "Not out of the crew. Off the ladders."

"That's the easy half of the sentence. What's the rest of it?"

"The rest is that you've spent this entire war reading a crisis faster than almost anyone I've ever worked beside, and that doesn't stop mattering just because your leg won't take a ladder anymore." Kanja considered the training hall down the corridor, still bearing the marks of the safety-certification classes he'd started years before. "Training. Every new recruit who comes through that hall learns to read a tunnel from you before they ever touch a tool. Nobody on this crew reads faster or teaches straighter."

Corren Halst was quiet for a moment, turning the offer over with the same care she gave any structural read.

"You turned down a permanent seat on the workers' council," she said finally. "Kept only the advisory chair, so the authority wouldn't sit with you by default just because you'd earned it once."

"I remember."

"This is the same shape, isn't it. You're not retiring me. You're moving the thing I'm actually best at somewhere my leg can't disqualify me from it."

"That's exactly what it is." Kanja held out a hand, and she took it, the handshake settling something neither of them needed to say aloud. "I've had eight decades of people trying to make me the exception to every rule this crew runs on. You don't get to be the exception either, not even the kind that quietly steps aside before anyone asks her to. You're too good at this to let a ladder decide where your knowledge stops mattering."

Word of the new arrangement reached Danne Sok by the following morning, and she found Corren Halst already at the training hall, a fresh line of recruits waiting for their first lesson.

"How does the chair feel?" Danne Sok asked.

"Feels like the same work I've always done," Corren Halst said, "just finally admitted out loud as the actual work, instead of something I was quietly doing around the edges of a job my leg was never going to let me keep pretending I still had."
