# The Record They Tried to Rewrite

*Locked canon, Batch 141, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-466`). Ide Chronicle II. Homage-era Chicago, Ide/Ase.
Territory Chronicle -- Ase as protagonist, Kanja an unnamed background guest with no command,
credit, or resolution authorship. New standalone material. No new named characters.*

---

Years after the granary-theft execution, a newly appointed successor to the old Magistrate's post
commissioned an official counter-account — a version of the events claiming the executed man had
confessed privately before his death, a detail invented wholesale to retroactively justify a
conviction Ase's own documentation had already made permanently, publicly indefensible.

"They can't erase what you already copied out and carried across the city," a fellow archivist told
her, the counter-account already circulating through official channels. "But they can try to make
people doubt which version is the real one."

"Then we do exactly what we did the first time. We don't argue the story. We show the account." Ase
gathered the same materials that had made the original documentation impossible to dismiss — the
witness list, cross-referenced testimonies collected independently across multiple routes out of the
city, the Magistrate's own recorded words from the day of the execution, none of it altered in the
years since because none of it had ever needed to be. "A fabricated account has one version of events
that has to hold together perfectly. A true one has dozens of independent witnesses who were never
coordinated with each other, and their accounts still agree on every detail that matters. That
difference is the whole case. It doesn't need me to argue it. It just needs people to actually look."

She spent the following weeks doing exactly that — not countering the fabrication with rhetoric, but
placing her original documentation and the new counter-account side by side in every venue that would
host both, letting the structural difference between a coordinated lie and independently corroborated
truth speak for itself to anyone willing to examine both closely.

The counter-account collapsed within the season, not because Ase had out-argued it, but because it
simply couldn't survive the same scrutiny her original account had already passed years before.

"You never raised your voice once through any of this," the fellow archivist observed, watching the
fabrication quietly discredit itself. "I kept expecting you to fight harder for it."

"I fought as hard as the method demanded. The method was never about volume. It's about making an
account permanent and independently verifiable enough that fighting for it later is barely
necessary — the documentation itself does the fighting, if it was built honestly the first time."
Ase closed the file, the original record intact and now doubly proven. "That's the whole reason I
build them the way I do."

An unnamed man who had once stood beside her at the execution itself, years before, read of the
counter-account's collapse from a distance and said nothing to anyone about having once been there.
