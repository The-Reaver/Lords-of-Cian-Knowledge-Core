# The Fever That Didn't Care About Flags

*Locked canon, Batch 258, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1202`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
LXIV, wave 22, first entry in the wave. An internal medical crisis aboard the fleet's own ship, no
enemy or rescue involved. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The sickness started with one man on *The Receipt* complaining of a headache he blamed on bad water,
and within four days it had put nineteen more on their backs with the same fever, the same rattling
cough, and no clear source anyone could point to. Efa Gol, who had commanded decoys and diversions
against Trust patrols for a decade without losing her nerve, found this a different kind of problem:
an enemy that didn't move, didn't retreat, and couldn't be out-maneuvered by reading a man's stance.

She quarantined *The Receipt* at anchor in a sheltered cove rather than risk carrying the fever to
the other two flagships, over the loud objection of a crew that had never in years of raids and
rescues been ordered to stay put and do nothing. Pell Ostra, whose gift for materials had never been
asked to work on bodies before, spent two days going through every crate of trade goods taken aboard
in the last month looking for the source, and found it: a shipment of grain the fleet had accepted
from a coastal town as partial payment for an earlier rescue, stored damp and gone to rot in the
hold's lowest tier.

Kanja came aboard once the source was confirmed and did the one thing his own crew hadn't expected —
he stayed. Not to fight anything, not to carry anyone through fire or water, just to sit with the sick
in shifts alongside Efa Gol while Pell Ostra burned the spoiled grain and Garren Hask logged every
name that fell ill and every name that recovered, the same as he logged everything else the fleet did.

"You don't need to be here for this," Efa Gol told him on the third night, watching him hold a cup of
water to a shaking man's mouth. "There's no enemy to read, no line to hold. It's just waiting."

"Then I'll wait," Kanja said. "The doctrine doesn't stop being the doctrine because the threat isn't
wearing a flag."

Three men died before the fever broke — the first losses this fleet had taken to something neither
Trust nor rival pirate had caused. Efa Gol logged their names herself, in her own hand, in a section
of Garren Hask's ledger that had never before needed to exist: not disabled ships, not freed
captives, not repaid debts, but simply the dead the sea and the fleet's own hold had taken between
them.
