# Lauris Chronicle LXII: The Stance Veska Kept Rebuilding

*Locked canon, Batch 300, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1679`). Sixty-second entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, the fifteenth entry of Strand K, drafted as part of a second 50-entry wave.
Dramatizes a training session at age ~400, within the age-200-600 window already locked at `MCD-170`
for the kinetic-anchor stance behind the Phalanx's Triad-Lock configuration, trained under Veska.
Uses "Triad-Lock" as a period-native Karth-Ven stance name, matching the precedent already
established by Chronicle XV (`MCD-1632`, "positioned herself in Triad-Lock -- the kinetic-anchor
stance her instructors at Karth-Ven had spent her first four centuries there building into her,
under Veska's own direct tutelage"). Deliberately does not use the name "Karth-Sera," does not
depict the curriculum as a named, unified system, and does not depict Spine of Dagon, the
Aristocrat, or Attia's Rite, consistent with `MCD-176`'s locked fact that those weapon-names bind to
their disciplines only later, on Cian. No new named characters. Full text below.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Vask Karth-Ven, age approximately four hundred: "Veska rebuilt the stance again
today. Fourth time this decade. She says it is not that I am doing it wrong. She says I keep
outgrowing what was built for me before I finish learning it."*

I have described already the deep-mountain training floor Veska Karth-Ven presided over, its sixty
meters of high-gravity-forged Drakma standing in continuous calibration for twenty-eight thousand
years before Lauris ever set foot on it, and I described it, at the time, as an instrument slowly
learning what a body was. I return to that floor now for an entry that concerns a single stance --
the one Chronicle XV would later name Triad-Lock, when I set down the day Lauris held a mountain's
own weight in argument with itself for three continuous hours. That entry did not explain where the
stance came from. This one does.

Veska built it, by her own account, as a kinetic-anchor discipline meant to solve a problem specific
to Lauris rather than to any prior Karth-Ven student: a body that absorbed force without needing to
discharge it through motion, the way every combatant before her had been trained to. Cooperative-era
doctrine assumed a defender redirected an incoming force somewhere -- into the ground, into a
counter-strike, into retreat. Lauris's own physiology, Veska recorded early in the process, did none
of these reliably, because her rising density meant the calculations that told a stance where force
should go were wrong again within a matter of months. What Veska built instead was not a fixed
position but a method for rebuilding the position, over and over, against a body that refused to
stay still long enough for any single version of it to become permanent.

*

I have the training log for the session the fragment above records, and it is, I think, the clearest
illustration I have found of what that rebuilding actually looked like from the inside. Voreth, by
this point some three hundred eighty years into her own tenure as Lauris's primary sparring partner,
opened the session running the stance exactly as it had stood the prior season -- weight distributed
across a four-point base, force redirected downward through the rear leg rather than absorbed
through the torso. Within the first quarter hour, Veska halted the session. Lauris's density had
climbed enough, in the interval since the stance was last calibrated, that the rear-leg redirection
was no longer moving enough of the incoming force fast enough; a fraction of it was beginning to
reach her upper spine instead, a margin so small Voreth had not noticed delivering it and Lauris had
not noticed absorbing it.

Veska's own note on the correction is one I have returned to more than once, because I think it
captures something true about how she understood her own role in Lauris's training that Karesian
formality rarely permitted her to say outright: *I am not training a stance. I am training a method
for building a stance faster than she can outgrow it. I do not expect to finish. I have stopped
expecting to finish. I have started, instead, to find the not-finishing satisfying in its own right,
which I did not anticipate when I agreed to take her on.*

*

Voreth's own entry for the same day, briefer, closes on a note I want to set down because it marks,
I think, the point at which the "year it took Voreth to stop expecting one of them to be lying" that
I recorded in an earlier account had fully resolved into something steadier: *Veska rebuilt the
stance around me today, not just around Lauris. I used to think my job was to test whether the
number in her file was true. I understand now that my job is to keep testing it, indefinitely,
because it will not stop becoming more true, and someone has to be standing close enough to notice
exactly when.*

Lauris's own closing line for the day, shorter than either instructor's, is the one I have chosen to
end this account with, because I believe it says, in five words, everything the two of them had
spent a decade working out at greater length: *The stance is not finished. Neither am I.*
