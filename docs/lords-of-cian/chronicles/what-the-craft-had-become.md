# What the Craft Had Become

Kanja found the apprentice alone at the rally point's edge the following morning, her voice still
carrying the low rasp six hours of sustained work always left behind, watching the last of the crew
break camp for the various roads that would carry them back to their own provinces.

"You could have run any of last night's operations yourself," he said, settling beside her rather
than standing over the moment. "You chose the reporting-network one specifically. Why that one?"

"It's the first thing you ever showed me could work with almost nothing behind it. No Braid, no
crew of singers, just careful listening and one well-placed lie in the right channel." She considered
the question longer before finishing the answer. "I wanted to run the version closest to where I
actually started, on the night we finally did something at the scale you always said the craft
could reach. Felt like it mattered, closing that particular circle myself."

"Does it feel finished? What you've built out of what I gave you?"

"No. I don't think it's supposed to feel finished. My student's already changing things I taught her
the same way I changed things you taught me. Hers will change it again, someday, further from either
of us than either of us are now from you." She said it without any sadness in it, only a kind of
settled clarity. "I used to think the goal was becoming as good as you were. I don't think that was
ever actually the goal. I think the goal was always this — more hands carrying it than just yours,
doing things with it you never would have thought to try, none of it needing you standing over any
of our shoulders to work."

"That was always the goal," Kanja agreed, watching the same dispersing crew she was watching. "I
didn't know, the night I first stuffed a coat with marsh grass, that it would ever reach five
provinces moving at once, five years and two more generations past anything I built alone. I'm not
sure I need to know exactly where it goes from here. I think that's rather the point of handing
something to someone who'll actually carry it further than you could."
