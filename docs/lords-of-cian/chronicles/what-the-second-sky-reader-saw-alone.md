# What the Second Sky-Reader Saw Alone

*Locked canon, Batch 190, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-557`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XVIII,
closing the fifth wave. Rebellion era, age 29, the Gale Straits. Not a territory Chronicle. New
standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters beyond the
already-locked Sephtis and his successor.*

---

Sephtis's student, months into her training, made her first fully independent prediction without his
review — a decision she made alone, on a night he was elsewhere, when a fast-moving squall threatened
a supply convoy with no time to reach him for confirmation first.

She called the warning herself. The convoy altered course in time, the squall arriving exactly where
and when she'd predicted it would.

"You didn't wait for me," Sephtis said afterward, more pride than reproach in it, having heard the
full account from the convoy's own grateful captain before he'd even found her to discuss it. "I
taught you to always bring predictions to me for confirmation until I judged you ready. You didn't
ask whether I judged you ready."

"There wasn't time to ask. The squall wasn't going to wait for my confidence to catch up with my
training." She met his observation without apology, genuine conviction in the choice she'd made. "I
had to decide, in that moment, whether I trusted what I'd learned enough to act on it alone. I did.
I don't know that I'd have made the same call a month ago. I think I would have frozen, waiting for
permission that wasn't going to arrive in time."

"That's exactly the judgment I was waiting to see, whether or not I ever got the chance to formally
confirm it." Sephtis felt something settle in him that had been quietly uncertain since he'd first
begun teaching her — the doctrine's own redundancy, built specifically because one person's gift
should never again be the fleet's only defense, finally proven real rather than merely intended.
"I wasn't training you to wait for my permission forever. I was training you to eventually not need
it. Tonight was that, whether either of us planned for it to happen exactly this way."

"Does that mean I'm finished training?"

"No. It means you're finally ready to keep training toward something beyond simply matching what I
already know how to do." Sephtis watched her with genuine respect, a teacher recognizing a student who
had already begun to outgrow him. "The fleet has two sky-readers now, not one backup waiting for the
first to fail. That's worth more than either of us fully understood when this training started."
