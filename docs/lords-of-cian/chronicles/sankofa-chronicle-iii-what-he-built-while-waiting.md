# Sankofa Chronicle III: What He Built While Waiting

*Locked canon, Batch 171, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-518`). Homage-era Los Angeles, Sankofa/Baálé. Territory
Chronicle — Baálé as protagonist, Kanja an unnamed background guest with no command, credit, or
resolution authorship. New standalone material. The forged-letter conspiracy from Chronicle II
(`MCD-360`) remains deliberately untouched and unresolved in this entry, per standing instruction.
No new named characters.*

---

The second unsigned letter had said nothing further in the weeks since it was found, no third message
arriving to clarify whoever had sent it or what they actually wanted — an unresolved threat Baálé had
learned to carry without letting it consume every waking decision. Kra, still his closest confidant
since the two of them had survived what neither fully spoke of aloud, watched him closely for signs
the waiting was wearing him down.

It wasn't, or if it was, he refused to let it show in his work.

"You could put everything else aside until we know more," Kra said, watching him instead pour real
energy into a new community health clinic — free screenings, basic care, the kind of institutional
building that had nothing to do with the unresolved threat hanging over both their heads. "Nobody
would think less of you for focusing on the letters until they resolve one way or another."

"Waiting for an answer that might never come was never going to be a plan. I refuse to let whoever
sent those letters decide what I do with my days in the meantime." Baálé reviewed the clinic's opening
roster, real and immediate work that mattered regardless of what the conspiracy eventually turned out
to be. "Kojo watches the district's edges now, same as he's watched them since the day he was bound to
serve rather than kill me. If something comes, I trust we'll know it when it does. Until then, I'd
rather spend this uncertainty building something real than spend it only bracing for a blow that may
never land."

The clinic opened within the month, its first patients treated the same week — ordinary, unglamorous
work continuing exactly as it always had, the unresolved letters neither forgotten nor allowed to
dictate the shape of Baálé's days.

"You've never once let the not-knowing stop you," Kra observed, watching the clinic's first line of
patients form. "I don't know that I could do the same, in your position."

"Some days it's harder than others. Most days, building something real is the only answer I actually
have to an uncertainty I can't otherwise resolve." Baálé watched the clinic doors open for the first
time. "I'd rather be caught doing something worth doing than caught doing nothing at all, waiting on a
letter that may never come again."

An unnamed man near the clinic's opening, present without comment, left once the first patients had
been seen.
