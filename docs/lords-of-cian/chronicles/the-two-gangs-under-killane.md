# The Two Gangs Under Killane

*Locked canon, Batch 131, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-441`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle VIII.
Rebellion era, age 20, the Sewer War of Killane. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material.
Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Two tunnel crews had worked opposite ends of the same drainage network for longer than either could
remember exactly why, their rivalry old enough that nobody currently working either crew could name
its original cause with any confidence — only that the east-gallery crew and the west-gallery crew
didn't share tools, didn't share information about collapsing sections, and had twice nearly gotten
workers killed by refusing to coordinate on shared structural supports during the war's opening
weeks.

"You want us to what, exactly?" the east crew's senior hand asked, arms crossed, plainly unconvinced.
"Just decide sixty years of bad blood doesn't matter because there's a war on?"

"I want you to decide whether sixty years of bad blood is worth more to you than the workers on both
your crews who could die because two galleries stopped sharing information about which sections are
about to come down." Kanja had spent the better part of a week in both galleries before calling this
meeting, learning both crews' methods well enough that neither could dismiss him as an outsider who
didn't understand the work. "I'm not asking you to like each other. I'm asking you to trade structural
reports twice a shift, the same way you'd trade them with your own crew, because the ground doesn't
care which gallery you're loyal to when it decides to give way."

"And if the west crew can't be trusted with what we tell them?"

"Then test it. Small information first — nothing that costs you if it's mishandled. If it holds, trade
more. If it doesn't, you'll have lost nothing but a week, and you'll have your answer about whether the
old grudge was ever really about safety or just about pride." Kanja met the man's skepticism without
backing down from it. "I've watched what happens when crews refuse to share what they know about the
ground under everyone's feet. I don't intend to watch it happen again if there's a way to stop it."

The trial ran two weeks before either crew fully trusted it, structural reports exchanged first
through Kanja himself as an intermediary before the two galleries began trading directly. The
sixty-year grudge didn't dissolve — neither crew pretended otherwise — but no worker from either
gallery died to a collapse the other side had already known about and failed to mention, for the
remainder of the war.
