# What Danne Sok Charted

---

Nobody outside the crew ever heard Danne Sok's name in connection with the ghost fleet's elusiveness,
which suited him precisely, since the work he did happened entirely below deck, hunched over charts
that never left *The Audit*'s hold and never would.

"Every route we've run for the past four months," he told Kanja, spreading the latest revision across
the table, "and every route the Trust's patrols have logged trying to catch us running them.
Overlapped." He traced a finger along a pattern only he could fully see in it. "We've been getting
lucky on instinct. I want us getting smart on pattern instead."

The work was slow and entirely unglamorous — cross-referencing months of patrol sightings, tide
tables, the specific hours different Trust commands changed shift, building a living document of which
waters were genuinely safe to cross at which hours and which only looked safe because nobody had been
caught there yet.

"You're mapping their blind spots," Kanja said, watching him work.

"I'm mapping their *habits*. Blind spots close the first time someone gets caught in one. Habits take
years to break, because breaking them means admitting the old pattern failed." Danne Sok didn't look
up from the chart. "The Trust rotates its Kothrane patrol every eleven days, always at dawn, always the
same three-hour gap while the relief crew gets briefed. That gap's been open for four months. Nobody's
closed it because nobody's had to yet."

He'd been doing this quietly since before the crew fully trusted each other, the same patient
attention that had once helped hold the line at the Black Trench now turned toward paper and ink
instead of a blade — every safe crossing the fleet had made in months owing something to hours he'd
spent alone with charts nobody else thought to keep.

"This should have a name," Kanja said eventually, looking over the accumulated months of work. "Your
name, specifically. You built this."

"It doesn't need my name on it. It needs to keep working." Danne Sok rolled the latest chart and set
it with the others, already thinking about the next gap in the pattern he hadn't finished mapping. "The
whole point of a good route is that nobody who's using it stops to wonder who found it. They just get
where they're going."

He went back to the tide tables without waiting for an answer, and the fleet crossed the Kothrane gap
four more times before the Trust ever closed it.
