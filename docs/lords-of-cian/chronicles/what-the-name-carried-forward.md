# What the Name Carried Forward

*Locked canon, Batch 161, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-508`). Captain Alias Chronicle XV, closing the fourth
wave — and closing the fourth wave for all eleven aliases. Rebellion era. Not a territory Chronicle.
New standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Garren Hask's own grandnephew, born generations into the crew's own extended family the way this
world's long lifespans allowed old hands to watch entire lines grow up around them, joined the crew
formally on his coming of age — too young to have witnessed any of the founding years firsthand,
raised instead entirely on the accumulated culture those years had left behind.

"Everyone talks about 'Captain' like it's just a name," the young man said, still working out which
parts of the crew's own culture were formal history and which were simply how things had always been
done in his own lifetime. "Uncle Garren talks about it like it's something he helped build. Is that
actually true, or is it just an old man's way of telling the story?"

"It's true. Your uncle named it, or near enough — the first one who ever called me that instead of
whatever the Directorate happened to be calling me at the time." Kanja considered how much of the
weight behind the name the young man was actually ready to hear. "It wasn't a title anyone gave me
from above. It was a name this crew built, one relationship and one hard day at a time, long before
either of us existed to inherit it."

"What am I supposed to do with that? I didn't build any of it. I just showed up into something
already finished."

"Nothing's ever finished, not really. What your uncle and the ones before him built isn't a monument
you inherit and leave untouched. It's a living thing you're now part of maintaining, the same way
every generation before you has." Kanja set a hand briefly on the young man's shoulder, the same
gesture he'd offered new hands for decades. "You don't have to have built it to be responsible for
what it becomes next. That's true of every name this crew has ever carried, and it'll be true of
whatever comes after this one too, eventually."

The young man carried the name forward the same uncertain way every new hand before him had, learning
slowly what it actually meant rather than what the stories alone could teach him — one more link in a
chain that had already outlasted the war it was born in, and showed no sign of stopping there.
