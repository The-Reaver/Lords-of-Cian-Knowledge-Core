# Ide Chronicle III: The Name She Almost Left Out

*Locked canon, Batch 166, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-513`). Homage-era Chicago, Ide/Ase. Territory
Chronicle — Ase as protagonist, Kanja an unnamed background guest with no command, credit, or
resolution authorship. New standalone material. No new named characters.*

---

The account came together the same patient way every prior one had — witnesses interviewed
separately, testimony cross-checked, nothing entered into the permanent record until it had survived
her own rigorous doubt. This time, the wrongdoing belonged to one of her own closest allies, a
grain-distribution organizer whose documented theft from the very relief effort he'd helped build
threatened to hand Ide's opposition a weapon neither Ase nor the movement could easily absorb.

She sat with the finished account for two full days before deciding whether to file it.

"You could leave this one out," a fellow archivist said, watching her weigh a decision that had never
once troubled her when the wrongdoer belonged to the other side. "Nobody outside this room would ever
know the account existed. It costs the movement nothing to simply not write it down."

"It costs the movement its own credibility, eventually, the moment anyone discovers I kept two
different standards — rigorous for enemies, forgiving for friends." Ase set the finished account
beside every other she'd ever filed, no different in format, no different in rigor. "The whole reason
'Named and Numbered' means anything is that it doesn't flinch depending on who the account is about.
The moment it does, it stops being a record people can trust and becomes just another weapon aimed at
whoever's currently inconvenient."

"This will hurt us. Badly, probably."

"I know. I've sat with that for two days, and I still don't have an answer that makes it hurt less."
She filed the account exactly as thoroughly documented as any other, the organizer's theft laid out in
full alongside the same corroborating detail every prior entry carried. "But an account that only
tells the truth about people I don't like isn't actually truth. It's just another kind of lie, dressed
up as documentation. I'd rather the movement survive this cost honestly than survive it by becoming
something I can no longer stand behind."

The account's release cost the movement exactly as much as she'd feared, in the short term — but it
also, in the months that followed, became the clearest evidence anyone could offer that Ase's own
record had never once been political, only true.

An unnamed man, present in the archive room during both days of her deliberation and saying nothing
throughout, was gone by the time the account was finally filed.
