# Areíto Chronicle III: The Two Houses He Brought to One Table

*Locked canon, Batch 162, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-509`). Homage-era NYC, Areíto/Kwame Ade. Territory
Chronicle — Kwame Ade as protagonist, Kanja an unnamed background guest with no command, credit, or
resolution authorship. New standalone material. No new named characters.*

---

Two of Areíto's own tenant associations, split for a decade over a dispute neither side could
precisely remember the origin of, had refused to coordinate on anything — not building maintenance,
not shared security, not the winter relief efforts that both, separately and less effectively, tried
to run each year. Kwame Ade, newly returned to public organizing after the barbershop and Adeyemi
both, chose the split as his first re-entry into the kind of patient work that had defined him before
either confrontation.

"You're asking us to sit at the same table as people who've spent ten years refusing to speak to us,"
one association's own elder said, deeply skeptical. "What makes you think that changes now?"

"I'm not asking you to forget ten years of grievance in one meeting. I'm asking you to test whether
the grievance is actually about anything real anymore, or whether it's outlived whatever started it."
Kwame Ade had spent weeks with both associations separately before ever proposing a joint session,
learning each side's account in full before asking either to hear the other's. "If it's real, we'll
find that out together, honestly. If it isn't, I'd rather this district stop losing a decade of
shared strength to a grudge nobody can even clearly explain anymore."

The joint session ran four hours, tense for most of it, before the two accounts of the original
dispute — recounted plainly, without the years of accumulated resentment coloring them — turned out
to differ only slightly from each other, a decade-old miscommunication rather than any genuine,
irreconcilable harm.

"Ten years," the elder said afterward, something between relief and quiet embarrassment in it. "Ten
years over a misunderstanding neither of us bothered checking against the other's account."

"That happens more than anyone likes to admit. Grudges outlive their own causes all the time, once
nobody's willing to be the first to ask honestly what actually happened." Kwame Ade watched the two
associations begin, tentatively, planning the district's next winter relief effort together for the
first time in a decade. "I didn't fix anything today that wasn't already fixable. I just made it
easier for both of you to be the first ones to ask."

An unnamed man near the meeting's edge, present throughout without speaking, was gone by the time the
joint planning session concluded.
