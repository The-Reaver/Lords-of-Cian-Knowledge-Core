*Archive fragment, present-day: "Two hundred, still. I count them the same way every time, not
because the number changes, but because the counting is the part of the debt I can actually pay
right now."*

She goes alone, and she goes without telling Ezio the specific date, only that it will happen
again sometime within the season — an arrangement they settled on years ago, she has told me,
because a debt this old does not need a witness for every payment, only a reliable confirmation
that the payments continue. I was permitted to accompany her only as far as the outer approach this
time, and I record what follows largely from what she chose to describe afterward rather than from
having seen it myself, which I think is, in its own way, appropriate — this is not an account meant
to be widely witnessed, and I am aware that setting it down at all is already more exposure than
the debt strictly requires.

The cave system sits roughly fourteen kilometers from where Site K-Theta itself once stood,
concealed by a redundancy of methods she has never fully catalogued for me — natural rock geology
doing most of the honest work, she says, with her own periodic maintenance doing only what geology
cannot. Two hundred subjects, relocated here in the single action she took, by her own account,
entirely outside Directorate authority for the first time in her career — the hinge point, though
she has never framed it to me quite that dramatically herself, between the contractor she had been
and the woman who would eventually walk away from the Directorate altogether.

What a maintenance visit consists of, in practice, is less dramatic than the stakes attached to it
might suggest. She checks the concealment's physical integrity. She confirms, through means she
described to me only in the vaguest terms, that nothing has disturbed the population's own
stability in whatever quiet, suspended state has kept them safe across the decades since. She
counts. Not by name — she does not have two hundred names to give them, a detail she stated
plainly, without apparent grief attached to the fact, simply as an accurate description of what she
does and does not know about the people she has spent this much of her life protecting. She counts
by number, confirms the number matches the number from her last visit, and records the confirmation
in an archive entry structurally identical, each time, to every entry before it.

I asked her once, carefully, whether the sameness of the count ever felt like progress or like its
absence. She considered the question longer than I expected before answering. "It is not
progress," she said. "It is not meant to be progress yet. It is meant to be a debt that has not
gotten worse, which is the only kind of good news a debt this size is currently allowed to give
me." She did not elaborate further, and I did not press her, though I have thought since about how
particular a kind of patience that answer requires — the willingness to call an unchanged number a
genuine, if modest, victory, for as many years as the larger discharge remains out of reach.

She has never told me, and I have never asked directly, what she imagines the day of that larger
discharge will actually look like, when it comes. I suspect she has not let herself imagine it in
much detail either, the way a person carrying a debt this old learns, out of necessity, not to
spend too much of the present on a future they cannot yet control the timing of. What I can report,
with confidence, is only what I witnessed at the outer approach on the day in question: she left
before dawn, alone, unremarked, and returned some hours later with nothing outwardly changed about
her except the particular quiet she carries for the rest of any day she has made that visit —
neither lighter nor heavier, only current, the ledger balanced for one more season against a total
she has not yet been permitted to close.

Her own closing note: *Two hundred, still. I will go again before the season turns. I have gone
every season for longer than I have kept most other habits. This is the one I do not intend to
break.*
