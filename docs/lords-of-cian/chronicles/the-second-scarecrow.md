# The Second Scarecrow

*Locked canon, Batch 112, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-383`). Crow King Alias Chronicle I. Rebellion era,
months after the Night of the Crow King (`MCD-236`, age 23). Not a territory Chronicle. New
standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The garrison captain assigned to the Korren Highlands checkpoint had studied the Voskharen Wetlands
report until he could recite Commandant Voris's exact encirclement geometry from memory, and had
built his own checkpoint doctrine specifically to deny the trick that had made it famous: no marsh,
no drainage channels, no cover dense enough for three hundred fighters to vanish into. Open highland
scrub, sightlines for a mile in every direction, and a rotating double patrol that never left more
than ninety seconds of any position unwatched.

He found the coat-and-feather-crown scarecrow propped against his own command post's support post
at dawn, and understood, before his patrol had finished confirming the compound behind the
checkpoint was already empty, that open sightlines had never been the actual problem.

"There's no marsh here," he said to his second, turning the scarecrow over in his hands as if a
closer look might explain what a mile of clear highland scrub apparently couldn't. "No channels. No
smoke. Nothing to hide three hundred people in. How does this trick even work twice?"

It worked because it had never been about the marsh at all. Kanja's people had spent four nights
studying the checkpoint's ninety-second patrol gap, and had used it not to hide but to move — never
present in any position long enough to be counted, the compound emptying not through concealment but
through timing so precise that the checkpoint's own doubled patrol had unknowingly become the
instrument of its own blind spot, each rotation covering exactly the ground the last rotation had
just vacated. By the time the captain's report reached his own superiors, the honest answer —
*we were never actually hiding anyone, we simply moved faster than your patrol pattern could ever
notice* — read, to anyone who hadn't lived through the four nights of preparation behind it, as
something closer to sorcery than tactics.

"He's mocking us," the second said, staring at the scarecrow's crude crown of gathered black
feathers. "Leaving the same calling card twice."

"He's not mocking us. He's telling us something, and it's worse than an insult." The captain set the
scarecrow down, finally, on the same support post where they'd found it. "The marsh trick worked
because of the marsh. This one worked with nothing. No terrain, no cover, no advantage we didn't
hand him ourselves by patrolling exactly the way every manual says we should. The crown isn't
bragging. It's a note. It's telling us the problem was never the ground under our feet. It was us."
