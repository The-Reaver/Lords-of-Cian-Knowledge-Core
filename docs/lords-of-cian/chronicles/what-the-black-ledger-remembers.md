# What the Black Ledger Remembers

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Bane Alias Chronicle XXV, first entry in the ninth
wave. Rebellion era, age 19, months after the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The settlement at Corvane Hollow had been burned eight months before Bane ever reached it, its
handful of survivors scattered, and the accepted account — the one the local Trust garrison
repeated, the one carved onto the memorial stone the survivors themselves had raised — named a
raiding band from beyond the border as the ones responsible. Onyx of Oblivion said otherwise the
moment its edge crossed the scorched ground.

The Black Ledger was not a power Bane called on often, or lightly. Where Cadence Ruin unmade
momentum and Veil Piercer cut past illusion, the Ledger reached further back than either — into
what a place had witnessed and could no longer say aloud, reading the residue of violence the way a
tracker read disturbed ground, except that what it read was intention, not footprints: who had
struck first, who had meant to kill and who had meant only to frighten, who had stood where and
done what, laid bare in a way no surviving witness's shaken, incomplete memory could match.

It took the better part of an hour, blade resting flat against the burned foundation stones, before
the Ledger yielded what the ground had kept. Not a raiding band from beyond the border. A Trust
requisition company, garrisoned four miles distant, sent to clear the Hollow's land for a supply
road the survivors had refused to sign away, dressed afterward to look like exactly the kind of
border violence everyone had been primed to believe.

"You're certain," the Hollow's eldest survivor said, when Bane told her what the Ledger had read,
watching the memorial stone she'd helped raise with her own hands to honor the wrong grief. "We
built that stone believing something else."

"I'm certain of what the ground remembers. I understand what that costs you to hear instead of what
you buried your dead believing." Bane didn't ask her to act on it, didn't press her toward
confrontation or forgiveness or anything else that wasn't hers to be pushed toward. "What you do
with it is yours. I only thought you deserved to bury them knowing the truth, even this late,
instead of a story it was easier for someone else to tell you."

She had the stone recut within the month — the same names, the same dead, a different hand named
responsible — and sent no word to the requisition company's garrison of what had changed, choosing
quiet knowledge over open accusation for reasons of her own that Bane never asked her to explain.
