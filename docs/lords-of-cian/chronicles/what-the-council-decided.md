# What the Council Decided

*Locked canon, Batch 230, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1028`). Bane Alias Chronicle LX, wave 20. Payoff to "The
Council That Asked Him to Speak Plainly" (wave 19, `MCD-939`). Not a territory Chronicle. Closes
wave 20.*

---

The verdict came six weeks after the arbiter council had sent Bane away disarmed and unthanked,
delivered by a courier who wouldn't say which side had sent him, which Bane took, correctly, as the
council's own idea of neutrality.

The ruling went against the Directorate's fabricated atrocity claims on every count Bane had refused
to concede to and against him on every count he'd volunteered honestly. It was, read plainly, exactly
the outcome an honest man should have expected from an honest testimony — and it changed almost
nothing about how the Directorate's own propaganda offices used his name the following month, the
fabricated claims still circulating in districts the ruling never reached.

"They'll keep printing what they printed before the council ever ruled," Corren Halst said, reading
the same pamphlets Bane was. "The ruling doesn't travel as far as the lie did."

"It doesn't need to travel that far to matter." Bane set the pamphlet down. "It matters to the next
arbiter council that hears a Directorate claim about someone else and remembers this one didn't hold
up. It matters to the historian who writes this down in forty years with an actual ruling to cite
instead of a rumor to repeat. I didn't testify to win the districts that already believe the
pamphlets. I testified so the record would exist for the people who'll eventually go looking for
it."

What the ruling did change, concretely, was smaller and more durable than any single district's
opinion: a standing evidentiary precedent, now citable by name, that an atrocity claim against a
combatant required more than a Directorate officer's word to stand — the first such precedent the
arbiter system had ever produced against a claim of this kind. Whether it would hold the next time
someone tried to weaponize the same tribunal against someone else was, Bane said, watching the
courier ride back out, a question for whoever needed it next rather than for him.

"You didn't ask what happens to you," Efa Gol said. "Only what happens to the record."

"The record outlasts me either way." He watched the pamphlets a moment longer before letting the
wind take the loose ones off the table. "Might as well make sure it says something true while I'm
still around to make it say anything at all."
