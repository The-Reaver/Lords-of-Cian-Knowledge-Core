# The Council That Wrote It Into Practice

*Locked canon, Batch 260, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1270`). Crow King Alias Chronicle LXXVIII, wave 26,
closing it. A liberated town's new governing council formally adopts a version of the craft's own
restraint principles into its own local practice. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The same council that had heard the captured soldier's testimony weeks before called the fourth
generation back for a different reason entirely -- not to extract information this time, but because
one of its own members, drafting the town's first set of local governing customs since the garrison's
fall, wanted to know exactly what principle had let the interrogation go the way it had, so it could
be written down and applied to more than one afternoon in a granary loft.

"We keep having to decide what we're allowed to do to people who used to be our enemies," the elder
said. "Prisoners. Informants. Even each other, some days, when the arguments get sharp enough. I'd
rather we decided it on purpose than kept improvising it case by case."

The fourth generation didn't write the custom himself -- he made a point of that, remembering the
third generation's own single written page and what it had taught her about a thing valued only
because someone else had approved it. He answered questions instead, for most of a day, about
restraint, about verified truth over manufactured pressure, about the difference between what a plan
required and what a wronged person felt they deserved.

What the council eventually wrote down was theirs, in their own words, adapted to their own disputes
-- nothing that named the craft, nothing that claimed its origin, only the underlying discipline
shaped to fit a town's ordinary business of settling arguments and treating captured men fairly. It
became, within a season, simply how the town did things, the way any local custom eventually stops
being a decision and starts being a habit nobody remembers choosing.

"They didn't copy us," the fourth generation told Kanja afterward. "They built their own version, the
same way I built mine instead of copying my teacher's. I think that's exactly what should happen to
something like this, once it's out in the world long enough. It shouldn't stay ours forever."
