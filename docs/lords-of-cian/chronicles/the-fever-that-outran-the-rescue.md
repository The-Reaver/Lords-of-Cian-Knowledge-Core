# The Fever That Outran the Rescue

*Locked canon, Batch 259, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1248`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LXXXIII, wave 28. Age
205, V3 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The raid itself went as cleanly as any in recent memory — a holding ship boarded at anchor, guards
disarmed before half of them understood the boarding had started, seventy-four captives freed inside
of twenty minutes with no casualties on either side of the fight. It was the fastest clean liberation
the crew had run in years. It also arrived four days too late for six of the seventy-four, who had
been carrying a fever since before the ship ever anchored and whose bodies, weakened past what
speed alone could fix, gave out within hours of finally being freed.

The Mend-Line's six reservoirs, built against bleeding and structural damage, had nothing to offer a
fever already running its course through a body with no reserves left to draw on. He knew that going
in — had known it the whole approach, watching the timing calculation come out the same each time he
ran it: even the fastest possible raid, mounted the moment the ship was located, would still have
arrived after the fever had already done most of what it was going to do. There had been no earlier
window to hit. The illness had a two-week head start no reconnaissance could have closed.

Sixty-eight freed and walked off the ship under their own power. Six others did not survive the
first night ashore, despite everything the crew's medical knowledge and the Mend-Line's design could
offer a body past fixing by either. Garren Hask logged all seventy-four honestly — freed and
survived separately from freed and lost — a distinction his ledger had needed to make only a handful
of times across two centuries, and one he'd never learned to make without it costing him something
to write.

Kanja stood with the bodies a while before the burial, the same stillness he'd have given a battle's
dead, though no battle had killed these six. Speed had done everything speed could do. It had simply
not been the thing that was going to be enough.
