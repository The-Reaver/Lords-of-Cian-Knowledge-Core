# The Boy Who Kept the Numbers Honest

*Locked canon, Batch 108, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-373`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle III, closing the
wave. Rebellion era, during the Furnace District Strike itself (`MCD-244`, age 21), told from Ezio
Valcari's perspective -- already a locked named character, roughly sixteen at this point, less than
a year past his first on-page appearance at the Sewer War of Killane (`MCD-234`, age 20). Not a
territory Chronicle. No new named characters beyond the already-locked Ezio Valcari.*

---

Ezio had compiled thirty-one pages of testimony by the third day, and the thing that struck him most
was not the size of the debt the smelting district was owed, which was staggering, but how rarely
anyone tried to lie about it.

He'd expected exaggeration. He'd braced for it, the way any careful record-keeper braces for the
version of an account that's been rounded upward by anger before it reaches the page. What he found
instead, sitting beside Kanja through four days of asking the same four words to four thousand
different people, was a kind of precision that unsettled him more than exaggeration would have — men
and women who had clearly done this arithmetic themselves, alone, at night, for years, and had
arrived at figures so exact and so conservatively stated that Ezio found himself, more than once,
quietly adding a margin the worker hadn't claimed, because the real number, when he cross-checked it
against the district's own production ledgers later, always turned out to be larger than what they'd
been willing to say out loud.

"You keep doing that," Kanja said, on the third night, watching him correct another entry upward by
lamplight. "Why?"

"Because they're being too honest for their own good." Ezio didn't look up from the page. "A worker
who's owed six years of wages and only claims four, because he's not sure the fourth year counts, or
because he's embarrassed to ask for more than he thinks he deserves — that's not a number I can
trust as complete. I trust the production records more than I trust a frightened man's modesty. So I
correct it. Quietly. I don't tell them I did it, because if I did, half of them would insist I take
it back out."

"That's not what I asked you to do."

"You asked me to compile a documented, unpayable debt. You didn't ask me to compile the debt people
were brave enough to say to your face. Those aren't the same list, and the gap between them is where
this district's whole problem has been living for years — people learning to ask for less than
they're owed because asking for the real number felt like tempting a fight they didn't think they
could win." He turned the page, dipped the pen again. "You get them to trust you enough to answer at
all. That's the hard part, and it's yours. Making sure what they say is actually the whole truth,
even when they don't trust themselves enough to say it — that part's mine."

By the fourth day's end, the completed ledger ran to thirty-eight pages: 4,000 names, a documented,
individually sourced account cross-checked twice against the district's own production records,
deliberately, structurally unpayable by any means short of the district administration admitting, in
writing, the scale of what it had taken. No weapon had been drawn across the entire four days. No
threat had been issued. The only thing that had moved, page by careful page, was the truth, corrected
upward every time a worker's own fear had quietly asked for less than they'd actually earned.

"They're going to call this the purest thing I ever did," Kanja said, reading the final tally by
lamplight, the alias already spreading through the district before the ink was fully dry. "And half
the reason it's true is a boy who kept correcting numbers nobody asked him to correct."

"Somebody had to keep them honest." Ezio closed the ledger and handed it over. "You get the myth.
I'll settle for the arithmetic being right."
