# What His Hands Remembered

*Locked canon, Batch 204, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-894`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XLVI, wave 16. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The brick-kiln district had a name for the tally-takers before the crew ever set foot in it: soft
hands. Word traveled ahead of the ledger the way it always did by then, and this district's foreman
had already told his workers what to expect — a well-fed stranger with a book, asking questions he'd
never have to live the answers to, gone again before the next firing.

Kanja arrived alone at the yard gate before first light, before the tally table had even been
unpacked from the cart, and asked the shift boss for a place in the line instead.

"A place doing what."

"Whatever the line's short a man for today."

The shift boss put him on the brick barrows, the worst rotation in the yard by the workers' own
ranking — hauling green brick from the molding floor to the kiln mouth in the building heat, a run
that broke most newcomers' pace inside an hour. Kanja didn't ask for a lighter rotation, didn't ask
for the tally table to be set up at all that day, and didn't say who he was to anyone who hadn't
already guessed.

By midday his hands had split in two places the way every new barrow-hand's did, and he wrapped them
the way the man beside him showed him to, without comment, and kept pushing. By the end of the shift
he'd fallen a full barrow behind the district's fastest haulers and matched the pace of its slowest,
and no one had corrected an entry, recorded a wage, or asked a single question about anyone's pay.

"You're slower than you looked," the barrow-hand beside him said, over the evening meal, not
unkindly.

"First day on a barrow. Give me a week."

"You don't have a week. You've got a book to fill and a district to move on from."

"The book can wait until the district decides it's worth talking to."

He worked the barrows for three more days before he asked for the tally table to be set up at all,
and when he finally sat down behind it, the line that formed wasn't the cautious, thinned trickle
that met the crew in every district where the reputation arrived first and the work happened later.
It was most of the shift, in order, unprompted — men and women who had watched him fail to keep pace
on a barrow run for four straight days before he ever asked them a single question about what they
were owed.

"Soft hands," the shift boss said, watching the line form, and held up his own for a moment beside
Kanja's newly split and re-split palms. "Guess the name doesn't fit anymore."

"It never fit anyone who actually showed up. That was always the point of coming."

*Continuity notes (not narrative): a genuinely new register for this alias — participatory manual
labor rather than documentation-first contact, earning a skeptical district's trust through four days
of unglamorous, failed-pace barrow work before the tally table is even unpacked, not through the
ledger method itself. Distinct from every prior "district resists at first" entry (the staged
worst-off testimony of "The Worst-Off First," the generational apathy of "The District That Wouldn't
Speak," the fabricated counter-tally of "The Numbers He Couldn't Prove") in that the resistance here
is class-suspicion of an outsider's body, not the tally method's accuracy, and it is resolved by
physical participation rather than by anything written down. No new named characters. First entry in
the Industrial Myth's sixteenth wave.*
