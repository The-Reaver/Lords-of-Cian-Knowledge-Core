# What the Marsh Wouldn't Ground

*Locked canon, Batch 284, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1497`). Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XCV, wave 32.
Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241). A detailed, battle-intense Trinity
combat showcase in the alias's first waterlogged marshland register. Not a territory Chronicle.
Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The reclaimed ore-drift sat at the edge of a tidal marsh nobody had thought worth defending until
the Directorate patrol proved otherwise, wading in at low tide across ground too soft to hold a
formation and too shallow to call water. It was, Kanja understood within the first exchange, exactly
the ground they'd chosen it for.

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System had never failed to ground a blow before — not at the Black
Trench, not through six weeks of frost, not against parched desert stone that radiated heat back
into the frame instead of drawing it away. It failed here for a plainer reason than any of those: the
marsh mud beneath his boots gave before it resisted, absorbing the redirected force into itself
instead of carrying it away clean, the way a struck bell wrapped in wet cloth won't ring. A blow
grounded into standing muck stayed half a blow, the rest of it lost to ground that wouldn't hold
still long enough to take it.

He adjusted without pretending the gap wasn't real. Obsidian Malice carried more of the engagement
than it had in any prior showcase, discharged not as a grounded release but as a direct strike each
time Mafesto's system came back only half-fed, the difference in output plain enough that Kanja
felt it in his own frame each time he called on it. Onyx of Oblivion did the rest. Cadence Ruin,
built to read rhythm through solid stone and timber, took longer to learn the marsh's own sucking,
irregular pulse than it had ever taken to learn a millrace or a canyon wall — water rising and
falling around root-mats instead of flowing in one direction — but it learned it before the
patrol's second wave closed the distance, and Whisper of Shadows moved across ground too unstable for
armored boots to follow at any real speed.

The patrol's own commander, pulled from the mud afterward, hadn't expected the fight to go as long
as it did. "Everything we know about you says you ground what's thrown at you and throw it back
twice as hard. Half of that's still true out here. The other half just isn't, on ground like this."

"No," Kanja agreed, wringing marsh water out of a torn sleeve. "It isn't. You picked good ground.
You just didn't stay on it long enough to use it properly." The drift held, the way every drift on
the tour had held, but he said as much to the crew that evening as he had to the captured commander:
the method hadn't won this one clean. It had won it by leaning harder on two legs of the Trinity once
the third stopped carrying its share, and that was worth remembering the next time the ground itself
decided which of them got to work at full strength.
