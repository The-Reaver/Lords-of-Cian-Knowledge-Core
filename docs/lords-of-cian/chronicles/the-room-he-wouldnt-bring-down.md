# The Room He Wouldn't Bring Down

The garrison's ammunition stores sat directly beneath the holding cells where the rebellion's captured
scouts were being kept, the same iron support framework running through both levels — collapse the
framework to deny the Trust its munitions, and the ceiling above forty prisoners came down with it.

"There's no version of this where I bring the whole structure down," Kanja said, tracing the
framework's resonance through the Aegis-Talisman with more patience than the engagement's urgency
technically allowed. "The doctrine's never been about finding the biggest failure point. It's about
finding the right one."

He spent nearly twenty minutes mapping the framework's full tension distribution before committing to
anything — every support column, every place the munitions level's own weight transferred into the
one below it — until he found what he was looking for: a single external buttress, load-bearing for
the storage level alone, structurally isolated from the cell level's own ceiling by a deliberate
seismic gap the original builders had included for reasons that had nothing to do with tonight.

The discharge took only the buttress. The munitions level came down clean, contained entirely within
its own floor, and forty feet above it the holding cells never so much as trembled.

"You could have collapsed the whole framework in under a minute," the rebel raid leader said,
watching the dust settle over a munitions store that no longer existed and a cell block that hadn't
moved. "Would've been faster. Would've still worked, probably, even with the prisoners in the way."

"Probably isn't good enough when the cost of being wrong is forty people I came here to get out
alive. I've built this doctrine on knowing the difference between what I can bring down and what I
should. Tonight the difference happened to sit forty feet above an ammunition store, and I wasn't
going to let urgency talk me out of finding it properly."

The prisoners were freed within the hour, the isolated seismic gap never explained to them, and the
raid leader carried away a version of the doctrine he hadn't previously understood: not a weapon that
simply found the biggest weakness, but one that could, when it mattered enough, find the smallest one
instead.
