# The Hull Dol Maren Wasn't Finished With

*Locked canon, Batch 199, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-794`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle XXXIX, wave 13 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The captured Directorate construct's hull sat in dry dock for six weeks after its capture, and Dol
Maren spent nearly every one of them crawling through its plating with a lantern and a notebook, far
more interested in what the engineers had gotten right than in what had ultimately failed them.

"You could scrap it for parts," Kanja said, finding him mid-inspection for the third time that week.
"Or build something new instead of studying something that already lost."

"It didn't lose because the design was bad. It lost because the design assumed no one would ever reach
the rudder housing." Dol Maren ran a hand along a seam of plating configured to disperse a magnetic
pulse, genuine admiration in his voice despite the hull belonging to an enemy who'd built it to kill
his crew. "This dispersal pattern is better than anything I've tried against our own anchor-chain
device. I want to understand it before I decide whether to fear it or steal it."

What emerged from six weeks of study wasn't a copy of the Directorate's construct but something new
entirely — a hull refit for *The Ledger*, the fleet's newest flagship, incorporating the dispersal
principle not as armor against the fleet's own weapon but as a genuine advance in how a hull handled
stress across its whole frame, faster and more resilient than anything Dol Maren had built before.

"You took their weapon and turned it into our shipbuilding," Kanja said, watching the refit take shape
on *The Ledger*'s exposed frame.

"I took their *engineering* and turned it into ours. The weapon was never the interesting part. The
interesting part was how they solved the problem of a hull that needs to survive stress it can't
predict in advance." He tapped the notebook, thick now with six weeks of careful diagrams. "Every
enemy who builds something good enough to nearly beat us is also, without meaning to, teaching us
something. I'd be wasting an education if I only ever studied their failures and never their
successes."

The refit took another two months to complete, and when *The Ledger* finally returned to open water,
she rode lower and faster than either of the two flagships that had come before her — not because Dol
Maren had built something to defeat the next Directorate construct specifically, but because he'd
never stopped treating even a captured enemy weapon as something worth genuinely understanding rather
than simply destroying.
