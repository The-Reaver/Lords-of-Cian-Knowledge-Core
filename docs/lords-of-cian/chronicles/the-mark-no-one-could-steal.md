# The Mark No One Could Steal

*Locked canon, Batch 262, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1316`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LXX, first
entry in the twenty-fourth wave. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241),
some months after "The Standard They Learned to Fake" (MCD-1084). A direct payoff to the
counterfeit-mark hook. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The successor had stopped sleeping properly over it, by her own admission — three counter-marks
tried and three eventually copied, each one buying the Open-Forge Standard a season or two of
trust before some patient forger somewhere worked out the geometry of the die. "I keep building a
better lock," she told Kanja, laying out the latest failed sample, "and I keep forgetting the lock
was never the actual problem. Anyone can copy a shape stamped into metal. That's what a shape is."

"Then stop putting the proof in the shape," Kanja said.

It took her two more weeks to build what came of that, and when she finally brought it to him it
wasn't a die at all — it was a question, a specific sequence of finishing steps taught only by
hand, mouth to hand, never written down anywhere a counterfeiter could steal the instructions from,
the last step deliberately counterintuitive enough that nobody guessing from the finished tool
alone would ever land on it by accident. Any genuine journeyman certified under the Standard could
demonstrate it on request, on any tool, in under a minute. No counterfeiter selling from a cart
three towns over could fake a demonstration they'd never been taught, no matter how good their
stamp got.

"It's slower," she admitted, the first time she made a buyer watch her do it before he'd trust the
mark on his own new hammer. "A stamp used to answer the question in a glance. This makes you stop
and watch."

"Slower and unstealable beats fast and forgeable," Kanja said. "Word will get out. Buyers will
start asking for the demonstration everywhere the Standard's name means anything."

It did get out, faster than either of them expected — settlements three tours behind adopting the
watch-and-verify habit within a season of hearing about it, forgers finding a market that had
learned to ask for proof no die alone could counterfeit. It didn't end counterfeiting outright;
nothing ever entirely would, not against a method built to be copied by anyone willing to learn it
honestly in the first place. But for the first time since the fakes started, the successor slept
through a full night without turning the problem over one more time before she did.

*Continuity notes (not narrative): the direct payoff to "The Standard They Learned to Fake"
(MCD-1084, wave 21), which deliberately left the counterfeit-mark problem open and ongoing — this
entry resolves it, not by escalating the physical-mark arms race further, but by moving the proof
of authenticity out of a copyable object entirely and into a taught, demonstrable, hand-to-hand
skill, a genuinely new kind of solution consistent with the Open-Forge Standard's (MCD-1052)
teaching-first ethos. A partial, not absolute, resolution — MCD-1084's own point (that a method
anyone can learn can be learned by the dishonest too) is left intact rather than overturned. The
senior smith's successor (MCD-923, MCD-1052, MCD-1084) appears consistently with her established
role. No new named characters. First entry in the Lord of Embers' twenty-fourth three-Chronicle
wave.*
