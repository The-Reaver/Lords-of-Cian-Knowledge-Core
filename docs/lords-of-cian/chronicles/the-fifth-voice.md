# The Fifth Voice

*Locked canon, Batch 282, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1479`). Crow King Alias Chronicle XCV, wave 32. The
long-deferred fifth-generation question is finally answered -- teaching begins. Not a territory
Chronicle.*

---

The runner's son came back a third time before anyone decided anything, which was itself, the third
generation pointed out, most of the answer.

He'd been brought to Kanja once already, publicly, the year before -- the fourth generation refusing
to decide alone, the way the craft had taught him to refuse. Nothing had been settled that day beyond
not settling it. Now he stood in front of all four of them at once: Kanja, the apprentice, the third
generation, the fourth generation, in a room too small for the moment it was actually holding.

"Why do you want it," Kanja asked him, the same question he'd once asked a two-year messenger-runner
who would go on to become the fourth generation himself, and the same one the third generation had
answered years before that, in her own words, to a man who hadn't known yet what he was building.

"Because I already do it," the runner's son said, "badly, on my own, whenever I hear something that
doesn't add up. I just don't know if I'm doing the actual thing or something that only looks like it
from where I'm standing."

Nobody laughed. The apprentice, who had answered a version of the same worry to her own teacher a
lifetime ago, recognized the shape of it exactly.

"That's the discipline talking already," she said. "Not knowing is the first honest thing anyone's
ever said to us before we started teaching them."

Kanja looked at the third generation, and the third generation looked at the fourth generation, and
nobody looked away first, because none of them wanted to be the one who decided this alone either.
It was the fourth generation who finally spoke, since it was his own recruit standing there.

"Listen before you act," he told the runner's son. "Restraint over cleverness. That's the whole of
it, and it hasn't changed once across four people now. Everything else you'll build yourself, the
slow way, the only way that's ever actually held."

They didn't call it a ceremony. Nobody sang, and nothing was written down beyond what the runner's
son already knew from a page he'd never seen. But when he left that evening, he left as the fifth
person alive who could rightly claim to be carrying the thing forward, and every one of the other
four watched him go with the specific, quiet unease of people who had just done something they'd
spent three separate seasons finding reasons to put off.
