# What Whisper of Shadows Was For

*Locked canon, Batch 255, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1122`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle LXV, wave 22.
Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench -- Onyx of Oblivion solo, Mafesto dormant and Obsidian Malice
undeployed (`MCD-232`). A dedicated, non-combat showcase of Whisper of Shadows' true purpose.*

---

The compound belonged, on paper, to a grain factor three districts over who had never once been
accused of anything, which was exactly the problem. Garren Hask's figures showed a gap that
shouldn't have existed — wages paid out on record, and yet the workers who'd supposedly received
them going hungrier every month than the numbers allowed for. Somewhere inside that compound, a
second set of books existed that would either confirm the theft or prove Hask wrong. There was no
way to ask for it. There was no fight worth starting over a suspicion.

So Kanja went in alone, unarmed but for Onyx, after full dark, with no intention of drawing the
blade at all.

He had used Whisper of Shadows before to un-hide an enemy already coming for him — dissolving the
confidence of a man standing in a blind spot, turning a hidden attacker's certainty against him at
the exact moment it mattered. This was the opposite application, and it took him most of the first
hour inside the walls to understand the difference. The gift did not make him invisible. Nothing
about the compound's two patrolling guards changed; they still walked their circuits, still carried
lanterns, still glanced into every doorway out of habit. What changed was smaller and stranger: every
time a glance should have caught him, some fractional hesitation intervened — a lantern-flame
guttering half a second early, a guard's own thought wandering toward his aching feet at exactly the
wrong moment, a floorboard's creak arriving a half-step behind where a creak should have been. Onyx
wasn't hiding him. It was finding, in each passing second, the one version of that second in which a
man looking straight at him simply didn't quite see him — and it cost something to keep finding it,
a drag behind his own eyes that grew heavier the longer the night went on.

He found the second ledger in a strongbox under a false floor panel behind the factor's own writing
desk, copied the relevant pages by hand rather than take the book itself — nothing to prove theft
retroactively so much as nothing to give away that anyone had ever been inside — and was back over
the wall two hours before dawn, the drag behind his eyes not fully gone until well past sunrise.

"You look like you haven't slept," Hask said, when Kanja laid the copied pages on the tally table.

"I didn't." He didn't explain further, and Hask, checking the new figures against his own, didn't
ask him to. The numbers matched exactly what the gap had implied. Two days later, confronted with
copies of his own hidden second book, the factor paid every owed wage inside the week rather than
have the district learn how the Trench Monarch had come to know what he knew.

Kanja never told anyone but Hask how he'd known it. Whisper of Shadows, he understood by then, was
never a weapon at all — not really. It was a way of being somewhere a fight would have made
impossible to reach, and it cost him something every time he asked it to hold that door open longer
than a single desperate second required.
