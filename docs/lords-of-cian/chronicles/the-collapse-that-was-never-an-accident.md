# The Collapse That Was Never an Accident

*Locked canon, Batch 283, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1490`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XCVII, wave 33, first entry. The doctrine's first application to a merchant's own deliberate insurance-fraud sabotage of his own property. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The warehouse had gone down clean, in the middle of a storm nobody could argue hadn't happened, and the merchant's claim against the trade guild's mutual indemnity fund was thorough enough to make three auditors uneasy in three different ways before any of them said so aloud. What none of them could prove was that the goods the warehouse had supposedly lost to the storm had, by the harbor-master's own separate ledger, left the building on wagons two full nights before the weather turned.

"We can't accuse a guild member on a suspicion," the senior auditor told Kanja, laying the two ledgers side by side on a table that had nothing to do with tension or discharge or anything the Iron Bastard's reputation usually got called in for. "We need to know if that roof came down because of the storm, or because of something done to it before the storm ever arrived to take the blame."

He read the wreckage the way he'd read any collapsed structure, and the storm's honest signature was there, real and unmistakable in the twisted iron banding — wind load, exactly as much as that night's weather should have put on a roof of that span. What didn't belong was underneath it: three support beams, sawn most of the way through weeks earlier and packed with sawdust and pitch to hide the cut from a casual glance, engineered to fail at a load well below what an honest roof of that design should have carried. The storm hadn't caused the collapse. It had only arrived in time to take the blame for one already waiting to happen.

"You're certain," the merchant said, when Kanja laid the finding out plainly in front of the guild council, no threat in his voice, no discharge anywhere near his hands. "A storm brought my roof down. Ask anyone who was there that night."

"Ask the beams instead. They don't care whose story they're supposed to support." Kanja set the sawn fragment on the table between them, the pitch still faintly visible under the lamp light. "The wind told the truth about itself. So did the cut. I only listened to both."

The merchant broke before the council had to press him further, admitting the goods had moved two nights early and the beams a week before that, the whole scheme built to collect the indemnity fund's payout on cargo he'd already sold twice over. The guild's judgment was restitution in full plus a decade's suspension from the fund, not blood — a resolution the doctrine had never once needed force to reach.
