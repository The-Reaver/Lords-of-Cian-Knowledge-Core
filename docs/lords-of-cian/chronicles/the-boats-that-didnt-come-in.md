# The Boats That Didn't Come In

*Locked canon, Batch 192, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-571`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XXVI, wave 9 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The two skeptical fishermen who'd left their boats at the second tide-mark weren't the only ones who
hadn't fully believed the warning. Three more boats, out on a routine run when the courier's sheet
arrived, hadn't made it back to harbor before the storm's outer edge closed the approach.

Kanja's crew reached the harbor an hour after the storm did, not to fight anything, but because
Sephtis's forecast had specified a rescue window as precisely as it had specified the storm itself —
a lull in the worst of the system's leading edge, roughly ninety minutes, before its second and far
stronger band arrived.

Mafesto went out into water no ordinary boat could have survived, the Kinetic Transfer System banking
the storm's own battering force with each wave rather than fighting it directly, and found the first
two boats within the lull's first half hour, their crews clinging to overturned hulls too exhausted to
swim further on their own.

The third boat was harder. It had drifted past the harbor's own charted waters into a stretch of coast
the settlement's fishermen avoided even in calm weather, and finding it in time meant trusting
Sephtis's ninety-minute window down to its final ten.

They found it with six minutes left in the lull, four fishermen alive, one already lost to the cold
before the rescue reached them.

"You saved three boats," the headman said afterward, standing over the one loss the night had cost
them. "You couldn't save the fourth man."

"No," Kanja said. "The window told us where to look, not what we'd find when we got there. I'd rather
tell you that honestly than let you think the forecast means nobody drowns. It means fewer people
drown than would have otherwise. That's not the same as none."
