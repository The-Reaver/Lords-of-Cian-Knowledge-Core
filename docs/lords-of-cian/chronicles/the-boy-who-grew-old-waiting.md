# The Boy Who Grew Old Waiting

*Locked canon, Batch 259, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1251`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LXXXVI, wave 29. Age
292, V4 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Eighty-two years is long enough for a cabin boy to become an old man, and long enough for the ship
he grew up on to have been broken up for lumber twice over and replaced twice more. Kanja found the
settlement almost by accident, a supply stop on a route he hadn't sailed in decades, and recognized
the old man on the porch of the settlement's largest house before the old man recognized him — not
by his face, which had never once been shown, but by the particular way he still moved through a
crowded dock, a rhythm eighty-two years hadn't managed to erase from either of their memories even
if only one of them had aged visibly across it.

The old man was telling the story to his own grandchildren when Kanja found him, the same drifted,
distorted version of the legend that had come back to him once already, decades earlier, from the
mouth of a boy too young to know he was speaking to the man who'd lived it. The story had grown in
the telling since then, the way stories do across two more generations of retelling — bigger,
stranger, further from what had actually happened on that ship than even the first version had been.

He didn't correct it this time either. He sat on the porch's edge, unremarked, long enough to hear
the whole thing through to its ending, and left before the old man's eyes settled on him long enough
to wonder. Whatever recognition might have passed between them stayed exactly where it had stayed
eighty-two years earlier — unspoken, ungiven, the anonymous quartermaster choosing, again, to remain
exactly that. The old man died four years later, by the account that eventually reached Garren Hask
through the crew's own scattered network, still telling the story, still not knowing he'd told it
twice now to the only person alive who could have corrected a single word of it and chose, both
times, not to.
