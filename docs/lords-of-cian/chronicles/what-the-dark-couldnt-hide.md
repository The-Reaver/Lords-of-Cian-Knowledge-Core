# What the Dark Couldn't Hide

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XVII, second

Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The charge that brought down the ceiling of the transit gallery took every work lantern in the
section with it, and for a stretch of nearly ninety seconds the fight that followed happened in
absolute darkness — no torchlight, no Hymn-Engine signal-glow, nothing but sound, breath, and the
grit of settling dust underfoot.

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System didn't need eyes to register impact; a blade catching the
plating registered as force and angle alone, the exoskeleton's joints reading the strike's vector
and grounding it whether or not Kanja ever saw the hand that swung it. Obsidian Malice found its
targets by weight-shift and breath — a discharge aimed not at a visible silhouette but at the exact
patch of displaced air where a body had just planted its forward foot, the same instinct a smith
trusts when working a forge by feel because the light's gone and the metal's color still tells the
truth. Onyx of Oblivion carried the rest: Whisper of Shadows needing no illumination to move
silent, and a blade that answered to grip and balance long after the eye had stopped being useful
at all.

When the dust settled enough for a single relit torch to show the gallery again, six attackers lay
disarmed and the rest had fled a fight they'd never actually managed to land a clean hit in.

"You fought that whole exchange blind," the crew chief said, still catching his breath, torch
raised to a scene none of them had watched happen.

"Not blind. Just not looking the way most people mean it." Kanja checked Obsidian Malice's housing
by touch before trusting the torchlight to confirm it, an old habit from years at a forge where the
eyes were never the first thing you relied on. "A smith works a piece by its weight and its heat
and the sound it makes under the hammer long before he trusts what it looks like. Stone's no
different underground. Sound carries true down here even when the light doesn't. I didn't need to
see them coming. I needed to hear where the weight was, and trust the rest to catch up."

The gallery's collapse cost them a day's work resetting timber, but not a single member of the crew
came out of the dark with more than bruises — a fact the crew chief noted plainly in the report
that made its way, weeks later, into the growing account of a fight nobody watching from outside
the tunnel had ever actually seen happen at all.
