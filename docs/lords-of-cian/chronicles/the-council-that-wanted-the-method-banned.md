# The Council That Wanted the Method Banned

*Locked canon, Batch 197, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-730`). The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XXXV, wave 12 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

A closed Trust council convened to debate a formal classification for the doctrine — not as a tactic
to be countered, but as a practice to be declared illegitimate outright, a violation of whatever
unwritten expectations governed how war was supposed to be fought between combatants who still, in
theory, answered to some shared notion of conduct.

The general who had once conceded the doctrine's adaptability was called to testify against the
proposal, an assignment several council members assumed would produce the condemnation his own
career-ending report had implicitly promised.

It didn't.

"You want me to call it dishonorable," he told the council, according to the account that eventually
reached Kanja secondhand. "I can't do that honestly. He's never used it against anyone who wasn't
already an active combatant, structure, or vehicle. He's turned down opportunities to use it on
occupied buildings when the people inside mattered more than the objective. I've read every account we
have. I can't find you the violation you're asking me to certify."

"Then what would you call it?" one council member pressed.

"Effective. Uncomfortably so. That's not the same charge as dishonorable, and I won't pretend it is
just because effective is harder for this council to sit with."

The proposal failed to advance, not because the council had been persuaded of anything beyond its own
inability to build the case it wanted, and word of the general's testimony reached Kanja weeks later
through channels neither man controlled.

"He didn't have to defend it," Kanja said, when the account reached him. "He'd already paid a price
once for being honest about what this doctrine does. He could have let the council have its
condemnation and stayed clear of the whole question."

"Maybe he's decided honesty costs him less the second time," the rebel intelligence officer who'd
brought the account replied. "Or maybe he just doesn't like being asked to certify a lie twice in one
career."
