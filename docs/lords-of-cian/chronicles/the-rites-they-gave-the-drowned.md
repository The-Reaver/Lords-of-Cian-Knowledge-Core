# The Rites They Gave the Drowned

*Locked canon, Batch 245, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1073`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
LXIII, wave 21, closing the wave. A quiet mourning register never used in this alias's prior entries.
Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The Trust patrol hadn't been sunk by the fleet at all. A squall the fleet itself had turned to run
from caught the pursuing hull broadside two hours after dark, and by the time *The Audit* doubled back
to check the weather's damage, there was only wreckage and eleven men in the water, four of them
already past saving.

Kanja gave the order to recover all of them anyway — the dead as carefully as the living pulled
aboard shivering and grateful in a way none of them expected to feel toward the ship that had spent
the evening evading them.

Efa Gol said what several of the crew were thinking before anyone else would. "Those men would have
fired on us an hour earlier if the storm hadn't beaten them to it. We don't owe them a service."

"You're right that we don't owe it to them," Kanja said. "It isn't for them."

He didn't explain further in the moment, and Efa Gol didn't press it, but she stood at the rail
anyway when the crew laid the seven dead out on *The Audit*'s deck at dawn, wrapped in sailcloth, and
said the names Garren Hask read off a single surviving log-book recovered from the wreck — names
belonging to men who, by every account the fleet had of the Sovereign Trust navy, existed only as
numbers in a muster roll until this morning. Kanja spoke the words of the rite himself, the same ones
he'd have spoken over any of his own crew, and the sea took all seven of them the way it took anyone,
without asking first which flag they'd sailed under.

"It isn't about what they deserved," he said afterward, to Efa Gol directly this time. "It's about
what we are. The day we start deciding whose dead are worth the words and whose aren't, we've already
become the thing we keep telling people we're not."

Garren Hask logged all eleven names in the same ledger that already held disabled hulls, freed
captives, and — more recently — a crewman's own repaid debt: seven marked dead and given rites at sea,
four marked living and returned, under Trust escort, to the nearest garrison willing to take them
back. He made no distinction in how carefully he wrote either half of the entry.

The four survivors said little on the short passage to shore, but one, older than the rest and
clearly senior enough to have opinions worth reporting upward, asked before he climbed down into the
transfer boat whether the ghost fleet did this for every crew it fought. Kanja told him the truth —
that this was the first time the sea itself had done the fighting instead — and let the man draw
whatever conclusion he wanted to draw from that on his own.
