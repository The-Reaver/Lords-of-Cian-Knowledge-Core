# What the Titan Carries

*Locked canon, Batch 109, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-375`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle II. Rebellion
era, the final night of the Sewer War of Killane (`MCD-234`, age 20) -- the Trinity's first
extended showcase in full combined use since its debut at the Black Trench (`MCD-232`), detailing
Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System, Obsidian Malice's discharge cycle, and Onyx of Oblivion working
as one coordinated system. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The extraction went wrong at the surface breach, the one place six weeks of careful silence had
always been most exposed: a rapid-response detachment of ninety, alerted by the last sabotage charge
going off ahead of schedule, converging on the exact drainage culvert Kanja's crew needed forty
uninterrupted minutes to clear through with the completed Registry copy.

He gave them the forty minutes standing in the open, alone, between the culvert mouth and the
detachment's advancing line.

Mafesto came fully online the moment the first volley closed — not a shield exactly, the exoskeleton
had never worked that way, but a receiving system, every impact that found him running down through
articulated joints into stored kinetic charge rather than bruised bone. The Kinetic Transfer System
took the volley's force and held it, grounded and converted rather than merely absorbed, the way a
smith's anvil takes a hammer's strike and gives nothing back except the shape the strike was meant to
produce. Twelve rounds landed against the plating in under three seconds. None of them slowed him by
so much as a half-step.

The charge had to go somewhere. It went into Obsidian Malice.

He met the detachment's forward line with the war club already carrying every joule the volley had
fed it, a single downward strike that didn't so much break the front rank's shield wall as unmake it
— six men thrown clear in a radius that owed nothing to Kanja's own muscle and everything to a
weapon built specifically to discharge what Mafesto had just finished collecting. The recharge that
followed took the better part of five seconds, the honest cost of a strike that heavy, and in those
five seconds Onyx did the work Obsidian Malice couldn't: two blades reaching for him in the gap found
Cadence Ruin waiting, their coordinated timing collapsing exactly as it had for a Compliance
detachment two years and a lifetime ago, and a third attacker who thought he'd found an opening in
the war club's silence discovered Veil Piercer instead, the blade finding the seam in his guard a
half-second before Obsidian Malice came back online to finish what the sword had only needed to
start.

It was not one weapon fighting ninety men. It was three systems handing the fight to each other in
sequence, each covering exactly the gap the others' own limits created — Mafesto absorbing what
couldn't be dodged, Obsidian Malice discharging what Mafesto had gathered, Onyx closing the recharge
window no armor or club alone could have covered — and the detachment's own after-action report,
recovered weeks later by Anansi's growing network, used a phrase that outlived the battle itself:
*he does not fight like a man carrying three weapons. He fights like one weapon that happens to be
wearing a man.*

By the time the forty minutes were up, the detachment had broken and scattered rather than pressed
its numerical advantage, and Kanja walked backward into the culvert mouth still facing outward,
Mafesto's plating scored but structurally sound, Obsidian Malice's charge bleeding back to zero,
Onyx already quiet again in his hand. The completed Scrip-Registry copy left Killane that same hour,
undiscovered, exactly as planned.

"You could have been killed holding that line alone," the crew chief said, once they were clear.

"I wasn't alone." He checked the war club's housing out of habit, the way a tradesman checks a tool
before putting it away. "I had three things doing a job built for three. That's not luck. That's
just knowing what each one's for, and trusting the gap between them isn't as wide as it looks from
the outside."
