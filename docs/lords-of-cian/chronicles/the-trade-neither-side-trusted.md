# The Trade Neither Side Trusted

*Locked canon, Batch 276, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1431`). Bane Alias Chronicle CI, wave 34. First formal
prisoner-exchange negotiation for this alias: a proactive diplomatic transaction rather than a
reactive tribunal defense (`MCD-939`) or a mercy-driven release (`MCD-431`).*

---

The proposal came through a neutral trade-broker who worked both sides of the conflict for a fee and
answered to neither: a straight exchange, three captured Directorate officers held by the column for
eleven rebels held in a Directorate camp, at a designated crossing neither army claimed, no weapons
carried by either party past the marker stones.

"Nobody trusts the other side to count straight," the broker told Bane plainly, before either party
had agreed to anything. "That's not an insult. It's just true of every exchange I've ever run. My job
is making sure both sides can verify it themselves instead of taking anyone's word."

Bane went to the crossing himself rather than sending Corren Halst, which the broker noted with some
surprise — most principals on either side preferred to stay at a safe remove from a trade that could
still go wrong. He didn't bring Onyx unsheathed or gear engaged; a negotiation conducted under a
loaded weapon wasn't a negotiation, it was a threat with paperwork.

The exchange itself was slow, procedural, and almost aggressively unglamorous. Names were checked
against lists twice, once by each side's own counters and once by the broker independently. Every
prisoner was walked forward individually rather than as a group, so no one could claim afterward that
a substitution had happened in the crowd. The Directorate's own escort commander insisted on
personally verifying that none of his returned officers had been harmed beyond what capture itself
required, and Bane let him check without objection, because an insistence on proof wasn't an insult
if the proof held up.

It took the better part of four hours to move fourteen people across a stretch of ground barely thirty
strides wide.

"You could have pushed harder," the broker observed afterward, once both columns had withdrawn in
opposite directions without incident. "Most principals get impatient by the second hour and start
cutting corners just to be done with it."

"Cutting a corner today is how the next exchange doesn't happen at all," Bane said. "If this works
exactly the way it's supposed to, boring and slow and fully accounted for, that's the best argument
either side has for trying it again next time instead of just fighting for the same people instead."

Neither side got everything it might have wanted from the terms. Both got exactly what the terms said
they would.
