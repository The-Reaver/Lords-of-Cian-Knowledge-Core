# The Ballad That Outgrew the Truth

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XXXIX, closing
wave 12. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The traveling singer had never met him, and made no secret of it, performing the ballad she'd composed
from secondhand accounts in three different settlements before she ever crossed paths with the
campaign itself. By the time Kanja actually heard it performed, the song had already grown past
anything that had literally happened — a burning district rebuilt not in eleven days but, in the
ballad's own verse, "before the ash had cooled," a five-site defense grown to "a hundred forges held
at once," each retelling adding weight the actual events hadn't required.

He listened to the whole thing without interrupting, in a tavern that had no idea the ballad's subject
was sitting in its own back corner.

"That's not quite how it happened," he told the singer afterward, catching her alone once the crowd had
thinned. "Most of it, anyway. The parts that are true are true. The parts that aren't are further from
true than I'd have expected."

She didn't apologize, studying him with more interest than alarm. "Songs aren't accounts, though.
People don't want the actual number of days. They want to feel something true about what the number
means. I could sing 'eleven days' and it wouldn't move anybody the way 'before the ash had cooled'
does, even though eleven days is the real, harder thing to have actually done."

He turned that over longer than he'd expected to. "You're saying the exaggeration serves something the
truth doesn't."

"I'm saying the truth and the feeling of the truth aren't always the same size, and most people only
ever get handed one of the two." She meant it without defensiveness, an artisan explaining her own
craft rather than excusing an error in it. "I can sing you the real eleven days, if you'd rather hear
it that way. But I don't think it's dishonest to sing the feeling instead. I think it's just a
different kind of accurate."

He let her keep the ballad as it was, unrequested and uncorrected, and found himself, months later,
in a settlement that had never seen the campaign firsthand, still able to hear its own children singing
a version of it that had drifted even further from what had actually happened — and did not, this time,
feel any need to correct that one either.
