# The Impostor Who Wore the Name Badly

*Locked canon, Batch 198, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-759`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XXXIV, wave 12 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

By the time Kanja's crew reached the glassworks district, the district owners had already paid a man
claiming to be the Industrial Myth a quiet sum to have their names struck from a ledger he swore
would otherwise ruin them, and they met the real one, days later, with the particular hostility of
people who believed they had already bought their way clear of the whole matter.

"We settled with you already," the senior owner said, before Ezio had finished opening the tent flap.
"Take your fee and your book elsewhere."

"I've never taken a fee from anyone in this campaign, and I've never carried a book I wouldn't show
you the moment you asked." Kanja set the ledger on the table, open, and turned it to face the owner
directly. "Whoever came before me showed you his book?"

The owner's silence answered before he did. The impostor had shown them nothing — only the threat of
a ledger, a claimed authority he'd never demonstrated, and a payment demanded to make the threat go
away, the whole arrangement built on fear of exposure rather than the exposure itself.

"Ask him to show you his figures next time, page by page, sourced to named workers, checked against
your own production logs," Kanja said. "If he does, compare it against what I'm about to compile
here and see if they agree. If he won't — and I'd guess he won't, because a man extorting a payment
to avoid documentation was never planning to produce any — then you already know which of us was
telling the truth about what we came here to do."

The real ledger took the better part of a week to compile, the same worst-off-first method, the same
cross-checked entries, the same open invitation to audit it that had answered every skeptic before
this one. The owner, still smarting from a payment made under false pretenses, watched the process
unfold in full view this time and found, grudgingly, that the difference wasn't in the two men's
manner or their claimed authority at all.

"He never once let us see his book," the owner admitted, near the end. "You've shown me yours every
day since you arrived, whether I asked or not."

"That was always the whole method. Anyone can claim to be feared. Being checkable is the part that
can't be faked."

The impostor was never found, never confronted, never punished — the district's own administrators
were left to pursue whatever recourse existed for the payment already made, if any did. What the
district gained instead was a second, unmistakably real account, open to audit from the first day,
that made the impostor's unverifiable one obsolete on its own terms.

first time -- an impostor extorting payment through the threat of documentation rather than
documentation itself. The resolution turns on transparency as the distinguishing trait, not identity
or confrontation; the impostor is never pursued or caught, a deliberately loose thread. No new named
characters. First entry in the Industrial Myth's twelfth wave.*
