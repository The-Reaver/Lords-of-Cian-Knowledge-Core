# Kiti Chronicle I: What Wore Down Instead

*Locked canon, Batch 92, 2026-09-09 (`MCD-355`). First entry in Kiti's own Chronicles, its
protagonist Owusu (`PH2-059`), not a Kanja Chronicle. Kanja appears only as a guest, present but
granted no command, no intervention, and no resolution credit, matching the established convention
from Xaragua Chronicle I (`MCD-334`) through Nyansa Chronicle I (`MCD-354`). Narration is
close-third on Owusu throughout, not Onyx of Oblivion. Completes a first Chronicle entry for all
five Detroit territories, and closes out territory-Chronicle coverage for every homage-era city.
Slots into no existing mainline battle — original homage-era material set in Kiti itself.*

---

Owusu had been blacklisted once, decades before the seat was ever his to hold, for the specific
crime of organizing men who weren't supposed to organize anything. He had learned, in the years that
cost him, exactly what attrition was built to do to a man, and exactly what it couldn't do to one
who'd already survived the worst version of it once and come out the other side still standing.

They tried attrition again now, differently dressed but the same instrument underneath: a
manufactured scandal, thin on fact and thick on repetition, run in the papers for three straight
weeks by men who'd never once managed to unseat him honestly and had stopped pretending, by this
point, that honesty was the goal. The old machine wanted him tired. Tired men made mistakes. Mistakes
gave an opening the machine could actually use.

The stranger had been a quiet presence around the office for most of that stretch, running errands
no one thought to question, present at enough late nights that the staff had stopped remarking on
him.

"They're not going to stop," the stranger said, one of those nights, watching Owusu read the same
manufactured allegation printed a third different way.

"I know they're not going to stop. Stopping was never the plan on either side of this." Owusu set
the paper down without any particular weight to the gesture. "They think every week of this wears
something away. What they don't understand — what nobody who's never actually been ground down by
something worse ever quite understands — is that a man who's already survived the real version of
this doesn't experience the fake version as wearing at all. It's not nothing. But it isn't what
they think it is either."

The scandal ran its course and died the way manufactured things generally do, starved for a fact
that never showed up to feed it, and Owusu's next election came in stronger than the one before —
not despite the three weeks of attrition, but in some way neither he nor the stranger could fully
articulate, because of them, the way a wall struck the same spot repeatedly sometimes ends up harder
at that spot than anywhere else on it.

"That's the gift, then," the stranger said, once the numbers came in. "Attrition makes you stronger
while you hold the seat."

"While I hold it. That part matters more than people give it credit for." Owusu looked out at the
office, quieter now that the immediate fight had passed. "I've watched other men in this position
make the mistake of thinking the strength travels with them once they're gone — that whatever let
them survive twenty years of this kind of pressure would still be there to lean on the day after
they left the chair. It won't be. I know that as certainly as I know anything about this. The day I'm
no longer sitting in this seat, whatever this is stops being mine to draw on, completely, the same
afternoon."

"You've thought about that day already."

"I think about it more than anyone watching me hold this seat for twenty years would probably
guess." Owusu picked the newspaper back up, folded it once, set it aside for good this time. "It's
purely institutional, whatever this is. Not personal strength I've built that travels with me
wherever I go afterward. The office itself is doing something to whatever wears at me while I'm in
it, and the office doesn't come home with a man once he's handed the keys to someone else. I intend
to hold this seat a long time. I don't intend to mistake that for something I'll still have the day
I don't."

The stranger was gone before the next session convened, the way he always seemed to be. He left
nothing carved, no phrase repeated hand to hand — only, the following week, one more folder of
constituent letters on Owusu's desk, sorted and answered in a hand no one on staff recognized,
already done before anyone thought to ask who'd done it.

completely the instant he leaves the seat, matching PH2-059's stated limitation exactly rather than

precedent for undetailed antagonists. No new named characters are introduced; no new proper nouns
