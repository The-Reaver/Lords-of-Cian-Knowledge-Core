# The Win He Wouldn't Take

*Locked canon, Batch 201, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-856`). The Crow King Alias Chronicle XLI, wave 14 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The Directorate column retreating from the Voss Corridor engagement was already beaten, its
tactician's own doctrine having cost it real advantage against genuine information arriving too
late to act on, its supply lines thin and its morale thinner — and a single well-placed false signal,
the crew's own planner calculated, would be enough to collapse the retreat entirely into a rout,
turning an orderly withdrawal into something considerably more costly for the column's exhausted,
already-defeated men.

"It would work," the planner said, laying out the operation with the same care as any other. "Clean,
fast, and it would end the corridor's whole campaign season a month early."

"I know it would work. I'm not going to do it."

"They're not surrendering. They're just retreating in good order. That's still a legitimate target."

"It is. I'm not questioning whether it's legitimate. I'm questioning whether I need to take it."
Kanja watched the column's distant dust on the horizon rather than the planner's growing frustration.
"They're beaten. Everyone in that column already knows it. Collapsing an orderly retreat into a
rout doesn't win anything strategic I don't already have. It just makes already-beaten men suffer
more on the way out, for a margin of advantage I don't actually need."

"That's not how anyone else fighting this war thinks about a retreating enemy."

"Maybe not. I've built an entire method around knowing precisely what a deception costs, and I don't
think that accounting stops mattering just because the target's already lost. I could win this
larger than I need to. I'd rather win it exactly as large as it actually needs to be, and let the
rest of them walk home instead of letting my own restraint decide the difference between beaten and
destroyed."

The column withdrew untouched beyond the corridor's own real losses, and the crew's after-action
review recorded, without further comment, an available advantage deliberately left unclaimed.

"You'll get asked why we didn't take it," the planner said, still not entirely reconciled to the
choice.

"Then I'll answer honestly. Some wins aren't worth taking just because they're available. I'd rather
be known for the ones I chose not to take than only for the ones I did."

*Continuity notes (not narrative): a genuinely new ethical register -- deliberate restraint against
a legitimate, already-defeated target, distinct from every prior entry's cost-weighing (which always
involved harm to bystanders or allies rather than mercy toward a beaten enemy). No new named
characters. Second entry in the Crow King's fourteenth three-Chronicle wave.*
