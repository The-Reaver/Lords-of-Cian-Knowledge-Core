# The Well That Went Dry

*Locked canon, Batch 279, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1454`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XCVII, wave 33, opening the wave. The alias's first drought/water-scarcity crisis, a civil-engineering register with zero enemy involvement, inverting every prior flooding entry.*

---

The first cistern went silent on a Tuesday, its draw-rope going slack a full body-length before it should have found water, and by Friday three more had followed it down into dry stone.

"We've fought water coming in from everywhere it shouldn't," Dol Maren said, turning the dry rope over in his hands like it had personally offended him. "I didn't think we'd end up fighting the opposite."

Kanja knelt at the cistern mouth and pressed both palms to the shaft wall, reading through rock that had gone quiet in a way he'd never had cause to listen for before -- no rushing water to track, no pressure differential to map, just an absence spreading slowly through strata that had held a reliable table for as long as anyone digging here could remember. The drought above ground had finally reached deep enough to starve the shallow aquifer the whole district's wells drew from, and worse than the thirst it threatened was the mortar: every repair crew still finishing shoring from the siege needed water to cure new joints, and a joint cured dry failed slow and silent, the kind of failure nobody would catch until the next real stress found it.

"We can't make water that isn't there," Dol Maren said. "Not even you."

"No. But I can find water that's still there and isn't where anyone's been looking." Kanja worked his way along three abandoned survey shafts nobody had drawn from in years, reading each one's strata against what he already knew of the district's original bedrock, until he found what he wanted two hundred feet down and forty feet east of the nearest dead well -- a deeper aquifer, untouched, sitting below a shelf of harder rock the original well-diggers had never had the tools to punch through.

"That's a lot of hard rock between here and water," Dol Maren said, studying the shelf.

"It's not that we can't reach it. It's that nobody before now needed to badly enough to try." Kanja set Obsidian Malice against the shelf's weakest seam, reading the grain the same patient way he'd learned quarrying fresh tunnel through solid bedrock years before, and worked the shaft down in careful, controlled strikes rather than a single brute effort that risked collapsing the very water table he was trying to reach. Four days of steady work opened a new well forty feet deeper than any other in the district, and it came up cold, clean, and steady.

Dol Maren tested the new draw-rope twice before he trusted it, then looked back at the dry cisterns they'd abandoned.

"You didn't fix the drought," he said.

"No. Nobody fixes a sky that won't rain." Kanja capped the new well's mouth with a proper stone housing, already sketching where a second and third shaft could reach the same deep table. "I just found where the water had been waiting the whole time for somebody to want it badly enough to dig this far."
