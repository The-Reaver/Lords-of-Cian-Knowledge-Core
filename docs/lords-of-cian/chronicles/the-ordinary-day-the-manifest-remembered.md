# The Ordinary Day the Manifest Remembered

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Captain Alias Chronicle, wave 14. Rebellion era, a
routine resupply day with no engagement of any kind. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters beyond already-locked crew.*

---

Garren Hask's ledger, decades later, would record the day only as an unremarkable entry between two
much larger ones — stores loaded, minor repairs logged, nothing worth a story. It was, by every
measure that made it into any account of the war, an entirely ordinary day.

Kanja spent most of it doing the least glamorous work available: hauling crates alongside the deck
hands rather than overseeing from a distance, helping Dol Maren re-caulk a seam that had started
weeping in the last storm, sitting with Garren Hask for the better part of an hour cross-checking a
tedious inventory discrepancy that turned out, after all that effort, to be a simple counting error
and nothing more sinister.

"You didn't need to spend your whole afternoon on a counting error," Hask said, once they'd finally
resolved it.

"I didn't need to. I wanted to. Most of what actually holds this crew together isn't the big days —
the battles, the rescues, the things that end up in a ledger with a name attached. It's days like
this one. Crates hauled. Seams caulked. An afternoon spent proving to you that even a boring
discrepancy is worth my attention, because your work matters whether or not anything dramatic is
happening around it."

By evening, nothing about the day had produced a story anyone would tell twice. That, in its own
quiet way, was the entire point — a captain who showed up the same for a counting error as he did
for a battle, and a crew that noticed the difference between someone who performed care only when it
would be seen, and someone who simply lived it, every ordinary day, whether or not it was ever
written down.

"Some captains only show their best selves when people are watching," Efa Gol said once, much later,
asked what had actually convinced her, all those years ago, that the reputation was real. "He was
exactly the same on the boring days. That's what did it, in the end. Not any single battle. Just
years of ordinary days that never once disappointed."
