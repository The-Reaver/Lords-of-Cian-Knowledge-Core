# The Harbor That Wouldn't Take Them

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Scourge Alias Chronicle, wave 7. Long Mask era,
age 128, V3 gear (Golden Terror era). Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose. No new named characters.*

---

Two hundred and six freed captives had nowhere to go by the third day after their rescue, because the
one deep-water harbor within safe range refused to open its gates to them, and no amount of Trinity
gear had ever been built to argue with a port authority's standing trade policy.

The harbor governor received him without ceremony, in a receiving room that made clear the meeting was
a formality being extended, not a negotiation being welcomed. "Two hundred mouths I didn't budget
for, arriving with a reputation attached that half my merchant houses are afraid of and the other
half resent. I have trade agreements with three nations who'd read granting you harbor as taking a
side in a war none of them want."

"I'm not asking you to take a side. I'm asking you to feed and shelter two hundred people for as long
as it takes to arrange onward passage, which I will pay for in full, in advance, from cargo already
sitting in your own bonded warehouse under a name your customs office has cleared twice this year
without ever connecting it to me."

The governor's expression shifted, arithmetic visibly running behind it. "That was you."

"Three seasons of it. You've taken my coin without knowing whose it was, and your city's done well
by the trade. I'm not asking for charity. I'm asking you to keep doing exactly what you've already
been doing, one more time, out loud instead of quietly."

"And if I say no?"

"Then I find another harbor, and this one loses a trading partner it's had for three years without
ever knowing it, over two hundred people it never had to actually see." He let that sit without
pressing further — no threat in it, only the plain shape of a choice the governor was going to have
to own either way. "I'd rather not make you choose that way. But I will, if that's what it takes."

The gates opened before sundown, quietly, under a cargo manifest that never once used the word
refugee, and the governor's own ledger — checked years later by a Trust auditor who found nothing
irregular in it at all — recorded the arrangement as an ordinary extension of an ordinary trade
relationship, exactly as both men had needed it to read.
