# The Vow Efa Gol Almost Broke

*Locked canon, Batch 258, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1207`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
LXIX, wave 23, closing the wave. A character-depth entry testing Efa Gol's own restraint against her
Black Trench trauma. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The man came aboard *The Receipt* under a flag of parley, claiming information on a raiding route in
exchange for safe passage, and Efa Gol recognized him within seconds of seeing his face clearly — one
of the two soldiers she remembered killing at the Black Trench had worn the same unit markings this
man now wore, and something in the set of his jaw put her back on that ravine floor with Tam Sullen's
body still warm under the cloth she'd covered him with.

She didn't kill him. That was the fact Garren Hask would later log plainly, without embellishment,
because it was the fact that mattered. But she came closer to it than she had in a decade of
commanding decoys and diversions with a level head others relied on precisely because it never
cracked — her hand on her blade, the man backing against the rail with no understanding of what he'd
walked into, and ten full seconds where nobody aboard was certain which way it would go.

Kanja didn't intervene. He watched from across the deck, close enough to stop it if it came to that,
far enough to let it be her choice rather than his. It was Pell Ostra, of all people, who broke the
silence — not with words to Efa Gol, but by quietly stepping between her and the man just far enough
to give Efa Gol something else to look at besides the target.

"He's not one of the two," Efa Gol said afterward, once the man had given his information and been
put back ashore unharmed. "I know that. I knew it while I was standing there. It just didn't matter
for about ten seconds, and that's the part that scares me."

"The doctrine doesn't work because nobody on this fleet ever wants to break it," Kanja told her. "It
works because when someone comes close, the rest of us are still standing there. You didn't break it.
That's the only fact that goes in the ledger."

Garren Hask logged exactly that — not the ten seconds, not what almost happened, only what did:
information received, man returned ashore unharmed, restraint held. Efa Gol asked him afterward,
privately, to log the near-thing too, in her own words, so it wouldn't just disappear into a clean
entry that made it look easier than it had been.
