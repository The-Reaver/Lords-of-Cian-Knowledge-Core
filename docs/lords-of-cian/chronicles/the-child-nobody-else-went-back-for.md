# The Child Nobody Else Went Back For

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Captain Alias Chronicle, wave 14. Rebellion era, a
brief stop at a coastal settlement, no larger engagement involved. Not a territory Chronicle.
Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

It wasn't a battle, or an ambush, or anything that would ever appear alongside the Twenty-Two
Victories or any campaign ledger. It was a single afternoon at a fishing settlement the crew had
stopped at only to resupply water, when word went around that a child had wandered off past the tide
line toward a stretch of rocks that flooded completely at high tide, and the settlement's own search
had already given up as the water started rising.

"We looked for an hour," the child's mother said, hoarse from crying and shouting both. "Nobody's
seen her since. The tide's coming in faster than we can search."

Kanja didn't wait for the crew to organize a formal search. He went himself, alone, into water
rising past his knees and then his waist, working the rock line the way the settlement's exhausted
searchers no longer had the strength or daylight left to work it properly.

He found her twenty minutes later, wedged into a gap between two rocks where she'd gotten stuck
trying to retrieve a dropped toy, water already at her chin, frightened past speech. He got her out,
carried her back across water now chest-deep on him, and set her down in front of her mother without
ceremony, without anyone from the settlement even fully realizing who had just waded out to do it.

"That's not what I expected from the stories," one of the settlement's elders said afterward, once
word spread of who the strange visitor had actually been. "I thought a man with reputation like
yours would send people. Not go himself, for one child, when nothing about it mattered to the war."

"It mattered to her. It mattered to her mother. That's the whole measure I use — not whether
something's large enough to matter to a campaign, but whether it matters to the person standing in
front of me asking for help." He was still soaked, still catching his breath, entirely unconcerned
with how small the moment might look set against anything else the crew had done. "The war doesn't
get to decide what's worth showing up for. I do."
