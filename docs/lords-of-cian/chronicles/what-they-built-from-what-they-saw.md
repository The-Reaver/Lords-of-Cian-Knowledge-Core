# What They Built From What They Saw

*Locked canon, Batch 284, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1501`). Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XCIX, wave 33,
closing the wave. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241). A detailed,
battle-intense Trinity combat showcase closing the wave. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose.*

---

The device the Directorate fielded against a reopened coastal drift looked, at first glance, like a
crude twin of Mafesto — a bulky iron harness worn over a soldier's forearm, built by an engineer who
had studied the aftermath of three separate engagements closely enough to sketch, with real
precision, what a grounded blow's scorch-pattern looked like without ever seeing the mechanism that
produced it.

It worked, in the sense that it did something. It failed in every sense that mattered. The harness
took the first blow it was used to block and rather than grounding the force through a calibrated
frame built specifically to carry it, fed the whole charge directly into the wearer's own arm,
shattering it from the inside and dropping him before Kanja had thrown a second strike. The engineer
watching from cover, close enough to see his own design fail exactly the way three separate advisory
memos he'd ignored had warned it might, kept watching anyway, taking notes on the failure with the
same care he'd taken sketching the success.

Kanja didn't let the observation continue unchallenged. Obsidian Malice's discharge collapsed the
observation post's cover before a second note went down; Onyx of Oblivion's Whisper of Shadows put
him at the engineer's side inside four seconds, close enough that the man had time to close his own
notebook but not to burn it. Mafesto, still carrying the charge from the failed harness's blow,
grounded it cleanly through the drift's own stone rather than let it go to waste, the difference
between the copy's failure and the original's function laid out for the engineer to see up close,
whether he wanted the lesson or not.

"You built the shape of it," Kanja said, turning the ruined harness over once before setting it down.
"You never asked what the shape was actually for." The engineer, notebook confiscated rather than
destroyed — Garren Hask insisted on reading it in full before it was burned, cataloguing exactly how
close the Directorate's own reverse-engineering had come — didn't argue the point. He'd broken a
man's arm proving it wrong himself.

"They'll try again," Hask said afterward, the notebook's contents copied and the original given over
to flame. "This one just told them exactly what not to build next time."

"Then we've bought however long it takes them to unlearn everything this taught them," Kanja said.
"That's most engagements, in the end. Time bought, not time won outright." He had the ruined harness
kept rather than melted down, set aside in The Anvil's own hold as a reminder of how much of the
Trinity's function had never been the metal at all.
