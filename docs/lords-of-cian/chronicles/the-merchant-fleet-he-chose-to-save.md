# The Merchant Fleet He Chose to Save

*Locked canon, Batch 137, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-459`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle VIII.
Rebellion era, age 29, the Gale Straits. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material.
Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Sephtis's prediction that week carried no tactical target at all — a storm building over open water
with no Directorate fleet anywhere near its projected path, and no engagement for the Storm That
Walks' reputation to attach itself to. What the prediction did find, three days out from landfall,
was a civilian merchant convoy sailing directly into the storm's eventual path with no warning system
of their own capable of telling them so.

Kanja took the fleet out anyway.

There was no battle to fight, no enemy to intercept — only a hard, unglamorous three days of running
the fleet's own faster vessels ahead of the merchant convoy's route, signaling a warning no merchant
captain in these waters had any established reason to trust from an unfamiliar fleet, and then,
once the warning was accepted, escorting forty merchant vessels through a course correction that
would cost them a week's delay but keep every one of them clear of the storm's actual landfall.

"This isn't the kind of story that gets repeated the way a battle does," Sephtis observed, watching
the merchant convoy's lanterns alter course in the gathering dark. "No enemy defeated. No treasure
recovered. Just forty ships that quietly didn't sink because we spent three days making sure they
wouldn't."

"That's exactly why it's worth doing." Kanja watched the last of the merchant fleet complete its turn.
"Every battle we've fought has built a reputation people repeat because it's dramatic. This builds
something quieter — forty captains who'll tell their own crews, for years, about the time a fleet with
no reason to care about them spent three days making sure they lived. That kind of story travels
slower than a battle does. It travels further, though, eventually."

The storm made landfall two days later, exactly where Sephtis had predicted, empty water where the
merchant convoy would otherwise have been. None of the forty captains ever learned the fleet's exact
identity beyond the black sails they'd been warned by — only that something out on the Gale Straits
had chosen, for reasons of its own, to spend three unglamorous days keeping strangers alive.
