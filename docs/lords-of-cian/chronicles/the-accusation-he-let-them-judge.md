# The Accusation He Let Them Judge

*Locked canon, Batch 193, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-603`). Captain Alias Chronicle XXVIII, wave 10 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The merchant arrived at the docks loudly enough that half the crew heard the accusation before
Kanja did: that the crew had taken a cut of a relief shipment meant for a raider-damaged settlement,
skimming supplies for themselves before the rest reached anyone who needed them.

It wasn't true. Kanja knew it wasn't true. He also knew that knowing it wasn't true, and saying so
himself, would look exactly like what a guilty man would say.

"Let the council hear it," he told Garren Hask, rather than answering the accusation directly.
"Full manifest, full delivery record, and let the merchant say his piece to them, not to me."

The crew's dispute council — the same body that had settled smaller quarrels for months — convened
within the hour, and heard the merchant's claim in full: a shortfall he'd calculated between what
was loaded and what arrived, enough to suggest deliberate skimming if the numbers were taken at face
value.

Garren Hask's ledger told a different story — every crate logged at loading, every crate logged at
delivery, and a shortfall that matched exactly the weight lost when two crates had gone overboard
during a storm three days into the run, an incident already recorded, already reported to the
settlement at the time.

The council ruled the accusation unfounded within the hour, and more importantly, ruled it in front
of the merchant himself, with the actual numbers laid bare rather than Kanja's word alone standing
against his.

"You could have just told him you didn't do it," the merchant said afterward, more subdued now that
the numbers had settled it. "Your word carries weight. Everyone says so."

"My word carries weight because I don't spend it convincing people I'm innocent of things I'm
accused of. I let the record speak, and if the record ever said otherwise, I'd want it believed over
me just the same. That's the only way trust like this stays honest instead of just being loyalty
dressed up as something sturdier."
