# The Ledger Garren Hask Still Kept

Garren Hask was old now by any measure that mattered to him personally, though this world's own
long reckoning of a life meant he still had decades of clear-eyed work left in him, and he'd kept a
private ledger of his own the entire time the Scourge legend grew into something the wider world
no longer connected to a Rebellion that most people alive had never personally lived through.

"You still keep count," Kanja said, recognizing the familiar tally-format the moment Hask set the
worn ledger down between them, decades of entries in a hand that had grown careful and slow with
age but never less precise.

"Somebody has to. The legend doesn't need a ledger. Legends do fine on their own, growing whatever
shape people need them to grow into." Hask opened to the current page, a running tally that had
outlived most of the men who'd started it alongside him. "But you asked me once, a lifetime ago, to
be the one who kept count of what this war actually costs and what it actually recovers. I never
stopped. Ships saved. Captives freed. The numbers the smoke and the amber eyes never show anybody,
because nobody watching a legend from the outside thinks to ask for the arithmetic underneath it."

"What's the current count?"

Hask told him — a precise figure, cross-checked and updated across decades, that Kanja hadn't heard
spoken aloud in years, having long since stopped needing the number for himself the way he'd once
needed it as a much younger man learning what the Trench Monarch's title was supposed to mean.
"You don't ask anymore," Hask observed, watching his face. "You used to ask every season."

"I know what the number roughly is now. I don't need it confirmed the way I used to." Kanja looked
at the ledger's worn cover, decades of careful keeping represented in a single object. "I think I
stopped needing it the same year I stopped being sure the man wearing this coat and the boy who
asked you to start counting were still entirely the same person."

"They're not entirely the same person. Nobody's the same person across this many decades, whatever
this world's lifespans let us pretend otherwise." Hask closed the ledger gently. "But the count's
never once lied to either version of you, and that's the part that's stayed constant the whole time.
I'll keep it as long as I've got hands steady enough to write. Somebody should still know the real
number, underneath whatever the legend's grown into by the time this is all finally over."
