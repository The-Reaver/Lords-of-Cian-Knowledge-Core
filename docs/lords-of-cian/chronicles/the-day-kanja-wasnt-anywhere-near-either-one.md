# The Day Kanja Wasn't Anywhere Near Either One

*Locked canon, Batch 282, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1485`). Crow King Alias Chronicle CI, wave 34. Two
simultaneous operations run entirely by the lineage, with Kanja aware of neither until both were
already finished. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

He found out about both of them the same way: over dinner, three days after the fact, told to him in
the same unhurried tone people use for news that has already stopped being urgent.

The apprentice and the third generation had run an extraction at one end of the province using the
Braid, three interleaved false signals against a garrison that had grown complacent since the last time
anyone had tested it. At the other end of the province, on the same night, the fourth generation and
the fifth had walked a corrupt tax assessor's own falsified accounts back into a public tribunal until
lying against the record stopped being worth the man's effort -- the same verified-truth method the
craft had used for years, run this time without a single person from the original teaching line present
to oversee it.

Kanja had known about neither operation before either one happened. Nobody had asked his judgment,
sought his approval, or sent word ahead. The four of them had simply looked at two problems that both
needed solving on the same night, split naturally along the two methods each pair knew best, and gone.

"You could have told me," he said, without much weight behind it, more curious than wounded.

"Told you what," the third generation said. "That we were doing exactly what you taught us to do,
the way you taught us to do it, somewhere you weren't standing? You've never once needed to be in the
room for the craft to work. That was always the entire design of teaching it to more than one person."

"I know that," Kanja said. "I taught it that way on purpose. I suppose I didn't expect to find out how
completely it had worked from two people telling me about it after supper."

The apprentice, watching him work through it, recognized something in his face she hadn't expected to
see there -- not doubt, and not pride exactly either, but the particular unease of a man realizing a
thing he'd built no longer needed him even to know it was running.

"That's not a loss," she told him quietly, later, once the others had gone. "You didn't stop being the
reason this exists. You just stopped being required for it to keep existing. Those aren't the same
thing, even though they feel like they should be."
