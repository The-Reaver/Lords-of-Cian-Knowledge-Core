# The Man Carrying Two Masters

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XLIX, wave 17. Rebellion era, during the active Sewer War of Killane (MCD-234, age 20). Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The schedules kept arriving on the surface a day ahead of the crew's own postings — small enough
that no one but Kanja, cross-checking his own maps against the Trust's shifting patrol patterns,
would ever have noticed the pattern was too clean to be luck.

He watched the crew for three days before he found the man: a brace-setter, steady-handed and well
liked, who left the work site each evening by a route that added twenty minutes for no reason anyone
had ever questioned.

"You don't have to tell me who's holding them," Kanja said, catching him alone at the mouth of a
side passage, keeping his voice level and his hands visible. "I already know it isn't money. A man
selling information for coin doesn't look at his own crew the way you've been looking at this one for
three weeks."

The brace-setter's face went the color of old mortar. "My sister. They took her the week the siege
started. Told me what to watch, when to report it, and what happens if I stop."

"Then you're not the problem I came here to solve." Kanja crouched to his level, deliberately smaller
than his own size allowed. "The problem is a woman being held to make an honest man do a dishonest
thing. I can find her. I can't promise it happens tonight, but I can promise it happens before this
siege ends."

"And until then? I keep feeding them what I've been feeding them?"

"Until then, you feed them exactly what I tell you to feed them." Kanja had already begun reshaping
the week's real schedule in his head against a second, false one — troop movements shifted by half a
day, a tunnel section marked active that had been sealed for a week. "You're not stopping. You're
switching which side the lie serves. Every report you send from tonight on helps this crew more than
it hurts it, and neither your handler nor your sister's guards will ever know the difference."

The brace-setter didn't ask what happened to the men who'd have shot him for exactly this if the crew
chief ever learned the truth without the rest of the story attached. Kanja didn't offer that answer
unprompted either — some ledgers, he'd decided, only needed two names on them until there was a reason
for a third.

His sister came out of a supply warehouse eleven days later, in a rescue that never mentioned the
tunnel war at all, carried out by a different arm of the resistance entirely, on information Kanja had
passed sideways rather than up his own chain. The brace-setter kept working the same section, kept
filing the same false reports for another week out of habit before anyone told him he could stop, and
never once told the rest of the crew what he'd carried alone for a month.

"You could've had him removed the day you found him," the crew chief said afterward, once the whole
shape of it had finally reached her secondhand. "Safer for everyone."

"Safer isn't the only thing I'm building down here." Kanja checked a brace the man himself had set
two days after his sister came home — steady work, honest hands, no different than before. "He didn't
choose to be a threat to this crew. Somebody made that choice for him and held it over his head. I'd
rather spend eleven days fixing that than one afternoon punishing a man for something that was never
really his to refuse."
