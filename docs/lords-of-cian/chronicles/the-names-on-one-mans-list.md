# The Names on One Man's List

*Locked canon, Batch 254, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1119`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXXXIX, wave 30. A reflective
entry through Corren Halst on the functional purpose of Kanja's multiple aliases, told entirely inside
the fiction.*

---

Corren Halst had taken over the column's operational records from Garren Hask for the evening, a
routine cross-check the two of them traded off every few months, and it was somewhere in the middle
of counting entries that he stopped and said it out loud, half to himself.

"You know how many names this column's fought and worked under, since I've been counting? Not just
Bane. All of it, going back to before I ever joined you."

Bane didn't look up from what he was doing. "More than most people would guess."

"That's not an answer."

"It's the honest one. I stopped keeping the number in my head a while ago. It wasn't useful to me the
way it might be useful to you, going through a ledger." Bane finally set down what he'd been working
on. "Why does it matter tonight?"

"Because I keep thinking it should feel like something's been hidden, all these different names for
one man. And it doesn't. It feels more like the opposite."

"It is the opposite." Bane considered how to put it plainly. "A name isn't a mask over something
truer underneath it. It's a door. Some people will walk through the one that looks like restraint.
Some need the one that looks like fear. Some, further off still, will only ever trust the one that
looks like a title their own crew gave a man rather than anything the Directorate named him. None of
those people are wrong to need a different door than the next person needs. I'm not less myself
behind any of them. I'm just not asking every single person on this continent to trust the exact same
face before they'll let me help them."

"That's a lot of doors for one man to hold open."

"It's fewer than the number of people who've needed one of them to be open at exactly the right
moment." Bane picked the work back up. "The names aren't the truth split into pieces, Corren. They're
the truth, offered enough different ways that it can actually reach the people who need it."
