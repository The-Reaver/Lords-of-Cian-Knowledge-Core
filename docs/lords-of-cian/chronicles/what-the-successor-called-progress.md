# What the Successor Called Progress

*Locked canon, Batch 261, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1303`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LXXXIV, wave 28, closing the wave. The completed aqueduct becomes a small symbol of détente between the two sides. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The successor found Kanja at the aqueduct's own midpoint some weeks after the repair, standing where he'd once stood to read the failing structure, now simply watching water move through a span both sides had finally stopped arguing over.

"I've had three separate reports cross my desk since the repair," the successor said, joining him at the rail. "Two calling it a dangerous precedent. One calling it the first genuinely honest thing either side has managed to build together in longer than most of my officers have been alive."

"Which one do you believe?"

"All three, honestly. That's mostly what progress looks like from where I sit — not a clean consensus, just enough people willing to keep doing the thing even while other people keep arguing it was a mistake." The successor watched the water a moment longer. "The old general used to say your doctrine's real weapon was that nobody could argue with an account that told the truth regardless of who was asking. I used to think he meant that as a compliment to how dangerous you were to fight. I've come to think he also meant it as a warning about how dangerous you'd eventually be to *not* fight — because an honest account, once enough people have seen it work, is hard to un-know."

"Is that what this was? A warning coming true?"

"I don't know yet. I know that eleven days ago, engineers from both our sides stood on the same span and fixed something together instead of arguing about who broke it, and none of the disasters my own command predicted actually happened." The successor allowed himself something close to a genuine smile, brief and unpracticed, as though the expression itself hadn't gotten much use lately. "I'm not going to pretend one aqueduct ends anything larger than itself. I've been fighting you, one form or another, since the report that ended the old general's career. I know better than to call a single bridge peace."

"What do you call it, then?"

"Proof of concept." The successor pushed off from the rail, preparing to head back toward whatever business had actually brought him this far from his own lines. "That's the only word I've found honest enough to use for it. The old general spent a career learning your doctrine couldn't be beaten by force. I'd like to spend whatever's left of mine finding out how far it can be trusted with something gentler than that instead."

He didn't offer a handshake, and Kanja didn't extend one — the moment didn't need the gesture, the water moving cleanly beneath them already saying most of what either man would have meant by it.
