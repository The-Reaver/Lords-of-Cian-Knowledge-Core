# What the Enemy Learned to Rebuild

*Locked canon, Batch 213, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-922`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XLVII, wave 16. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Callum Breck brought the report himself rather than send it through the usual chain, which told Kanja
something about its contents before he'd read a line of it.

"A garrison Directorate scouts hit last season," Breck said. "Burned to the waterline, or near enough.
Trust records put the rebuild at nine weeks, going off every prior garrison they've had to raise from
nothing. This one came back in nineteen days."

Kanja read the intercepted report twice before he said anything. Staged material caching ahead of the
work rather than after. Crews rotated on fixed intervals instead of run to exhaustion. Every hand
assigned one task and drilled on nothing else until the task was reflex. None of it was new to him. All
of it was his.

"They didn't invent this," he said finally.

"No. Way I hear it, they pulled it off a captured smith who watched us work a season back, before
anyone thought to keep him from carrying it out with him when he escaped. Which he did — escaped, that
is. Nobody handed it to them on purpose."

"Doesn't much matter how it traveled. It traveled." Kanja set the report down, the particular
discomfort of it settling somewhere he couldn't immediately name. "I built a method and taught it to
anyone who'd stand still long enough to learn it, on purpose, because I wanted it to outlive me and
keep working in a hundred places I'd never personally reach. I don't get to be surprised when 'anyone'
turns out to include people I'd never have chosen."

"So what do we do about it?"

"Nothing we can do. You can't unteach a method once someone's watched it work. I could stop teaching
it in the open, and the next honest settlement that needs it would suffer for the ones who don't
deserve it." He turned the report over, reading the labor tally at its foot a second time. "Conscripted
crews. Every hand on that garrison's rebuild was there because they'd be punished for refusing, not
because they chose to be."

"Nineteen days is nineteen days, whoever's swinging the hammer."

"Is it." Kanja looked up. "Read further down. Half the tally's marked 'replaced mid-task' — workers
pulled off and swapped for fresh ones partway through, more than the method should ever need. Ask
yourself why a crew running a method built to move fast would need that many mid-task replacements."

Breck read it again, slower. "Sabotage. Foot-dragging. Enough of it, spread thin enough not to get
caught outright, that they had to keep swapping people to hold the pace at all."

"The method travels. The reason people work it the way we work it doesn't — not the same way, not
without the same thing underneath it. I never taught anyone to move fast. I taught them to move fast
for something they'd chosen to rebuild for themselves. Take the choosing out, and you can copy every
motion exactly and still not get what we get." He didn't sound entirely settled by his own answer, and
didn't pretend otherwise. "Doesn't mean I like knowing it's out there being used the other way. I just
don't get to decide who's watching, once I've decided to teach in the open. Never did."
