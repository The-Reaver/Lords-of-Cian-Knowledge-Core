# The Calm Bought for a Handshake

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LV, wave 19. Long Mask era, before "The Strait They Named for a Man Who Read It." Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. Names Sephtis's successor; no new named characters.*

---

Two delegations, from parties that had spent a generation raiding each other's coastal settlements,
had finally agreed to meet and talk terms — on the condition, insisted on by both sides separately,
that neither would arrive first and neither would arrive exposed. The meeting site sat on a small
island in contested water, reachable only by open crossing, and either delegation's fleet approaching
alone through that water would have looked, to the other's lookouts, exactly like the opening move of
an attack.

The solution wasn't a show of force. It was a shared window of calm water, called jointly, that let
both delegations' escort vessels cross into sight of each other at the same moment, under conditions
too gentle to hide an ambush in, with no weather at their backs either side could have used as cover
for treachery.

"You're not calling this for us," the successor said, when Kanja asked her to make the reading. "You're
calling it for both of them at once. If I'm wrong for either side, the talks never happen, and it
won't matter whose fault the storm was."

"Then don't be wrong for either side," Kanja said. "This isn't a fleet you're protecting. It's a
conversation that only gets one chance to start."

She called a four-hour window, flat and windless, long enough for both crossings and the first
exchange of terms beneath a truce flag neither side fully trusted yet. The window held. Both
delegations arrived within minutes of each other, in full sight of one another the whole crossing,
with no cover available to either for the kind of betrayal both still half expected.

The Trinity was present on Kanja's own vessel throughout, visibly, and used for nothing at all — not a
deterrent brandished, simply a fact both delegations could see and account for in their own
calculations without anyone needing to say a word about it.

"You could have used that calm to move against either of them," one delegation's senior negotiator
said afterward, watching the fair weather hold exactly as promised. "Instead you handed us both the
one thing neither of us was willing to give the other first."

"The calm wasn't a weapon today," Kanja said. "It was a place to stand where neither of you had to be
brave first. That's worth more than anything I could have done with a storm."

The talks did not resolve everything. They resolved enough to continue, which was more than either
delegation had believed possible walking in.
