# The Collective That Borrowed His Name

*Locked canon, Batch 268, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1401`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XCII, wave 31. The alias's first institutional-legitimacy-poaching entry -- a rival post-war collective invokes the reputation the tradesmen's association's own charter deliberately refuses to name.*

---

Garren Hask brought him the pamphlet before anyone else in the district had, the way he brought Kanja anything with numbers that didn't balance.

"Someone's started a second collective," Hask said, "three streets over from the association's own hall. Calling itself the Founders' Guild. Their recruitment sheet claims direct endorsement from the Blue-Collar Titan himself -- unofficial, unquoted, entirely made up, but printed clean enough that half the district's believing it."

It wasn't the first time the alias's reputation had been used without permission, but every prior instance -- an impersonator, an extortion racket, a forged repair order -- had used the name to threaten or steal. This one wanted only to borrow legitimacy, dressing an ordinary trade collective in the appearance of his blessing to draw membership away from the tradesmen's association he'd deliberately founded without his own name attached to it at all.

"They're not doing anything wrong, exactly," Kanja said, reading the pamphlet twice. "Wrong association. Wrong problem to solve with a lie, when the honest version of the same claim would've worked just as well."

He found the collective's founder training three new members in a rented yard, competent work by every measure Kanja could read in it -- decent joint-fitting, honest material, nothing fraudulent in the craft itself, whatever the recruitment pamphlet claimed. The founder saw him coming and went pale before a word was spoken.

"I never said you endorsed us outright," the man said quickly. "The sheet implies it. People wanted to believe it. I let them."

"I know. That's the part I actually mind." Kanja set the pamphlet down between them rather than crumple it. "You're a real tradesman running a real collective. You didn't need my name for any of that to be true. You borrowed it because you didn't trust the truth to be enough on its own."

He didn't demolish the man's collective or expose him publicly, both well within easy reach for an accusation this clean. Instead he did the one thing the association's own charter had insisted on from its very first day: he refused, in front of the man's own recruits, to confirm the endorsement at all -- neither denying the collective's worth nor granting it his name, making unmistakably clear that neither was his to give.

"The association I helped start doesn't carry my name because the day it needs my name to survive is the day it's already failed," Kanja told him. "Yours doesn't need it either. Build the reputation the slow way. It holds up better."

Within a season, the Founders' Guild had dropped the false claim from its recruitment sheet entirely, and its membership, smaller but honestly earned, held steady rather than collapsing the way a reputation built on a lie eventually does.

"You could have just shut them down," Hask said afterward, still turning the old pamphlet over in his hands.

"I could have. It would've proven the association only survives by force, which is the exact opposite of what I built it to prove." Kanja handed the ledger back. "Let the truth outcompete the lie on its own terms. It's slower. It's also the only version of a win that actually holds."
