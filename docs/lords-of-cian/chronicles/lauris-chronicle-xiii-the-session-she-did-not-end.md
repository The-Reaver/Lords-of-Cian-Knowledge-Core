# Lauris Chronicle XIII: The Session She Did Not End

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1630`). Thirteenth entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, the sixth entry of Strand K, drafted as part of a 50-entry wave across eight
strand-agents under Abad's blanket authorization. Dramatizes the no-ceiling calibration period of her
first century at Vask Karth-Ven (age 14-114, `MCD-168`) directly for the first time -- the
Sister-Hold's abandonment of projected density ceilings, the multi-instructor rotation her sustained
non-fatigue forced on her training, and the already-locked quotes from Veska Karth-Ven and Voreth
Karth-Ven (`MCD-1558`) placed in their full original context for the first time. No new named
characters. Full text below.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Vask Karth-Ven, age twenty: "Eleven substitutions today. I did not count them at
the time. Voreth counted them for me, afterward, and seemed more tired by the counting than I was by
the day."*

I have set down already how Lauris's first century at Vask Karth-Ven began -- the introductions, the
floor, Voreth's dry wager about who was lying to whom. What I have not yet set down is how that
century ended, and I think it is, of everything in her early training, the single fact that best
explains the combatant Ezio Valcari would eventually hand an unread report to, centuries and a whole
ocean of distance later.

The Sister-Hold's original training model, inherited from Karth-Ven's own long cooperative-era
tradition, assumed a projectable ceiling -- a density figure toward which a combatant's development
curved, flattening as it approached, the way every prior student Veska Karth-Ven had trained across
her own long career had flattened in time. Lauris's early readings gave the Hold no such curve to
work from. 4,200x at twenty. 5,600x at thirty. 8,400x at fifty. 11,500x at one hundred. Tiramen, whose
particular discipline was building methodology for problems without precedent, is recorded in the
Vask's own training log abandoning ceiling projection formally on the morning of Lauris's
hundred-and-first year, with a note that reads, in its entirety: *We are no longer training toward a
number. We are training toward whatever number arrives next.*

The consequence of this, more than the density figures themselves, is what the Hold found hardest to
accommodate, and it is the detail I find myself circling back to whenever I try to explain to someone
who has only fought beside her what makes Lauris different from every other density combatant I have
catalogued. She did not fatigue. Not slowly, not eventually, not after some heroic threshold -- simply
not at all, in any way the Vask's existing training architecture had a method for measuring. A
training session that would have exhausted any prior Karth-Ven student within an hour would find
Lauris, at the two-hour mark, precisely as capable as she had been at the first minute, and the
session would not end because she required rest. It ended because her instructors did.

I have the log for her twentieth year, the entry behind the fragment I have placed at the head of
this account. Eleven substitutions in a single training day -- Veska, Tiramen, Voreth, and eight
further instructors drawn in from elsewhere in the Vask, rotating through in shifts the way a relay
team rotates a single burden among many bodies, because no single body among them could sustain what
a fourteen-year-old, and then a twenty-year-old, could sustain without apparent effort. Voreth's own
account of that day, preserved alongside Lauris's, is the one I return to most: *I have trained since
I was young enough to remember nothing before it. I have never, in all that training, needed to be
relieved by a colleague in the middle of a session with a child. I was relieved four times today. She
asked, afterward, whether she had done something wrong.*

It is Veska's line from that same period, though, that the Vask's own archive preserved most widely,
repeated by Karth-Ven instructors for generations after as something close to doctrine: *The child
does not stop. We end the session because we tire. She continues. She would continue indefinitely if
we did not stop her.* I have quoted this line before, in an earlier account, without the context
behind it -- I set the context down now because I think the line means something different once you
understand it was not admiration. It was, by every account I have gathered from the Hold's own
internal correspondence of that year, something closer to concern. A body that does not need to stop
is not, to a civilization that had spent seventy-five thousand years learning the hard way what
happens when something exceeds a system built to contain it, an unqualified gift.

Lauris herself, by her own record, understood none of this as concern at the time. She understood
only that the sessions kept running longer than her instructors seemed to want them to, and that she
was, without exception, the last person in the room each time one of them finally ended.
