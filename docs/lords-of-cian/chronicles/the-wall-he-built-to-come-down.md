# The Wall He Built to Come Down

*Locked canon, Batch 203, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-891`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XLVI, wave 16. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The causeway crossing had been the first real structural work Kanja did in Killane, three weeks into
the siege — a reinforced span over the district's widest flooded gallery, rebuilt from a near-collapse
into something that could bear a loaded cart without groaning. Now the crew chief was asking him to
bring it down.

"The Trust's relief column crosses there or nowhere else for six miles. If that span's standing when
they reach it, they resupply the garrison and this whole siege starts over." The crew chief didn't
enjoy asking. "You built that crossing back from nothing. I know what I'm asking."

"You're asking me to be as careful taking it down as I was putting it up. That's not the same as
asking me to be careless about it." Kanja studied the span he'd reinforced with his own hands,
tracing the load path he'd once corrected timber by timber. "A demolition done badly brings down
more than the target — cracks the gallery ceiling above it, maybe drops half the district's water
table into a section we still need. Done right, it takes exactly the crossing and nothing else."

He spent the better part of a day reading his own work in reverse — identifying the three load-bearing
points he'd deliberately overbuilt weeks earlier, the ones that would need to fail cleanly rather than
the dozen incidental supports around them that had to stay intact for the gallery itself to hold.
Obsidian Malice's discharge, calibrated to a fraction of what it could deliver in open combat, went
into each point in sequence rather than a single overwhelming strike — enough force to shear a
load-bearing joint precisely, not enough to shatter stone that had no part in the failure he needed.

"You're demolishing it the same way you'd repair it," a crew member said, watching him check the
second point before committing to the third. "Same care. Just backwards."

"It's not really backwards. It's the same question either direction — what does this structure
actually need, and what's the smallest true answer to that question." Kanja set the final charge,
satisfied with the placement. "Building it, the answer was three sound joints holding a load. Taking
it down, the answer's the same three joints failing cleanly so nothing around them has to fail with
them. I didn't stop being the man who built this crossing just because the district needs it gone
today."

The span came down in under four seconds, exactly as reasoned — the three points failing together,
the gallery ceiling above holding without so much as a settling crack, the water table undisturbed.
The relief column, six hours later, found no crossing at all and no safe alternative for six miles in
either direction.

"First thing you've built in this district that you were glad to watch fall," the crew chief said,
surveying the clean break.

"I'm not glad. I'm satisfied it fell the right way." Kanja looked at the water where the causeway had
stood, already reshaping the district's tactical map in his head. "There's a difference. I'll build
it back once this siege doesn't need it gone anymore. Until then, I'd rather be the one who takes it
down carefully than let someone else take it down badly."
