# Daba Chronicle LVII: The Correction That Announced Itself

*Locked canon, Batch 350, 2026-10-02 (`MCD-1878`). Daba Chronicle LVII. Close-third on Daba, no
dedicated narrator, matching the series' established convention. Dramatizes Harek Vondel's killing
by 1804 (`CC-147`/`MCD-1855`) for the first time -- queued since Batch 312, explicitly flagged in
Daba's own profile doc as a candidate beat for a future wave. Set during the network's
post-founding, pre-dormancy years (mature network era, after Block F's own timeframe). Reuses
already-locked 1804 members Kether and Rhyne Cadec; no new named characters. The kill is deliberately
not attributed to Kanja or any Alias, consistent with `MCD-1569`'s standing separation. Abad's
approval: "lock it."*

---

Harek Vondel had corrected eleven settlements in the Shattered Kingdoms' rural interior over four
years, and he thought of each one the way another man might think of a promotion he had personally
arranged: a line in a career ledger, accomplishment recorded and filed. His method was always the
same, and he was proud that it was the same, because a method repeated and perfected was, to him,
the whole definition of professionalism -- send word three days ahead that the correction is coming,
let fear do the softening, then arrive to find a district too frightened to resist and too
demoralized to hide anything worth finding.

He announced the twelfth correction the way he'd announced the first eleven: openly, days in
advance, confident that the warning itself was the weapon.

Kether read the warning the morning it reached her, and understood within the hour that she was
looking at the first piece of real information 1804 had ever been handed for free.

"He's telling us exactly where he'll be and exactly when," she said to Rhyne Cadec, laying the
correction notice on the table between them. "He thinks the fear is the weapon. He's never once
considered that the notice is."

Daba watched the planning from a remove he had kept deliberately for years now -- present at the
first council where Kether laid out the route Vondel's detachment would have to take through the
Sarrow approach, offering two corrections to her timing and nothing else, then absent from
everything after, by his own long-standing rule that an operation planned this far from the Rookery
belonged to the people running it, not to him. He had learned, the hard way, that watching too
closely was its own kind of taking credit.

The ambush took four days to prepare and six minutes to execute. Vondel's detachment, confident in a
route no one should have known to defend, walked into a defile Rhyne Cadec had spent two of those
four days quietly collapsing at both ends, the same narrowing-into-a-bottleneck logic a much younger
Kanja had once learned on a disused footbridge and never forgotten. Vondel himself died in the first
minute, still calling out the correction's opening formalities to a district that had already
emptied two days before his column arrived.

What reached Daba afterward was not a battle report so much as an irony he turned over for longer
than the operation itself had taken. Vondel had kept his own ledger -- eleven settlements, eleven
completed lines, a career measured the way a man measures anything he has learned to survive by
counting. Daba understood that impulse better than he wanted to. The difference, he said to Kether
once the cell had stood down and the district's survivors had quietly begun returning to houses no
one had burned, was not that one man counted and the other didn't.

"He counted what he'd taken," Daba said. "I count what it costs. He thought the ledger made him
careful. It only ever made him predictable."

The kill went unclaimed, unannounced, and unattributed -- not to 1804 by name, not to any crew, not
to any Alias the Directorate might have reached for instead. Within a season, the Directorate's own
internal accounting would list Harek Vondel's death as an unsolved field loss, one line among many,
the exact anonymity his own methodical reputation had never once considered that an enemy might turn
back on him.
