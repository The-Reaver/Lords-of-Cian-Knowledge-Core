# The Children Who Played Crow King

*Locked canon, Batch 201, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-855`). The Crow King Alias Chronicle XL, wave 14 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The children of a border settlement had turned the Crow King into a game long before any of
Kanja's people ever passed through it — one child crowned in a ring of gathered black feathers,
crouching in tall grass while the others pretended, with great and serious ceremony, to search for
soldiers who were never actually there, the "trick" always ending the same way, with the hidden
child leaping out to general delighted alarm.

Kanja saw the game by accident, passing through under a different, unremarkable errand, and stayed
to watch longer than the errand strictly required.

"They've got the crown right," he told the apprentice, who'd stopped beside him, equally caught by
it. "Everything else is wrong. There's no leaping out. There's no moment where anyone's supposed to
notice at all."

"Does it bother you? Watching them get the actual craft backwards?"

"No. It worries me a little, in a different way." He kept watching the game finish its round, the
crowned child already being replaced by the next volunteer. "If children two provinces from anywhere
I've operated already think the trick ends with a dramatic reveal, that's the version that'll spread
fastest, and it's the version that gets someone genuinely hurt if they ever try acting on it for
real, expecting a game's ending instead of what the craft actually requires."

He didn't correct the children directly — there was no way to do so without drawing exactly the kind
of attention the moment didn't call for — but he spoke with the settlement's elders before moving on,
gently, about what the story actually meant and what it cost the people who'd lived it, careful never
to claim any part of it as his own.

"You can't stop children from playing at something that's captured their imagination," the elder
told him afterward, unaware of who she was actually speaking to. "The story's already theirs now,
more than it's anyone else's who actually lived it."

"I know. I'm not trying to take it back. I'd just rather the next generation understand, even a
little, that the real version was never about a dramatic reveal. It was about nobody ever noticing
at all. That's a harder game to play, and a considerably more useful one to understand, if any of
them ever need the actual lesson someday instead of just the story."
