# Lauris Chronicle XCII: Those Who Never Knew Outside

*Locked canon, Batch 300, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1709`). Ninety-second entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, part of Strand L. Opens a distinct debt within the same population Corin Halvet
belongs to (Operation 37's roughly 280, `MCD-191`, protected in concealment for two centuries per
`MCD-1658`): a first native-born second generation, children of subjects who formed families of
their own within the concealment network, has reached adulthood knowing nothing else. Deliberately
left open. No new named characters. Narrated by Fermand Aurelias.*

-----

*Archive fragment, present-day: "I built this place to hold what the Directorate would have
taken. I did not build it thinking about what it would mean to be born inside it and never see
the outside at all. I am recording that I should have thought of this a great deal sooner."*

I have written, in the entry immediately preceding this one, of the two unvoiced questions Lauris
found surfacing quietly among Operation 37's protected population during her most recent visit. I
want to record a third discovery from the same week separately, because I do not believe it
belongs beside the first two, however closely they arrived together in time. The first two were
questions individuals had not yet asked themselves plainly. This one was a fact she had simply
never counted correctly, and the distinction, once she explained it to me, struck me as genuinely
different in kind.

Two centuries is, by any Cian-human measure, a very long time. It is long enough, within a
population whose engineered biology carries substantial Karesian-derived longevity but not,
Lauris has confirmed through her own count, immunity to ordinary reproduction, for subjects who
arrived already adult -- or mid-process, requiring years of the network's own stabilization before
anything resembling an ordinary life became possible -- to have formed families of their own, and
for those families to have had children. She has always known this in the abstract. She recorded
the settlement's overall numbers faithfully on every visit, the way she records every population
she protects. What she told me she had never done, until this visit, was separate the number into
generations and ask what it actually meant for a meaningful fraction of the total to have been
born within the concealment's walls and to have never once, in their entire lives, stood outside
them.

*

The eldest of this second generation -- I do not have a name to give, and would not use one even
if I had it, given how carefully Lauris protects the anonymity of everyone in this account who has
not chosen, as Corin Halvet chose, to be seen -- approached her on the third day of the visit, not
with Corin Halvet's request, but with one she told me she had genuinely never anticipated. This
person did not ask to leave. They asked, instead, whether what they had grown up inside could
still be called a choice at all, given that it had never, for them, been chosen -- only inherited,
the way a child inherits a language or a faith, without ever having weighed the alternative.

"Corin Halvet knew what he was giving up when the network took him in," Lauris told me, recounting
the conversation. "He had an outside to compare his protection against, and he has now decided,
after two centuries, that he wants it back. This person has never had an outside to compare
anything against. I do not know whether that makes the protection I have given them a gift or a
cage, and I do not think I am equipped, on the strength of one conversation, to decide which word
is more honest."

*

I asked her, because I thought it the plainest available question, whether she considered this a
harder version of Corin Halvet's own request or a genuinely different debt entirely. She was
quiet long enough that I understood, before she answered, that she had not yet settled the
question herself. "Different," she said finally. "Corin Halvet is asking me to trust a decision he
has already made about his own life. This is asking me to account for a decision I made, two
centuries ago, on behalf of people who were not yet born to consent to it or refuse it. I did not
build this place thinking about what it would become to the children raised inside it. I do not
think that failure of imagination makes the protection wrong. I think it makes the protection
incomplete in a way I am only now equipped to see."

She has made no commitment regarding this thread beyond continuing to visit and continuing to
listen, exactly as she has told Corin Halvet she intends to do with his own request. She has not
conflated the two debts, and asked me specifically to record that she considers conflating them --
treating one decision as though it settles the other -- the exact mistake she is trying hardest
not to make.

Her closing note, entered the same night as Corin Halvet's own follow-up entry: *He asked me to
let him leave what he chose to enter. This one is asking me something I do not yet have language
for -- what I owe a person for a choice I made before they existed to be asked. I am recording the
question honestly, because I do not yet have an honest answer to set beside it.*
