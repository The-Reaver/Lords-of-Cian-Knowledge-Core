# The Compact of Silence

*Locked canon, Batch 214, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-929`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle LIV, wave 18. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Seven districts in a row turned the crew away at the gate, and every one of them used almost the same
words to do it.

"No trouble here. No need for a tally. You can move on."

It wasn't staged testimony this time — Ezio checked for that first, out of habit, and found none of
the usual signs. It was simpler and harder to answer: word had reached every administrator across the
province, by whatever channel administrators used to reach each other, that letting the tally crew
past the gate at all was the mistake, not anything the crew might find once inside. No fabrication
needed. No fight to be picked. Just a closed gate, seven times running, each one polite and each one
final.

"We can't force a gate," Kanja said, on the seventh refusal, standing outside a district he could see
plainly was in no better condition than any of the six behind it. "That's not a rule we're willing to
bend because it's inconvenient this month."

"Then we're finished," Ezio said. "Not in this district. In the province. If every administrator's
agreed not to let us in, there's no testimony to take anywhere in it."

Kanja didn't answer that right away. He sat outside the seventh gate for three days instead, doing
nothing that could be mistaken for forcing entry — no crowd gathered, no demand made, no attempt to
climb or push past a line of hired men standing at the fence with nothing to do but watch him not try.

It was the district's own workers who broke the compact, not the crew. Word of a tally-taker sitting
patiently outside a locked gate for three days, refusing every provocation to force his way in,
traveled faster inside the district than the administrator's own instruction to ignore him had. On
the fourth morning, forty workers walked out through the gate they'd been told not to open for him and
sat down in the road beside him instead.

The administrator opened the gate himself an hour later, not because Kanja had done anything to make
him, but because there was no longer a district left inside it that hadn't already chosen to be
outside.

"You didn't force anything," the administrator said, watching his own workers file back in ahead of
the tally table.

"I never do. That's not a strategy I picked because it's slower. It's the only version of this that
doesn't stop being what it says it is the first time someone tests it."
