# The Night They Came for the School

*Locked canon, Batch 239, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1053`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LVIII, wave 20. A detailed full-Trinity combat showcase defending the storm-reading school's coastal compound from a raid timed to exploit storm cover -- set after Sephtis's death, with his successor now the school's sole senior authority. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The smugglers had lost more hulls to accurate forecasting than to any fleet action in a decade. A storm
that used to hide them now arrived on a schedule the whole coast could read, and the school that had
built that schedule was, to them, worth more than any ship they'd ever run. They chose their night the
old way -- under weather -- not knowing the one place on the coast where that no longer bought silence.

Kanja was there within the hour, in full gear, because the successor's warning bell reached him before
the raiders reached the archive house.

They came in three boats through breaking surf, counting on the noise of wind and water to cover the
approach. Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System caught the first boat's ramming charge against the seawall
and threw the load back through the hull's own timbers rather than into the stone the students slept
behind, splitting it below the waterline before a single raider set foot on the beach. Onyx's Whisper of
Shadows folded Kanja into the storm's own dark between one lightning-flash and the next, closing the
distance to the second boat's landing party before they'd finished forming a line; Cadence Ruin timed
three strikes to the exact half-second gaps between gusts, when the wind itself would mask no sound at
all and a wrong move would be heard by everyone -- deliberate, not necessary, a message as much as a
tactic. Obsidian Malice discharged once, low and wide, into the sand ahead of the third boat's charge,
not to kill but to collapse the dune face they were using for cover and strand them in the open where
the archive house's own lookout could count every one of them by storm-lit silhouette.

No raider reached the door. No student left their bunks. Nine were taken alive; the rest fled back into
the water they'd trusted to hide them and found it doing the opposite -- lit, briefly, by Onyx's Veil
Piercer cutting the last of the dark from around the retreating boats so the compound's own crew could
be certain no fourth landing was coming behind the first three.

The successor met Kanja at the seawall afterward, soaked, the ledger of the night's weather still
clutched under one arm out of habit.

"They came for the charts," she said. "Not us."

"They came for what the charts mean," Kanja said. "That there's nowhere left for them to hide that
someone hasn't already learned to read. You didn't build a school. You built the thing that ended their
trade, and they finally noticed."

She looked at the archive house, dark windows, no light lost, nothing burned. "Then we keep building
it."
