# What the Chase Could Not Outrun

*Locked canon, Batch 247, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1078`). Crow King Alias Chronicle LXII, wave 21. A
detailed full-Trinity combat showcase fought entirely at a dead run, deception and battle woven
together while nothing on either side stands still. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

Every deception the crew had ever thrown had bought them stillness — a checkpoint held, a garrison
stalled, a patrol turned somewhere else while the real work happened at rest. This time there was no
stillness to buy. A Directorate courier company, mounted and fresh, had picked up the supply
convoy's trail an hour outside the border crossing and was gaining, and there was no ground left to
choose. The chase would end on the causeway or it wouldn't end in the crew's favor at all.

Kanja fell back to the rearmost wagon and let the lead riders close the gap, reading them the way
Onyx of Oblivion had never had the leisure to read a moving target before. Cadence Ruin didn't map a
room this time — it mapped six horses' strides against the causeway's own uneven stone, picking out
which rider's mount was favoring a leg before the rider himself had noticed. Veil Piercer read the
gap closing in real time, correcting for it stride by stride instead of once and done. There was no
four seconds to stand still and listen. There was only motion, and the craft had to move with it or
be useless.

The false signal came first, thrown backward over his shoulder in the plain, worn voice of a man
relaying an order rather than performing one: word that the border garrison's own outriders had
already reached the crossing and were holding it, so the chase was pointless, the convoy already
lost to them either way. It wasn't true. It didn't need to be — only credible enough, at a dead
gallop with no time to check it, to buy three riders' hesitation for four strides each, which was all
Mafesto needed. The Kinetic Transfer System took the lead rider's own momentum as he closed the last
length and redirected it sideways into the second rider's mount rather than through the wagon both
had been converging on, spilling both without breaking stride himself. Obsidian Malice discharged
once, low across the causeway stones rather than at either rider, enough to spook the trailing
mounts into a wide break that cost the company the tight formation its speed depended on.

The fourth rider through, faster than the rest and unbothered by the false signal, was the one who
mattered. Onyx caught him not by sound this time but by the changed weight of the causeway itself
under a horse pushed past what the stone could safely carry at speed — Veil Piercer read the crack
starting under the far span before the rider did, and Kanja let it happen rather than warning him,
timing his own last redirect to put the rider's fall on the shallow side of the break instead of the
deep one. The company that finally reined up did so not because they'd been beaten by a trick but
because three of six mounts were down, one causeway span was failing under them, and the convoy was
already past the point where catching it meant anything.

Efa Gol, riding rear guard, said afterward that she'd never seen the craft used while everyone
involved was still running. "Every other story about it, somebody's standing still to make it work."

"Somebody usually is," Kanja said. "This time nobody had that long."
