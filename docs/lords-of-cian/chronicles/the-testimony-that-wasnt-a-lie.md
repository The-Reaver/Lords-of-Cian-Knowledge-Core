# The Testimony That Wasn't a Lie

*Locked canon, Batch 260, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1268`). Crow King Alias Chronicle LXXVI, wave 26, opening
it. A captured soldier is brought before a liberated town's council, and craft principles extract
truthful testimony rather than deception. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The council had no formal standing yet -- the town had only been free of Directorate garrison
control for eleven days, and the makeshift hearing room was a granary loft with benches borrowed from
three different households. But the captured soldier sitting at its center had information the
council needed and no particular reason to give it freely, and someone had asked, half-joking, half-
serious, whether the Crow King's people had a trick for this too.

They did, though it wasn't the trick anyone expected. The fourth generation sat across from the
soldier for the better part of an afternoon, not threatening him, not baiting him with a false
signal designed to make him slip -- simply laying out, calmly and with exact recall, everything the
crew already independently knew and could verify, piece by piece, until the soldier understood there
was no gap left in the picture for a convincing lie to hide inside.

"We're not asking you to confess to anything we can't already prove," the fourth generation told
him. "We're asking whether you'd rather the council hear it from you, in your own words, or piece it
together themselves from everything around you that already says the same thing."

The soldier talked. Not because he was tricked into it, and not because he was threatened into it,
but because the craft's usual weapon -- constructing a picture convincing enough to be believed --
had been turned instead toward constructing a picture true enough that lying against it had stopped
being worth the effort.

The council's presiding elder, unused to either Directorate interrogation or crew tactics, said
afterward she'd expected something closer to a performance. "I expected you to trick him into
talking. Instead you just... showed him there was nothing left to hide."

"That's most of what the craft actually is, once you take the songs away," the fourth generation
said. "Verified truth is its own kind of leverage. We don't always need the other kind."
