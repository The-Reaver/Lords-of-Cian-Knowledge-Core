# The Day They Carried Him

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Captain Alias Chronicle, wave 7. Rebellion era. Not
a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters beyond
already-locked crew.*

---

Three days of running fights across open water had bled Mafesto's charge down further than Kanja
liked to admit out loud, and by the time the crew finally broke contact with the last pursuing
vessel, he was standing on deck on will alone, the Trinity's resting density doing most of the work
his own legs weren't managing cleanly anymore.

He didn't say anything about it. Pell Ostra noticed anyway, the way she noticed everything
mechanical, including the particular way a body moves when it's compensating for something it won't
name.

"Sit down," she said. Not a question.

"I'm fine."

"You're not, and I don't need Trinity telemetry to know it — I've watched you carry half this crew
out of worse than this without slowing down. You're slowing down." She didn't wait for permission,
just got a shoulder under his arm the way she would for anyone else on the ship, and two more hands
joined without being asked, and between them they got him seated against the mast rather than
standing through pure stubbornness.

"This isn't how this usually goes," he said, half a protest, half something closer to
embarrassment.

"No," Garren Hask agreed, arriving with water and refusing to hand it over until he'd actually sat
still to drink it. "Usually you're the one doing this for us. Doesn't mean it only runs one
direction. You've spent three years teaching this crew that nobody carries this ship alone. You
don't get to be the one exception to your own rule just because you're strong enough to pretend you
don't need it."

He let them finish — let Pell Ostra check the housing seals without waving her off, let Hask keep
the ledger of the day's cost the same as any other loss, let the deck hands who'd normally have been
elsewhere stand watch around him without being asked to. It took the better part of an hour before
he was steady enough to stand on his own, and nobody made it a story afterward, the way the crew
never made a story out of anything that was simply true.

"Thank you," he said finally, to all of them at once.

"Don't thank us," Efa Gol said. "Just remember it, next time you think this only works one way."
