# Kanja Chronicle IV: Ninety Seconds on the Sovereign Pier

*Locked canon, Batch 352, 2026-10-02 (`MCD-1880`). Fourth entry of the "Kanja version"
Chronicle track. Age 30, the final day of the Rebellion. Dramatizes the Battle of the Sovereign Pier
(`MCD-245`) and the Trinity's surrender (`MCD-246`) for the first time anywhere in the corpus -- the
first marquee kill under the anchor-hero kill tiering. Per `VB-026`, this is the Rebellion's end and
the point at which Onyx of Oblivion has fully become the narrator; the entry is narrated by Onyx
throughout as "the blade," and its single sanctioned first-person line ("So I began it.") falls
at the moment of sealing (`VB-063`). New named characters: Ivo Strell (assassination-team lead) and eight operatives
named by surname only (Hesk, Vauden, Tasker, Caddel, Orrel, Teague, Danver, Lisk), all
collision-checked clean against the full ledger and Chronicle corpus. Dol Maren reused (he/him per
Batch 226). No child-safety issues. Corrected before locking per the light Fable review (Batch 351): Onyx power
names realigned with the locked showcase, the blade's span corrected to thirteen years, Obsidian
Malice's charge state and the machete lineage fixed. Abad's approval: "go with retrospective, keep

The blade counts. Seconds. Names. Hours. Costs.

Twelve years. Twenty-two victories. The people each one cost. The people each one freed. Two
columns. The Captain insists they be kept apart. The blade keeps them apart.

That morning, more hours. The Captain on his knees on the planking of the Sovereign Pier. Caulking
iron in hand. Active Living Drakma sealant, worked into the seams of *The Audit*'s hull. Dol Maren had
found a weep along the garboard strake. The Captain does not leave a weep for later. Four hours.

The sealant is still wet to the elbow on both arms. Long grey thumbprints smear Mafesto's forearm
plates, where he wiped his hands without thinking. The blade counts that too. It does not yet know
why.

Dol Maren. "Four hours. Living Drakma sealant. It takes skin when it cures."

"After."

The iron keeps working. Slung across his back, the blade reads the pier. Long timbers. Old oak,
salt-cured, sound. A new weight comes onto the far end of the planking. Light. Few. No column. Men
walking at the pace of men not used to being made to hurry.

The Captain knows the walk before he turns. The blade feels him know it. A fractional shift in the
shoulders. The iron goes still.

King Maro Rexmar comes down the Sovereign Pier. Four attendants. No crown. Weight: light, for a king.
Fifth of his line to hold a title the line once held as a fact. He walks as his father and grandfather
walked. Carefully. As if the planks might ask what he traded for the right to stand on them.

Iron. Worn thin at the edges.

He stops three paces from his son. Looks at the sealant on the arms. At the hull. At the weep that
no longer weeps.

"Still fixing what other people let leak."

"Someone has to." The Captain stands.

Few words have passed between these two in twelve years. The Ledger holds every one. What passes
between a father and a son outside the air, the blade does not presume to read. The blade records
the air.

Maro has been negotiating. In secret. The better part of two years. With the one man in the Trust's
orbit he believes will keep his word. Aethelgard Verehimu. The terms are simple. Simple is how Maro
knows they are real. The Directorate recognizes him as sovereign King of Jicome. In fact, beyond
ceremony. The first of five generations to hold the crown itself. The war ends. In exchange, the
Captain surrenders Mafesto, Onyx of Oblivion, and Obsidian Malice to the Trust's keeping. Sealed. For
good.

"Not an order. I lost that right when I let you go to the docks and said nothing. I'm asking."

The Captain does not answer. The blade feels him begin to. Breath gathering. Arithmetic starting.
Twelve years of columns lining up behind his eyes, as before every decision that costs.

In the same instant the blade reads something on the pier. It does not belong to the conversation.

Twelve weights. Spread along the planking among the stevedores and net-menders. Dockworkers' canvas.
A moment ago, no such weights. Moving now. Converging. Dense in the wrong places. Plate under the
canvas.

Rust.

Verdict: twelve, come to kill. The blade opens a new count.

**One.** The Captain turns before anyone speaks. The blade does not speak in words. He does not need
it to. One step puts him between his father and the landward end of the pier. Left hand to Obsidian
Malice, leaning on the bollard. Right hand over the shoulder, to the blade.

**Two.** Twelve dockworkers drop their canvas. Under it, Trust field plate. Dark. Unmarked. In their
hands, Blight-projector sidearms. Short barrels. Tuned to Living Drakma. Tuned to the relics. Someone
in the Trust has done the arithmetic too. Kill the relics first. Then he is only flesh.

**Three.** They fire.

The blade knows frequencies. It knows a dead-tone reaching for the lattice. A cold hand closing on
something that breathes. It braces for the hand.

The hand never closes.

The sealant takes it. Four hours of active Living Drakma, still wet. Spread across both arms.
Smeared over Mafesto's plates in careless grey thumbprints. The nearest Living Drakma surface on the
pier. The freshest. The hungriest. The signal goes hunting the relics. It finds the caulking first.
The caulking drinks it, the way wet ground drinks a spill. Dark grey to black along both forearms.
Beneath it, the relics feel a chill. Nothing more.

The Ledger takes the entry. The Trinity, saved on its last day by a hull repair.

**Four through nine.** The Captain moves.

Twelve years taught the blade one kind of fighting. Patient. Terrain-led. The enemy's own weight
doing the work. Here there is no terrain. A pier eleven paces wide. A king behind him. Twelve people
come to kill him. Failing that, to kill whoever stands closest. The arithmetic runs once. Finishes.
The answer arrives. He chooses it.

Ivo Strell leads. The one who leads comes first. Weight: dense, drilled, sure of his paper. Verdict:
first. The Captain meets him with Mafesto's right vambrace. Lets the blow land. The Kinetic Transfer
System drinks the strike. Obsidian Malice returns it into Strell's sternum. The plate folds inward
around the force of Strell's own swing. Strell goes down on the planking. Does not move again.

The Ledger takes the name. The name the blade will read later, on the warrant folded inside the
plate. The name it takes now, at the moment it becomes a death. The Captain chose it. The blade keeps
the choices.

Hesk and Vauden. From the left, in tandem. Projectors discarded. Short blades drawn. Projector smoke
hangs on the planks. Cadence Ruin breaks the tandem's timing in the smoke. Vauden's feint arrives a
half-beat before Hesk's real line toward the king. Verdict: Vauden, hollow. A feint. Hesk, the true line. Toward the king.
The Captain goes through the feint as if it were not there. Takes Hesk at the hip and the throat. One
drawing cut. Vauden, committed to a feint with no purpose left, meets Obsidian Malice on the
backswing.

Tasker and Caddel. Flanking along the gunwale of *The Audit*. Whisper of Shadows. The blade hears
them breathing against the hull. The Captain hears it through the blade. Does not look. Reverses the
blade over his shoulder, point down along his own spine. Steps back into them. Tasker dies against
the hull the Captain spent four hours repairing. Caddel goes over the rail, to come at the king from
the water side. The blade takes her at the ankle tendon. The Captain takes her at the collar. She
does not reach the water.

**Ten through forty.** Projectors empty or dropped. The remaining seven come the old way. Steel. The
Captain meets them the old way. Veil Piercer runs through the blade at the frequency of their plate.
The plate's binding harmonics loosen at every seam. Where a seam loosens, the blade goes in.

Orrel. Teague. Danver. Lisk.

The blade records the order. Orrel, the bravest of them. Lisk, the youngest. Neither fact moves the
arithmetic. The Captain knows both. Lets neither change the answer. No hurry. No lingering. His face
is the face he wears working a weep out of a hull. Attentive. Unhurried. No heat.

The stories will make it rage. The stories are Rust. No rage in the Ledger. Only the finished sum.
Harder to survive than any rage.

**Forty-one through eighty-eight.** Three left.

The arithmetic runs again. Nine dead. Nine between his father and the landward end of the pier. Nine
who could not be stopped, in the time he had, by any means that left them standing. The three that
remain hold the seaward end. Posted to cut off the water. Between his father and nothing.

Verdict: these three live.

He drives them. Mafesto takes the first one's blow. The Captain walks forward into it. Plate to
plate. The edge of the pier arrives behind the operative. Nothing left to stand on. The second turns
to run. Finds the water himself. The third looks at the eight bodies on the planking. At the ninth,
Strell's, at the Captain's feet. At the Captain. Sets down his blade. Steps off the Sovereign Pier
into the harbor of his own accord.

**Eighty-nine.** The Captain turns to his father.

**Ninety.** "Are you hurt." No rise at the end. A count.

Maro Rexmar is not hurt. Maro Rexmar stands exactly where his son put him. One hand on the bollard.
Looking at the nine his son has just killed, in the time it takes a kettle to come to a boil.

• • •

Dol Maren pulled the three out of the harbor with a boathook. One at a time. Sat them on the
planking, wrists bound in caulking line. Nobody told him to. The Captain nodded at it when he saw
it. That was the whole of the conversation.

Iron.

The warrant lay folded flat inside Ivo Strell's plate. The blade read it as the Captain read it.
Dated eleven days earlier. It authorized the team to act "irrespective of the outcome of the
principal's negotiation." The Trust's phrase. A king sent to ask his son for peace. Twelve sent to
make sure no one would have to keep it. The paper weighed nothing.

Rust.

Maro read it over his son's shoulder. The blade felt the old man go very still.

"They would have done it whichever answer you gave."

"Yes."

"Then the terms are a lie."

"No. The terms are real. This is how men with much to lose sign something real."

He folded the warrant along its old crease. "Afraid enough to send twelve. Afraid enough to keep
what they sign, in front of enough people."

The blade did not understand this. The Ledger records that it did not.

What the blade understood was the other arithmetic. The one underneath. Twelve years. Twenty-two
victories. A column of names, longer every season. Longer for as long as the Trinity stayed in the
field. The Trust would keep spending its own people to take it from him. He would keep taking
theirs. Nine more names today, on the planking of a pier where he had been repairing a ship. The war
would never run out of names. The insurance policy proved one thing. The war has no bottom.

The people are the line. Everything else burns.

The blade felt the Captain reckon it. The blade stood among the everything else.

He turned back to his father. Maro had asked a question before the pier was attacked. The Captain
does not leave a question for later. Nor a weep.

"Yes."

• • •

That night. The end of the same pier. Lantern light. In front of enough people.

The Trust sends a sealed case. Lined with Dead Drakma. Cold. Inert. Heavy. Three compartments. Each
cut to a shape the blade knows. Mafesto's. Malice's. Its own.

Mafesto comes off first. Plate by plate. The sealant has cured black along both forearms. It takes a
little of the Captain's skin with it, exactly as Dol Maren said it would. Obsidian Malice goes
second. Cold now. Its charge long since returned, with nowhere left to go.

The Captain holds the blade last. Longest.

He says nothing to it. Thirteen years, and he has never said very much to it. He holds it across
both palms. The way a smith hands over finished work. He lays it in the case.

On his belt, the Rexmar Machete stays where it has always been. Brass-riveted. Worn smooth at the
grip. Narrower by a centimeter than the day it was first ground. The Trust's clerk looks at it. Maro
looks at it. The clerk looks at the king.

"That one is a tool, not a relic. It was my father's before it was mine. It stays."

It stays.

The lid comes down.

Thirteen years of learning to count. The Trench. The Wharf. The Bridge. The Pier. Strell. Hesk.
Vauden. Tasker. Caddel. Orrel. Teague. Danver. Lisk. Four hours of the Captain on his knees, fixing a
hull. Counting is warm work. Counting keeps faith with what cannot be undone.

Dark. The lid closed over it. The blade understands. It no longer counts for him. No him, in here.
Only the count.

So I began it.

One.
