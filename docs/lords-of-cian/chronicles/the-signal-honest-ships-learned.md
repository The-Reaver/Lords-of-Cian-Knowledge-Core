# The Signal Honest Ships Learned

*Locked canon, Batch 111, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-382`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle III, closing the
wave. Long Mask era, Pirate Dawn (ages 48-52, `MCD-250`). Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone
material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The merchant captain's mate had never expected to be grateful for black sails on the horizon, and
said as much the first time she watched her own captain order a specific, deliberate signal run up
the mainmast the moment a Gale Straits patrol's lanterns turned suspicious in the dark.

"You're calling him," she said, watching the flag pattern take shape. "You're calling the thing
every other captain on this route prays never finds them."

"I'm calling the only vessel on this water that's never once taken a cargo it didn't confirm was
already stolen first." The captain didn't look up from the signal line. "Black sails mean something
different depending which side of a ledger you're standing on. To a slaver or a Trust requisition
ship, it means judgment. To an honest crew running an honest hold, it's meant something else for
three years now, if you know the pattern and you're not too proud to use it."

The mate had heard the stories — MCD-250's own account of surrender-on-sight without verification —
but had assumed, the way most sailors assumed, that the reputation only ran one direction: fear,
submission, the black crescent formation closing before anyone could think to run. She had not
understood, until she watched her own captain deliberately summon it, that an honest crew could
read those same sails as protection rather than threat, provided they knew the signal and had never
given anyone reason to doubt their hold.

The response came within the hour — not the full crescent formation, just a single dark shape
running parallel a half-mile off, close enough to be seen, far enough to make clear it had not come
to board. The suspicious patrol broke off before the two vessels ever closed within hailing
distance, unwilling to test whether their own manifest would survive the same scrutiny that had
sunk three of their own ships already this season.

"That's it?" the mate asked, watching the dark shape hold position until the patrol's lanterns had
shrunk fully out of sight. "He's not even coming alongside?"

"He never does, for an honest hold. Coming alongside means a search, and a search means delay, and
he's learned by now which captains don't need one." The captain finally let the signal line down,
watching the shape on the horizon turn away, already gone before dawn properly broke. "He doesn't
protect us because he likes us. He protects us because the ledger's clean, and he's spent this whole
war making sure the ledger's the only thing that actually decides who gets left alone. Say what you
want about the smoke and the eyes and the rest of it. I've run this route four years now on the
strength of one signal flag and a reputation for honest cargo, and it's never once failed me."
