# Anirak Chronicle II: The Second Harness

*UNLOCKED DRAFT, PENDING ABAD'S APPROVAL, 2026-10-04 (fourth draft, after three independent review rounds). Second
entry of Anirak's Character Chronicle series (Game Plan pitch 3, locked at Batch 372). Close-third on
Anirak per `VB-065`. The Long Mask, roughly Kanja 175, after Maw-11 (`MCD-264`) and before Ren comes
aboard. A thirteen-strong Sealbound Directorate Retrieval Detachment (`SBD-050`) comes for a subject of
its own and brings a damping rig built against her. Notable register, no kill. New named character:
Ardith (the subject), collision-checked against the full ledger and Chronicle corpus (near in shape to
Vask Aldreth, distinct in register). The rig's maker stays unnamed. Anirak, Edda, Hamund, and Odile

---

Tideglass at night. A free port. The Scourge's mark painted small on the harbor-master's door. The
Trust's tax-house three streets back, shuttered longer than most of the dockhands had been alive.

Rain on slate. Tar off the boat sheds. Kelp rotting on the slip. Lamps in the tavern windows along the
front, and the water black between the piers.

She lay awake in the loft over the net-maker's shop, and the sea told her why.

Hulls. Three moving, or out of place. A small one inside the second pier, low in the water, no lamp, oars muffled and dipping
slow. A second small one, still, nosed in under the seawall. A long keel just outside the harbor
mouth, holding its place against the swell.

She was down the ladder with the chains in her hands before the oars went quiet.

• • •

The woman was called Ardith. Twenty, or near it. She had come into Tideglass nine days before on a salt
barge, under a tarp, with a number cut into the skin behind her ear and nothing else she would talk
about. The harbor council had put her in the rooms over the chandler's. The crew had told the council
they would watch the rooms.

Edda had the door. Hamund had the lane. Odile had the roof across the way.

Anirak came round the corner of the chandler's at a run and took it in at one look.

Grey oilcloth over dull plate. No marks.

Edda down on one knee against the wall beside the door, a hand to her head, blood in her hair. A club lay on the flags
beside her.

The chandler's door open. Two of them at the foot of the stair. Four more up it, near the top, and
Hamund and Odile on the stair below those four, pressing up. Staves cracking on the crew's
blades in the narrow well.

Six more in the lane mouth, close-packed, in no hurry. Two of them held a long canvas bundle between
them that clinked when they shifted it.

Behind the six, alone, a thin man in the same grey with a slate in one hand and a stylus in the other.

The stair. The two at its foot. Those first.

The six in the lane parted and let her through.

Too easy. She saw it as she passed them. Stopping would cost the stair.

The first man at the stair-foot swung a weighted strap low, for her arm. She took it on the cuff of her
left forearm. The strap wrapped the chain. She turned her wrist and reeled, and the strap brought him with
it, into her shoulder, and her shoulder put him into the doorpost. He slid down it.

The weight of him came back up her arm.

The second man crouched to come in low. A Fang went out on its chain along the flags and hooked his
ankle. She pulled. His leg went out from under him. His head met the bottom step.

The links began to warm across her fingers.

Behind her, iron weights clinked on cords. A whirring. Canvas falling away.

• • •

It came down over her from behind.

A net. Heavy. Thick bands of quilted leather stitched in a lattice, every band packed tight with lead
shot, mail under the leather. Weighted cords at its rim. It dropped over her head and shoulders, over
her arms and the chains, and the six from the lane were round her before it settled, each with a cord
in his fists, and they leaned back on the cords together.

The lattice drew tight. It pinned her arms to her sides and pressed the Fangs flat against her thighs.

She drove an elbow back. It sank into a band. The shot shifted and swallowed the blow. The blow died in
the lead and came back up her arm as nothing at all.

She ground a Fang's edge against the band across her hip. Leather parted. Mail under it. The edge
skidded.

The net's crown cinched as the six leaned back. A hood sewn into it came down over her face, coarse,
sodden. Cold water ran down her neck. It closed over her mouth and her throat, the
gorget and all. The lamps went out.

She could move a hand's breadth any way. No more.

The links cooled under the bands. Her heart slowed with them. She let it, to keep her head. The
weight she had gathered began to leak out of her arms. Down. Out through her shoulders. Out of her
chest. Rain from a cracked jar. Every breath of stillness took more of it.

They harnessed me. So the blows wouldn't stack.

• • •

On the stair above her, blades on staves. Hamund's grunt. Odile's breath hissing through her teeth. Then a muffled
sound through a hand, and boots going up and away, across the rooms over her head, and a shutter banging
open at the back of the building, the side over the water.

Hamund's voice. "Window! They're out the back!"

Outside the hood, close, a calm voice. From behind the cords.

"Hold tension. Four minutes."

A stylus scratching on slate, close behind the cords.

A man reading a gauge. No heat in it. Every word chosen.

Another, rougher. "She's stopped pulling."

Then, nearer. To her.

"Anirak." The way a clerk reads a name off a docket. "Do not exert yourself. No one here is authorized
to injure you."

She could not move enough. Every breath of it bled her more.

Her voice was under the wet cloth, ready. She held it.

Hamund and Odile were on the stair, before her. In the line it would take. On a stair.

She held it.

Boots on the steps, coming down fast. Hamund. Odile. Past her, one on either side. To the cords at her
flanks.

Two cords in front of her, heavy. Nothing else on the steps. Edda's breath, off to her left, against
the wall.

She let it go.

Low. Under hearing. It went out through the wet cloth, forward.

It reached the two before her. She felt it reach them, down the cords. The forward lines shuddered.
One went slack in a jerk. Then tight. Then slack. A man retching on the flags.

Behind her, the calm voice, a half-beat late. "The hood --"

They built it against a sound.

"Forward lines. Hold."

The forward lines went slack again.

The slack came. An arm's length.

She turned into the slack. Hard. All her weight. The net came round with her, the lead bands swinging
out on their stitching. The cords on the far side snapped taut, and a man's whole weight hit the end of
his.

That came back up her arms.

She turned the other way. Another cord came taut. Another man's weight hit the end of it and came
stumbling in across the stones.

That came back up her arms too.

Her heart came up. The links warmed again under the bands.

"Left cord!" Hamund. A blow. A grunt. The left cord went dead.

"Right!" Odile. The right one too.

Her two, on the cord-men. They held the ground around her and took the
cords off her one at a time.

She worked one arm up through the slack lattice. Got a hand to the crown cord. Tore the hood back.

Lamps. Rain. The lane.

Every face in the lane on her.

The thin man stood three paces back from all of it. The slate in one hand, the stylus in the other. No
weapon. His face held on her with all the rest. He looked down at his slate. Looked up.

She went out through the last of the lattice. The bands fell round her feet on the stones.

The cord-men were down, or backing off with their hands open. Not one of them came at her.

The thin man stayed where he was, three paces back.

She went past him.

• • •

The lane. The slip. The end of the second pier, at a dead run, the chains warm and singing.

The long keel lay a few cables past the mouth. The small hull's oar-beat had stopped against its bulk.
As her feet hit the last planks, the keel's wake lengthened. It heeled and gathered way before the land
breeze.

She set a foot on the last pile and looked at the swell.

Too far. Too fast.

At the end of the pier she paced. Three strides each way. Rain on the planks. The weight in her arms and
nowhere to spend it.

The keel went out and out across the swell, a long mile and more. Farther every breath.

It went over the edge of what she could feel. Then there was only the swell.

• • •

Hamund met her at the head of the slip.

"Four went with her. Rest over the seawall, the boat under it." He wiped rain off his face. "Thirteen
in all. Nobody dead."

She had felt that boat under the seawall from the loft. She had let it go for the keel.

The net lay in the lane where it fell. Edda sat on the chandler's step with a rag to her head. Odile held
the rag.

Hamund kicked the net. It clinked.

"They made that for you."

"Yes."

"How long, a thing like that?"

She looked down at it. The bands. The mail. The stitching. Six men drilled on six cords until they could
close it on a moving body in one breath. A hood sewn in, soaked to stop a sound.

"Long."

"Who?"

"The one with the slate."

Odile looked up. "Gone."

"It's on his slate."

She picked the net up. The lead dragged in every band. She carried it to the end of the slip and threw it
in the harbor. It went down fast.

Upstairs in the chandler's rooms, the bed was still made. A cup of water on the sill. The back shutter
hung open on the rain.

She stood in the doorway and looked at the cup.

Then she went down to the harbor council, and asked for paper, and wrote the name down herself. Ardith.
Twenty, or near it. A number behind the ear. Taken.
