# The Failure He Didn't Catch in Time

doctrine's first fatal misdiagnosis. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters.*

---

The granary's support pillar had carried the same resonance signature Kanja had read a hundred times
before — iron reinforcement under load, straightforward, the kind of read he'd stopped needing to
double-verify on structures this simple. What the reading hadn't shown him, because nothing about the
pillar's own tension signature could have shown him, was the laborer repairing a feed chute two levels
below, out of sight, inside the collapse radius he had no way of knowing existed.

The discharge brought the pillar down clean, exactly as intended, denying the garrison its grain
stores before a siege that would otherwise have starved the rebel-held district first.

The laborer didn't survive the fall of debris he'd had no warning to move away from.

Kanja found out within the hour, from a runner too shaken to deliver the news gently. He didn't ask
for it delivered gently.

"The doctrine's never failed to read a structure correctly," he said, later, to Onyx, in a voice that
carried none of the certainty the words themselves suggested. "It failed to tell me what was inside
it. That's not the same kind of failure I've prepared for. I've spent years learning to verify what
the metal tells me. I never built a way to verify what I can't hear at all — what isn't part of the
structure's own signature to begin with."

"You couldn't have known."

"That's true, and it doesn't change anything about what happened because of what I didn't know."
He set the Aegis-Talisman aside, not discarding it, simply unwilling to hold it for the moment. "I've
always verified the read. Starting today, I verify the space around it too — every level, every room,
everything a structure might be hiding that has nothing to do with its own tension. That should have
already been part of the doctrine. It wasn't. A man is dead because it wasn't."

The new verification step cost time on every engagement that followed it, a permanent tax the
doctrine would carry from that day forward, and Kanja never once treated the cost as anything but
owed.
