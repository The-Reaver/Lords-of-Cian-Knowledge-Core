# The Trade He Wouldn't Make

*Locked canon, Batch 216, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-949`). The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle LVI, wave 19. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The offer came through an intermediary, the way careful offers always did — a mid-ranking Compliance
officer with something genuine to trade: the exact location and timing of a planned raid against a
different district's tally crew, three days before it was set to happen, information that would let
Kanja get every worker at that site to safety and expose the raid publicly before a single blow landed.

The price was silence. Not betrayal, not violence, not anything that would have cost another worker a
single coin — only that Kanja look away, for one night, from a smuggling operation the officer himself
ran on the side, moving stolen Drakma scrip through a canal route Kanja's crew had only just started
tracing.

It was, by any practical measure, a good trade. One night's willful blindness against a saved district
and a raid exposed before it could happen. Corren Halst, hearing the terms laid out, said as much
without much hesitation — the smuggling could be dealt with later, once the district in danger was
safe, and nobody at the endangered site would ever know the difference.

Kanja turned it down.

"If I take this," he said, "then the next honest thing I catch, I have to ask myself whether I'm
catching it because it's true or because it's convenient this time. I don't get to be the version of
myself people trust with a tally sheet and also be the version who trades one truth for another when
the math works out in my favor. Those aren't the same man, even if nobody but me ever finds out."

"People could die at that site," Halst said, flatly, not arguing so much as making sure the cost was
said aloud.

"I know. I'm not pretending it isn't a real cost. I'm telling you I've decided which cost I can live
with carrying, and it isn't the one where I start deciding which lies are worth letting stand."

He warned the endangered district the only way he had left — openly, through the crier network,
naming the threat without the officer's advance timing, giving the site less warning than the trade
would have bought but warning all the same. The raid still happened, three days later, and cost the
site real damage the earlier warning might have prevented. Nobody died. The smuggling route, traced
independently over the following month without the officer's help, was exposed and shut down anyway,
slower and harder-won than the trade would have made it.

Halst never brought it up again as a mistake, though it took him longer than most decisions Kanja made
to stop turning it over privately.

"You were right that it worked out," Kanja told him, weeks later, unprompted. "I want to be honest that
I don't actually know if I'd have been right to take the trade, if it hadn't. I made the choice because
of what kind of man I wanted to still be afterward, not because I could prove in advance it would cost
less. Sometimes it might. I don't think that makes it the right math to run."

Garren Hask's ledger recorded the raid's damage plainly, without softening it, and beneath it a single
line that named no officer and no trade: *An offer was refused here. The cost of refusing it is
recorded above. It is recorded because it was real, not because the choice was wrong.*
