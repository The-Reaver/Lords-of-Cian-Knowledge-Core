# The Command He Left Behind

*Locked canon, Batch 264, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1364`). Captain Alias Chronicle LXIV, wave 22. Corren Halst leads the crew through a full crisis alone while Kanja is away, an informal test of the succession question neither of them has named yet.*

---

The message that pulled Kanja away came from a different coast entirely, an old debt from a different alias's era that only he could answer, and there was no time to do anything but tell Corren Halst he was leaving and trust the rest to sort itself out behind him. He was gone eleven days. In the ninth, a squall drove a merchant convoy onto the shoals off Pier Nine at the worst possible hour, three hulls breaking at once, cargo and crew both going into cold water in the dark.

Halst didn't send for him. There was no way to, and she didn't waste breath wishing there were. She split the response the way she'd split engagements before — Pell Ostra rigging line-and-float recovery off the Second Chance's stern, Efa Gol running the count from the pier so nobody went back into the water for someone already accounted for, Callum Breck's shore-watch signals keeping three separate rescue parties from working the same stretch of wreckage twice. Garren Hask, still under Sera's standing orders to stay off anything that moved on open water, ran the intake ledger from dry land instead, cross-checking names against the manifest as fast as survivors could give them.

Nineteen of twenty-two souls came off the wreck alive. Halst made the calls that mattered — which hull to abandon first, when to pull the recovery lines rather than risk a fourth boat in that surf, who went into the warming tents first — without once reaching for a decision that wasn't hers to make.

Kanja came back to a settled harbor and a full accounting already written up in Hask's careful hand. He read it twice before he said anything.

"You didn't need me for any of this."

"I needed you for plenty of things, over the years," Halst said. "This wasn't one of them. That's not an insult to either of us."

He didn't press it further — not yet, not before it was time — but he carried the shape of it with him afterward: eleven days, one crisis, and not one decision that had waited for a name that wasn't in the room.
