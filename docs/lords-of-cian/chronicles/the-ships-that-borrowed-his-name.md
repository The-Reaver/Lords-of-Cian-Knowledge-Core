# The Ships That Borrowed His Name

*Locked canon, Batch 199, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-786`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle XXXI, wave 11 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The first report reached *The Audit* as a curiosity rather than a concern: a fishing fleet three days'
sail north had taken to painting their hulls dark and running without lights whenever Trust patrol
boats came near, and the patrols, spooked by rumors they couldn't confirm or dismiss, had started
giving that stretch of coast a wide, nervous berth.

"They're pretending to be us," Dol Maren said, reading the report a second time as if it might say
something different. "An entire fishing village, playing ghost fleet to keep the Trust from
requisitioning their catch."

"Is it working?"

"Better than most things we've actually done on purpose."

Kanja sailed north to see for himself, and found exactly what the report described — a dozen ordinary
fishing boats, hulls hastily darkened with soot and tar, running silent past a Trust patrol that gave
them a full mile of clearance rather than risk finding out whether the rumors were true.

The village elder who met them on the beach was less apologetic than braced for consequences. "We
didn't ask permission. Figured a reputation like yours was either something you'd guard jealously or
something that belonged to whoever needed it most. We needed it. So we took it."

Kanja was quiet for a long moment, watching the darkened fishing boats bob at anchor, borrowed fear
doing for a fishing village what the real fleet had built through years of careful, dangerous work.

"You're not wrong that I could feel that as theft," he said finally. "A reputation this costly to
build, and you took the shortcut without earning any of it."

"And?"

"And it's not costing me anything you're taking, and it's clearly saving your catch from Trust
requisition boats that would otherwise starve you slowly instead of fighting you outright." He looked
at the elder directly. "The reputation was never supposed to be something I own. It was supposed to
be something that protects people the Trust would otherwise hurt. If you can wear it better than I
can reach you, wear it."

"Just like that?"

"Just like that. One condition." His voice hardened slightly. "The day it protects a raid instead of a
catch, the day someone flying our colors hurts an innocent instead of just scaring off a requisition
boat, that's the day it stops being yours to borrow. Until then, it was never mine to hoard."

The fishing fleet kept its darkened hulls for the rest of the war, and the reputation — genuinely
shared now rather than merely feared from a distance — grew wider and stranger with every village that
quietly adopted the same trick, exactly as Kanja had decided it should.
