# What the Rumor Cost Them

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
XXVIII, first entry in the tenth wave. Rebellion era, age 21, some months after Ghost Harbor
(`MCD-235`). Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material — the reputation's first genuine
unintended harm. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The merchant hull that limped into the nearest friendly port had never been anywhere near *The Audit*,
never carried a single crate the ghost fleet had touched, and had still been fired on by a panicked
Trust patrol whose captain swore, in his own defense, that her low silhouette and dark running lights
matched every description he'd ever been briefed on.

Three of her crew were dead. Her captain, uninjured but furious, demanded to speak to whoever was
responsible for the reputation that had gotten his sailors killed by his own side's fear.

"You don't understand," he told Kanja, once word reached the fleet and a meeting was arranged under
truce. "Your ship never touched mine. Your *rumor* did. My patrol saw a dark hull running without
lights and didn't wait to confirm anything, because three years of ghost fleet stories have every
Trust captain on this coast firing first and asking questions never."

Kanja said nothing for a long moment, the specific silence of a man confronting a cost he hadn't
priced into a strategy he'd been proud of. "That's not a consequence I intended."

"Intent doesn't un-kill my crew." The captain's voice cracked, briefly, before he brought it back
under control. "You built a fear you don't fully control anymore. It doesn't just work on the people
you're aiming it at. It works on everyone standing anywhere near the shape of what you've become in
the stories."

There was no clean answer to give him, and Kanja didn't try to manufacture one. "I can't undo what my
own reputation did to your crew. I can make sure it costs fewer ships like yours going forward." He
had Dol Maren draft, over the following weeks, a set of unmistakable running-light and hail signals
specific enough that no genuine merchant vessel should ever again be mistaken for the ghost fleet by
a captain willing to actually look before firing — distributed quietly through neutral ports, an
answer built not to protect the fleet's own reputation but to blunt the damage it had already done to
people who'd never chosen to be part of the story.

"It won't stop every panicked captain," Kanja admitted, when the merchant captain returned weeks later
to confirm the signals had reached his own guild. "Fear doesn't always wait for confirmation. But it's
something. It's more than doing nothing and calling the deaths an acceptable cost of a strategy that
was never supposed to cost anyone who wasn't already trying to hurt someone."

The merchant captain took the signals back to his guild without forgiving what had happened, and
without needing to — the fleet's reputation had, for the first time, cost something it couldn't simply
absorb as a clever tactic, and Kanja carried that weight forward rather than setting it down.
