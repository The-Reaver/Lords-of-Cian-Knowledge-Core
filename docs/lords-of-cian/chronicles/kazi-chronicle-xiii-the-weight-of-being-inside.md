# Kazi Chronicle XIII: The Weight of Being Inside

*Locked canon, Batch 289, 2026-09-12 (`MCD-1532`). Eighth entry in Kazi's own Chronicles, Femi
(`PH2-066`) as protagonist, not a Kanja Chronicle. Kanja appears only as a guest, present but
granted no command, intervention, or resolution credit. Narration is close-third on Femi throughout.
A quiet, personal-register closer to Femi's first five-Chronicle arc, dramatizing the isolation of
holding an institutional seat trusted fully by neither side of the divide it sits between.*

---

There was a particular kind of quiet that settled over Femi after a trustees' meeting ran long, one
he had come to recognize by its second year in the seat: not the tiredness of the work itself, which
he didn't mind, but the specific loneliness of having spent three hours being the only person in a
room who was fully believed by no one in it. The other four trustees had stopped treating him as an
outside threat somewhere around the fund audit, but they had never quite stopped treating him as
something other than one of their own, either — a useful instrument the seat had put among them
rather than a colleague who belonged there on his own account.

It was worse, some evenings, on the floor itself. A younger hand who'd started at the plant the same
season Femi won his seat had said it to his face once, not unkindly, the way a man says a thing he
believes is simply true and assumes everyone already knows: "You're one of them now. Office, salary
line, five-man vote. Don't matter how straight you read the books — you don't feel the line the way
the rest of us do anymore." Femi had said nothing back that day. He had thought about it for most of
a week afterward.

"You could tell them what you actually gave up to hold it," Kalamu said one evening, having watched
him work through the same quiet more than once by then, notebook closed for once, not writing. "The
salary line's smaller than the shop floor rate you left. I could put both numbers side by side, plain,
and let four thousand readers do the arithmetic themselves."

"I don't think the arithmetic's the part that would land, even if it's true." Femi turned his cup
without drinking from it. "A man who thinks I've become something other than what I was isn't going
to be moved by proof that the something pays less. He's not measuring my wallet. He's measuring
whether I still flinch at what he flinches at, and no set of numbers answers that for him. Only time
does, if it answers it at all."

"And if it doesn't? If some of them go on calling you the movement's man to your face and the union's
man behind your back, both at once, for as long as you hold the seat?"

"Then I hold it anyway, and I read the books straight anyway, and I let the two names cancel each
other out over enough years that eventually neither one sticks." Femi finally drank. "I didn't take
this seat to be loved by everyone standing on both sides of it. I took it because somebody needed to
be believed enough by just enough of them, on just enough occasions, to keep one door open that used
to only ever get shut."

A stranger who had been quietly restacking a returned box of case files near the office's far wall —
present at the tail end of nearly every long evening that season, credited with none of them, thanked
for none of them, and never once asked to explain why he kept turning up — set the last folder in
place and left without a word, the way he always did, leaving Femi alone with a cup gone cold and a
door he meant to keep holding open regardless of who believed, on any given evening, that he still
had the right to hold it.
