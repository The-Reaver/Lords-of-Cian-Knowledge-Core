# The Family Behind the Numbers

*Locked canon, Batch 130, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-437`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle VII, first
entry in the third wave. Rebellion era, age 19, the Furnace District Strike. Not a territory
Chronicle. New standalone material. Kept deliberately unarmed, per this alias's established ethos.
Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The tally sheets Kanja worked from during the four days of the Furnace District Strike reduced
everything to numbers — hours owed, wages withheld, years of service against years of pay — and the
numbers were the whole point, the thing that made the strike's demands impossible to dismiss as mere
sentiment. But numbers were also, deliberately and by design, not the whole picture, and on the third
day he set the tally sheets aside for an afternoon to sit in a hauler's cramped quarters with a family
the numbers could only ever gesture toward.

Three generations lived in two rooms — a grandmother who'd worked the same furnace line for forty
years before her hands finally gave out, a son who'd inherited the same position and the same
eventual fate, and two children too young yet to understand why their father left before dawn and
returned after dark, six days out of seven, for wages that had bought less and less every year of
their short lives.

"I'm not here to add another number to the sheet," Kanja told the grandmother, who watched him with
the particular suspicion of someone who'd seen organizers come and go without ever once sitting in a
room like this one. "I've got the figures. I wanted to understand what they actually mean, in a room
like this, before I stand in front of the district and use them."

"They mean my son will lose his hands the same way I lost mine, working the same job for less than I
was paid, and his children after him unless something actually changes this time." She didn't soften
the answer for him. "I've heard promises before. I've watched every one of them turn into nothing
once the organizers got what they needed and moved on. Tell me why this time is different."

"I can't promise you it will be. I can promise you I'm not leaving this district until the numbers on
that sheet either change or I've proven beyond doubt that the people holding them refuse to let them
change no matter what evidence sits in front of them." He met her eyes without flinching from the
weight of the question. "That's not the same as a guarantee. It's the most honest thing I can offer
you."

She studied him a long moment, then nodded once, a small concession that cost her something to give.
"That's more honesty than I've gotten from anyone else who's sat in this room. Go finish your work."
