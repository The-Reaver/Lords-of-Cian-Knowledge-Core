# What Garren Hask Wrote Down First

*Locked canon, Batch 194, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-623`). The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XVIII, wave 6 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Rebellion era, before the Dredge-Line Ambush. Not Hask's recruitment -- that happened earlier, at the Scrip-Forge Raid's Forge-7 evidence (`CC-115`) -- but the moment he formally took over the crew's ledger-keeping. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Garren Hask, fifty-three years old, had already joined by then, recruited the day his wife's years of
suspecting Scrip fraud were confirmed by the Forge-7 evidence, but joining and being trusted with the
actual books were two different things, and for weeks he'd done nothing more than what any dock-smith
might do for a cause he believed in — hauling, watching, keeping his own battered ledger on the side,
mostly out of habit, never once imagining it would matter to anyone beyond his own shop.

He started writing the crew's own numbers into it almost by accident — a shift tally he'd heard read
aloud at the site, copied down because the figures interested him professionally, the way any
tradesman notices when a set of books is kept unusually clean.

By the third site, he wasn't copying anymore. He was cross-checking.

"Your figures from the Ferrymouth correction don't match what I heard read out two days before," he
said, holding out his own ledger like a challenge rather than an offering. "Either somebody's numbers
are wrong, or somebody's memory is."

Kanja looked at the two sets of figures a long moment before answering. "Yours are right. Mine were
rounded for the reading, and I said so at the time, but you weren't there to hear the rounding
explained. That's worth fixing — not the number, the habit. If a set of figures can only survive
being heard once, correctly, by everyone who needs them, it's not a record. It's a rumor with good
posture."

"That's an odd thing to say about your own method."

"It's the truest thing I know about it. A method that depends on nobody ever mishearing it isn't a
method. It's luck, and luck runs out." Kanja studied the dock-smith's ledger again, the neat
columns, the honest crossings-out where Hask had caught his own early mistakes rather than hiding
them. "You keep better books than I do. Would you be willing to keep mine?"

Hask hadn't expected the offer, and took several seconds longer to answer than the question seemed
to warrant. "I have a shop. I have work of my own."

"I'm not asking you to give that up. I'm asking whether the numbers matter enough to you to make
sure they're never wrong again, the way you just proved they can be." Kanja closed his own thinner,
rounder ledger and set it beside Hask's. "I think they do matter to you. I think that's why you
checked them in the first place, instead of just believing what you overheard the way everyone else
did."

Hask kept both ledgers that night, merged into one, in handwriting steadier than either original —
the first entry, as the crew's own appointed counter rather than merely one more pair of hands, in
what would become, across decades and two named ships, the single most trusted set of books on the
canal.
