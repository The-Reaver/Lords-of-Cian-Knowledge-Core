# What Garren Hask Wrote Down First

*Locked canon, Batch 194, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-623`). The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XVIII, wave 6 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Before Garren Hask was the crew's counter, before he'd named either of the flagship-class ships that
would carry his tally books across two oceans, he was a thirty-one-year-old dock-smith who kept one
battered ledger of his own, mostly out of habit, and had never once imagined it would matter to
anyone outside his own shop.

He started writing the Trench Monarch's numbers into it almost by accident — a shift tally he'd
overheard being read aloud at Silt Row, copied down because the figures interested him
professionally, the way any tradesman notices when a set of books is kept unusually clean.

By the third site, he wasn't copying anymore. He was cross-checking.

"Your figures from the Ferrymouth correction don't match what I heard read out at the dock two days
before," he said, the first time he ever spoke to Kanja directly, holding out his own ledger like a
challenge rather than an offering. "Either somebody's numbers are wrong, or somebody's memory is."

Kanja looked at the two sets of figures a long moment before answering. "Yours are right. Mine were
rounded for the reading, and I said so at the time, but you weren't there to hear the rounding
explained. That's worth fixing — not the number, the habit. If a set of figures can only survive
being heard once, correctly, by everyone who needs them, it's not a record. It's a rumor with good
posture."

"That's an odd thing to say about your own method."

"It's the truest thing I know about it. A method that depends on nobody ever mishearing it isn't a
method. It's luck, and luck runs out." Kanja studied the dock-smith's ledger again, the neat
columns, the honest crossings-out where Hask had caught his own early mistakes rather than hiding
them. "You keep better books than I do. Would you be willing to keep mine?"

Hask hadn't expected the offer, and took several seconds longer to answer than the question seemed
to warrant. "I have a shop. I have work of my own."

"I'm not asking you to give that up. I'm asking whether the numbers matter enough to you to make
sure they're never wrong again, the way you just proved they can be." Kanja closed his own thinner,
rounder ledger and set it beside Hask's. "I think they do matter to you. I think that's why you
checked them in the first place, instead of just believing what you overheard the way everyone else
did."

Hask kept both ledgers that night, merged into one, in handwriting steadier than either original —
the first entry in what would become, across decades and two named ships, the single most trusted
set of books on the canal.
