# What Even He Couldn't Save

*Locked canon, Batch 154, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-485`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle X, first
entry in the fourth wave. Rebellion era, age 20, the Sewer War of Killane. Not a territory
Chronicle. New standalone material — the first genuine structural-failure entry in this alias's run.
Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The eastern gallery had been failing for longer than anyone realized before Kanja was called to it —
decades of unaddressed water damage compounding under the district's own negligence, the stone
itself no longer structurally sound enough for any shoring technique, however skilled, to hold
indefinitely.

He tried anyway. Mafesto's sensors mapped every fracture line, every point where reinforcement might
buy time; Obsidian Malice discharged carefully, redistributing structural load rather than breaking
anything, an attempt to relieve pressure the stone itself could no longer bear. For six hours he
worked the gallery the way he'd worked every prior tunnel crisis, certain skill and effort would find
the answer the way it always had.

The gallery came down anyway, slow enough that every worker cleared it safely, but final in a way no
amount of skill could reverse — decades of decay past the point any intervention, however capable,
could have saved.

"You did everything anyone could have done," the tunnel crew's foreman said afterward, watching Kanja
survey the collapsed section in silence. "Nobody who watched today thinks this is a failure on your
part."

"I know it isn't a failure of skill. That doesn't make it easier to accept." Kanja didn't dispute the
foreman's reasoning, didn't need to be told the outcome had never been within his control. "I've
built a reputation on reading stone well enough to save what other people can't. Today I read it
exactly right, and the reading told me there was nothing left to save. That's a harder kind of
knowing than not knowing at all would have been."

"What happens to the gallery?"

"It's gone. We reroute around it, same as we'd have to if I'd never shown up at all." He set his
tools down, the admission plain rather than dramatized. "I don't think every story about this alias
needs to end with something saved. Some of them need to be honest about what decades of neglect
leaves behind before anyone with any skill ever arrives to help. This is one of those."
