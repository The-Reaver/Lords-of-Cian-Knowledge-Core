# The Night He Wrote Nothing Down

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle I, wave 15.
Rebellion era (age 19-21), unarmed and non-combat throughout, matching the alias's established
ethos. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The old furnace-tender sent word through his daughter that he wanted to speak to the Industrial Myth
before he died, and Kanja went that same evening, expecting, from every prior night like it, that
there would be a testimony to take and a figure to write.

The old man was too far gone for any of that by the time Kanja reached him — past caring about wages
owed, past the strength to answer four careful words with a considered number. He wanted, it turned
out, almost nothing that a ledger could hold.

"I don't want you to write anything down," he said, when Kanja set the blank pages within reach out
of habit. "Put that away. I've spent my whole life being a figure in somebody's book — hours logged,
output counted, wages docked. I don't want to end as one more entry, even a fair one, even one that
corrects everything the yard ever cheated me of. I just want someone to sit here and listen while
I'm still able to talk, without writing any of it down anywhere."

Kanja set the pages aside, further than within reach, and sat.

The old man talked for most of the night — not grievances, mostly, though some of those came too, but
long stretches about a wife forty years gone, a trade he'd been proud of despite every way the yard
had used it against him, a daughter he'd watched grow up mostly through the hours between shifts.
None of it was the kind of testimony the method had ever been built to capture, and Kanja didn't try
to shape it into that kind either — didn't redirect toward a wage figure, didn't ask the four words
he'd asked four hundred times that season, just listened, the way the old man had asked him to,
until the talking slowed and then stopped sometime before dawn.

He died two days later, with nothing about that night in any ledger anywhere, no entry, no tally, no
correction Ezio would ever cross-check against a production log.

"You spent a whole night with him and there's nothing to show for it," Ezio said, gently, when Kanja
told him where he'd been.

"There's nothing to write down for it. That's not the same thing as nothing to show." Kanja didn't
explain it further than that, and Ezio, for once, didn't press. "He didn't want to be a debt someone
paid off. He wanted an hour where he wasn't a figure at all. I could give him that much without a
ledger anywhere near it."
