# What the Old Dam Remembered

*Locked canon, Batch 262, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1317`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LXXI, wave
24. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241), a river settlement the campaign
had aided months earlier during the ore/coal embargo (MCD-456). A detailed, battle-intense Trinity
combat showcase against a long-tail structural failure rather than an attack. Not a territory
Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The causeway had been a good fix at the time — a diversion dam raised in four rushed days out of
salvaged war material during the embargo, meant to buy a marginal reopened mine working enough
water pressure to run its own bellows without coal. It had held for months. It was not, Kanja
understood the moment he felt the ground shudder under a crack he hadn't put there, built to hold
forever, and forever had never been the promise.

"It's failing from inside," he said, one hand flat against the stonework, Mafesto's Kinetic
Transfer System reading the structure's own stress the way it read an incoming blow — not a clean
break but a slow internal shear, months of pressure finding every seam the rushed original build
had never had time to properly seal. "Downstream settlement doesn't know it yet. They will, in
about an hour, whether we're there or not."

There was no enemy in this fight, which made it stranger to run than most. Onyx of Oblivion's
Cadence Ruin mapped the dam's whole failing structure by feel, finding three more hairline
fractures behind the visible one before they'd widened enough to matter, while Obsidian Malice's
discharge cycle — normally spent breaking things apart — ran instead as a series of precisely
timed, precisely placed pulses fusing failing seams solid from the inside, buying the old
causeway hours it hadn't earned on its own. Apprentices worked the surface in a human chain,
packing what Kanja's own reinforcement couldn't reach with salvaged stone and raw Dead Drakma
paste, racing a clock with no enemy on the other end of it, only time and a rushed decision made
under a different kind of pressure entirely.

It held, in the end, long enough for the downstream settlement to move livestock and stores to
higher ground on a warning that arrived with an hour to spare instead of none. It would need a
proper rebuild before the season turned — not a patch, an actual engineered structure this time,
with the kind of care the original emergency had never allowed for.

"I built it to solve one problem," Kanja told the mine's overseer, once the immediate danger had
passed. "I didn't build it to still be standing when the problem it solved was long forgotten.
That's on me, not on whoever forgets a fix was ever meant to be temporary."

"You came back for it," the overseer said. "That's more than the thing that built it owed us."

"It's exactly what it owed you," Kanja said. "I just didn't say so at the time."
