# What Left the Forge and Kept Going

*Locked canon, Batch 249, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1085`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LXIII, wave
21, closing the wave. Set decades after the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241), during the Long
Mask era. A legacy beat extending "The Boy Who Refused the Forge" (MCD-884). Not a territory
Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The healer's house sat at the edge of a district Kanja hadn't walked in over thirty years, and he
almost didn't recognize the man who answered its door — gray at the temples now, hands scarred
in the particular way a lifetime of setting bone scarred them rather than the way a lifetime at a
forge did, until he smiled, and it was the same flat, unimpressed refusal Kanja remembered from a
boy who'd once set down a half-finished scrap of iron without a second glance.

"You never came back to check if I regretted it," the healer said, waving him inside past a front
room crowded with drying herbs and two younger apprentices grinding something at a low table.
"Everyone else did. My mother came back every year for a decade."

"I didn't need to check. You told me what you wanted the one time I asked." Kanja looked past him
to the apprentices, who'd both stopped grinding to watch a stranger their teacher seemed to know
well. "Those two look like they're learning the way I remember teaching."

"They're learning the way you taught me to learn, is closer to it." The healer sat him down at
the room's single good chair without ceremony, the same way he'd once been sat down and asked what
he actually wanted. "I didn't take the hinge lesson. I took the other one — that you don't press
skill into a shape someone didn't ask for, you find out what they actually want to build and you
teach toward that instead. Turns out that works on setting bones same as it works on tempering
iron. I've never once told an apprentice what kind of healer to become. I ask, and then I build
the lesson around the answer."

One of the two apprentices, a girl no older than the healer himself had been at their first
meeting, had gone still at the low table, listening now without pretending not to. "Is that true?"
she asked. "That you get to choose?"

"It was true for me," the healer said, "and it's true here too. That's the whole rule of this
house." He looked back at Kanja, something almost rueful in it. "I used to think I'd walked away
from what you built. Took me twenty years to understand I'd just carried the part of it that
actually mattered somewhere the campaign itself never went."

"The method was never the iron," Kanja said. "It only ever looked like it was, because iron was
what I happened to know."

The healer laughed at that, short and genuine, and put a kettle on rather than answer further,
and the two apprentices went back to their grinding with something new in the set of their
shoulders that hadn't been there a few minutes before.
