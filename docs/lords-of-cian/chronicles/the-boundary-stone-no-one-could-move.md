# The Boundary Stone No One Could Move

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LV, wave 19. Rolling Foundry Campaign, age 27. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Two families claimed the same half-acre the crew had cleared to raise a forge terrace on, each with
a version of the boundary that put the good ground on their own side of it, and each convinced
enough of their own account that the dispute had gone unsettled for longer than either could
remember not being angry about it. They brought it to Kanja only because the terrace itself had
stalled work on both their claims at once.

"Settle it," one of them said. "You cleared the ground. You can say who it belongs to."

"I can't, actually. I don't know this ground the way either of you does, and I've got no standing
here that predates your grandparents' argument." Kanja set down the terrace plans he'd been
reviewing. "What I can do is stop building until it's settled by someone who does have that
standing."

"That's not settling anything. That's just stopping."

"It's stopping the part I've got no business deciding." He looked between them. "This district's
got an elders' circle that's heard boundary disputes longer than this campaign's existed. I'll ask
them to hear this one, and I'll build whatever they decide, on whichever side of that stone they
put it. What I won't do is use six months of forge-work and a war-name to settle a fight that isn't
mine to settle, just because I happen to be standing on the ground when it comes up."

The elders' circle took four days, testimony from both families and from neighbors old enough to
remember the boundary before either family's current dispute began, before ruling — not cleanly in
either family's favor, but a redrawn line neither had proposed, splitting the disputed ground in a
way that matched an older, half-forgotten survey mark both families had genuinely forgotten existed.

"You could have built the terrace on day one and let us keep arguing around it," the losing side's
elder said afterward, watching the crew finally break ground on the correct side of the new line.
"Most outside forces that come through here just build where it's convenient and let the locals sort
out the wreckage after."

"Most outside forces aren't planning to leave the ground standing here after they've moved on."
Kanja marked the terrace's true corner against the ruling. "I don't get a vote in whose grandfather
was right. I get a vote in whether I help build on ground that's actually settled, or ground I've
just made convenient for myself and left somebody else to keep fighting over. Rebuilding's not worth
much if what I hand back is a stronger reason to keep the fight going."
