# The Weight the Mask Kept Count Of

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Scourge Alias Chronicle, wave 10. Long Mask era,
age 305, V4 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters.*

---

Two hundred and eighty-three years after a coat had finished burning on a harbor's edge, he sat alone
on a quiet stretch of coastline that had never seen a raid, an ordinary man in a plain traveler's
coat with the Forge-Coat folded in a sea-chest below deck, and did something he rarely allowed
himself the stillness to do: counted.

Not captives — the crew's own ledgers held that number more precisely than memory ever could. He
counted years instead, and found the arithmetic strange in a way it had never quite struck him as
strange before. Two hundred and eighty-three years was longer than most lives this world's shorter-
lived peoples got to live entire, start to finish, and he had spent every one of them, in some
measure, being someone else's fear before he was ever anyone's father, husband, or simply himself.

He didn't resent it. That surprised him more than the number did. He turned the thought over slowly,
testing it for the bitterness he half expected to find underneath and not finding much of it at all —
only a kind of weight, settled and familiar, the way a limb carries the memory of every mile it's
ever walked without needing to relitigate any single one of them.

The Scourge had never been chosen, back at the start — that much he remembered clearly, the coat
still smoking, Efa Gol asking if it was a problem and neither of them having an answer yet. Two
hundred and eighty-three years later, he still didn't have a clean answer. It had simply become the
shape a very long stretch of his life had taken, the way a riverbed takes the shape of however long
the water's been running through it, not chosen so much as worn.

He thought, briefly, of the girl on the caravan road who'd grown up to map safe crossings without
ever needing him to walk them with her. Of the boy in the barbershop-quiet six minutes of a hostage
standoff, learning to move before fear finished deciding for him. Of an old man who hadn't lived to
walk off a barge, and a name that had never belonged to the legend, only to the people who'd actually
known him.

None of that weighed the same as the years themselves. All of it was, somehow, still the reason the
years hadn't broken him the way two hundred and eighty-three years of being anyone's fear might have
broken someone with less to set against it.

He didn't reach a conclusion, sitting there. He hadn't expected to. He simply sat a while longer,
watching a coastline that didn't need saving that night, and let the weight be exactly as heavy as it
actually was, without asking it to be lighter than that.
