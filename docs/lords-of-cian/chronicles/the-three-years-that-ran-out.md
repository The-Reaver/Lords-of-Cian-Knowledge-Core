# The Three Years That Ran Out

*Locked canon, Batch 286, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1514`). Captain Alias Chronicle XCIV, wave 32. The sub-series' first real test of the rotating council-chair structure (`MCD-1380`): Corren Halst's three-year term reaches its actual end, and a second chair-holder is chosen for the first time.*

---

Nobody had to remind Corren Halst what day it was. She brought it up herself, the way she brought up anything that mattered enough to get wrong by silence: at the start of the council session, before Efa Gol had finished pouring, before Garren Hask's old chair — kept at the table still, nobody willing yet to move it — could sit through another meeting unremarked.

"Three years," she said. "To the week. Which means my term is up, and I'm not standing for another one, and I don't want anyone in this room being polite about it."

Efa Gol pointed out, not unreasonably, that no charter clause actually forbade a second term. Halst had done the job well. Nobody doubted it.

"That's exactly why it matters that I don't," Halst said. "If the same name just keeps winning by default because nobody wants the argument, it's not a rotation. It's a chair with my name carved into it that we're all too tired to admit isn't rotating at all. I said the whole point was that this crew doesn't need one irreplaceable person. I meant it about myself too."

The nominating went the way it always did on this crew — slowly, honestly, with more silence than speeches. Efa Gol named herself unfit for it and meant it; she'd spent thirty years perfecting the discipline of counting things precisely, not weighing them against each other in a room full of disagreement. Maret Vos, still newer to full standing than most in the room, said plainly he hadn't earned the right to ask for it yet, which was its own kind of answer.

It was Efa Gol, in the end, who said the name everyone else had been circling. "Breck."

Callum Breck didn't answer right away. He'd built an entire second life, after the four months of silence following Nev Torr, out of speaking only when it moved something forward — and for a long moment it looked like this might be one of the times he simply didn't. "I coined a name for him once without asking," he finally said, nodding once toward Kanja, "because I thought a man needed a crown for what he'd survived. Took me years to understand that wasn't what he needed at all. A monarch rules over what happens to his people. A captain answers for it." He looked around the table. "I'm not sure I trust myself with a title that big a second time."

"That's the only kind of trust the seat was ever built to run on," Kanja said, his own vote no heavier than any other's in the room and no lighter either. "Not certainty. Just someone willing to answer for it."

Breck accepted before the silence could stretch long enough to talk him out of it. Halst slid the plain tally-slate that had marked the chair's seat across the table to him without ceremony — no gavel, no vote tallied aloud, just one hand passing something forward to another.

"Three years," she told him. "And then it's someone else's turn to prove the point again."
