# The Name He Didn't Choose

*Locked canon, Batch 107, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-368`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle I. Rebellion era,
weeks after the Dredge-Line Ambush (`MCD-231`, `CC-118`), where Callum Breck unilaterally chalked
the "Trench Monarch" name on a captured officer's forehead without Kanja ever choosing or sanctioning
it. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material outside the already-locked Twenty-Two
Victories list. Narrated in neutral third-person prose, no Onyx coda, consistent with the
pre-`VB-026` early-Rebellion state. No new named characters beyond the already-locked Callum
Breck.*

---

The second dredge site never got the fight the first one had earned.

Kanja's crew came up the flooded canal the same way they'd come up the last one — planks over
sludge, lighter fighters moving where the Trust's armored column couldn't follow without sinking to
the waist — and found the site foreman standing alone on the loading dock with both hands already
raised, a rolled canal-survey map held out in front of him like a white flag made of paper.

"I'm not going to pretend I can stop you," the foreman said, before anyone had leveled a weapon at
him. "Everyone on this stretch of canal has heard what happened three sites down. You don't need to
flood this one. Take the manifests. Take whatever you came for. Just — leave the district standing.
Please."

Callum Breck, walking a half-step behind Kanja with a coil of rope over one shoulder, went very
still. He had chalked a name on a captured man's forehead three weeks earlier without asking anyone's
permission, thinking of nothing more ambitious than his own daughter and the particular shape of his
own anger that night, and he had not expected the chalk to travel faster than his crew did.

"He's talking about you," Breck said quietly, once the foreman had been walked back to a safe
distance and the manifests were being loaded onto the crew's own carts. "Three sites down and they're
already surrendering to a name. You didn't do anything to this site. You didn't even know it existed
until this morning."

"I know." Kanja watched the foreman standing at the edge of the dock, hands still half-raised, not
quite trusting that the raising was finished. "That's the part that doesn't sit right. He's not
scared of me. He's scared of something you wrote on a man's forehead with chalk, and it's already
bigger than the thing that actually happened."

"Does that bother you?"

"It should. I keep waiting for it to." He picked up one of the manifests himself, scanning the
debased Drakma percentages the way he'd scanned the last site's — the number was the thing he'd
actually come for, the number was the thing that was real. "But there's four hundred people on this
stretch of canal who are going to eat because a foreman surrendered a manifest instead of calling in
a garrison to protect a lie. If the name did that, I don't get to decide I don't like carrying it. I
just have to make sure it keeps meaning what he thinks it means."

Breck didn't say anything to that for a while. He watched the foreman being escorted, unharmed, back
toward the canal-side offices, and thought about the daughter he'd been thinking of when the chalk
went onto Tavin Greer's forehead, and did not yet understand — would not understand for months,
would not fully understand until long after his own voice went quiet at the Black Trench and came
back changed — that the name he'd given away that night without asking had already stopped being
his to take back.

"I didn't mean to make you a king," he said finally.

"You didn't." Kanja folded the manifest and handed it to one of the lighter fighters to carry back.
"You made a district believe a king was already here, doing the work a king's supposed to do. I just
have to be the one who shows up and does it, so nobody finds out the difference."
