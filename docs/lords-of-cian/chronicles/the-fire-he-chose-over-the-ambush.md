# The Fire He Chose Over the Ambush

*Locked canon, Batch 209, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-911`). Bane Alias Chronicle XLVIII, wave 16. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

They had been three days positioning for the convoy at Serrin's Cut, a supply train the Directorate
had rerouted twice already to avoid exactly this kind of interception, and the window Maret Vos had
mapped for it — a half-mile stretch of dry ravine road with no room to turn wagons around — would
close by the following dawn and not reopen for a month. Every hour of the three days had been spent
protecting that window: no fires after dark, no scouting parties larger than two, nothing that
would tell the convoy's outriders they were expected.

The smoke reached them a little after midday, from the wrong direction to be the convoy at all —
south, off the dry grass slopes above Adren's Hollow, a herding settlement of maybe sixty people
that belonged to neither side of the war and had stayed that way by staying small and staying quiet.
Pell Ostra smelled the accelerant in it before anyone else placed the smoke at all — not brush
catching naturally in a dry season, but something set, deliberate, likely a Directorate patrol
burning graze-land behind it to deny the rebellion forage, indifferent to what else stood in the
fire's path.

Bane read the wind for perhaps four seconds. The ambush window at Serrin's Cut needed the column
positioned and unseen by dawn. Adren's Hollow needed everyone who could carry water there within
the hour, or it would burn with the people still trying to save it.

"Strike the position," he said. "All of it. We're not coming back to it."

Maret Vos didn't argue, though the look on his face said what three days of careful work were worth
losing. Nothing in the Trinity was built for this — Mafesto's kinetic transfer had nothing to
ground against in loose grass and running flame, Obsidian Malice's discharge would have done
nothing but spread the fire faster, and Onyx stayed sheathed the entire day, of no more use here
than any other blade. What moved sixty people and their animals off the slope and what cut the
firebreak that finally turned the flame back on itself was three hours of ordinary, unglamorous
labor — shovels, wet blankets, a chain of buckets from the Hollow's one deep well, Bane cutting
break-line alongside conscripts and herders both with nothing about him marked as anything other
than another back bent to the same work.

The convoy passed through Serrin's Cut clean the next morning, exactly as scheduled, and was three
weeks gone by the time another window opened.

"A month of Vos's work," Halst said that night, meaning it as neither complaint nor praise, just
an accounting. "Gone, for sixty people who'll never know we were the reason the convoy got through
instead of the reason the Hollow didn't burn."

"They'll know the Hollow didn't burn. That's the part that was ever going to matter to them." Bane
looked at his own hands, blistered from the shovel in a way nothing about the Trinity had prepared
him for. "The convoy comes again. Adren's Hollow doesn't get a second dry season if we let this one
take it."
