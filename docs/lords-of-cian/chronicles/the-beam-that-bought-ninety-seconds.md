# The Beam That Bought Ninety Seconds

*Locked canon, Batch 264, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1377`). Captain Alias Chronicle LXXVII, wave 26. A detailed rescue-engineering showcase: a collapsing tenement, Kanja's own post-surrender gear used purely to hold structure rather than fight anyone. Corrected Batch 321, 2026-10-02: retitled and rewritten to remove a post-surrender Trinity anachronism (the entry was built entirely on Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System and Obsidian Malice, decades after the Trinity's locked age-30 surrender, `MCD-246`) and a false claim that Mafesto was built to save one man's life at the Black Trench (Mafesto was bonded roughly two years before the Black Trench, per `MCD-232`/`ARS-342`); also corrects Corren Halst's pronoun to he/him per `CC-158`.*

---

There was no enemy in the tenement collapse, no ambush waiting in the dust, nothing to disarm and nothing to break — only a badly rebuilt post-war building giving way at its worst possible hour, with a stairwell full of people and a support beam already three-quarters through its own failure when word reached Pier Nine. Kanja was there before the district's own emergency response finished assembling, and what the moment called for wasn't a fight at all. It was engineering, applied under a collapsing ceiling with people still inside it.

The Forge-Coat's own plating had always been built around taking force and carrying it somewhere else — a blow's shock sent down through the Ironfall Boots' grounding soles rather than absorbed in place. Nobody had ever tried using that principle to hold a failing structure up rather than turn an attack aside, and there was no time to test the idea gently. Kanja braced the Ironhand Gauntlets directly against the beam's own failing joint and let the coat and boots carry the building's collected downward force into the ground rather than letting it complete its collapse, buying — by Hask's later careful reconstruction of the timing — perhaps ninety seconds more than the beam would otherwise have held.

Ninety seconds was enough. Corren Halst ran the evacuation line through the stairwell himself, counting bodies out the way Efa Gol would have counted coin, while Callum Breck held the ground floor exit clear of the panicked crush that could have cost more lives than the collapse itself. The building came down in full less than a minute after the last person cleared the door, Kanja pulling free of the joint himself only when the redirected force finally exceeded what even the grounded coat and boots could keep bleeding away.

"That's not what that gear was built for," Corren Halst said afterward, watching him work dust and grit out of the gauntlets' housing.

"Most of what this crew's gear ends up doing wasn't what it was forged for," Kanja said. "Everything since has just been finding out how many other ways the same idea can be useful."
