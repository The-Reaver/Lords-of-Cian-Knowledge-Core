# The Ledger He Corrected in Front of Everyone

*Locked canon, Batch 194, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-622`). The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XVII, wave 6 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The error wasn't the owners' this time. It was his own crew's — a miscounted shift tally at the
Ferrymouth site that had, for two weeks running, credited the dredge line with four hours more than
they'd actually worked, paid out of the owner's pocket on the strength of a number nobody had
checked closely enough.

Kanja found it himself, going back over the site's figures a third time out of habit rather than
suspicion, and the moment he found it, he didn't wait for anyone to bring it to him.

"We owe him back-pay," he told the assembled dredge line the next morning, standing where he always
stood to read the numbers aloud. "Not the other way around. Two weeks of overcounted hours, my
crew's mistake, not his. I'm not going to quietly fix it and hope nobody notices. I'm telling all of
you, and I'm telling him, and we're paying it back exactly the way we'd expect him to pay us back if
the error had gone the other direction."

A murmur went through the line — not anger, something closer to disbelief. One of the older
dredgers spoke up. "You could've just let it sit. Nobody was checking your numbers as hard as you
check his."

"That's exactly why I have to check them harder than anyone else does. The whole reason any of this
works is that when I say a number is true, people believe it without needing to verify it
themselves. The day I let a false number stand because it happened to favor us instead of him, that
belief is gone, and it doesn't come back because I apologize for it once."

He walked the corrected tally to the owner's office himself that afternoon, laid it on the desk
without preamble, and waited while the man read it twice, clearly expecting some kind of trick
buried in the correction.

"You're paying me back for a mistake you could have kept," the owner said finally.

"I'm paying you back for a mistake that was true, whether I kept it or not. The truth doesn't change
because it's inconvenient to whichever side it favors." Kanja didn't wait for gratitude. "I'd ask
you to remember this the next time your own foreman tells you one of my figures is wrong. Sometimes
he'll be right. I'd rather you find out from me than from him."

The story reached three sites down before the week was out, carried not by Callum Breck's chalk this
time but by the owner himself, telling anyone who'd listen that the boy they called a king had walked
into his office and handed back money nobody would have made him return.
