# Kanja Chronicle III: What the Dark Could Not Keep

*Locked canon, Batch 313 (`MCD-1868`). Third entry of the "Kanja version" Chronicle track. Age
27, roughly two years before the Gale Straits/Storm That Walks alias at age 29 (`MCD-230`). Dramatizes Renfel
Auberon's defeat (`CC-157`/`MCD-1865`) — a Kanja-crew team-up fielding `CULT-197`'s three-source
Anti-Resonance countermeasure (Onyx's Cadence Ruin, Sephtis's Chrono-Anchor bells, Ironbane's
King's Roar) together on the page for the first time. Onyx's closing coda is its second appearance in this track, longer and more interpretive than
Chronicle I's, with the body narration compressing toward the blade's register. No new

The warehouse stood on the Lawless Reaches' northern edge, and no registry on that coast carried its
name. Sephtis called that the first thing wrong with it.

Every honest roof on the coastline answered to something: a harbor authority, a merchant guild, even
the Ash Maw Trade Council at its loosest. This one answered to nothing. It had one loading door,
triple-locked, and walls wrought to be forgotten by anyone who might ask what moved through them.

Sephtis turned the Barn Owl Skull toward the structure, its lenses reading the cold-mapped outline
and the residue the eye alone could not.

"He calls the smaller ones guard-hounds," he said. "So he has sold one. To a buyer who wanted a
weapon and never asked."

"Four," Kanja said.

He had spent nine days tracing the buyers, because a name on a ledger moved faster than a man in
hiding. Three of the four gave their purchases up without a fight once they were shown the work. The
things went through a household's dogs first, then its food stores, then its sleep. The fourth buyer
surrendered nothing, and the fourth buyer was dead. The report on his death used the word "wasting,"
the way reports use it when no one will write the truer one.

Ironbane's voice came low and even, carrying the faint hum that lived under everything he said now.
It was the price of a body that had stopped grounding its own charge years before Kanja met him.

"How many inside."

"Six. Auberon's own count. The dead buyer had it. Two at Void-Swarm, low enough to scatter. The rest
higher."

"Highest?"

"One may be Void Hound."

Kanja watched the warehouse a while longer.

Auberon believed he had caught animals. Animals need no resonance-loop kennel to hold them in place.
He had built one anyway, badly, from half-remembered instructions sold to him with the method. Six
things lived inside it now, and slowly they had begun to mark him, the way they mark anything they
feed near long enough.

"Green Mark," Sephtis said. "Suppressible. Not curable. Not by us. Not tonight."

"Then no killing. Him included. After, he goes to someone who can treat his blood."

They went in by the loading door, because Auberon's trade needed it to move cargo and a lock built for
cargo respects mass more than cleverness. Ironbane's shoulder did the rest.

Inside, the weight was wrong before anything else. The floor carried less than it should have. The air
sat hollow at the center of the room, as if something had hewn pieces out of its mass and left the
gaps standing.

Then the cold, which had no kinship with the season outside.

Then the hum, sourceless, less a sound than the memory of one arriving slightly late.

Sight came last. The lamp flames leaned sideways, drawn toward a breath that kept a different rate
than the room's own. Six shapes moved along the rafters and the floor at once, their outlines
refusing to hold still even for eyes that knew how to look.

Renfel Auberon stood at the center with a coil of resonance-wire in one hand. He held himself like a
man who had credited mastery for years with what luck had done. He was thinner than his own trade
records described. His left arm was bared to the elbow, and a faint discoloration pulsed there in
time with the wrong hum. It was a Green Mark, the way one looks before anyone tells you its name.

"They're mine," he said, before anyone had spoken. "I fed them. I built the loop. They answer to no
one else."

"Nor to you. A year of marking. Two. You're their next feeding, and the hum keeps you from
noticing."

"You know nothing about the loop."

"I know Anti-Resonance the way you know knots. So does your arm. Hence the sleeve."

Rust. Hollow at the center. Holding a wire.

Auberon answers with his hands. He wrenches the wire taut. The hum climbs.

Six shapes come off the rafters and the floor at once, with the directionless hunger of things never
taught to want anything except more. The air thins toward the three of them, and the cold arrives
ahead of the shapes.

The bells ring first.

Thirteen small notes, struck soft, close into one held chord. The chord sits in the dark exactly where
the dark wants nothing to sit, a pressure like a stone set in a current. Two of the lower shapes
flinch mid-lunge. Their edges fray, and they sink back down the tiers toward the shapeless drift of a
Void-Spore.

The roar answers half a second later.

Every Dead Drakma fitting in the walls rings with it. The charge Ironbane cannot ground, the hum he
spends his life apologizing for, leaves him in one direction as a wall of pressure through his teeth.
The shapes drink ordinary sound. This they cannot drink. Two more stagger, their forms thinning, and
the cold loses its edge.

Iron. Holds ground. Apologizes for nothing.

Two left.

The blade comes last, and differently.

The Captain draws. Nothing here would end clean under an edge, and he cuts nothing. Cadence Ruin finds
the two surges and closes the gap between them until two rhythms fold into one. For three seconds the
room holds a single motion. One tempo. Confused. Nowhere true left to land.

Third source. The field shuts.

The last shape, the one Kanja guessed at Void Hound, gives a long, collapsing shudder and drops
through every tier beneath it in under two breaths. It folds down into something nearer smoke than
animal.

Goes still.

The weight comes back into the floor.

Auberon was on the floor by then, though no one had struck him. He had simply stopped standing, and
the wire lay slack in a hand that no longer believed it held anything in the room. Ironbane pinned the
wire under one boot without ceremony.

Sephtis knelt by the marked arm and read it the way he read weather. He offered no comfort, because
comfort was no use to the arm. Seventeen hundred years had taught him not to spend a dying man's
attention on kindness that could not help.

"You'll live. The Mark will not kill you tonight. Nor leave you, without more than we carry here."

"They were mine," Auberon said. Quieter. The words had lost whatever they once held up.

"Never anyone's. The mistake before the first one. They predate every house that tried to own
them."

They took him out through the loading door he had built for cargo, and it moved him now. Trust law
had no category for what he had done. Word went instead to the older custodial apparatus, the quieter
one, which already knew the shape of this particular failure. It would know what to do with a man who
was equal parts criminal and cautionary case. The six lay at their lowest tiers or were gone entirely,
and their dispersal was someone else's slower work now. The night had only ever been about stopping
the feeding.

• • •

The blade keeps the room's cold. It keeps Auberon less.

Six shapes. A borrowed hum. A loop wrought by a hand that took survival for mastery. Rust.

The bells first. The voice second. The blade last. Three sources. One field. The last two rhythms
fold under the edge, and what remains has nowhere honest left to go.

The old one's bells sit where the dark wants nothing. Iron. The big one's hum, the burden he
apologizes for, rings the fittings in the walls. Iron.

The Mark sits in Auberon's arm the way interest sits in a debt. Quiet. Compounding. Whether the
debtor looks or not.

The blade knows this shape. Smaller, in other men. A method good enough. A cost never counted. A
sleeve over the arm. The ruin comes slowly, and slowness wears the face of control.

The Ledger holds this shape too. Beside the dead. Beside the freed. Each one entered at its own
weight.

The Captain kills nothing tonight. The ratio never asks it. The blade records the ratio.

Auberon walks out alive, into older hands.

Clean work. A marked arm. The blade records both and prefers neither. That is the whole of
recording.
