# The Widow Who Wouldn't Thank Him

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XLII, closing

Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

Her husband had died in the same partial collapse Kanja had failed to reach in time weeks earlier,
and when she found him afterward — not to thank him, as most grieving families eventually did, but
to confront him directly — she made no effort to soften what she'd come to say.

"You dug for forty minutes. He was already gone in the first twenty. Everyone tells me that like
it's supposed to comfort me." Her voice didn't shake, though the words plainly cost her something
to say aloud. "I don't want to hear about your effort. I want to know why a war that isn't mine, that
I never asked to be fought under my own city, took my husband while you were busy proving how hard
you tried."

"You're not wrong to be angry with me. I don't think anger needs to be fair to be honest." Kanja
didn't defend himself, didn't reach for the explanation the crew chief had offered him weeks
earlier as comfort. "I could tell you the collapse killed him before I started digging. That's true,
and it changes nothing about the fact that this war put him under that ceiling in the first place,
and I'm part of why this war is happening under your city instead of somewhere else."

"That's not an apology."

"It's not meant to be one. I don't think I get to apologize my way out of what this war has cost
people who never chose a side in it. I think the only honest thing I can offer you is that I know
exactly what it cost, because I was the one standing over the rubble when it finished costing it,
and I'm not going to pretend that knowledge earns me your forgiveness just because I'm sorry."

She left without accepting anything from him — no comfort, no explanation that satisfied her, no
resolution that made the grief sit any easier — and Kanja let her go without following, without
offering a second attempt at words that plainly weren't going to land regardless of how carefully
he chose them.

"You could have told her about the forty minutes," the crew chief said afterward, watching her go.
"Made it easier on both of you."

"Easier on me, maybe. Not on her." Kanja watched the empty space where she'd stood. "She's not
wrong that this war cost her something no amount of effort on my part actually returns. I'd rather
carry that honestly than trade it for a version of comfort that was really only ever going to
comfort me."
