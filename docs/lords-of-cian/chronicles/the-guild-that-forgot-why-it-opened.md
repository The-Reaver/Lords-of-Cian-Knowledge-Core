# The Guild That Forgot Why It Opened

*Locked canon, Batch 262, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1318`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LXXII, wave
24, closing the wave. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241), a settlement
the campaign had helped establish an open forge in over a year earlier. An institutional-decay
entry, the alias's first, distinct from every prior positive-legacy return. Not a territory
Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The forge itself looked exactly as it should have, from the terrace's edge — busy, well-tended,
the same open-door policy painted on its gate that every campaign stop carried away with it. It
was only once Kanja was inside that he saw the second door, the one that hadn't been there when he
left, and the small, unofficial fee a self-appointed council of senior journeymen had started
charging anyone who wanted to actually use the equipment behind it.

"It's not much," the man collecting it said, when Kanja asked him plainly what it was for. "Keeps
the riffraff from wearing out good tools on nothing worth making."

"The whole point was that anyone gets to decide for themselves what's worth making," Kanja said,
and watched the man's face do the specific thing a face does when it realizes the person asking
already knows the answer isn't going to hold up. "Whose idea was the fee?"

It had started small and reasonable-sounding, the way these things generally did — a modest charge
to cover wear on shared tools, agreed among the handful of journeymen who'd trained first and
therefore felt entitled to gatekeep who trained after. It had grown, over a year of nobody from the
campaign checking back in, into something closer to a toll, quietly pricing out exactly the people
the open forge had been built for in the first place.

He didn't burn the second door down, and he didn't need the Trinity for any of it. He called every
journeyman who'd had a hand in the fee to the terrace, in front of the apprentices the fee had
priced out, and asked each of them the same question: whether they remembered what it had felt like
to be turned away from a forge before this one existed. Most of them did remember, uncomfortably,
once made to say it out loud. The fee came down within the week, not because Kanja ordered it but
because being asked to defend it plainly, in front of the people it hurt, was worse than giving it
up.

"You could have just told us to stop," the man who'd been collecting it said, afterward, something
sheepish in it.

"I could have," Kanja agreed. "It wouldn't have stuck. You have to remember why the door was open
on your own, or the next thing that closes it quietly won't have me there to notice."
