# The Contract He Wouldn't Sign

The envoy came under a truce banner and asked for an hour, and Kanja gave her the hour, unmasked
nothing, and let the Forge-Coat's collar stay open rather than sealed against the harbor wind.

"A letter of marque," she said, sliding the folded contract across the deck rail rather than into his
hand, "sealed by the Trust's own maritime authority. Recognition. Immunity from prosecution in every
port that answers to us. Supply rights in six harbors currently closed to a ship flying no flag at
all." She let the offer sit before adding the rest of it. "In exchange, you operate against a list of
targets we specify, and the Scourge becomes, formally, an instrument of the Trust."

"You've read the reports on what the name's worth to the people who signal for it," Kanja said. "Ask
yourself why it's worth anything at all."

"Because it's reliable."

"Because it isn't yours. The moment your seal is on that contract, every captain who's ever struck
his colors on sight starts wondering whether he's surrendering to a man or to whoever's currently
paying that man's supply bills. It stops being a thing they can trust and starts being a thing they
have to negotiate with." He didn't touch the contract. "You'd be buying the name and killing the only
part of it that's worth buying."

"The targets we'd specify are precisely the ones you already hunt."

"Today. This administration. This list." He met her eyes. "Contracts outlast the people who write
them. Mine doesn't answer to whoever holds your seat in ten years, and it never will."

She folded the contract back into her coat without pressing further, professional to the last. "The
offer doesn't expire on our end."

"It doesn't need to. My answer won't change."

The envoy's ship withdrew before nightfall, and no formal record of the meeting was ever filed on the
Trust's side — a courtesy, Kanja understood, extended less to him than to her own career.
