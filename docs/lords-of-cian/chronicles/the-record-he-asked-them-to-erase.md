# The Record He Asked Them to Erase

*Locked canon, Batch 278, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1450`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle CII, wave 34, closing the wave. The method's first request to erase, rather than withhold or soften, an honest finding — extending the archive's permanence theme (MCD-1174) with a new standing practice for reformed wrongdoers.*

---

The letter came from a man neither of them had thought about in years — a mill administrator whose wage fraud the crew had proven and corrected a decade earlier, now dying, asking not for forgiveness but for something narrower: that his name be struck from the posted finding before his grandchildren grew old enough to read it for themselves.

"I paid every coin back," he wrote. "I've spent ten years since trying to be a different man than the one your ledger caught. I'm not asking you to say it didn't happen. I'm asking you not to make my children carry it after I'm gone."

Ezio read it twice before saying anything. "We've never erased a finding. Not once, not for anyone."

"We've never been asked by someone who actually changed," Kanja said. "That's different from every prior request we've had to refuse."

It was, and it wasn't. The withdrawn-complaint entry years earlier had taught them that even a request to soften a record could serve a truth the ledger hadn't fully accounted for yet. This was the opposite kind of request — not preserving an unresolved account for someone's later benefit, but asking a resolved one to disappear so its long-past shape wouldn't outlive the man who'd earned it.

Kanja didn't erase it. He went to see the administrator himself, and told him plainly why the original finding would stand exactly as written — not as a punishment still being carried out, but because a ledger that erased its own true entries on request stopped being a record anyone could trust, including the very families whose fraud claims it existed to protect.

"But I can add to it," Kanja said. "Not instead of the finding. Beside it. That you paid every coin back inside the year, that you've spent a decade since living differently, and that this note exists because you asked, unprompted, for your children to be spared shame you'd already spent ten years trying to earn your way clear of."

The administrator wept at that, not from relief exactly, but from something closer to it. The amended entry went into the volume as written — the original fraud finding untouched, a second dated note beside it recording everything true that had come after.

"It's not what he asked for," Ezio said.

"No. But it's truer than what he asked for, and it's the only version of mercy this ledger was ever built to hold. We don't get to decide a debt didn't happen. We can decide to write down, honestly, what a man did about it afterward." Kanja closed the volume gently over both entries. "That's not erasing the record. That's letting it finish telling the truth."
