# The Years He'd Have to Watch Them Lose

*Locked canon, Batch 264, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1387`). Captain Alias Chronicle LXXXVII, wave 29, closing the wave. Kanja and Efa Gol confront the mortality gap directly, without a crisis forcing the conversation.*

---

It was Efa Gol who raised it, not Kanja, on an ordinary evening with nothing wrong and nothing urgent, which was somehow harder for him than any of the crises that usually pried this particular subject open. They were counting the day's coin together, a task she'd let him share more often since her hands had slowed, and she set the pen down mid-column in a way that told him she'd been building up to this for longer than the moment suggested.

"I've done the arithmetic," she said. "Not the coin kind. The other kind. Hask's heart, my hands, this whole crew aging the ordinary way while you don't. I want to know if you've done it too, or if you've been avoiding the sum the way the rest of us sometimes avoid a column we already know we don't like the total of."

He didn't deflect it. "I've done it. Not once — I do it every time one of you gets sick, or slow, or tired in a way you didn't used to be. I know roughly how many of these conversations I have left with you specifically. I know it's fewer than either of us would want. I've known since the first funeral I spoke at."

"Does knowing help?"

"No. It just means I'm not surprised when it comes, which isn't the same thing as being ready." He looked out at the pier, at Mira's shift ending, at the ledger's new keeper closing out the day's figures, at a wall with names on it that would keep growing long after every person currently alive to grieve them was gone too. "I used to think the hardest part would be losing you all. I don't think that anymore. I think the hardest part is going to be everything after — watching this thing you all built keep going, generation after generation, and never once getting to introduce myself to whoever's running it by then as the same man who was there at the start."

Efa Gol picked the pen back up, but she didn't return to the column yet. "Then don't wait for that to be a private grief you carry alone the way you always have. Tell us now, while we're still here to actually hear it, instead of saving it for a version of us that can't."

He didn't have a ready answer for that. For the first time in a long while, he simply let the silence sit, and let her watch him sit in it, rather than reach for composure before either of them was ready to let it go.
