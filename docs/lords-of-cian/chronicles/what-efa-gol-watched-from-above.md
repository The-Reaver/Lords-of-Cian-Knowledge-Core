# What Efa Gol Watched From Above

*Locked canon, Batch 223, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1012`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle LVI, wave 19. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Efa Gol had run diversions above a dozen operations by the time the tunnel network needed one, and she
still made Kanja walk her through the below-ground timing twice before she'd commit her people to the
surface half of it.

"You need the garrison's attention on the surface for forty minutes, starting the moment your crew
breaches the supply cellar," she said, tracing the surface streets on her own separate map, one that
shared almost no reference points with his. "I can hold their attention that long. What I can't do is
know, from up here, whether forty minutes was enough down there or whether I'm pulling my people back
into the open five minutes before you actually need the noise to stop."

"That's the part I can't fix from my side either. I don't have a way to tell you the breach succeeded
except the breach succeeding." Kanja had turned the same problem over for two days without a clean
answer. "We're both committing to a plan neither of us can confirm is working until it's already
finished."

"Then we don't build the plan around confirmation. We build it around what each of us can control
without needing to know what the other one's doing." Efa Gol reworked her own timing on the spot — a
staged diversion rather than a single continuous one, three separate flashpoints across forty minutes
instead of one sustained distraction, each one loud enough to read as escalation from the garrison's
side regardless of what was or wasn't happening underground. "If your breach runs long, my third
flashpoint buys you extra minutes without me needing to know you need them. If it runs short, I stand
my people down after the second and nobody's exposed longer than the job required."

It worked exactly the way she'd redesigned it to work, though Kanja only learned the shape of it
afterward, secondhand — the garrison's attention pulled hard toward a burning supply cart at the
district's western edge just as his crew breached the cellar wall, a second flashpoint at the eastern
gate timed almost precisely to a delay he hadn't been able to avoid, and no third flashpoint at all,
because Efa Gol's people had read the quiet from the tunnel mouth correctly as success and pulled back
before the risk outlasted its purpose.

"You read the end of it from the surface, with nothing but a quiet tunnel mouth to go on," he said to
her afterward, genuinely unsure how she'd managed it. "I couldn't have told you it was over. I didn't
even know it was over until I was already back at the mouth myself."

"I wasn't reading the tunnel. I was reading my own people's exposure, same as you were reading load and
grain down there instead of trusting a lucky guess." Efa Gol folded her map, unbothered by a plan that
had worked without either side confirming the other's half in real time. "That's the whole trick of
running two operations that can't see each other. You don't trust the plan to hold because you
predicted it right. You trust it because you built it so that being wrong about the other half never
costs either of you more than you can afford."
