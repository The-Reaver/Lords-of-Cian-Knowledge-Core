# The Chart the Enemy Couldn't Steal

---

Corren Halst had fought at the Black Trench before Maw-9 ever added a name to the crew's own ledger,
which was the specific, quiet seniority that made him the one Kanja trusted to stand watch over the
one thing the entire fleet's elusiveness actually depended on — Danne Sok's accumulated charts, the
patrol-pattern maps that had kept *The Audit* three steps ahead of the Trust for months.

The infiltrator came aboard disguised as a conscripted deckhand, patient enough to spend eleven days
earning ordinary trust before making his first careful move toward the chart room during a night watch
he'd volunteered for specifically because Corren Halst was supposed to be off duty.

He wasn't. Corren Halst had swapped the watch without telling anyone, a habit he'd kept since the
Black Trench of never fully trusting a schedule that looked too convenient for someone else's purposes.

"You're reaching for a door that isn't yours," he said, quiet, from the shadow of the companionway,
before the infiltrator's hand had fully closed on the chart room's latch.

The man froze, then moved fast — a short blade already in hand, eleven days of patience spent in a
single desperate lunge that Corren Halst turned aside with the flat economy of someone who'd survived
worse before most of the current crew had joined it, disarming rather than killing, pinning him to the
deck with a knee and a grip that left no room for a second attempt.

"Directorate?" Corren Halst asked, once the man had stopped struggling.

"Doesn't matter who sent me."

"It matters to me. But it doesn't matter to the charts." Corren Halst didn't so much as glance toward
the door the man had failed to open. "Danne Sok's work stays where it is. Eleven days of patience,
wasted, because I've learned not to trust a watch schedule that changes right before something
valuable needs protecting."

Kanja arrived to find the matter already settled, the infiltrator bound and the charts untouched,
Corren Halst standing watch over both with the unhurried patience of a man who considered the whole
incident a minor, expected inconvenience rather than a crisis.

"How long have you been swapping watches without telling anyone?" Kanja asked.

"Since the Black Trench. Learned that year that the men you trust most are exactly the ones an enemy
studies hardest to imitate." Corren Halst finally allowed himself something like satisfaction, checking
the chart room's latch out of habit rather than necessity. "Danne Sok builds the routes. Somebody's got
to make sure nobody walks off with them before they're finished being useful."
