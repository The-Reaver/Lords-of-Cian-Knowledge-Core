# The One Who Walked the Other Way

*Locked canon, Batch 276, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1430`). Bane Alias Chronicle C, wave 34. First
desertion-in-reverse entry: a fighter recruited under Bane's own command deserts out of fear and is
later found serving the Directorate under coercion, met with the same amnesty logic already applied
to enemy soldiers in "The Garrison He Let Walk" (`MCD-1102`).*

---

He'd been recruited eight months earlier, one more name among the column's newer fighters, unremarkable
until the night after a brutal engagement when he simply wasn't there at roll call. No note, no
warning. Corren Halst assumed the worst kind of desertion — a man who'd lost his nerve and run rather
than face another fight like the one before.

The truth, when it surfaced months later, was harder than that. Directorate agents had found his
family first, threatened them with reprisal reserved for known deserters' kin, and given him one way
out: return to serve, quietly, or watch what happened to people who'd done nothing but share his name.
He'd run not from the fight but toward the only thing that might keep his mother and sister alive.

They found him again at a garrison Bane's column was moving to take — recognized mid-engagement,
frozen at the sight of a face he clearly hadn't expected to see across a battle line again, weapon
half-raised and going nowhere.

Bane didn't kill him. Didn't strike him. Pulled him bodily out of the fighting instead, hauled him
behind a wall while Corren Halst held the line without either of them, and asked him one question:
"Is your family still alive?"

The man could barely get the answer out.

"Then that's the only fact that matters right now," Bane said. He arranged it within the hour — a
route out of the region entirely, false papers Danne Sok's contacts could produce faster than anyone
expected, a place three provinces distant where a Directorate reprisal order would never reach. Not
forgiveness exactly, and not punishment either. Just the same math he'd already applied once to an
enemy garrison walking free under amnesty terms: that a man cornered into serving the wrong side
because of a threat against people he loved wasn't the same category of problem as a man who'd chosen
it freely, and deserved a way out that didn't run through a blade.

"He fought against us tonight," Corren Halst said afterward, not quite an objection, more an
accounting. "Some of the men who saw him cross back over won't forget that easily."

"They don't have to forget it," Bane said. "They just don't get to decide his family dies for it. I
already let a garrison full of strangers walk once on worse math than this. I'm not doing less for one
of our own."

The man and his family were three provinces away within the month, and no one in the column ever saw
him again.

*Continuity notes (not narrative): first entry to apply the amnesty/mercy doctrine (`MCD-1102`,
`MCD-431`) to a deserter from Bane's own recruited ranks rather than to enemy soldiers or civilians --
the desertion itself is a genuinely new register for this alias, distinct from the trusted-insider
betrayal of "The Guide Who Sold Them an Inch at a Time" (`MCD-1392`, a coerced informant who never
left) and from every prior crew-loyalty entry. Deliberately does not resolve the discomfort some
fighters feel at the man's return to enemy lines, matching the sub-series' preference for unresolved
moral texture over clean absolution. No new named characters -- the deserter and his family are
unnamed; Corren Halst and Danne Sok reused. Opens wave 34.*
