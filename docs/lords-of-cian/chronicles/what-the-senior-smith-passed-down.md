# What the Senior Smith Passed Down

*Locked canon, Batch 202, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-887`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XLII, wave 14 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

She had been with the campaign longer than almost anyone aboard The Anvil save Kanja himself, and had
spent the last several months, quietly and without announcement, training a successor — a younger
smith from the crew's own ranks, chosen not for raw talent alone but for the same patient temperament
the campaign's method had always depended on more than skill.

"You've been grooming her for a while now," Kanja observed, watching the younger smith run a floor
inspection the senior smith would once have run herself.

"Longer than you've probably noticed. This campaign won't run forever, and neither will I." She said it
without sentiment, the plain accounting of someone who had long since made peace with her own eventual
absence. "Someone needs to carry the standard forward after I'm not the one holding the floor to it
anymore. Wasn't going to leave that to chance."

"What did you actually teach her? Beyond the craft itself."

She considered the question longer than he'd expected. "The same thing I watched you teach every
apprentice this campaign's ever reached, whether you meant to be teaching it or not. That the floor
judges the work, not the person doing it. That a reheat rushed is a reheat you'll answer for later.
That the cost of doing something right quietly, without needing anyone to see it, is worth paying
anyway." She glanced toward the younger smith, still working the inspection without knowing she was
being discussed. "I didn't invent any of that. I just made sure it didn't end with me."

"It won't end with her either, if she does with someone else what you just did with her."

"That's the idea." The old smith allowed herself something close to satisfaction, watching a floor she
had helped build run correctly without her direct hand on it. "I've watched this whole campaign prove,
over and over, that nothing worth building depends on one person staying in the room forever. Didn't
see any reason the lesson should stop applying to me, just because I'm the one who's been in the room
the longest."
