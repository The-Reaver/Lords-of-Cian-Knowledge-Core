# The Surrender That Wasn't One

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Bane Alias Chronicle XLVII, wave 16. Rebellion era, within the "Bane" window, before the Corrow ravine engagement — not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The white banners went up over the Threnmoor outpost an hour after first light, and the garrison
commander who'd sent word the night before had every reason to be believed — a besieged position,
supply lines cut for eleven days, no relief column within reach. Bane walked the parley ground with
Corren Halst at his shoulder and Onyx of Oblivion sheathed, the way he always carried it into a
surrender he expected to accept.

Six wounded lay along the near wall on stretchers, bandaged, still, waiting to be handed over with
the rest of the garrison. It was the wounded that Onyx noticed first — not through sight, which saw
exactly what they were meant to be seen as, but through the blade's own quieter sense, the one Bane
had learned by then not to override with his eyes. Veil Piercer had no interest in what a thing
looked like. It answered a different question: what a thing actually was, underneath whatever it
had been arranged to look like.

The bandages were fresh — hours fresh, not the eleven-day-old wounds a genuine siege casualty would
carry. The stillness was too even, six men breathing in a rhythm too close to identical to be pain
rather than discipline. And beneath the linen on the nearest man's leg, where a real wound would
have shown the particular disorder of torn muscle, Veil Piercer read only unbroken skin, tensed and
waiting.

Bane didn't stop walking. He didn't change his pace or his face, because whatever was waiting for
him to react was watching for exactly that.

"Six wounded," he said to the garrison commander, still approaching, still within easy reach of the
stretchers. "That's generous of you, keeping them close enough to hand over first."

"They can't be moved far."

"No. I don't imagine they can." He stopped two full strides short of the nearest stretcher, farther
than courtesy required, close enough that the six men on it would need to cross real ground before
they reached him. "I'll take your surrender. I won't take it standing this close to men who aren't
wounded."

The commander's face didn't move fast enough to hide what it meant. Halst's hand had already found
the hilt at his own hip, unhurried, and behind Bane the column had already begun spreading wide
along the wall line the moment he'd stopped walking — a signal they'd drilled without ever needing
to name it.

"They stand up," Bane said, "or the parley ends here and we finish this the way you were hoping to
avoid. Either way, I already know which one it is. I'd rather you choose it than have it chosen for
you."

Three of the six stood on their own, slowly, weapons already loosening from beneath the stretcher
frames. The other three stayed down, genuinely wounded after all, which was its own kind of answer
— whoever had built the plan had used real casualties as cover for the rest of it, spending men who
were already hurt to hide the men who weren't. The garrison surrendered in full twenty minutes
later, the ambush abandoned before it had ever truly begun, and the commander never learned exactly
how Bane had known.

"The eyes," Halst said afterward, meaning it as a question.

"Not the eyes." Bane looked back once at the three genuinely wounded men, already being carried
toward the column's own medic rather than left behind. "Something else saw it first. I've stopped
asking which part of me does the seeing. I only trust that it does."
