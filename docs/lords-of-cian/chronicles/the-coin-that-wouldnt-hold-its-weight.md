# The Coin That Wouldn't Hold Its Weight

*Locked canon, Batch 284, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1496`). Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XCIV, first entry
in the thirty-second wave. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241). Not a
territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Ezio Valcari noticed it first, the way he noticed everything: not the coin itself but the arithmetic
around it. Three settlements running the campaign's tool-and-material credit scheme were reporting
the same complaint within a week of each other. The stamped trade-tokens the crew issued against
future labor kept losing weight — not stolen, not counterfeited, just shaved, a sliver at a time,
by hands too many to trace, until a token that should have bought a full day's iron bought three
parts of one.

"It's not an enemy," Ezio said, laying four clipped tokens on the counting table beside four whole
ones, the difference barely visible to the eye and unmistakable on the scale. "It's arithmetic
again, just running the other direction. Enough small hands shaving enough small slivers, and the
whole credit system we built to replace coin starts behaving exactly like the coin it replaced."

Garren Hask had already run the numbers twice before Kanja asked him to. "Every token that's come
back to us this month is light. Not by much, any one of them. By enough, all of them together, that
the settlements holding the most tokens — the poorest ones, the ones with the least to spare in the
first place — are the ones absorbing the loss."

There was no district to march toward, no prefect's office to burn down, no single hand to catch
mid-shave. The debasement wasn't sabotage; it was the ordinary erosion of any object people were
willing to trust a little too far past its actual substance. Kanja turned one of the clipped tokens
over twice before setting it down. "We can't police a sliver at a time. We stamped iron because we
didn't trust coin. Maybe the answer isn't a harder stamp. Maybe it's not trusting the token past
what it can actually prove."

What the crew built instead, over the following weeks, wasn't a new coin at all. Every settlement
already kept its own tally of labor owed and labor given — Hask's own ledger method, taught wherever
the campaign had passed through long enough to leave the habit behind. Kanja had those local tallies
cross-checked directly against each other, settlement to settlement, without a token changing hands
in between at all: a debt owed a smith in one district settled by a delivery of grain credited
against a debt owed in the next, the tokens themselves demoted from currency to nothing more than a
receipt for a trade already verified elsewhere. A shaved token could still lie about its own weight.
A cross-checked ledger entry, witnessed on both ends, couldn't.

It solved nothing overnight, and Kanja said so plainly to the assembled settlement stewards. "This
doesn't stop anyone from shaving a coin. It just stops the coin from being the thing that matters."
The poorest settlements, holding the most worthless tokens, were the first to stop caring how much
those tokens weighed.
