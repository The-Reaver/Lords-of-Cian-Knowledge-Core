# Lauris Chronicle XXIV: The Floor of the Maelstrom

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1641`). Twenty-fourth entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, a Strand D entry giving full-scene treatment to Operation 5, the Maelstrom Beast
(previously only summarized at `MCD-1544`): her three-hour walk along the channel floor of the deep
tidal Voskharen Wetlands to a fourteen-meter apex predator's primary chamber, the engagement itself
-- surviving both its bite and an eighty-thousand-kilogram compression coil -- and its termination
with the Spine of Dagon, drawn and swung underwater. Puts `CC-134`'s defining combat-joy trait on
the page in a pure-physicality register distinct from Chronicle I's crowd-control showcase. No new
named characters.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Apprentice Contracts, Operation 5: "The offshore artillery could not follow it
into the channels because the channels do not float. Neither do I. This was, in the end, the only
advantage the contract required."*

Operation 5 is, among her Apprentice Contracts, the one Lauris has described to me most often in
purely physical terms, without the surrounding weight of institutional consequence that colors so
much of what came after it, and I have come to treasure the account for exactly that reason -- it
is, I think, the clearest record I have of what her body simply enjoys doing, unencumbered by
anything larger than the doing itself.

The contract's shape was straightforward enough on paper: an apex predator, roughly fourteen meters
and forty thousand kilograms by the Directorate's best estimate, had killed some two hundred
residents of the Voskharen coastal communities across three months, withdrawing after each strike
into deep tidal channels no Directorate assault vessel could navigate and no offshore artillery
could follow. The Voskharen Wetlands' channel system, at its deepest, ran some twenty-two meters
below the surface swell -- viable, by every Directorate calculation, only to specialized diving
gear the creature's own habitat made impractical to deploy.

Lauris did not deploy diving gear. She walked.

Karesian density, she has explained to me more than once with a patience I suspect she reserves
specifically for questions she considers obvious, is fundamentally incompatible with floating. What
the Directorate's engineers treated as an operational constraint requiring elaborate technical
accommodation, she treated as simply the terrain she was built to cross on foot. Three hours along
the channel floor, current and silt and the particular blind pressure of deep water registering, by
her own account, as background rather than obstacle, brought her to the creature's primary chamber
-- a hollow some twenty-two meters down, into which it had, by every indication, withdrawn to rest
between hunts, confident that nothing capable of following it there existed.

It was wrong within moments of her arrival.

I will record the engagement as she has recorded it to me, which is to say without excessive
ornament, because I have learned that Lauris's own accounts of her most physically demanding
contracts are, paradoxically, her plainest: the creature struck first, its bite closing across her
torso with a force that fractured its own teeth against her sternum rather than the reverse. It
followed with a full-body compression coil -- something in the neighborhood of eighty thousand
kilograms of applied force, by her later estimate, distributed across her frame in a way that
would have collapsed a conventional target's ribcage before the creature had finished tightening.
Her Hexa-Lamellar Lattice distributed the load laterally instead, spreading what should have been a
single catastrophic failure point across a structure built across generations of crushing gravity
specifically to refuse to have one.

She has told me, and I believe her, that some part of what followed was genuine pleasure -- not at
the violence for its own sake, which she has always been careful to distinguish, but at the plain
fact of a body doing, without compromise, precisely what an opponent of considerable and genuine
mass and force could not prevent it from doing. She severed the creature's spine at close range and
finished the termination with the Spine of Dagon, drawn and swung fully underwater, a technique her
own instructors at Karth-Ven had trained into her for exactly this kind of engagement without, I
suspect, ever imagining the particular sea creature it would eventually be used against.

The total engagement, from her entry into the channel to her emergence with the body some hours
later, ran approximately seven hours -- the largest single opponent by mass she had faced on Cian
to that point in her career, though its measured density, at roughly eighteen hundred times
baseline, fell well below the thresholds her later Branded-tier engagements would come to treat as
routine. I mention this not to diminish the account but because I think the proportion matters: this
was not, by the standards her career would eventually set, a difficult fight. It was, by every
account she has given me, simply an enjoyable one -- a full day's honest use of a body that had
never once, in her considerable experience of it, betrayed her.

Her closing note, characteristically brief: *Seven hours, one beast, two hundred debts partially
repaid. I would do the walk again tomorrow. I have told Sephtis this and he has stopped being
surprised by it.*
