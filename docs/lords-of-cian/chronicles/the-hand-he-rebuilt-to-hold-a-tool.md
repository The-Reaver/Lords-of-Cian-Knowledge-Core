# The Hand He Rebuilt to Hold a Tool

*Locked canon, Batch 279, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1455`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XCVIII, wave 33. The alias's first disability-accommodation register, extending the dignity-of-labor theme (`VB-061`) into accessibility rather than teaching or institution-building.*

---

The tradesmen's association's new certification board turned the old digger away twice before Pell Ostra thought to mention it to Kanja, and even then she framed it like a complaint about the board rather than a request on the man's behalf.

"He's been reading rock longer than half the certified journeymen combined," she said. "They won't pass him because he can't grip a standard chisel properly. Not since he lost the hand."

Kanja found him at the tunnel mouth, sitting apart from the crews still waiting on assignment, working a length of scrap timber with a knife held awkwardly in his remaining hand, still practicing a grip that would never again work the way it once had.

"They tell you the trade's closed to you now," Kanja said, sitting down across from him.

"They tell me the standard tools are closed to me. Which they say is the same thing." The old digger didn't look up from the timber. "I've spent thirty years reading this rock with both hands. I don't have both hands anymore. I understand why the board doesn't want to certify a man who might drop a chisel mid-strike."

"That's a fair worry about a standard chisel." Kanja turned the timber over, studying the man's grip, the angle his wrist could still manage, the strength that remained in what was left of his forearm. "It's not a fair worry about you. Those are two different problems, and the board's only solved for the first one."

He spent three days building what the digger needed rather than asking him to relearn what he'd lost -- a harness that seated across the shoulder and forearm, distributing grip force away from the missing hand entirely, and a modified chisel head that locked into it at an angle built specifically around the wrist motion the man still had full command of. It was not an elegant piece of gear by Trinity standards, nothing like the precision engineering of Kanja's own equipment, just honest fitted work built to one specific body's actual capability rather than an assumed standard one.

"Try it on stone, not timber," Kanja said, handing it over. "Timber lies about what it'll take. Stone won't."

The old digger worked a test seam for the better part of an hour, adjusting his stance twice, and by the end of it had cut a clean, even line no less precise than any two-handed journeyman on the crew.

"This isn't charity," he said finally, flexing the harness experimentally.

"No. It's the same thing I'd have done for anyone the standard tool wasn't built for. You're not the exception to the trade. The chisel was." Kanja watched him work the seam a second time, faster now, more sure of the harness's give. "Take it back to the board. Let them certify what you can actually do instead of what a tool assumes everyone can do."

The board certified him within the week, on the strength of the seam itself rather than any argument made on his behalf. Pell Ostra, watching him leave with the harness strapped secure, allowed herself something close to satisfaction.

"You didn't lower the standard for him," she said.

"I never intended to. I just stopped pretending the standard chisel was the only honest way to meet it."
