# What Garren Hask Packed That Week

*Locked canon, Batch 255, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1146`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle LXXXIX, wave 30.
Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. A quiet, unexplained instinct for preparation, with no fight
attached to it.*

---

Nobody asked Garren Hask why, in one otherwise unremarkable week, he spent every spare hour restocking
and double-checking supplies that hadn't needed restocking in months — emergency dressings at every
site the crew regularly used, a second backup copy of every current ledger carried to a separate,
undisclosed location, spare tools laid in at three depots that had gone half a year without needing
any repair at all.

He couldn't have said why himself, if asked directly. There was no report of an imminent raid, no
specific threat Hask's own figures had flagged, nothing in the district's ordinary rhythm that
explained the sudden thoroughness. It simply felt, that particular week, like a week where being
over-prepared mattered more than it usually did, and Hask had learned by then to trust instincts of
that kind even when he couldn't account for them on a ledger page.

"You're spending resources we don't obviously need to spend," Kanja observed, watching him inventory
a third set of emergency dressings nobody had requested.

"Probably." Hask didn't stop what he was doing. "I've kept this ledger long enough to notice when a
number feels wrong before I can prove it's wrong. Doesn't mean I'm right every time. Means I've
learned it costs less to be over-prepared and wrong than under-prepared and right too late."

Kanja didn't argue the point, and didn't ask him to stop. He'd learned, across two years of watching
Hask's figures catch things no one else noticed, to give that particular instinct room rather than
question it out loud. Whatever it was Hask felt coming — and neither of them named it, that week, as
anything more specific than a feeling — the district's supply lines finished the season stronger and
more redundant than they'd been at any point since the crew's earliest days, for reasons that would
only make sense, if they ever made sense at all, from somewhere further down the road than either of
them could see yet.

The extra dressings sat unused for weeks after. The backup ledgers stayed exactly where Hask had
placed them, undisturbed, a second copy of everything the district had built existing quietly
somewhere no single raid could reach both at once. Hask never explained the week to anyone, and
nobody, watching him work, felt the need to make him try.
