# Lauris Chronicle XI: The Number Recalibration Could Not Undo

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1628`). Eleventh entry in Lauris Letitia's own Chronicle
series, the fourth entry of Strand K, drafted as part of a 50-entry wave across eight strand-agents
under Abad's blanket authorization. Dramatizes directly, for the first time, the age-fourteen cohort
density assessment already locked at `MCD-166` -- Mira Threnarr-Olmedrin's fourfold recalibration
confirming Lauris's 2,800x reading, the Sister-Hold's three-day deliberation, and Lauris's own
recorded priority on learning she would relocate to Vask Karth-Ven. Closes on Velith's own log entry
for the departure. No new named characters. Full text below.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Vask Threnarr, age fourteen: "The instrument was recalibrated twice. The number
did not change. I asked whether I would still see my cohort. No one answered quickly enough, so I
asked again."*

I have already set down, in an earlier entry, the page Lauris wrote at eight, and what I called then
the first evidence of what she would become. This is the second piece, and unlike the first, this
one does not sit quietly in a private note. This one reached the Sister-Hold's own formal record
within the week, and it is the reason, more than any other single day, that today I know her as a
woman trained at Vask Karth-Ven rather than one who lived out an ordinary Threnarr life among the
archives.

The assessment itself was routine -- Mira Threnarr-Olmedrin, the cohort's physical instructor, ran
density calibration on all forty-seven children of Lauris's generation twice yearly, a standard
measure against which the Sister-Hold tracked development. Cohort norms at fourteen ran, by the
Threnarr archive's own tables, between 900x and 1,400x, consistent with the 800-1,400x range Lauris
herself had tracked within since age three. Mira's instrument read Lauris at 2,800x. Exactly double
the ceiling any child in her generation, or several generations before it, had ever registered.

Mira did not announce this. She recalibrated the instrument, quietly, and tested Lauris again, alone,
the other children already dismissed for the day. The second reading matched the first. She sent for
a third instrument from the Vask's central archive, the kind used to certify adult combat ratings
rather than measure children, and tested a third time. I have read Mira's own account of that third
test, preserved in her later report to the Sister-Hold, and it is, for a woman Lauris herself once
described to me as cool-tempered by nature, unusually plain about what she felt: *I did not expect
the number to hold. I have run this instrument on grown Iron-Speakers who read lower. I recalibrated
a fourth time because I did not trust myself before I trusted the child.*

The Sister-Hold -- Selene, Drenneth, Mira, Veth, Olda, five women who had shared daily authority over
Lauris's upbringing for fourteen years -- deliberated for three days before deciding anything. I do
not think three days sounds, to anyone reading this from outside Karesian time, like very long. I am
told it was, for a decision of this weight, unusually fast, and that the speed itself says something
about how little room the number left them to disagree. A density reading double the cohort ceiling,
confirmed four times against three separate instruments, was not a question the Sister-Hold could
resolve by continuing to teach Lauris exactly as they had been teaching her.

Lauris was told on the morning of the fourth day, in the same room where Selene had once explained
smaller things to her in smaller ways. I have her own archive entry for that morning, and it is,
characteristically, shorter than the occasion seems to warrant. She was told she would relocate to
Vask Karth-Ven, for training the Sister-Hold could no longer provide her at Threnarr. She asked one
question, and her own record insists it was the only question that mattered to her that morning:
whether she would still see her cohort. Selene's answer, by Lauris's account, took a moment longer to
arrive than she expected, and she asked a second time before it came. The answer, in the end, was
honest rather than comforting -- sometimes, when the Vasks' training schedules allowed it, and less
often than either of them would have liked.

She left Threnarr two months later, with Selene accompanying her for the crossing and the first
season of settling in. I have found no record of an elaborate farewell -- Karesian custom, even in
the Sister-Hold's memory-thick culture, did not run to ceremony for a relocation within the same
civilization's own borders. But Velith's own log for that week survives, three lines, the longest
entry from the cohort's own children preserved anywhere in the Threnarr archive concerning Lauris's
departure: *She asked if she would still see us. She did not ask if she would be different. I think
she already knew the second answer and only needed the first one confirmed.*

I set this down because I think it is the clearest early instance of a pattern that would define the
whole of the woman I now know: faced with news that would remake the shape of her life, Lauris's
first question was never about herself. It was about what she would still be permitted to keep.
