# The Town That Was Already Afraid

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Scourge Alias Chronicle, wave 6. Long Mask era,
age 36, roughly a year into the V1 Forge-Coat's service. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The militia had barricaded both piers before the first liberated ship ever came into sight of the
harbor mouth, working off nothing but three weeks of drifting rumor and a lookout who swore the
black sails on the horizon meant the Scourge had come to burn the town for trading with slavers
twenty years past, whether the current harbormaster had ever laid eyes on a chained hold or not.

He came ashore alone, unarmored beneath a plain traveler's coat, and found forty men with borrowed
spears standing between him and a dock that had done nothing wrong in living memory.

"Stand down," the harbormaster called, voice cracking on the second word. "We know what you are.
We know what you did to the Salt Keep."

"You know a story about what I did to the Salt Keep." He stopped well short of spear range and
raised both empty hands, deliberately, the way he'd learned to when the fear in a crowd outran the
facts feeding it. "I'm not here for your harbor. I'm here because a Trust cruiser three days behind
me is running captives through this strait, and I need a place to put two hundred people who haven't
eaten properly in a month. That's the whole of it."

"The stories say you burn towns that so much as looked the other way."

"The stories are wrong about a lot of things. That's usually the ones that survive best — the ones
that make the fear do work the truth can't." He let the silence sit a moment, watching the spears
waver, men glancing sideways at each other rather than at him. "I've never burned a town for looking
the other way. I've burned exactly one thing on purpose in my life, and it wasn't a town, and every
person who watched it happen is still alive because I did."

The harbormaster didn't lower his spear, but he didn't raise it either. "Prove it."

"I can't prove a negative to you standing here. What I can do is anchor outside your harbor instead
of inside it, send the captives ashore under your own men's escort instead of mine, and let you see
for yourselves what two hundred freed people actually look like before you decide what I am from a
rumor." He turned back toward the water without waiting for an answer, already trusting the offer
more than the spears. "Your choice whether that's enough."

It was. By dusk the militia had become an escort instead of a wall, and the harbormaster — watching
children who hadn't stood on solid ground in a month stumble gratefully onto his own dock — never did
apologize for the spears, only muttered, half to himself, that the stories ought to mention this part
too, the part where the fear had nearly cost two hundred people a proper meal on dry land.
