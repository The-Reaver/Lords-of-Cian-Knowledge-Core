# The Thirty Feet He Refused to Lose

*Locked canon, Batch 116, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-396`). Captain Alias Chronicle II. Rebellion era, a new
engagement distinct from any already-locked battle, centered on protecting the crew rather than
confronting an institutional opponent. A detailed Trinity showcase in a purely defensive, crew-
protective register. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The ambush caught the supply column at its most exposed thirty feet — a narrow causeway crossing
between two warehouses, wide enough for the carts but nothing else, with eleven of the crew's
newest recruits strung out along it carrying nothing heavier than requisitioned grain sacks when the
first shots came from the rooftops on either side.

Kanja closed the thirty feet in under four seconds.

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System absorbed the first volley meant for the recruits directly, taking
rounds that would have found unarmored backs and converting their force into stored charge before a
single one reached past him — he did not dodge, did not seek cover, positioned himself instead as the
only thing standing between the rooftops and eleven people who had signed on four days earlier and
had no idea yet what a real engagement felt like. Obsidian Malice discharged upward in a single strike
against the nearer rooftop's parapet, not to kill the shooters positioned there but to collapse enough
of the ledge that their footing broke and their fire scattered wide for the several seconds that
mattered most. Onyx handled the second rooftop directly, Veil Piercer finding the gap between two
shooters' overlapping fields of fire that neither had realized existed until the blade was already
past it.

The recruits reached cover without a single casualty. Kanja held the causeway's exposed center the
entire time, absorbing what he could and deflecting the rest, until the ambush broke and scattered
rather than press an attack that had cost them two positions and gained nothing.

"You could have told us to run first," one of the recruits said afterward, hands still shaking,
watching Mafesto's scored plating where round after round had struck and been converted rather than
let through. "Instead you came to us."

"Running across open ground under fire gets people killed who don't know yet how to run under fire.
You didn't. So I came to you instead, and made the thirty feet between the warehouses the only ground
that mattered until you were clear of it." He checked the war club's housing, already bleeding back
toward zero charge. "That's the whole job, far as I understand it. Not being the person everyone runs
toward when things go wrong. Being the reason they don't have to run at all."
