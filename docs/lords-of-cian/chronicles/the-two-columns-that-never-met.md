# The Two Columns That Never Met

*Locked canon, Batch 254, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1110`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXXX, wave 27. A storm-washed
road and a miscarried messenger leave two allied columns unable to reach a rendezvous in time --
unlike "What He Couldn't Be in Two Places For," there is no clean solution here, and a real loss
occurs on the side not chosen.*

---

Nobody failed at anything. That was the part that made it worse, not easier, once Bane understood the
whole shape of it afterward. The road the second column had been meant to take washed out two days
before either side knew it would matter, and the runner sent to warn them of the delay took a route
that put him a full day behind the very news he was carrying.

By the time word reached Bane, both obligations were already failing at once: the allied column,
stranded and expecting relief that couldn't have physically arrived in time no matter what anyone
did, and a separate liberation window closing the same night, forty miles in the other direction,
that would not come again for weeks.

There was no version of this that split cleanly. Maret Vos had run an interception alone before and
succeeded; there was no equivalent hand-off here, no one positioned to cover both, no route fast
enough to reach the stranded column before whatever was already happening to them had already
happened.

Bane chose the liberation window. It was the choice with a chance of actually changing anything in
the time available — the stranded column's fate was already largely decided by the time he had to
decide anything at all. He didn't pretend the choice was costless, and didn't let anyone else pretend
it for him.

The allied column took losses waiting for relief that structurally could never have come in time. Nine
people, by the report that eventually reached him. He read the names out loud himself, once the
report arrived, rather than let Garren Hask fold them silently into the ledger.

"No one made a mistake," Corren Halst said, watching him read the names a second time. "Not the
runner. Not you. Not them."

"I know. That's what makes it sit worse than the ones with a mistake in them somewhere. I can't even
tell myself I'd choose differently with the same information. I'd make the same call again. Nine
people are still dead because of a road and a runner and a night that only had room for one of two
things to go right."
