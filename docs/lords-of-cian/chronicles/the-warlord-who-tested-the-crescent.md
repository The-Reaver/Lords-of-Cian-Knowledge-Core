# The Warlord Who Tested the Crescent

*Locked canon, Batch 122, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-415`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle VI, closing the
second wave. Long Mask era, Pirate Dawn (ages 48-52, `MCD-250`). A rival warlord's direct challenge
to the Gale Straits crescent formation's authority. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone
material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The warlord who'd carved out a smuggling monopoly along the Gale Straits' southern edge had built
his own reputation on refusing to recognize anyone else's, and he sailed directly into the
crescent formation's recognized waters specifically to prove the black sails' authority was a bluff
nobody had actually tested in years.

"Everybody surrenders to you on sight," he called across the closing water, his own ships arrayed in
a deliberate challenge formation. "Nobody's actually seen you fight in years. I've started wondering
if the crescent's just a story that's outlived whoever it was originally true of."

Kanja didn't order an attack. He brought his own flagship alongside, alone, within easy weapons
range, and simply waited.

"You want to test whether the reputation's real," he said, once the ships were close enough to talk
without shouting. "Fine. Here's the test. I'm not going to fight your whole fleet to prove a point.
I'm going to fight you, personally, one exchange, right now, and whatever happens to you personally
is going to travel through every crew watching this exactly as fast as the reputation you're trying
to disprove."

The warlord, having built his own career on exactly this kind of personal-stakes challenge, couldn't
refuse without conceding the very point he'd sailed south to make. The exchange lasted less than a
minute — the Rexmar Machete meeting an experienced blade with the same unhurried, plainly skilled
precision that had ended a slaver captain's life years earlier at a different latitude, disarming
rather than killing, because the point had never been the warlord's death, only the demonstration.

"The crescent's not a story," Kanja said, standing over a warlord disarmed but unharmed, loud enough
to carry to every ship watching. "It's a standing offer. Recognize it, and you never have to find out
what happens when someone tests it personally instead of taking my word for it. You just did, and
you're walking away from this exchange, which tells every crew watching exactly as much as your
death would have — that the offer's real, and that I don't need to kill you to prove it."

The warlord's own smuggling operation folded within the season, not from any further confrontation,
but from the specific, permanent damage of having personally, publicly tested a legend and found it
entirely true — a story that traveled through the Straits' underworld faster and more thoroughly than
any battle report could have.
