# The Boy Who Counted Instead of Sang

*Locked canon, Batch 247, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1077`). Crow King Alias Chronicle LXI, wave 21, opening
it. The fourth generation's first solo field use, built around exact recall rather than rhythm,
song, or percussion. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The third generation did not tell him what to build. That was the point of the test.

"Your teacher taught you to listen before you speak," she said. "I'm not going to teach you to sing
before you're ready, because I don't actually know if you're supposed to. Go find out what the craft
looks like when it's yours."

He spent four days in a supply depot's records shed before he said a word to anyone about a plan,
copying requisition ledgers not because anyone had asked him to but because the columns of numbers
held still in his head the way sound never had for him — he could recite a week-old tally back
correct to the unit without straining for it, the same gift that had made him unremarkable as a
runner and unteachable, everyone had assumed, as anything more. What he built from four days of
reading was not a false signal thrown at a checkpoint or a sensor grid fooled by layered voices. It
was a phantom garrison: a supply requisition trail for a company that did not exist, entered into
the depot's own record-keeping with figures so internally consistent — rations matching headcount,
headcount matching equipment wear, equipment wear matching three separate seasons of weather — that
when a records auditor cross-checked it against two independent inquiries a week apart, both came
back clean.

It held for eleven days before anyone thought to ask why a garrison that consumed real supplies had
never once filed an incident report. By then the diversion had done its work: two full requisition
convoys meant for an active Directorate position had been quietly rerouted to feed a company that
existed only on paper, starving the position they were actually meant to reinforce by exactly the
margin the crew needed to take it without a fight.

When the auditor finally caught the gap and reported a fraud investigation upward, there was nothing
left to trace it to — no voice, no signal, no pattern of false radio chatter for a cryptographer to
study. Just a set of numbers that had been true enough, for eleven days, to survive being checked.

The third generation read his own account of it afterward and said nothing for a long moment. "I
built mine out of sound because sound was what I had," she finally said. "You didn't need any of
that. You needed a clerk who trusts his own numbers more than his own eyes." She didn't call it a
success in front of him — she asked instead whether he understood why it had worked, and made him
explain the whole chain back to her, tally by tally, before she'd say the word herself.

Kanja, told of it later, didn't compare it to the marsh trick or the Braid or anything that had ever
carried the legend's name. "It's not smaller," he said, when someone suggested it was a quiet way to
start. "It's a different shape entirely. That's what I hoped for when I told her to let him find his
own version instead of teaching him mine again."
