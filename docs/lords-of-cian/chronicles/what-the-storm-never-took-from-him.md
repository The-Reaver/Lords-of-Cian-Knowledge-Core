# What the Storm Never Took From Him

There was no storm coming, no forecast pending, nothing for the fleet to time itself against. Kanja
stood at the rail alone, the way he rarely allowed himself to, until Efa Gol found him there and
didn't leave.

"You're thinking about him," she said. It wasn't a question. Everyone who'd sailed with him long
enough had learned to recognize the particular stillness that settled over him whenever Sephtis's name
came up, even unspoken.

"I'm thinking about all of it," Kanja said. "Every reading he ever made. Every one she's made since.
Every ship that turned in time because an old man was willing to say, out loud, in front of people
whose lives depended on it, that he might be wrong — and then be right often enough that being wrong
stopped being the thing anyone remembered about him."

"Is that what this alias means to you? The readings?"

"No," Kanja said. "It's what the readings cost to make. Bane frightens people into stillness. The
Blue-Collar Titan builds things with his hands. This one — this one taught me something none of the
others did. That the bravest thing a person can do isn't fighting. It's standing in front of everyone
who trusts you and saying you don't know for certain, and then doing the work anyway, in full view,
where a wrong call can't hide."

"You didn't teach him that," Efa Gol said. "He taught you."

"He did," Kanja agreed. "I've worn a dozen names since I was young enough that none of them fit right.
This is the only one that was never really about me at all. It was always about a man who read the
sky honestly, and a woman who learned to do it after him, and now a third who'll learn it after her.
I just happened to be the one standing on the deck when it started."

The water was calm, unremarkably so, the kind of evening that needed no forecast because nothing in
it demanded one. Kanja stayed at the rail a long while after Efa Gol left him to it, watching a sky
that, for once, had nothing at all to say.
