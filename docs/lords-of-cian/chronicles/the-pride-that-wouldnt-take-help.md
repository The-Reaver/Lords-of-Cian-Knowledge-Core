# The Pride That Wouldn't Take Help

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XXXIV, wave 11.
Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The settlement's headman refused the crew at the district border, before The Anvil had even finished
mooring — not out of fear, and not out of suspicion of the reputation, but out of a plain, stated
unwillingness to accept help his own district had nothing to offer back in return.

"Every other district on your route's traded you something," he said, arms folded, blocking the path
up from the dock himself. "Grain, timber, labor, something. We've got nothing left to trade after two
bad seasons. I won't have my people take charity and call it anything else."

Kanja didn't argue the point directly, recognizing, in the headman's own stiff posture, something he'd
seen before in a different register entirely — the same refusal a resentful local smith had once
carried into an early rebuild, dressed now in a different reason but built from the same root.

"I'm not offering charity. Charity's given with nothing expected back, and I don't actually work that
way, whatever the reputation says." He let that sit for a moment. "What I need, this district's the
only one on the whole route positioned to give me. You sit on the best stone quarry within two weeks'
travel. I need quarried stone for the next four rebuild sites more than I need anything grain or
timber could buy me."

The headman hadn't expected that answer, and took a long moment weighing it. "You'd take stone as
payment. Real payment, not a face-saving gesture."

"I'd take it because I actually need it, and because your district's the only one that has it in the
quantity the next four sites require." Kanja meant it as plainly as he'd meant anything on the whole
campaign. "I'm not interested in a trade that only exists to spare your pride. I'm interested in one
that actually moves the campaign forward. Turns out those aren't different things here."

The quarry crews worked alongside the campaign's own for six days, and the district's headman signed
the trade ledger himself, in full view of his own people — not as a man who had accepted help, but as
one who had struck a fair bargain on his own district's terms.
