# The Night Before the Last Coat

*Locked canon, Batch 270, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1408`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle XCIII, wave 31,
closing the wave. Age 314, the eve of the persona's already-locked final mission. Not a territory
Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

No one aboard knew yet that it was the second-to-last night rather than any other. The year Kanja had
chosen to stay for was nearly spent -- the window at the tide-cut road closed and freed, the ledger
of names filed in its own binding, more than eighty individually followed lives added to what Garren
Hask still kept and Sena still tracked apart from it -- and the operation scheduled for the following
night was, on paper, no different from a hundred that had come before it: a holding compound, a
timed approach, a route the successor had already walked twice in scouting.

Efa Gol noticed anyway, the way she noticed most things about him before he said them aloud. She
found him checking the Forge-Coat's forty-two compartments one at a time, slower than two hundred and
eighty years of muscle memory should have required, and didn't ask why. Garren Hask brought the
ledger down from where it usually stayed and set it open on the table without being asked to log
anything yet, as if the blank space at the bottom of the current page were reason enough to sit near
it a while. The successor came last, and did not, for once, bring an operational detail with her --
she came to sit, which she had rarely done in all the years since the raid where Kanja first watched
her work from the rigging and trusted it.

Nobody said the word ending. Nothing about the compound scheduled for the following night suggested
it needed to be said. Kanja closed the last compartment, set the coat across the back of the chair
rather than folding it away for the night, and sat with the three of them a while in the kind of
quiet that two hundred and eighty years of shared work makes room for without needing an occasion.
Whatever the next night would turn out to be, it hadn't announced itself as anything but ordinary, and
none of the four people in that room yet had reason to treat it as more than that.
