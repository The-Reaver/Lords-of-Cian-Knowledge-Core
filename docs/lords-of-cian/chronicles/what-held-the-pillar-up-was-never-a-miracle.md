# What Held the Pillar Up Was Never a Miracle

*Locked canon, Batch 261, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1299`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LXXX, wave 27. The doctrine reveals the pillar's genuinely mundane structural truth, delivered with deliberate care. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The priest gave permission at dawn, standing beside Kanja rather than at the temple's threshold this time, close enough to hear the read himself rather than wait for a secondhand account he'd have no way to verify.

The pillar's signature came back clean under Kanja's palm — not miraculous, not mysterious, just extraordinarily well-cut ancient stonework, load distributed through a joinery technique three generations more sophisticated than anything the settlement's current builders had inherited the knowledge to replicate. The hairline crack, when he traced it fully, ran through a much later repair section rather than the original work at all — a patch added, by the temple's own records, less than a century ago, by masons who hadn't matched the original joinery's precision and whose imperfect seam had finally begun to fail on its own unrelated schedule.

"The original pillar is sound," Kanja said, choosing the words with more care than he usually spent on a technical readout. "More than sound — it's some of the finest structural work I've ever put a hand to, built by people who understood tension in ways your settlement's own current masons don't fully have anymore. The crack isn't the pillar failing. It's a repair, added generations after the original build, finally showing its own age."

"So it was never held up by anything but stone."

"It was held up by stone cut with a skill I don't have a name for beyond calling it exceptional. I don't know who built it, or why they built it this well for a pillar that could have been built adequately instead. That's not a question the doctrine answers." Kanja met the priest's eyes directly. "I can tell you the tension is real and the joinery is real and neither of those facts has anything to say about whether your god chose to be present in the work of whoever built it this carefully. I've spent this whole doctrine's life learning that a structure only ever tells me the truth about itself, never the truth about what it means to the people who built it or the people who still pray beneath it."

The priest was quiet for a long moment, tracing the crack's actual path with his own eyes now that he understood which century it belonged to.

"You could have told me it was a miracle holding," he said finally. "I wouldn't have known the difference."

"I could have. I've never once used this doctrine to tell someone a comfortable lie instead of an honest truth, and I wasn't going to start with something this important to you." Kanja stepped back from the pillar, giving the priest room to approach it on his own terms. "The honest answer is that the people who built this understood something extraordinary about stone. Whether that understanding came from skill alone or from something guiding the skill isn't a question my hand against the rock can ever settle for you."
