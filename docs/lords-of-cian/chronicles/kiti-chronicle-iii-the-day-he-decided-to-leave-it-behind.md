# Kiti Chronicle III: The Day He Decided to Leave It Behind

*Locked canon, Batch 180, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-527`). Homage-era Detroit, Kiti/Owusu. Territory
Chronicle — Owusu as protagonist, Kanja an unnamed background guest with no command, credit, or
resolution authorship. New standalone material. No new named characters.*

---

Decades into the seat, decades past the six-week illness that had once shown him exactly how
completely "The Long Tenure" depended on his own physical presence, Owusu made a choice the illness
had never forced on him: he decided, deliberately and on his own timeline, to leave the seat rather
than hold it until something else eventually made the decision for him.

"You could have another decade in you, easily," his own chief aide said, genuinely startled by the
announcement. "The strength's never wavered since the illness. Nothing's forcing this."

"That's precisely why I'm choosing it now, rather than later." Owusu had thought the decision through
carefully, the same patient deliberation he'd once brought to every obstruction he'd worn down over
the years. "I learned something important during those six weeks I was away — that the strength was
never really mine, only the seat's, borrowed for as long as I occupied it honestly. I've spent years
since knowing that on principle. I'd like to actually act on it once, deliberately, while I still can,
rather than wait for illness or worse to make the choice for me."

"What's the difference? The seat empties either way."

"The difference is everything about how it empties. Six weeks of illness took the strength from me
without my consent, and I felt every bit of what that meant while it happened. This is different. This
is me handing the seat back on my own terms, while I'm still capable of choosing it, rather than
holding on until circumstance decides for me." Owusu set his resignation letter on the desk, final and
unhurried. "I don't know exactly what happens to the strength the moment I walk out that door for the
last time. I know it isn't mine to take with me, and I'd rather set it down myself than have it taken."

He left the seat within the month, the institutional strength lapsing exactly as it always had the
moment he stepped away — permanent this time, chosen rather than imposed, a different ending than the
illness had once rehearsed for him.

"Any regrets?" the aide asked, on his last day.

"None that outweigh the choice itself. I'd rather be the man who set this down deliberately than the
one who had to be pried from it eventually." Owusu looked back at the empty chair once, briefly, before
turning fully away from it. "That's worth more to me than whatever years I might have held onto
instead."

An unnamed man near the building's entrance, present for the quiet departure, was gone before Owusu
reached the street.
