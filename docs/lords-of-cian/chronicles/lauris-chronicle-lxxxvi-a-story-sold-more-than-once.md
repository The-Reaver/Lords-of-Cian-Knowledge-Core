# Lauris Chronicle LXXXVI: A Story Sold More Than Once

*Locked canon, Batch 300, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1703`). Eighty-sixth entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, continuing the second Strand L push. Advances the Tevan Kesk reputational-debt thread
(`MCD-1660`): Lauris resumes the search for the source of the misused "Petite Catastrophe" name and
finds the "itinerant trader" account is not a single loose thread but a story circulating, in
recognizably similar form, along at least three separate trade roads well beyond Kesk's own territory.
Deepens rather than resolves -- the story's ultimate origin, and whether it is being deliberately spread
rather than merely retold, remain open. No new named characters; Tevan Kesk already locked. Part of the
second Lauris 50-Chronicle wave (Chronicles LX-CIX), drafted under Abad's continued blanket
authorization for this run.*

-----

*Archive fragment, present-day: "One man became three roads. I do not yet know if three roads become
one source. I am recording the multiplication because it changes the shape of the debt, even though it
does not yet change its size."*

I have written before of the particular quality Lauris's unease takes on when it attaches to a name
rather than a person — the discomfort of a debt she did not choose, spent in places she cannot see,
against a balance she has no way of fully auditing. This account concerns the season in which that
discomfort acquired real evidence rather than remaining, as it had been, a single confirmed instance
and an unanswered question about where it came from.

She had not intended to pursue the matter further. She told me, plainly, when the season began, that
she considered the Kesk affair closed in every sense that mattered to her — the extortion corrected,
the sums returned, the man himself unlikely to risk repeating it. What reopened the question was not
her own initiative but a piece of unsolicited intelligence, relayed through one of Aerelin's network
couriers on an entirely unrelated matter, mentioning almost in passing that a debt-collector operating
along a trade road four hundred kilometers from Kesk's own territory had, within the same general
season, begun invoking a nearly identical phrase — not "the Petite Catastrophe" by name, but a
description close enough in its particulars (a small woman, an implied unstoppable violence, a warning
rather than a boast) that Lauris recognized the shape of it before she had finished reading the report.

*

She went looking, deliberately this time rather than by accident, and found a third instance within the
month — a moneylender's enforcer working a route along the Kesmara Eastern Hills' northern extension,
using language close enough to Kesk's own original phrasing that she began to suspect, for the first
time, that she was not looking at three men who had each separately heard a rumor, but at one story
being told, and retold, from some common point of origin she had not yet located.

"Kesk told me an itinerant trader gave him the phrase, years ago, as a warning rather than an
instruction," she told me, reviewing the pattern with me directly for the first time since the original
account closed. "I accepted that as a frightened man's honest first answer, because pursuing it further
in that moment would have produced a confident lie rather than a true one. I did not, at the time,
consider that the trader in question might have told the same story to more than one listener, in more
than one place, across more than one season. I am considering it now."

*

What she has not been able to determine — and what she told me directly she does not currently have the
means to determine without a search considerably larger than the one that found the pattern in the
first place — is whether the story's spread is accidental, the ordinary drift of a good warning-tale
along trade routes that share travelers and taverns in common, or whether someone is deliberately
seeding it, road by road, for reasons that have nothing to do with collecting on unpaid debts. She has
asked Vael Korr-Drennen, whose own registry search she has come to trust more than most collaborations
she has ever permitted herself, whether any Directorate-adjacent record might account for the phrase's
spread through channels neither of them have yet traced. The historian has agreed to look, though both
women were honest with each other that a rumor's travel is a considerably harder thing to trace through
old paperwork than a filed sample or a transfer record.

Her closing note, recorded the evening the third instance was confirmed: *Three roads, one phrase, one
unnamed trader neither confirmed nor disproven as a single point of origin. I corrected one man's theft.
I do not yet know how many men there are to correct, or whether correcting each one individually will
ever actually close this account.*

I do not know, setting this down, whether Lauris will find the source of the story before it reaches a
fourth road, or a fifth, or whether some part of her own legend has simply passed, by now, beyond any
single point she could still locate and silence. I know only that what she once treated as one
afternoon's tidy correction has begun to look, on closer examination, considerably less like an isolated
incident and considerably more like a debt with a life of its own.
