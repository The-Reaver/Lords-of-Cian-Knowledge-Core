# Lauris Chronicle LXV: The Last Ordinary Afternoon

*Locked canon, Batch 300, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1682`). Sixty-fifth entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, the eighteenth and closing entry of Strand K for this wave, drafted as part of a
second 50-entry wave. Set at age ~1,180, roughly twenty years before Velith's death in a Vask
Threnarr defensive operation (age ~1,200, already locked at `MCD-165`/`1631`), this entry
deliberately closes the ~1,100-year gap opened at Chronicle LX by ending at the exact threshold of
Chronicle XIV without depicting the engagement itself, matching the wave-one precedent of Chronicle
XXI closing at a threshold rather than a resolution. No new named characters. Full text below.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Vask Threnarr, age approximately one thousand one hundred eighty: "Velith
corrected my footwork today. I let her. She has been right about it for longer than she has been
willing to say so directly."*

I have set down, across this run of entries, the long middle distance of a life most accounts of
Lauris Letitia skip entirely -- the century that stopped being an emergency, the stance that kept
being rebuilt, the strike that had not yet earned a name. I close this stretch of her record with the
entry I have found hardest to place correctly among the others, not because it records anything
unusual, but because I already know, from an account I wrote some time ago and which the reader of
this one has likely already encountered, what waits roughly twenty years past its final line. I have
resisted, in transcribing it, the temptation to write it as an ending. It was not one, to either woman
in the room. I have tried to give it back the ordinariness it was entitled to at the time.

Lauris had, by this visit, kept the Threnarr rotation for nine hundred years -- a fixed handful of
weeks, roughly once a decade, the same arrangement Selene had first negotiated on her behalf at
fourteen, held to with a consistency I have not found matched anywhere else in her Kares Prime record.
Velith, by this same visit, had spent nearly a millennium in Threnarr's defensive complement, long
enough that the young woman who had once asked to have her stance corrected honestly had become, by
Iron-Speaker Vann's own later account, one of the complement's steadiest hands -- trusted to hold a
position under pressure without needing to be told twice what the position required of her.

*

The afternoon this entry records held nothing that either woman's own archive marks as significant.
They walked the outer terrace together, by both accounts, discussing a dispute between two younger
defenders that Velith had been asked to mediate and that Lauris, reflexively, helped her think through
using the same careful-witness method Veska had once borrowed for a boundary record eight hundred
years earlier. Velith, partway through the walk, stopped Lauris and corrected her footwork on an
uneven section of terrace stone -- a small adjustment, weight carried a fraction too far forward on
the descent, the kind of correction Lauris's own instructors at Karth-Ven had long since stopped
offering her, because by this age there was little left for them to correct that she had not already
noticed herself first.

I have Lauris's own entry for the moment, and it is, I think, the clearest evidence in this entire
stretch of her record of what the friendship had actually become by its final years, stripped of
everything except plain trust: *Velith corrected my footwork today. I let her. She has been right
about it for longer than she has been willing to say so directly. I told her this. She told me she
had been waiting for me to notice on my own, and that she was relieved I finally had, because she had
grown tired of being tactful about a habit I clearly did not mind having named.*

*

Velith's own entry for the day, the last of hers that survives concerning this particular visit,
closes on a line I have chosen to end this account with, and I want to be honest that I do not know,
reading it now, whether she wrote it as anything more than an ordinary close to an ordinary afternoon.
I have chosen to let it stand exactly as she left it, without weighting it further than she weighted
it herself: *We walked the terrace and argued gently about footwork and a dispute that was not ours to
settle, and neither of us said anything that needed remembering more than anything else we have said
to each other across the last thousand years. I have decided this is what an ordinary friendship is
for -- not every conversation needs to matter more than the last one. I intend to have a great many
more exactly like it.*

She did not know, writing that, how few remained. Neither, I believe, did Lauris. I have chosen to
close this account here, at an uneven section of terrace stone and a corrected footstep, rather than
carry the reader any further -- the entry that follows this one in her archive is the one I have
already transcribed, and I do not think it needs a second telling.
