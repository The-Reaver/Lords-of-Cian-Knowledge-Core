# The Window That Ended the Smuggling

*Locked canon, Batch 285, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1510`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XCIX, wave 33, closing the wave. The smuggling faction that raided the school (`MCD-1053`) and exploited its one blind spot in total windless fog (`MCD-1338`) is finally cornered, using the overlap-window method codified at `MCD-1418` -- the sub-series' first entry to close a long-dangling recurring antagonist thread, and a detailed Long Mask-era gear combat showcase. Corrected Batch 321, 2026-10-02: an earlier draft staged this as a full-Trinity showcase, despite this Chronicle's placement well within the Long Mask era of just over 283 years, after Kanja's already-locked age-30 surrender of the Trinity to its sealed vault (`MCD-246`); replaced with his correct Long-Mask-era kit (`ARS-344` through `ARS-356`, plus the Rexmar Machete), and an inline rule-ID citation is removed from the narrative prose.*

They had never been fully driven out, only pushed further into the coast's blind spots -- the windless-fog corridor that had once let them slip a shipment past the school entirely, the school's own early institutional years before it had learned to defend itself. For seasons since, the faction had survived by working exactly the gaps every credited tradition still openly admitted to. It took three formally blended traditions agreeing on the same narrow window, for the first time since the strait crossing months before, to finally close the last one.

The apprentice called it: an eleven-hour overlap where Sephtis's storm-timing lineage, the rival fleet's independent method, and the northern pilot's ice-reading chapter -- watching three entirely different signs -- converged on the same stretch of the Reef-Chain's narrower channels being genuinely, verifiably clear of the fog the smugglers had always sailed by. No blind spot left to hide the run in. They came anyway, committed to a shipment too large to abandon, and sailed straight into water three traditions had already agreed no one could hide in.

Kanja met the lead vessel first, the Sovereign Eyes active before the smugglers had finished registering he was there at all -- cutting cleanly through the decoy hulls' false markings and dummy cargo silhouettes rigged to draw fire toward empty holds, exposing the one ship among four actually running the real contraband. The Forge-Coat and Ironfall Boots took the boarding fight that followed on the real hull's deck, redirecting a boat-hook's swing back through the man who'd thrown it and a second attacker's own momentum into the rail behind him, while the Ironhand Gauntlets held a tight guard across the companionway to deny anyone below decks a clean run at the cargo they were trying to dump overboard rather than surrender.

It ended with the faction's remaining leadership disarmed on their own deck and the shipment intact for a magistrate's inventory, rather than at the bottom of the channel. Kanja didn't hand them straight to a cell. He let the student make the offer instead, the same offer she'd have made to any rival tradition that had finally run out of gaps to hide in: the school's own growing reach meant legitimate coastal trade routes now existed that hadn't a decade ago, routes that needed captains who already knew every channel on this coast better than anyone certified to sail them. Half the faction's crews took it. The other half didn't, and were handed to the magistrate exactly as caught.

"No blind spot left," the apprentice said afterward, watching the real cargo hull towed in behind the decoys. "First time in this coast's whole history nobody's had one."
