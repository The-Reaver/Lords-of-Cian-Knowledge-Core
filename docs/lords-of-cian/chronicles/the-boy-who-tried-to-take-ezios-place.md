The boy had been watching Ezio correct entries for three straight days before he finally said
anything, hovering near the tally table with the particular persistence of someone who'd decided
something and was working up to announcing it.

"Teach me to do what you do," he said, on the fourth morning. "Not write the testimony down. The
other part. Knowing which numbers are too small before anyone tells you they are."

Ezio's first instinct, unexamined and a little embarrassing to notice in himself once he had, was
reluctance — not doubt in the boy's honesty, but something closer to possessiveness, a sense that the
corrective method was his, earned across a year of districts, and that teaching it to someone else
meant giving away the one thing that made his role in the crew more than just a fast hand with a pen.

He didn't say any of that. He said, instead, that he'd think about it, and spent that evening turning
over why the reluctance had come so easily and whether it deserved to win.

"You're worried he'll be wrong and it'll cost someone," Kanja said, when Ezio finally admitted the
hesitation aloud. "Or you're worried he'll be right, and it won't need to be only you anymore."

"Maybe both."

"The second one's not a reason to say no. The first one is worth taking seriously." Kanja didn't tell
him what to decide. "Test him properly before you teach him anything. If he can't be trusted with the
judgment, that's the actual reason to hold back — not what it costs you to share it."

Ezio spent the next week testing rather than teaching — handing the boy completed testimony pages
and asking him to find the entries that read too modest against the district's own production
records, watching whether his instincts landed on the real discrepancies or only the ones that were
easiest to spot. He was wrong more than he was right at first, flagging entries that checked out fine
and missing two that genuinely needed correction, and Ezio told him so plainly each time rather than
softening it.

By the second week, the boy's instincts had sharpened enough that Ezio handed him a full afternoon's
worth of fresh testimony unsupervised, checked his corrections against his own afterward, and found
them accurate to a standard he hadn't expected this soon.

"You're not just a fast hand anymore either, once someone else can do the other part," Ezio told him,
finally, once the training had gone as far as one district's time allowed. "That used to worry me
more than it should have."
