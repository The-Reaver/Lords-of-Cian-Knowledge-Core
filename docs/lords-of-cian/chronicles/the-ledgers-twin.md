# The Ledger's Twin

*Locked canon, Batch 258, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1212`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
LXXIV, wave 25. Garren Hask meets a Trust auditor keeping his own private, honest account. Not a
territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Garren Hask heard about the other ledger before he ever saw it — rumor from a captured Trust supply
clerk that somewhere in the Directorate's own bureaucracy, a career auditor had spent three years
keeping a private, meticulous account of every ghost-fleet encounter on record, cross-referenced
against official reports specifically to catch where the official version diverged from what had
actually happened.

It struck Hask as either the most dangerous thing he'd heard in years or the closest thing to a
kindred spirit the Trust's own ranks had ever produced, and he wasn't sure which until Kanja arranged,
through a channel neither side could later fully explain, for the two record-keepers to meet.

The auditor came alone, unarmed, and older than Hask had pictured — a man who'd spent a career
watching institutional reports get quietly rewritten to protect careers and morale, and had started
his private ledger not out of loyalty to the ghost fleet but out of a stubborn refusal to let the
truth get overwritten on his own watch, regardless of which side it favored. "Your fleet disables more
than it sinks," the auditor said, laying his own book open on the same table as Hask's. "My official
reports say the opposite in eleven of nineteen cases I've cross-checked. I wanted to know which one of
us was wrong before I decided what to do about it."

They spent the better part of a night comparing entries line by line, two men who cared more about an
honest account than about which flag it served, and found their books agreed on every fact that
mattered even when the official Trust record didn't. The auditor didn't defect, didn't offer
intelligence, didn't ask for anything — he simply confirmed what he'd suspected and returned to his
post the next morning, his own private ledger a little heavier with corroboration he had no intention
of ever officially filing.

"He'll never publish it," Hask told Kanja afterward. "Not while he still answers to people who'd bury
it. But he'll keep writing it true, same as I do."

"That's enough," Kanja said. "The truth doesn't need to be published to matter. It just needs someone
willing to keep it honest."
