# The Overseer Who Thanked Him for the Finding

*Locked canon, Batch 256, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1165`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle LXXXI, wave 27, closing the wave. A rare register: an administrator genuinely relieved to be forced into a fix he'd wanted but could never justify to his own superiors without external proof.*

---

He didn't stall. He didn't deny a figure, didn't produce a second set of books, didn't offer Kanja the coin the last resistant administrator had. He met Ezio's testimony-taking with something closer to gratitude than either of them had ever seen from a man about to be found responsible for a real shortfall, and by the second day it was clear why: he'd wanted to fix the wage structure himself for three years and had never once been able to get his own board of owners to approve the expense without proof harder than his own word.

"I've told them twice," he said, watching Ezio's cross-referencing take shape. "They didn't believe the numbers when I brought them. Bring them the same numbers and they'll believe you, because you don't work for me and you don't stand to gain anything by lying about it."

It made for an unfamiliar rhythm. Ezio was used to administrators who fought every figure, who forced him to prove each line twice against resistance. This one handed over records unprompted, flagged discrepancies Ezio hadn't found yet, and asked pointed questions about how to phrase the finding so it would carry the most weight with a board that trusted outside audits over internal advocacy.

Kanja was careful not to let the administrator's cooperation soften the finding itself — a real shortfall was still a real shortfall, three years old, and the workers owed it were owed exactly what the arithmetic said regardless of how welcome the news was to the man who'd have to authorize paying it. But the tone of the closing conversation was one neither of them had held before.

"I've spent three years knowing what was owed and not being able to make anyone above me care," the administrator said, once the finding was final. "You made them care in four days."

"We didn't make anyone care. We made the numbers impossible to argue with. Whether your board cares is their own business." Kanja's caution held anyway — an administrator this eager to be found guilty was, he told Ezio privately afterward, worth double-checking rather than simply trusted, in case gratitude was itself a performance meant to earn a softer finding. It wasn't. The figures held up against every cross-reference exactly as given, and the board, faced with an outside audit they couldn't dismiss as one man's advocacy, approved the full back-payment inside a week — faster than any case the crew had ever closed, resolved almost entirely by an administrator who'd wanted the ledger's authority more than he'd ever wanted to escape its finding.

"It's strange," Ezio admitted, packing up early for once. "Being thanked for catching someone."

"Get used to being surprised by it. Most people aren't enemies of the truth. Some of them are just waiting for someone with no stake in the lie to finally say it out loud."
