# The Boy He Once Cut Free

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Scourge Alias Chronicle XLVIII, wave 16, closing the wave. Long Mask era, age 122, V3 gear (minimal use). Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The manifest his crew intercepted named a trader running a small coastal operation out of a cove two
days south, nothing large enough to warrant the full crescent formation — a routine matter, until he
went ashore alone to confirm the numbers and recognized the trader's face.

He'd been a boy of perhaps nine the first time, one of forty-three captives freed off a grain barge
decades before, too young then to be more than a name on Garren Hask's tally. Now he was a grown man
running the very trade that had once been done to him, four captives chained below his own deck.

"You don't remember me," the trader said, not yet understanding who stood in his doorway, "but I know
what you are. You're going to take my cargo and leave me the ruin of it, same as always."

"I remember the barge," Kanja said. "I remember your face at nine years old, looking at the open hold
door like you didn't believe it was real." He didn't reach for the coat's clasps. "I need you to
explain the rest of it to me. Not the trade — the distance between that boy and this."

The trader's composure didn't survive the question the way his bravado had. "You think freedom feeds
a man. It doesn't. I had nothing, coming off that barge — no trade, no family left, nothing but a
story people wanted to hear and no use for me once they'd heard it. This was the only work anyone
taught me that paid. I told myself I'd never take a child. I've kept that much, at least."

"That's the line you drew."

"It's the only one I had left to draw."

He freed the four captives without a fight, the trader offering none, and stood with the man a long
while afterward rather than simply handing him to the nearest port authority the way the crew's own
doctrine would ordinarily call for.

"I'm not going to pretend the four people you chained matter less than the harm that made you,"
he said finally. "They don't. What happened to you explains this. It doesn't undo what it cost
them." He held the man's eyes. "I'm taking you in. Not to a cell — to Garren Hask, and whatever work
the ledger can find for a man who still knows what a hold door looks like from both sides. That's the
only mercy I have to offer that isn't also a lie about what you did."

The trader didn't ask for more than that, and didn't get it.
