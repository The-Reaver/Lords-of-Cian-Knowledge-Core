# Six Buildings, One Set of Hands

*Locked canon, Batch 261, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1296`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LXXVII, wave 26. A detailed full-Trinity rescue showcase racing aftershocks across six failing structures. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The aftershock came four hours into the six careful reads, and it came without warning the way aftershocks always did — no diagnostic signature to catch ahead of time, no structure Kanja could have listened to that would have told him the ground itself was about to move again. All six of the district's genuinely uncertain buildings shifted at once, and three of them had people still inside, trapped by rescue crews who'd been working the careful evacuation exactly as the day's slower protocol demanded.

There was no time left for careful anymore. There was only six buildings, failing on six different timetables, and one man who could hear which timetable was shortest.

He read the nearest first — a leaning tenement with four residents pinned behind a collapsed stairwell — and found its true failure point not in the visible crack but in a support beam two floors up, already past the threshold where a wrong discharge would finish what the aftershock had started. Obsidian Malice's discharge went not into the beam directly but into the debris pinning the stairwell, three controlled pulses shearing exactly enough rubble to open an exit without disturbing the beam's own precarious hold one degree further.

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System grounded the building's own continuing settlement tremor through Kanja's frame as he moved inside it, redirecting each fresh shift into the ground beneath his own feet rather than letting it compound against the already-weakened frame — buying the rescue crew behind him seconds they didn't know they were being given. Onyx's Cadence Ruin cleared a collapsing ceiling section from the evacuation path with the blade's own economy of motion, no wasted strike, every cut placed to redirect falling weight rather than simply stop it.

Two buildings later, the pattern repeated with a bakery whose owner had refused to leave her ovens — Veil Piercer opening a seam in a jammed service door that manual effort alone hadn't managed to force, buying the final minute needed before that structure's own slower collapse finally came. The third and most urgent — a rooming house with six residents on its upper floor and a failure timeline measured in single minutes — took every piece of the Trinity working at once, Obsidian Malice, Mafesto, and Onyx's full sequence in a compressed span shorter than any prior full-Trinity engagement in the doctrine's run.

All six buildings' occupants came out alive. Two of the six structures themselves didn't survive the hour, condemned outright once the people inside them were safe — a trade Kanja made without hesitation each time it came up.

"You didn't try to save all six buildings," the second student said afterward, watching the sun set over a district that had lost fewer homes than it might have and no lives at all.

"The buildings were never the actual math today," Kanja said. "I built this whole doctrine originally to bring things down. Today it was mostly about deciding, six separate times in under two hours, exactly how much of a failing structure I could afford to leave standing long enough for someone still inside it to get out first."
