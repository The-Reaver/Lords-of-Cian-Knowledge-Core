# What the Scholar Finally Published

*Locked canon, Batch 188, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-551`). The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XV, closing the
fifth wave. Rebellion era, age 25, several years after the events of "The Scholar Who Measured the
Impossible" (`MCD-421`). Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters beyond the already-locked Trust scholar.*

---

The Trust scholar whose honest report on the resonance phenomenon had once been quietly shelved by his
own institution finally saw it published — not through official channels, which had never reopened
the question, but through a smaller, independent academy willing to print research the Trust itself
preferred left buried.

He sought Kanja out personally to share the news, the first time either of them had spoken since the
original shelving.

"It took years, and it's a smaller platform than I'd once hoped for," the scholar admitted, though
something in him carried real satisfaction despite the modest venue. "But it's published. Properly
peer-reviewed, properly cited, no longer just a report gathering dust in an archive nobody was ever
going to read."

"That matters more than the size of the platform, I'd think." Kanja reviewed the published work with
genuine interest, years of careful documentation finally seeing daylight. "What made the difference,
after all this time?"

"Enough independent field accounts accumulated that the phenomenon stopped being deniable, even by an
institution invested in denying it. Yours wasn't the only name attached to accounts like this one by
the time I found a publisher willing to print it. I was just the first person patient enough to keep
documenting it properly the whole time nobody wanted to hear it." The scholar set the published work
aside. "I don't know that this changes anything about how the Trust treats the doctrine officially.
I know it means the honest research now exists somewhere a future scholar can actually find it,
whenever they're finally ready to take it seriously."

"That's not nothing. I've built this whole doctrine on the idea that an honest account outlasts
whoever tries to bury it. I'm glad to see that proven true for your own work, not just mine." Kanja
extended genuine respect for years of patience that had cost the scholar his standing within his own
institution to maintain. "Whatever comes of this publication, you kept the honest record alive long
enough for it to matter. That's the actual victory here, whatever the Trust decides to do about it."
