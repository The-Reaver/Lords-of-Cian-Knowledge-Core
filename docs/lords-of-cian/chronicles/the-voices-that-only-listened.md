# The Voices That Only Listened

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Crow King Alias Chronicle XXVII, wave 9, closing
entry. Rebellion era, roughly two and a half years after the Night of the Crow King (`MCD-236`, age
23). Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Every use of the Hymn-Engine anyone in the crew had ever discussed involved putting a false signal
into the world — a lie sung, seeded, or thrown across water or stone until someone believed something
untrue. It had never occurred to most of them that the same trained listening could be turned the
other direction, toward information nobody had bothered to hide at all.

The garrison at Duren Watch spoke openly, within its own walls, about supply timetables, patrol
gaps, and a coming reinforcement convoy — confident that ordinary conversation, carried on open air
between men who trusted their own perimeter, needed no encryption at all. It didn't occur to anyone
inside those walls that trained ears, positioned correctly outside them, could reconstruct entire
schedules from nothing but the ordinary rhythm and content of routine talk drifting across open
ground on a still night.

No chant was sung. No false signal seeded. Kanja's people simply listened, for four consecutive
nights, cataloguing what an unguarded garrison said to itself without ever once lying to it.

"We didn't deceive them at all," the crew's own intelligence officer noted, reviewing the full
timetable the listening had produced, more complete than any forged document could have safely
claimed to be. "We just let them tell us everything, the same way we'd trick a sensor grid into
telling us something false. It's the same discipline pointed at true information instead of
manufactured information."

"That's most of what the craft actually is, underneath the parts people find impressive," Kanja
said, reviewing the schedule himself. "Everyone assumes the Hymn-Engine is about lying well. Lying
well is the smaller half. The larger half was always listening well enough, first, to know exactly
what lie would actually work — and it turns out that same listening, aimed at people who don't
realize they're being listened to, doesn't need a lie attached to it at all to be worth just as
much."

The reinforcement convoy's actual timing let the crew avoid it entirely rather than deceive around
it — an operation that produced everything a false signal usually bought, without a single voice ever
singing a note.
