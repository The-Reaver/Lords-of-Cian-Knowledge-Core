# The Fight He Fought Angry

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Scourge Alias Chronicle, wave 15. Long Mask era,
age 168, V3 gear (Golden Terror era). Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose. No new named characters.*

---

Word reached him three days after the fact: a holding barge his own crew had marked for liberation
the following week, raided early and emptied not by rescue but by its own operators, who had drowned
forty-one captives rather than let them be freed as evidence against a trade already under pressure
from every direction the Scourge's years of work had pushed it.

He boarded the slaving consortium's flagship two nights later with none of the controlled, unhurried
precision that had defined every engagement the crew's records had ever praised him for.

The Forge-Coat's grounding weave absorbed a boarding pike's strike and he didn't merely disarm the
man who'd thrown it — he drove the disarmed guard bodily into the two behind him, a use of the coat's
mass and momentum that had nothing tactical about it, everything personal, and the successor watching
from the boarding line noted it without comment, understanding this wasn't a night for correction.

Obsidian Malice discharged a full two-year charge into a bulkhead that hadn't needed nearly that much
force to breach, the excess venting as a concussion that dropped three uninvolved crew members who'd
been nowhere near the fight, an overcorrection he registered only after the fact, with a flicker of
something that looked, on his own face, uncomfortably close to shame.

The consortium's senior partner, cornered in the flagship's own hold, made the mistake of speaking
before assessing the room correctly. "They were evidence. We did what any reasonable—"

The Ironhand Gauntlets closed the distance faster than the sentence finished, and it was the
successor's own voice, sharp and unhesitating, that stopped what came next. "That's enough."

He stopped. Stood there a long moment, breathing hard, the flagship's hold silent around him, and set
the man down disarmed rather than finished, the anger not gone but, with visible effort, no longer
steering.

"That wasn't like you," the successor said afterward, quiet, not accusing.

"No." He didn't defend it. "Forty-one people who were three days from being free instead drowned by
men protecting a ledger entry. I let that be the thing doing the fighting tonight instead of me, and
it almost cost someone who didn't earn what it would have cost them." He looked back at the hold,
still working through it honestly rather than excusing it. "I don't get to only be controlled when
controlled is easy. Tonight it wasn't, and I came closer to the line than I should have. That's worth
remembering, not burying."
