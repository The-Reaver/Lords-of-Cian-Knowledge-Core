# What the Canyon Carried Sound Of

*Locked canon, Batch 262, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1326`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LXXX, wave
27. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241), an arid canyon defile en route
to an inland settlement. A detailed, battle-intense Trinity combat showcase in the alias's first
desert/canyon environmental register. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose.*

---

The canyon was the only road the ore convoy could take to reach the inland settlement before its
water ran out, a two-day defile of bare rock and standing heat where the campaign's usual advantages
— cover, moving water, open sightlines — simply didn't exist. The Directorate had picked the ground
for exactly that reason, a small, dug-in force that didn't need to win a fight so much as delay one
long enough for the heat itself to finish the work.

"There's nowhere to hide in this," Kanja said, feeling the dry, unbroken quiet of the place settle
over him differently than any forest or coastline the campaign had crossed. "Which means there's
nowhere for them to hide from what the canyon does to sound, either."

He turned the terrain's own emptiness against the ambush waiting in it. Onyx of Oblivion's Cadence
Ruin, which had read frozen ground and flooded stone and a full sea storm across prior entries,
found something new in bare rock — a canyon wall carrying the faintest scrape of a repositioning
boot for a hundred feet in either direction, amplified rather than muffled by the same dead, dry
air that gave the ambush its cover from sight. Every dug-in position gave itself away the moment
anyone in it moved at all.

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System, built for grounding force into wet earth or solid stonework,
found the parched canyon floor a harder conductor to read — heat radiating up off the rock as
readily as any impact traveled down through it, forcing Kanja to route force through his own frame
in short, controlled bursts rather than the sustained grounding cold or wet terrain allowed. Obsidian
Malice answered the dug-in positions themselves, a series of precisely placed pulses collapsing
cover from the flanks rather than the front, driving the ambush into open ground where a fight
this one-sided couldn't be sustained.

It was over inside twenty minutes, the convoy's water reserves never touched, the ambush broken
before its own dug-in patience could outlast the heat the way it had been built to. The convoy
master, watching from behind the last wagon, hadn't understood a word of what had actually happened
in the fight until Kanja explained it plainly, walking the canyon wall and pointing out the specific
places sound had betrayed a position no eye could have found first.

"I've crossed this pass a dozen times," the convoy master said. "I never once thought about what
the rock itself was telling anyone willing to listen."

"Most people don't," Kanja said. "Ground always has something to say. You just have to be quiet
enough, and desperate enough, to actually go looking for it."
