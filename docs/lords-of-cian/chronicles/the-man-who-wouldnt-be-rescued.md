# The Man Who Wouldn't Be Rescued

*Locked canon, Batch 257, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1194`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle LXXXIII, wave 28. A despair-focused rescue entry -- a trapped worker in shock initially refuses extraction, believing himself already lost. The alias's first crisis of a victim's own will rather than the hazard itself.*

---

He wasn't pinned by anything Kanja couldn't have moved in minutes. That was the part that took longest to understand, working toward the voice calling back weaker with each exchange -- the man trapped past a partial collapse had enough clearance to breathe, enough space to wait, and had decided somewhere in the dark that waiting for a rescue that wouldn't come was worse than accepting it wasn't coming at all.

"Don't waste the time," the voice said, when Kanja finally got close enough to be heard clearly. "I heard the section come down behind me. I know what that means."

"It means you're trapped," Kanja said, working steadily at the debris between them. "It doesn't mean what you've decided it means."

The man wasn't wrong about the danger, only about its size, and no amount of calling that correction through a few feet of rubble seemed to be reaching him. He'd gone quiet twice already, breathing shallow in a way that had nothing to do with the air supply and everything to do with a mind that had already started grieving itself. Every prior crisis this alias had answered was a fight against stone, water, fire, gas -- obstacles with no opinion about whether they were beaten. This one had an opinion, and the opinion was actively working against its own rescue.

Kanja kept talking through the clearing work, not reassurance exactly, just steady, factual narration of what he was doing and why it was working -- the same voice he used walking a nervous apprentice through a first real repair, applied here because it was the only tool that mattered as much as his hands did.

"I've got clearance," he said, finally breaking through enough to see a hand reaching weakly back. "You have to want to take it, though. I can clear the rock. I can't make you decide you're worth pulling out."

The hand hesitated a long moment before it closed around his.

They came out together into a crowd that had already started the process of not expecting a survivor, the man blinking against light he'd stopped believing he'd see again, saying nothing for a long while after, and then only one thing, once he could speak steadily.

"I'd stopped fighting for it," he said. "You didn't just dig. You made me want it back before you finished."
