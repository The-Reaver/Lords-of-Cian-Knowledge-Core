# What the Anvil Needed From Itself

*Locked canon, Batch 262, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1334`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LXXXVIII,
first entry in the thirtieth wave. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241).
The Anvil's own major structural refit after sustained years of service, the alias's first
base-maintenance entry. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The hull survey took two full days, and the shipwright the campaign had brought aboard for exactly
this purpose didn't sugarcoat what it found — years of converted ore-barge service, forge heat
working the deck timbers from above and open water working the hull from below, seams that had
held through raids, storms, and a battle at sea (MCD-1314) starting to show the ordinary,
unglamorous wear that no amount of Trinity strength could substitute for actual maintenance.

"Nothing here is an emergency yet," she told Kanja, laying the survey out across the terrace table.
"That's exactly why it's the right time to fix it. Wait for it to become an emergency and we're
doing this refit in the middle of a crisis instead of ahead of one."

It meant three weeks at anchor in a settlement with the right timber and the right forge capacity
to do the work properly — the longest deliberate stop the tour had taken anywhere that wasn't
already an emergency, and Kanja felt the itch of it the whole first week, the sense of a campaign
that measured itself in settlements reached sitting still by choice rather than necessity.

"You've never once stopped to maintain the thing that makes every other stop possible," the
shipwright said, catching the restlessness in him without being told about it. "You'll fix a
district's causeway before it fails. You've never once fixed your own ship before it failed the
same way."

He worked the refit himself where his own hands were useful — reinforcing deck mounts that had
taken the worst of the forge heat, restoring hull seams the sea storm had strained past their
original tolerance, apprentices learning shipwright's craft alongside blacksmith's craft for the
first time as part of the same open teaching the terrace had always offered. It wasn't glamorous
work, and it produced nothing anyone outside the crew would ever see or thank them for.

"Three weeks nobody will remember," he said, near the end of it, testing a reinforced deck mount
against a full smith's weight.

"Three weeks that mean the next eighteen months don't end in a hull failure nobody saw coming," the
shipwright said. "That's not nothing. That's just the kind of work that doesn't get a ballad
written about it."

The Anvil sailed out of anchor stronger than it had been at the tour's own start, and nothing about
that strength would ever be visible to a settlement it later saved, except in the simple,
unremarkable fact that it kept sailing.
