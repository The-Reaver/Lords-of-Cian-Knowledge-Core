# The Names the Ledger Kept Track Of

*Locked canon, Batch 270, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1407`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle XCII, wave 31. Age
313, V4 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Garren Hask's ledger had always counted -- freed, dead, missing, returned -- a number at the end of
every entry across two hundred and seventy years of keeping it. What it had never done, until the
week Sena asked him to, was follow a name past the count it belonged to and find out what became of
the person attached to it.

Sena had her own list, kept separately and shown to no one until now: forty-one people she'd
personally carried out of forty-one different holds since the night in a market square when she'd
nearly killed an already-surrendered man out of grief that wasn't his to answer for. She'd tracked
what she could of most of them over the decades -- a handful dead of ordinary age, most simply lost
to distance and time, and a smaller number she'd stayed close enough to actually know: a boy grown
into a harbor pilot, two sisters running a trading post neither of them could have afforded before, a
man who still couldn't sleep with a door closed and likely never would.

She brought the list to Hask not to fold it into his own -- his counted operations, hers counted
people, and she didn't want the two conflated -- but because she wanted someone besides herself to
know it existed before the year ran out on both of them. Kanja read it with them longer than either
expected him to, stopping twice on names he recognized from entries of his own, decades old. One had
died the winter after his own rescue. One, he learned for the first time, was still alive four hundred
miles east, running a school for children who'd never been anywhere near a slaver's hold and, because
of people like her, never would be.

Hask didn't copy the list into his own. He filed it separately, in its own binding, and told Sena it
would still be there when she wanted it back. Kanja said nothing about what the number at the bottom
of every ledger the crew had ever kept might have looked like, read that way instead -- not an
operation closed, but a life still being lived somewhere past the last line anyone had thought to
write about it.
