# The Three-Cornered Fight

*Locked canon, Batch 235, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1041`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LVIII, wave 20. A
detailed V2-gear combat showcase against two hostile parties who refuse to cooperate with each
other. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The slaving depot at the border strait had been marked for three weeks before the raid went in, and
nothing about the approach suggested it would be anything but the usual work — disable the guard
watch, breach the hold, get people onto the boats before the alarm bell finished its second ring.
What the approach hadn't shown was the Sovereign Trust cutter already standing off the far side of
the depot, running dark, waiting for its own reasons to move.

The slavers saw it first and made the worse choice twice in a row: they tried to use the Scourge's
own boarding as cover to run their most valuable cargo out the seaward door straight into the
cutter's boats, gambling that a confiscation was survivable in a way a liberation wasn't. The cutter's
crew, for their part, had no interest in freeing anyone — Trust salvage law let them claim
"recovered persons" as seized property pending adjudication, a legal fiction Kanja had spent decades
making sure never touched anyone he reached first.

That put three parties on one deck with three different endings in mind, and none of them talking to
each other.

He went in low and fast through the smoke he'd already vented — Concealment mode, ten meters of
obscuring cloud dropped the instant the alarm rang, so that for the first ninety seconds nobody
aboard, slaver or Trust marine, could say with certainty who else was even in the fight with them.
The Ironhand Gauntlets took a cutlass meant for a chained man's throat and returned it hard enough to
put the slaver holding it into the deck planking. The Ironfall Boots' impact soles cleared a
three-man knot of Trust marines off their feet without breaking a bone that would keep any of them
from walking again — that distinction mattered, and he held it under pressure the way he'd trained
himself to for thirty years. Mafesto moved between the two fronts almost faster than either side
could track, its Kinetic Transfer System redirecting a boarding pike's own momentum into the man who'd
thrown it, never once striking with force he hadn't first absorbed from someone else.

Obsidian Malice he used exactly once, on the Trust cutter's rigging rather than its crew — a short,
precise discharge that dropped their mainsail into a ruined tangle and cost them the maneuvering speed
to give chase, without a single Trust marine bleeding for it. That was the whole of the calculation:
free the hold, deny the slavers their cargo, deny the Trust its claim, and leave before either side
could put a name or a face to what had happened to them. Efa Gol's decoys drew the cutter's attention
seaward at the exact moment the last of the freed captives went over the depot's landward wall, and by
the time the smoke cleared enough for anyone to see clearly, there was nothing left to see.

The Trust filed the incident as an unidentified pirate action against a licensed salvage operation.
The slavers, what was left of them, never filed anything at all.
