# The Wall He Chose Not to Bleed For

*Locked canon, Batch 246, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1075`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LXII, wave 21. A
genuine-failure entry: the sub-series' first full tactical retreat, zero captives freed that night.
Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The rock island had been a fortress before it was a slaving depot, and whoever had converted it
hadn't bothered improving what already worked. Sheer sea walls rose straight from deep water with no
beach to land a boat on unseen. Iron obstruction chains, strung between anchor points below the
tideline, caught anything that tried to approach low and quiet. Three watch towers held clear lines
on every approach at once, manned in rotating shifts that never left a gap longer than four minutes.

He'd studied worse ground and taken it. This was the first time studying it told him something
different: that taking it that night would cost more than it saved.

The probing assault went in anyway, because the only way to be certain was to test it, and the first
ninety seconds confirmed everything the reconnaissance had suggested. The obstruction chains fouled
the approach exactly as designed, costing precious time in open water under the towers' sightlines.
Mafesto absorbed and redirected what it could, but the towers had the numbers to keep firing faster
than any one man's Kinetic Transfer could spend the force back at them, and for the first time in
years he felt the specific, unfamiliar sensation of a fight where his own effort was the limiting
factor rather than the enemy's.

He called it off at the ninety-second mark, before it became a fight worth losing people over. The
withdrawal itself cost nothing — the same speed and cover that had gotten them in got them back out,
clean, no one hit, no one left behind. What it cost was the forty-some captives still inside the
walls, who stayed inside them that night because the alternative was staking their rescue on odds
he'd decided, in the water, weren't good enough to bet on.

Garren Hask took the report without comment, the way he always did, and entered it in the ledger the
way he entered every other night: a location, a number, an outcome. This was the first entry in years
where the outcome column read *withdrawn* instead of a count of the freed.

"We're not done with it," Kanja told him, back on the ship, still dripping. "I need that clear. This
isn't the kind of thing we let go because it was hard once."

"I didn't think it was," Hask said. "I've kept your ledger long enough to know the difference between
a debt you're walking away from and one you're just not ready to collect yet."

Efa Gol, who'd run the decoy line that drew two of the three towers' attention long enough to make the
retreat possible at all, said less. She'd seen him make harder calls with worse information and less
time to make them. What she said, once, quietly, before the subject was closed for the night: that a
persona built on the promise that no wall stayed standing forever could survive one wall standing a
little longer than he wanted it to, as long as it wasn't the promise that broke first — only the
timeline.

The island's name went into Hask's ledger with no closing date beside it. It stayed there,
unresolved, when the entry was written.
