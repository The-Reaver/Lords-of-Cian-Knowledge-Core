# The Manifest Written in Chains

*Locked canon, Batch 155, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-489`). The Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias
Chronicle XI. Rebellion era, age 21, Ghost Harbor. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone
material — a detailed night-boarding combat showcase per Abad's craft instruction. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The slaver vessel ran dark and unregistered, its own manifest a deliberate fiction covering nearly two
hundred captives held below deck — the kind of target the ghost fleet's reputation had quietly begun
attracting on its own, merchants and dockworkers alike learning to report suspicious dark-running
ships to the black sails rather than the Trust's own indifferent patrols.

Kanja brought the flagship alongside in full darkness, Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System reading the
slaver's hull displacement and crew movement through vibration alone long before any lookout aboard
could have registered the approach. Obsidian Malice discharged once against the slaver's own deck
gun before it could be brought to bear, the strike precise enough to disable the weapon without
igniting its powder store — a boarding, not a battle meant to sink the ship its captives depended on
staying afloat.

Onyx of Oblivion cleared the slaver's crew from the upper deck in a sequence that left the hold's own
guards cut off from any word of what was happening above, Whisper of Shadows carrying Kanja below
before the alarm could properly spread, Cadence Ruin breaking the remaining resistance into isolated,
disorganized men rather than a coordinated defense. The hold's own chief guard, cornered with no
retreat and no reinforcement coming, surrendered rather than test a fight he could see was already
lost.

Freeing nearly two hundred captives from a single hold took longer than the fighting itself — hours
of careful, methodical work, Kanja personally breaking chain after chain rather than trust the task
to anyone less certain of the mechanism's weak points, each freed captive helped above deck into open
air and a fleet ready to carry them somewhere the manifest's fiction could no longer reach them.

"The manifest called this ship a grain hauler," the fleet's own quartermaster said, reviewing the
slaver's forged papers afterward with open disgust. "Nearly two hundred people, and the only record
of them was a lie about cargo."

"Then we make sure the true record outlasts the lie." Kanja watched the last of the freed captives
settle into the fleet's own care. "Every one of these names gets written down, accurately, starting
tonight. The manifest they were held under doesn't get the last word on who they actually were."
