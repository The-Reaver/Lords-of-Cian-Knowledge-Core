# What the Smith Heard in the Quiet

*Locked canon, Batch 197, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-722`). The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XXVII, wave 9 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The village smith had no idea who the traveler repairing a wagon axle in her forge actually was, and
Kanja, between engagements and glad of the anonymity, didn't offer it.

She worked the way he'd learned, years earlier, to work himself — hands more than eyes, listening to
the metal's own complaint as it heated and cooled rather than trusting appearance alone. When she
struck a flawed weld and heard it ring wrong before it ever showed a visible crack, she set it aside
without a second thought, the way anyone who'd spent thirty years at a forge simply knew to.

"You've got an ear for it," she said, watching him true a bent fitting with more care than the small
job strictly needed. "Most travelers who stop through just want the work done fast. You're listening
to it the way I do."

"I had to learn to listen to metal a certain way, for reasons that don't matter much here. I didn't
expect to find someone who already did it without ever needing a reason as big as mine."

"Every smith worth her forge does it. You can't fix what you can't hear complaining first. I've known
that since I was younger than you probably were when you learned whatever you learned." She examined
his work with real satisfaction. "Doesn't take a name or a reputation. Just takes paying attention
long enough that the metal starts telling you things instead of the other way around."

He worked beside her the rest of the afternoon, trading nothing but craft — no names, no history, no
mention of any doctrine or any war beyond the village's own quiet distance from it — and found, in the
plain unremarkable rhythm of an ordinary forge, something the years of resonance and Crawlers and
countermeasures had never quite offered him: the reminder that what he'd built into a doctrine capable
of unmaking siege engines had started as the same thing every good smith already knew, practiced at a
scale nobody had asked a smith to reach before.

"Come back through anytime," she said, when he finally left near dusk. "Don't get that kind of quiet
company often. Metal doesn't talk back to most people the way it talks back to you and me."
