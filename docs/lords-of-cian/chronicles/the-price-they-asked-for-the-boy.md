# The Price They Asked for the Boy

*Locked canon, Batch 262, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1324`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LXXVIII,
wave 26, closing the wave. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241). A
coercion/hostage-pressure entry, the alias's first, resolved without violence. Not a territory
Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The message came folded inside an ordinary supply crate, addressed to no one in particular and
understood by everyone who read it within a minute of its arrival: an apprentice's younger brother,
taken three days prior from a settlement two tours behind, held against a single demand — that the
campaign hand over its full Dead Drakma reserves at the next handoff point, or the boy wouldn't be
returned at all.

The apprentice it concerned, seventeen and steady-handed at the forge until that morning, went
white reading it over Kanja's shoulder. "I'll go alone," he said immediately. "Whatever they want
from me instead of the reserves — I'll go."

"You won't," Kanja said, not unkindly, taking the letter back before the boy's own fear could turn
it into a decision made too fast to survive. "That's exactly the trade they're hoping someone here
panics into. It doesn't get your brother back any faster. It just gives them a second hostage."

He didn't rush the response, which cost the apprentice three of the hardest days of his life,
watching a campaign that could rebuild a burned district in a fortnight take three full days to
answer a letter. Kanja spent them differently than the letter's authors expected — not preparing a
handoff, real or false, but quietly tracing the letter's own courier back through two settlements
to the actual holding site, using the same Ghost-Lattice relay that had carried a dozen other
warnings the campaign over the tour.

The extraction, when it finally came, never touched the exchange point the kidnappers had named at
all. Onyx of Oblivion's Whisper of Shadows crossed the holding site's perimeter before its own
watch finished a single circuit, and the boy was out, unhurt and confused and asking where his
brother was, a full day before the deadline the letter had set. The reserves never moved. The
kidnappers, arriving at their own named exchange point to collect a payment that was never coming,
found nothing waiting for them at all.

"You could have told me what you were actually doing," the apprentice said afterward, arms around
his brother, something between relief and reproach in it.

"I could have," Kanja said. "You were scared enough to do something that would have gotten you
both taken. I needed you steady enough to keep working while I did the part that actually needed
patience instead of speed." He put a hand briefly on the older boy's shoulder. "Your brother's
safe. That's the only part of this that mattered enough to explain."
