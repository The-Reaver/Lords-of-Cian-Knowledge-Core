# The First Time He Said No

He was fifteen the first time anyone in that House learned he had a spine of his own separate from his
father's, and the General was careful, telling it to me, to say that it wasn't much of a spine as
these things go — no principle grand enough to hang a name on, no line drawn that anyone outside that
one household would have thought worth drawing. He said that mattered to him, keeping it small in the
telling. He didn't want it dressed up into something it wasn't.

House Verehimu ran, in those years, on a system of minor household justice that Aethelgard had
inherited from his own father and never much liked but never quite dismantled either — small
disputes among the staff, breakages, missed quotas, the ordinary friction of some two hundred people
living and working inside the same walls, were brought before whichever senior Verehimu happened to
be nearest the matter and settled on the spot, in front of whoever else was present, as a lesson to
the rest of the household as much as a judgment on the one at fault. It was efficient. It was also, the
General said, exactly as humane as any system designed around an audience tends to be.

The dispute that morning was a woman named Marta, who ran the House's linen stores and had, through
no fault the General was ever able to establish to his own satisfaction, allowed a shipment of dyed
Trust cloth to be miscounted against its manifest — eleven bolts short, a sum large enough to matter
to the House's accounts and small enough that it could as easily have been a clerk's error three hands
removed from her as any negligence of her own. The Seneschal wanted it settled publicly, before the
full linen staff assembled for the morning's work, as a warning against the kind of carelessness that
cost a House money it didn't need to lose. He wanted it read out, the shortfall named aloud, her wages
docked on the spot in front of the women she supervised. And because Aethelgard was riding the eastern
boundary that week and Ozmund, fifteen, had lately begun sitting in on such matters as part of what his
father called "learning the weight of the small decisions before you're ever trusted with the large
ones," it fell to him to pronounce it.

He said no. Not loudly — the General was firm about that too, that there was no scene, no raised
voice, nothing that would have made a story on its own merits if it hadn't been the first time. He
simply told the Seneschal that he would hear the matter, and that he would decide it, but that he
would not decide it in front of forty women whose only fault, if there was one at all, was working
under someone who had made a mistake she hadn't yet been given the chance to explain. He had Marta
brought to him alone instead, in a side room, and asked her plainly what had happened, and she told
him plainly — a delivery split across two carts on a day the second driver had fallen ill, a manifest
copied in haste from a note half-soaked by rain, a shortfall that turned up, eventually, three weeks
later, sitting uncounted in a storeroom nobody had thought to check because nobody had told the new
boy keeping it where the old boy used to. Ozmund fined her a fraction of what the Seneschal had wanted,
enough to mark that the House took its accounts seriously, not enough to be cruelty dressed as
discipline, and said nothing of it to anyone else in the linen stores at all.

The Seneschal complained to Aethelgard when he returned, not furiously, but as a man flags a concern
about a boy still learning his own authority. Aethelgard asked his son why he'd done it, and the
General remembered the answer he'd given, all those years later, more clearly than he remembered
almost anything else from that age: that a lesson taught by frightening forty people to correct the
mistake of one wasn't a lesson at all, it was only cruelty that had found itself a schedule. Aethelgard
didn't overrule him. He didn't praise him either, not in any words Ozmund could point to. He simply
said that he'd remember it, and rode out again the following week, and let the matter close there.
