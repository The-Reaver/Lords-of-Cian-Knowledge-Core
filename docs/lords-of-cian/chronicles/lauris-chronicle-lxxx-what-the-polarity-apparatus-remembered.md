# Lauris Chronicle LXXX: What the Polarity Apparatus Remembered

*Locked canon, Batch 300, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1697`). Eightieth entry in Lauris Letitia's own Chronicle
series, a Strand D entry giving full-scene treatment to Operation 23, the Iron-Halls of Velkar
(previously only summarized at `MCD-181`), opening this wave's run through the Specialist
sub-period. Dramatizes the malfunctioning polarity-control apparatus and the cooperative-era Karesian
technical instrumentation Lauris found written into it -- the evidence that let her independently
conclude, well ahead of her later Directorate-sourced confirmation, that the K-strand decline
(`MCD-153` through `MCD-157`) had been deliberate engineering rather than natural catastrophe, and
that whoever was responsible had obtained material directly from Kares Prime during the deep
cooperative era. Notes the already-locked naming coincidence distinguishing this site from Kares
Prime's own Iron-Halls of Vask. No new named characters. Zero collisions.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Specialist designation LL-001-S-023: "The instrumentation was not merely built by
someone who had touched Kares Prime. It was built by someone who could still read her, fluently,
centuries after her own people stopped being able to teach the reading to anyone new. I have spent
every operation since asking who that someone still is."*

Operation 23 arrived to her as an industrial-hazard contract, unglamorous by the standard her career
had by then set: a subterranean polarity-control apparatus beneath the river settlements collectively
known as the Iron-Halls of Velkar had begun cycling erratically, its malfunction already responsible
for two collapsed excavation shafts and the deaths of eleven Directorate-contracted miners before the
contract reached her. The Directorate wanted the cycling stopped and the apparatus's own hazard
assessed. It did not, by any indication in the brief, expect the assignment to matter beyond that.

I record here, because I believe the coincidence deserves the small notice Lauris herself gave it,
that the Iron-Halls of Velkar share their name with nothing more significant than an accident of
regional naming convention — unrelated to Kares Prime's own Iron-Halls of Vask, the archive complex
in which Lauris herself trained across the centuries of her Long Operational Period. She noted this in
her own entry, briefly and without elaboration, the way she notes most coincidences that turn out to
mean nothing: as a fact recorded for completeness rather than significance.

What she found, once she had traced the apparatus's cycling fault down to its source housing, meant
a great deal more. The polarity-control mechanism itself was unremarkable Directorate-era engineering,
old but conventional. What was not conventional was the instrumentation layered beneath it — a
secondary control architecture, plainly older than the apparatus it had been retrofitted to regulate,
its calibration plates and maintenance annotations rendered in a script she recognized on sight, and
recognized with a certainty that startled her more than the malfunction itself had.

Cooperative-era Karesian technical language. Not the ceremonial or archival register she had grown up
reading under Olda's instruction at Vask Karth-Ven, but the clipped, functional dialect of working
engineers — the kind of language, she has told me, that appears only on equipment meant to be read by
people who already knew how to operate it, never on anything meant to travel beyond Karesian internal
use. She had seen it, until that day, only in the Sister-Hold's own preserved archives and in her own
early training texts. She had never once expected to find it stamped into a maintenance plate beneath
a river settlement on Cian, retrofitted by hands that plainly understood what they were reading well
enough to keep the apparatus functioning for however many centuries it had already run.

*

She spent four additional days at the site, well beyond what the contract's hazard-assessment scope
required, tracing the secondary architecture's full extent before she resealed and reported it. Her
archive entry from those four days is, by her own later admission to me, one of the more carefully
reasoned pieces of private analysis in her entire career, and I have read it enough times to render
its substance faithfully. Cooperative-era Karesian technical language was never exported. It could
not have been copied from a stray fragment, a single recovered artifact, or even an extended period
of study conducted at a remove — the fluency the instrumentation displayed, its idiomatic shorthand
and its casual assumption of a trained reader's prior knowledge, could only have come from direct,
sustained access to living cooperative-era Karesian engineering culture, obtained while Kares Prime
still had a cooperative era to draw from.

This was not, she was careful to note in her own entry, proof that the K-strand decline itself had
been engineered — only proof that whoever had built the apparatus beneath Velkar had reached Kares
Prime deeply enough, and early enough, to walk away with more than biological material. But she has
told me, directly, that Operation 23 is the operation at which she first allowed herself to hold the
harder conclusion as a working theory rather than a discarded possibility: that the catastrophe which
had ended her own people's cooperative era, the collapse the Threnarr archivists had spent seventy-five
thousand years investigating without ever settling its cause, might not have been the natural failure
every account she had been raised on described it as.

She would not have this confirmed for her, by Ezio's own briefing drawing on Sephtis's archives, for
a great many operations still to come. She has told me plainly that Operation 23 was not that
confirmation. It was, instead, the first time the possibility stopped feeling to her like a private
and possibly unsound suspicion, and started feeling like a conclusion she was simply still gathering
the evidence to state aloud.

Her closing note for Operation 23, recorded the night she resealed the housing and filed her report
naming only the apparatus's hazard, not its architecture: *Someone was still fluent, long after my
own people stopped being able to teach the fluency to anyone new. I do not yet know whether that
means someone survived the decline, or someone caused it, and kept the language as a trophy. I intend
to keep asking until I know which.*
