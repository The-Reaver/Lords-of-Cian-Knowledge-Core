# What the Cold Taught the Ledger

*Locked canon, Batch 263, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1342`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LXIX, wave 23, closing the wave. The northern pilot's ice-reading method is formally added to the school's training as its own separate chapter, credited under her own name, rather than absorbed into Sephtis's storm-timing tradition.*

---

The pilot came south once, the following spring, at the student's invitation and against what she called her own better judgment about warm-water cities and their crowded harbors. She stood in the school's main hall -- Sephtis's original chart table still at its center, worn pale where three generations of hands had rested on the same corner -- and looked at the students gathered to hear her, none of whom had ever watched ice close a channel in their lives.

"I'm not here to teach you my whole craft," she told them. "You'd need twenty winters for that, same as I did. I'm here to teach you the one thing that matters more than any of it: that when the sky you know stops telling you anything, the answer isn't to guess harder at the sky. It's to go find whoever's been watching the thing you don't understand, and ask them plainly, the way I was asked."

The student had insisted the new chapter carry the pilot's own name rather than fold quietly into the school's general method -- *Northern Ice-Reading, as taught by* -- the first time any addition to the training had been credited to someone outside Sephtis's direct lineage at all. The retired successor, reading the finished chapter, thought that mattered more than the technical content itself.

"Sephtis built this to be believed on its own evidence," she said. "Not to be the only tradition worth believing. You just proved the second part without breaking the first."

The pilot left a season later, back north, still the only reader of her own ice for a strait that hadn't produced a second one in twenty years -- though she'd taken, quietly, the names of two of the school's younger students who'd shown real patience for cold water and colder math, in case either of them wanted to spend a winter learning something the ledger alone couldn't teach them.

Kanja, watching her go, thought of how many separate traditions this alias had now touched without ever once absorbing them whole -- the rival squadron's own weather-reading, the village folk-forecasters, and now a northern ice-pilot with no successor of her own until, perhaps, one of these. "You keep finding people who already knew things," he said to the student.

"That's the whole method," she said. "Sephtis never claimed to be the only one who could read anything. He just made sure somebody was keeping score honestly. Turns out that part travels."
