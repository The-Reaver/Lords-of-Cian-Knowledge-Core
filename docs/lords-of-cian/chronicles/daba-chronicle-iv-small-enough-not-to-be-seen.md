# Daba Chronicle IV: Small Enough Not to Be Seen

*Locked canon, Batch 296, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1574`). Entry 4 of 50 in Daba's own Chronicle series,
launched under the Character Chronicle Gameplan's own protagonist-series model. Dramatizes the
specific improvised act -- scattering the Rookery's survivors into small, separated groups ahead of
a rumored Trust "processing" sweep -- that plants Daba's own instinct for dispersal and
doctrine-over-mass, extending `MCD-1567`'s already-locked founding lesson ("anything large enough
to be seen is large enough to be burned") back to its first, pre-organizational instance. No new

The warning came from a boy who ran laundry for a Trust garrison kitchen three streets from the tannery and owed Odalys a debt older than the fire, the kind of debt that gets paid in information instead of coin when coin isn't what's needed. He said the word going around the garrison was that the survivors were to be gathered for a "processing" — names taken again, this time onto Trust paper rather than Odalys's, and anyone whose account of the fire didn't match the posted record flagged for what the boy's own overheard sentence had called *further clarity*.

Daba did not need Torvin to translate that phrase for him. He heard it the way he'd heard the boots on the stair four nights before — an administrative word wearing a plain coat over something with teeth.

"They know we won't hold to undetermined origin," he said, finding Sana first because she was already awake, already sorting ration bundles by lamplight with the same tight efficiency she brought to everything since the fire. "Two hundred of us in one building, all telling the same true story to anyone who'll listen. That's not survivors to them anymore. That's a witness list."

"So we run," Sana said. It wasn't a question so much as a flinch already reaching for its answer.

"Not all of us the same direction. Not all of us at all." He said it slowly, feeling the shape of it arrive as he spoke rather than before, the way the thought about Torvin's lie-built-to-outlast-us had arrived three days earlier without quite finishing itself. "If two hundred people leave one tannery together, that's still one target, just moving. They only have to catch the front of the line to have all of us. But six people leaving in six different directions to six different rooftops we already know from before the fire — that's not a witness list anymore. That's nothing worth the manpower to chase."

Torvin didn't like it, and said so plainly, propped against the wall with his bound arm and the particular stubbornness of a man who had kept one building's water running for thirty years and trusted the safety of staying where the water was. "You scatter two hundred grieving people across a district at night, half of them will get lost, some of them will get taken alone with no one to speak for them, and you'll have handed the Trust the easiest hunting season of its life."

"I'm not talking about two hundred people wandering," Daba said. "I'm talking about groups. Small enough that one caught group doesn't lead them to the next one. Six to a group, maybe eight. Someone who knows the district leading each one. Not scattering — hiding, on purpose, in pieces too small to be worth the paperwork."

It took the rest of the night to argue it properly, Torvin testing every seam of the idea the way he'd have tested a cistern joint before trusting it to hold pressure, and by the grey hour before dawn it was Torvin himself who split the tannery's roll into eight groups and picked the routes, because he knew the back ways of the district better than anyone left standing. Daba took the group with Mika and the ten others from the first night, led them out through a tanner's drying yard he'd never once had reason to walk before the fire, and did not look back to see whether the sweep came at all.

It came at midmorning, and found a tannery holding perhaps thirty stragglers too slow or too stubborn to have moved, and a great deal of Trust paperwork with nothing new to add to it. Daba heard the account of it two days later from a runner, secondhand and already half legend in the telling, and felt something settle in his chest that was not relief exactly — relief would come later, if it ever fully came — but closer to recognition. He had not fought anyone. He had not needed to. He had simply made the thing they were looking for too small to find, and it had worked precisely because it had never been large enough to be worth the looking.

He did not have a name for what he had just done. It would be weeks yet before he did.
