# Sworn Before the Salt Table

*Locked canon, Batch 280, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1464`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
XCVIII, wave 33. A new legal register: Kanja and Garren Hask are summoned to testify before a neutral
maritime tribunal over a piracy accusation, resolved through honest testimony and the ledger rather than
combat or diplomacy. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The summons came under a truce flag, addressed not to "the ghost" but to whoever could speak for the
black-sailed fleet, and it asked for something no Trust patrol, privateer, or foreign envoy ever had:
an appearance, unarmed, before a standing council of merchant-nations convened at the Salt Table to hear
a formal accusation of piracy brought by a Sovereign Trust delegation.

"They want you to walk into a room full of people who'd love an excuse to hold you," Efa Gol said. "No
guarantee of safe passage out."

"They offered one anyway," Kanja said. "And if the doctrine only holds up when nobody's watching
closely, it isn't a doctrine. It's a rumor."

He went with Garren Hask and nothing else — no weapon, no Trinity, no armed escort, just Hask's ledger
under one arm, three volumes deep by now and carrying every disabling instead of sinking, every
prisoner released instead of held, every convoy escorted rather than raided that the crew had ever
logged.

The Trust delegation built its case on wreckage and lost cargo, real numbers from real ships the fleet
had genuinely engaged. Hask answered every figure with the corresponding entry from his own ledger —
dates, outcomes, names where names existed — until the council's own presiding voice observed, dryly,
that the accusation's numbers and the ledger's numbers told the same events from two different angles,
and only one of the two angles bothered to record what happened to the crews.

The council didn't rule the fleet innocent of raiding — it had raided, plainly, and Hask's own ledger
said so without flinching from it. It ruled instead that the accusation as framed, piracy without
qualification, didn't match a documented pattern of restraint no pirate crew in the council's own long
memory had ever bothered to keep records of. The case was dismissed, not the reputation cleared.

"That's not a victory," Kanja said afterward, on the walk back to the boats.

"No," Hask agreed. "It's just true. Some days that's the only kind we get."
