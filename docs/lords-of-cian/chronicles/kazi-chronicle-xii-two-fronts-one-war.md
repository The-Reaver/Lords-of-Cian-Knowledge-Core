# Kazi Chronicle XII: Two Fronts, One War

*Locked canon, Batch 289, 2026-09-12 (`MCD-1531`). Seventh entry in Kazi's own Chronicles, Femi
(`PH2-066`) as protagonist, not a Kanja Chronicle. Kanja appears only as a guest, present but
granted no command, intervention, or resolution credit. Narration is close-third on Femi throughout.
Dramatizes direct friction between Femi's institutional-insider path and Irin's confrontational
floor-based one, resolved as complementary rather than competing.*

---

The dispute that finally put Femi and Irin on opposite sides of a decision was not a large one on its
face — a reclassification of six positions in the plant's finishing section, downgraded on paper in a
way that quietly stripped the men holding them of a pay grade and a seniority protection they'd earned
over a decade — but it arrived at a moment when both men had, independently and for their own good
reasons, already decided how they meant to answer it.

Irin wanted to call a halt within the week. The six men were bound tightly enough into the
finishing section's own chain that he was certain the call would hold, certain enough to have already
walked the section twice to confirm it. "A halt says the floor won't carry this. Said loud enough,
often enough, and the plant backs off it inside a month, same as it always has."

Femi had a formal reclassification grievance already filed, working its way through exactly the
process the union's own contract specified for exactly this situation — slower, unglamorous, and, if
it won, binding in the contract's own language for every position like it going forward, not just
these six. "A grievance that wins sets a rule. A halt that wins sets a precedent that the plant can
quietly work around the next time it tries something similar, dressed up different enough that the
men affected don't feel bound together the same way."

"You're asking six men to wait out a process that could take four months while their pay's already
been cut."

"I'm asking six men to choose which fight they want, and I think that's their choice to make, not
either of ours." Femi didn't raise his voice, the way he never did in a room he thought might get
louder without him. "If they'd rather have the fast answer, I won't stand against your halt.
But I don't think you should call it before you've asked them, any more than I'd file this grievance
without having asked them first myself."

They asked together, which neither man had originally planned to do, standing at the finishing
section's own end of the floor with the six men gathered loosely around them. The answer split three
and three, which settled nothing on its own until one of the six, older than the rest, said the thing
that ended up deciding it: "Do both. Call the halt now so the plant knows today what six men not
working costs it. Keep the paper moving so that in four months it isn't just these six who never lose
this again."

Irin called the halt that afternoon. It held, the finishing section's own tight chain proving exactly
as bound as he'd measured it, and the plant restored the six men's pay within nine days rather than
risk a longer standoff over a decision it could not easily defend even to itself. Femi's grievance
kept moving through the process regardless, unhurried by the halt's own quick win, and four months
later it closed with the reclassification rule itself struck from the contract's language entirely —
not just reversed for six men, but foreclosed for whichever six, or sixty, might have come after them.

"I was ready to call this one without you," Irin admitted afterward, watching Femi close the file on
a matter that had taken four times as long as his own halt had. "I've learned something I didn't
expect to learn from watching you work it slower. A win that ends today can still be won again
tomorrow, differently dressed. Yours doesn't leave that door open."

"Yours put pressure on the door before mine ever got the chance to seal it shut. I don't think either
one of us wins this in four months without the other one moving first." Femi set the closed file on
the stack beside the others. "I used to think we were running different races. I think now we're
running the same one, on legs that don't move the same way, and neither leg gets the body anywhere
alone."

The stranger who had stood at the finishing section's own edge through both the vote and the halt
that followed it said nothing through any of it, gone by the time the grievance closed four months
later, present for none of the credit either man extended the other in the room where it was finally
settled.
