# The Names He Started Writing Down

*Locked canon, Batch 286, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1519`). Captain Alias Chronicle XCIX, wave 33, closing the wave. Kanja takes his first concrete step toward the promise made at "The Promise for After He's Gone" (`MCD-920`) and renewed at Garren Hask's memorial (`MCD-1423`): becoming the one who remembers.*

---

He didn't call it anything grand. He simply asked Efa Gol, on an evening with nothing urgent pulling at either of them, whether she'd sit with him for an hour and tell him about Tam Sullen — not the Black Trench account already written into the crew's own history at `MCD-233`, not the ten seconds her hands had shaken before she picked her weapon back up, but the ordinary things. What Sullen had sounded like laughing. Whether he'd been any good at cards.

"That's not the kind of thing that goes in a ledger," Efa Gol said, though she sat down anyway.

"I know," Kanja said. "That's exactly why nobody's written it down anywhere yet."

Hask's ledger had always been the crew's honest accounting of what things cost — names, numbers, casualties tallied so precisely that a debt could never quietly disappear into rounding. It had never once tried to be the other thing: what the people behind those numbers had actually been like, before the number was the only part of them still on paper for anyone born after they were gone. Kanja started a second book that night, in his own hand, with no ledger lines ruled into it at all — just Efa Gol's voice, as close to word for word as he could keep it, about a rigger who used to hum off-key while he worked and never once noticed he was doing it.

He went to Maret Vos next, a week later, and asked about the two times he'd nearly left — not the decisions themselves, already accounted for in the crew's own memory, but what it had actually felt like standing at that edge both times. Vos talked for two hours and cried once, briefly, without apologizing for it.

"You're going to run out of people who remember things worth writing down long before you run out of years to keep writing," Efa Gol told him, some nights later, watching him fill another page.

"I know that too," Kanja said. "That's not really the point. The point was never finishing it. Hask taught me that about his own ledger without ever saying it in so many words — the entry that matters isn't the last one. It's that the next day's line is always still there, waiting for someone to fill it honestly." He closed the book carefully, the way he'd watched Hask close his a hundred times. "I told him once I'd become the one who remembers. This is just what that actually looks like, one ordinary evening at a time."
