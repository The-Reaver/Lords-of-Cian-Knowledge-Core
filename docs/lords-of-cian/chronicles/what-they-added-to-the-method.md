# What They Added to the Method

*Locked canon, Batch 192, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-587`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XLII, wave 14 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Kanja reinstated the officer who had refused the order, not as a pardon exactly, but as a direct
acknowledgment that the man's underlying objection — that the forecast couldn't see everything a
lifetime of local seamanship could — had been proven true within days of his formal punishment.

"I'm not restoring you because you were right to disobey," Kanja told him. "You weren't. An order
refused is still refused, whatever comes after. I'm restoring you because the thing you were trying to
protect against turned out to be real, and I'd rather have your knowledge inside the doctrine than
outside it resenting it."

The arrangement they built together, with Sephtis's full agreement, folded local pilotage knowledge
into the forecasting process for the first time — not replacing the weather charts, but requiring any
storm-timed approach through unfamiliar coastal water to be cross-checked against whatever hands-on
knowledge a veteran officer with real time on that specific stretch could offer, before the timing was
finalized.

"I spent this whole war building a method for reading the sky," Sephtis said, reviewing the new
protocol with something closer to relief than reluctance. "I never claimed it read the sea floor too.
I should have said that more plainly, before it took a near-grounding to make the gap obvious to
everyone."

The officer who had once refused the order became, within the month, the doctrine's first formally
recognized local-knowledge liaison — a role that would outlast the specific dispute that created it,
folded permanently into how Sephtis's network planned any approach through water its charts alone
couldn't fully vouch for.
