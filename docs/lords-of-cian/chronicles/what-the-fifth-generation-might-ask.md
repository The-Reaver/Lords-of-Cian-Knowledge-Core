# What the Fifth Generation Might Ask

*Locked canon, Batch 260, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1258`). Crow King Alias Chronicle LXVI, wave 22, closing
it. The fourth generation is approached by an outsider wanting to learn, and brings the question
back rather than deciding it himself. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The boy who'd asked wasn't crew. He was a runner's son from a settlement three days out, who'd heard
the phantom-garrison story secondhand, badly, the way stories travel, and had walked the three days
on the strength of a version that was already half wrong.

"Teach me the trick with the numbers," he said, when the fourth generation finally agreed to hear him
out. "The one where you don't need to sing."

"It's not a trick," the fourth generation said. "And I don't know if I'm the one who decides whether
anyone outside the crew gets taught anything at all."

He didn't send the boy away. He also didn't teach him. He walked him back most of the three days
himself, asking questions the whole way -- why the boy wanted it, who he'd tell, what he thought it
was for -- and left him at his own gate with nothing but the promise of an answer, someday, one way
or the other.

He brought the question to the third generation first, then to Kanja, laying out everything he'd
asked and everything the boy had said in return. "I could teach him," he said. "I think I could tell
if he'd use it right. But that's exactly what worries me -- I think I could tell, and I'm not sure
that's enough of a reason by itself."

"You already did the thing that matters," the third generation said. "You asked before you decided.
That's the only rule I actually gave you."

Kanja didn't answer the question either, not directly. "When I taught you," he said to the third
generation, "nobody asked me if it was time for a second generation to exist. It just happened,
because you were ready and I trusted it. When he taught his student" -- a nod toward the fourth
generation -- "he asked me first, because the craft was old enough by then to want a process. Maybe
by the time there's a fifth, it needs an answer none of us have built yet. I don't know what that
answer looks like. I don't think it's mine to build alone anymore."

No one taught the boy that season. The question went unanswered, deliberately, sitting between them
the way the very first scarecrow had once sat on an empty platform -- not resolved, just left where
everyone could see it.
