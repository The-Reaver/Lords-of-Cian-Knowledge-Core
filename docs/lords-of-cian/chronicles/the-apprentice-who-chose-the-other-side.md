# The Apprentice Who Chose the Other Side

*Locked canon, Batch 279, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1453`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XCVI, wave 32, closing the wave. The alias's first entry where a taught skill is used against the cause without malice or coercion -- distinct from every prior betrayal, defection, or reconciliation register.*

---

Efa Gol brought him the name like she expected it to land as a wound. "He's on the Trust reconstruction crews now. Rebuilding the granary wall we spent two weeks arguing whether to bring down."

Kanja recognized the young man before he recognized the name attached to him -- one of the first six he'd ever pulled off a labor gang and taught to read a load line properly, back when the safety-training institution had been nothing but Kanja himself and a borrowed level. He found him on a Trust scaffold, checking a lintel's seating with the exact unhurried precision Kanja had once drilled into him, and the young man saw him coming and didn't flinch, didn't run, and didn't stop working.

"I figured you'd hear eventually," he said, setting the level down. "I'm not going to pretend I didn't know how it'd look."

"Tell me how you'd have me look at it instead."

"You taught me a wall doesn't care whose war it's standing in. It just wants to stand or it wants to fall, and either way somebody has to answer for which." He gestured at the granary behind him, half-rebuilt, honest scaffolding straight up its face. "This one's going to feed people this winter no matter which side wins what's left of the argument. I didn't see a version of what you taught me that said I should let it fall out of spite."

It would have been easy to hear that as a wound dressed up as principle, and easier still to answer it with the crew's own anger rather than his own judgment -- Efa Gol was already standing a half-step forward, jaw set, waiting for a word that would let her treat this as the theft it might have looked like from any distance shorter than the one Kanja was actually standing at. He didn't give her that word.

"I didn't teach you loyalty," Kanja said. "I taught you a trade. I can't hand you the first and take back the second just because you used it somewhere I wouldn't have chosen."

"That's not the answer I expected."

"It's the only one that's actually true to what I built here." Kanja looked past him at the granary wall, checked its lintel line out of old habit, and found nothing wrong with it. "If the only version of this trade that's safe to teach is the one that only ever gets used exactly how I'd use it myself, then I never actually taught anyone anything. I just borrowed their hands for a while."

The young man didn't ask for reconciliation, and Kanja didn't offer forgiveness for something he'd already decided wasn't a wrong to forgive. Efa Gol walked away from the scaffold in tight, unhappy silence, and it was three streets before she spoke again.

"You're not angry."

"I'm not glad, either. I don't have to be either one to know that a trade I taught honestly doesn't stop being honest just because it ends up serving an argument I didn't choose." Kanja glanced back once at the half-finished granary. "The wall doesn't know whose side built it. That was always the whole point of teaching it that way."
