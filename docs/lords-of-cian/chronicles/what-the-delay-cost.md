# What the Delay Cost

*Locked canon, Batch 151, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-477`). Bane Alias Chronicle XI. Rebellion era, age 19,
shortly after the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material — the first
genuine failure entry in Bane's run. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters.*

---

Bane held back from a compound where reports suggested some prisoners might be Directorate
informants planted among genuine captives — waiting a full extra day to verify the intelligence
properly rather than risk freeing men who would carry word straight back to the garrison that
still stood between the crew and the rest of the district. It was the same caution that had kept
the fear-reputation honest since the beginning: never act on incomplete information, never let the
legend outrun what was actually true.

The extra day cost two of the prisoners their lives.

The compound's overseers, informed by an actual informant among the guards rather than the
prisoners, moved two men flagged as troublemakers to a separate holding cell and executed them
quietly before the liberation ever began — a loss the verified intelligence, arriving a day late,
could do nothing to prevent.

"You couldn't have known," the crew's forward scout said afterward, watching Bane review the final
count against what it should have been. "The informant was inside the guard detail, not the
prisoners. No verification of the prisoner list would have caught that."

"I know exactly why the delay happened, and I know it was the right call given what I actually knew
at the time. That doesn't change what it cost." Bane didn't accept the deflection, didn't let
himself off the reasoning's own hook. "I've built this entire reputation on the idea that acting
correctly matters more than acting fast. I still believe that. I also have to sit with the fact that
correct and fast aren't always the same choice, and sometimes the gap between them has a cost with
two names attached to it."

"What happens to the caution, going forward?"

"Nothing changes about it. I'd make the same call again, with the same information." Bane closed the
report, the two names recorded plainly rather than smoothed over. "That's the harder truth underneath
this, not a comfortable one. Sometimes the right decision still costs something real. Pretending
otherwise would be its own kind of dishonesty, and I've built too much of what this name means on
honesty to start lying to myself about the price of it now."
