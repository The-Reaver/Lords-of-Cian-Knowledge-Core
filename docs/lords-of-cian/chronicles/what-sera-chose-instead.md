# What Sera Chose Instead

*Locked canon, Batch 240, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1057`). Captain Alias Chronicle LIX, wave 20. Not a
territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The summer Sera turned sixteen, Callum Breck asked Kanja to come to the healers' hall himself and
hear it from her directly, rather than secondhand through him. He hadn't asked for anything from the
crew in years that wasn't practical — a supply run, a watch swap — and Kanja understood, walking up
from the pier, that this was neither.

Sera was already known on the ship before she could walk under her own power: the infant daughter
Garren Hask had once relayed milestones about to a silent father in the field, the girl whose naming
ceremony Breck had missed to run the Scrip-Forge Raid, the reason he'd broken four months of silence
speaking only to her. Everyone assumed, the way people assume things nobody has actually said aloud,
that she would join when she was old enough. The healers' hall training was meant to be a bridge to
that, not an alternative to it.

"I'm not coming aboard," Sera told him, plainly, the way her father talked when he'd decided
something and stopped needing to rehearse it. "Not this year. I don't think ever, actually, though I
know that's not what anyone expected me to say."

"Nobody expected anything," Kanja said. "I don't think your father did either, whatever he told
himself."

"He didn't say it, but he was braced for it. I could see it in how carefully he didn't ask." She set
down the bandage roll she'd been winding, giving the conversation her full attention the way she'd
clearly been taught to give a patient hers. "I've spent three years learning what a body actually
needs when it's failing, and I'm good at it — better than good, Healer Orenn tells me, though she'd
never say it where I could hear it twice. I don't want to be a name on a manifest who happens to also
set bones. I want to be the person this whole coast comes to because I'm the best at exactly one
thing, and that thing isn't fighting anyone's war, old or new."

He didn't argue it, and he didn't perform the not-arguing either — she would have caught the
performance instantly, being her father's daughter. "The charter your father's council wrote says
nobody's here because they have to be. I didn't understand until just now that it was supposed to
cut both ways — that not coming aboard needed protecting as much as staying did."

"That's exactly what I needed you to understand." For the first time, something in her posture
eased. "I love this crew. I've loved it my whole life, the way you love a family you were born
into and didn't choose. I don't have to serve on its ship to prove that."

Breck, waiting by the door the whole time without once stepping through it, exhaled something that
had clearly been held a long while. Kanja caught his eye on the way out and found no disappointment
in it at all — only the particular, complicated relief of a man watching his daughter become someone
he hadn't built, and finding he preferred her that way.

Sera ran the healers' hall for the rest of her working life and never once set foot on a crew ship
under sail, and no one — least of all Kanja — ever again let it be assumed that she should have.
