# What the Ventilation Couldn't Cure

*Locked canon, Batch 279, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1452`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XCV, wave 32. The alias's first contagious-illness/public-health engineering register, entirely non-combat and explicitly outside Kanja's own competence to cure.*

---

The barracks fever had been three days in the crowded worker shelters before anyone thought to send word below ground, and by the time Kanja climbed up to see it for himself, eleven people were down with it and a twelfth had stopped answering to her own name.

"I can't fix this," he said, plainly, to the healer standing over the row of pallets. "I want to say that first, before anyone starts hoping I can. This isn't stone or timber. I don't know what's making them sick, and I can't read a fever the way I read a crack."

"Nobody's asking you to heal them," the healer said, exhausted past the point of politeness. "I'm asking whether you can slow it down while we try."

That, he could answer. Danne Sok had already traced the shelter's single shared ventilation duct -- a narrow bored channel that had seemed clever when it was cut, pulling one steady draft through every sleeping chamber to keep the winter cold from pooling in low corners -- and understood before he said it aloud that the same design pulling air efficiently through the whole barracks was pulling whatever the sick were breathing out straight into the lungs of everyone still well.

"It was built to share warmth," Kanja said, running a hand along the duct's mouth. "It's sharing this too."

He spent the day cutting three new branch channels through solid earth, each one bored fast and rough rather than fine, diverting the shared draft into isolated loops so the ten worst cases breathed only their own air and vented it up and out through a separate shaft entirely, while the rest of the barracks kept a clean, unshared draft of its own. It was crude work by his own usual standard -- no elegant single solution, just three redundant paths cut quickly because quickly mattered more than clean -- and it would not cure a single person already sick.

"This buys the healers time," he told Danne Sok, checking the new draft with a hand at each vent mouth. "It doesn't buy anyone health. I want that said plainly to everyone in there, so nobody thinks a tunnel fix is the same thing as a cure."

Danne Sok watched the sick barracks' air pull cleanly away from the well barracks' for the first time in three days and said nothing for a while.

"You could have let them believe you'd fixed it," she said eventually. "Most people would take the credit and let the relief do the rest."

"Most people aren't the ones who'll be standing here in a week if it turns out I was wrong about which duct mattered." Kanja checked the last vent seal and stood. "I'll take credit for moving air. I won't take credit for something the healers are still fighting with nothing but their own hands and whatever herbs they've got left."

Six days later the contagion had not spread past the original eleven, and four of those eleven were sitting up and asking for food. Nobody called it a cure. Danne Sok noticed that Kanja never once let anyone call it that in his hearing, correcting the phrase gently every time it drifted toward him.
