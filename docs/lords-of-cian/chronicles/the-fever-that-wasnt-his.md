# The Fever That Wasn't His

*Locked canon, Batch 255, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1139`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle LXXXII, wave 28.
Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. A mass outbreak among the dredge workers, where the tally method
has nothing at all to offer.*

---

The same canal-borne cistern fever that had once put Kanja himself flat on his back for a fortnight
returned that winter in a form the district hadn't seen before — not one man struck down and cared
for by three devoted friends, but dozens at once, spreading through the dredge-line barracks faster
than any single household could nurse its own sick, let alone anyone else's.

There was no owner to blame for it and no wage theft hiding inside it. Garren Hask's tally table sat
untouched for the better part of two weeks, every worker who might otherwise have brought a grievance
either sick themselves or caring for someone who was.

"I don't have a figure for this," Hask said, which had become, by then, a sentence he found himself
saying more often than either of them liked.

Kanja didn't try to make one. What the outbreak needed had nothing to do with verification or
restitution — it needed boiled water, distributed faster than any single well could manage; a
quarantine line drawn carefully enough to slow the spread without abandoning anyone already sick
inside it; and enough hands rotating through care duty that no single family collapsed under the
weight of nursing its own dying alone. He organized all three the way he'd once organized a raid,
except the enemy here was invisible and indifferent, and there was no clever tactic that would make
it care about a flood-timing calculation.

Efa Gol ran the boiled-water distribution with the same logistics instinct she'd once turned toward
decoy routes. Pell Ostra, whose chemistry knowledge had never before been asked to do anything but
build or dissolve something, worked out which of the barracks' contaminated cisterns needed to be
sealed entirely rather than merely boiled from, tracing the fever's spread back to a single collapsed
drainage joint feeding three wells at once. Danne Sok carried the dead — there were dead, seven of
them by the time the fever finally broke, an outcome no amount of organizing fully prevented — without
being asked and without complaint, the same wordless service he gave to everything the crew needed
from him.

Kanja himself spent most of the two weeks doing the least specialized work available: emptying
slop-basins, sitting with the delirious so their families could sleep in shifts, carrying water that
needed no particular skill to carry. It was, he told Hask once the worst had passed, the least
Trench-Monarch thing he'd done since the name existed — no duel, no ledger, no owner to confront, just
the slow, unglamorous work of getting a district through something no reputation could touch.

The permanent sanitation rule instituted after his own earlier illness was expanded that winter into
a standing quarantine protocol, drawn up jointly by Pell Ostra's contamination-tracing and Hask's
own record-keeping discipline, kept in the ledger beside every wage figure the district had ever
disputed.
