# Lauris Chronicle XXXIX: The Photographs She Has Not Shown Him

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1656`). Thirty-ninth entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, part of the wave-one push of Strand L. Advances the Twin Anomaly photographs
(`MCD-1535`): development-record pages Lauris photographed before the Operation 6 compound's
demolition, still unanalyzed, still unshown to Sephtis, whom she expects would recognize them.
Deepens rather than resolves -- the Operation 38 and Sample K-403 inquiries elsewhere in this wave
give her fresh cause to reconsider showing him, and she comes closer than she ever has before, but
still declines. No new named characters. Narrated by Fermand Aurelias.*

---

*Archive fragment, present-day: "Six pages. I have looked at them more times than I have let anyone
else look at them at all. I took them out again tonight. I put them away again tonight. I am
recording both facts, because I think the second one matters as much as the first."*

I have transcribed enough of Lauris's private archive by now to recognize when a habit has become a
pattern worth naming, and I believe this is such a case. The photographs from Operation 6 -- pages
from the development records of the assassin twins Velek and Velka, recovered moments before the
compound that housed them was destroyed -- have sat in her personal archive, by her own account,
essentially untouched for centuries. She privately suspects, without confirmation, that the twins were
a product of the same engineering tradition she would later identify as T.D.K.'s. She has told me,
more than once, that she expects Sephtis would recognize the pages on sight. She has never shown him.

What changed this season, insofar as anything changed, was not the photographs themselves. It was the
accumulation of everything else this wave of her archive has surfaced alongside them: the Operation 38
facilities she reported rather than withheld, moved somewhere she cannot yet trace; Sample K-403,
filed by her own hand decades ago and now reclassified into an archive she cannot locate; the Drowning
Vault's hundred and twenty, whose revival Sephtis himself has just helped her understand as far more
complicated than she had assumed. Each of these, in its own way, involves a piece of evidence she
once judged small enough to set aside, only to discover later that setting it aside had a cost she had
not priced correctly at the time.

She raised this connection herself, unprompted, on the evening she took the photographs out for what
I understood to be the first time in several years. "I have made this mistake before," she said. "I
decide a piece of evidence is not yet actionable, and I let 'not yet' become 'indefinitely' without
ever consciously choosing the second version. K-403 sat filed for six thousand years because I never
went back to it. I do not want these pages to become the same kind of oversight."

I asked whether she intended to show them to Sephtis that evening. She did not answer immediately,
which I have learned, across this chronicle, is generally itself an answer. She looked at the six
pages for what I estimate was close to twenty minutes -- longer than I have ever seen her spend on
them before -- turning each one over in the particular unhurried way she handles anything she is not
yet certain how to hold. Then she closed the folio and returned it to its place in the archive,
unshown.

"Not tonight," she said, when I asked why. "If I show him and he confirms what I suspect, the
suspicion becomes a fact, and a fact obligates a response I am not yet positioned to give. I have six
other debts open that I cannot currently discharge. I do not think I am avoiding him because I am
afraid of what he will say. I think I am declining to open a seventh door before I have made real
progress on any of the six already open."

I do not know whether I believe this is the whole of her reasoning, and I record that doubt here
because I believe an honest chronicle requires it. I have watched her open considerably more
dangerous doors than this one without hesitation -- a compound rigged with a crude density ward, a
facility that killed six Directorate teams before her. I do not think the hesitation here is about
danger. I think, though I offer this only as my own impression and not as anything she has confirmed,
that it is about certainty, and about how much longer she can hold a suspicion loosely once someone
else has looked at it and told her it is true.

She has not set a date for showing him. She has, for the first time in the years I have known her,
told me plainly that she intends to, eventually, rather than leaving the question open indefinitely
the way she left Sample K-403 open for six thousand years without noticing the omission had become
permanent.

Her closing note: *Six pages. Not tonight. I have written "not yet" in this archive more times than I
can currently count without reviewing every entry. I am trying, this time, to make sure "not yet"
still means what it says.*
