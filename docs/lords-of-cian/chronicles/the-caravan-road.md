# The Caravan Road

*Locked canon, Batch 200, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-802`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle XVII, wave 6 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Every liberation the legend had accumulated so far had happened on water or within sight of it, and
the escort captain running forty chained laborers up an inland caravan road had planned his route
specifically around that fact, three weeks from any coastline, confident that whatever haunted the
Gale Straits had no business this far from salt air.

He was wrong by one man and one road's worth of dust.

The Forge-Coat's V2 grounding weave, built for absorbing cannon concussion and boarding-pike impact
across a rolling deck, adapted without complaint to a different kind of violence entirely — a
mounted outrider's lance took the coat's shoulder plating dead-on and transferred its force into the
weave's structure instead of the man wearing it, the horse veering off riderless a heartbeat later.
Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System, tuned across years of naval boarding actions to absorb the jolt of
grappling hooks and falling rigging, found the caravan's own thrown stones and swung cudgels a
strange but entirely serviceable substitute, storing each impact as readily as it had ever stored a
wave's slap against a hull.

Dust replaced smoke as the medium the Sovereign Eyes' amber lenses had to read, and read it they did
— the escort's flanking riders picked out through a haze that would have hidden them from any
ordinary pursuer, each one down before the next understood the first had fallen.

The caravan captain met him at the wagon line with a crossbow already loading, hands shaking not
from cold. "You're supposed to be a sailor's ghost story. What are you doing three weeks from the
sea?"

"Following the chains. They don't stop being chains just because the ground under them dried out."
Obsidian Malice's two-year charge, still full, went unspent — the wagon locks gave to the Ironhand
Gauntlets alone, riveted plate tearing hasps that had never been built to survive a man's bare
strength applied with intent. "You picked this road because you thought it was safe from me. It
wasn't the road that made you safe before. It was that nobody who could stop you had bothered to
follow this far."

Forty laborers walked free onto a caravan road that had, until that afternoon, never seen the
Scourge and never expected to.

"Word's going to travel wrong from this," the captain said, disarmed and pinned in the dust, more
resigned than afraid. "Sailors' ghost inland. Nobody's going to believe it."

"They don't have to believe the story. They just have to stop trusting the road." He was already
turning back toward the freed line, done with the conversation. "That part travels fine on its own."
