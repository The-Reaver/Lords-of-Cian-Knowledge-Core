# Five Years, As Promised

*Locked canon, Batch 264, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1379`); corrected Batch 320, 2026-10-01 (Corren Halst
reconciled to he/him, matching `CC-158`). Captain Alias Chronicle LXXIX, wave 27. Direct payoff: Kanja asks Corren Halst the succession question again, exactly as promised five years before.*

---

Kanja remembered the date the way he remembered every casualty-list name he'd ever checked himself — not because he'd marked it anywhere, but because some promises simply didn't need a ledger to stay counted. Five years, almost to the week, since Garren Hask's notebook had come out of hiding and Corren Halst had asked him to wait before naming him anything.

He found Halst checking rigging on the Second Chance, not the Receipt, the way he always did when he wanted a quiet hour before whatever came next. He didn't announce the question. He just sat down on the rail beside him and let him get there himself.

"Five years," Halst said, not looking up from the line in his hands.

"Five years," Kanja agreed. "You said ask you again once you'd actually thought about it instead of reacting to Hask nearly dying in front of us. I'm asking."

He finished the knot before he answered, which Kanja had learned by now meant he wasn't stalling — he simply didn't do anything halfway, not even a conversation this size. "I have thought about it. Longer than five years, if I'm honest — I started actually turning it over the week after Hask asked, not the week before you came to collect the answer." He set the rope down. "And I don't think the answer's as simple as yes or no. I think that's the mistake in the question itself."

"Then tell me what the question should have been."

"Whether one person taking over for you is even the right shape for what this has become. You built something that runs on Halst leading a rescue while you're gone, and Hask running the ledger while his own heart gives out, and Efa Gol training a second set of hands so the count stays honest after hers slow down. That's not one successor waiting in the wings. That's an institution that's already been quietly training itself not to need any single person, including you. I don't think I should be the answer. I think I should help build whatever the real answer turns out to be."
