# The Bridge That Bought Them Time

*Locked canon, Batch 195, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-667`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XXXII, wave 11 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The timber supply bridge spanning the district's eastern ravine was the crew's only surface route
for moving heavy shoring material into Killane, and a Trust engineering unit had orders to burn it
before the week's final shipment could cross.

Dol Maren had surveyed the bridge's own construction two days earlier, flagging a weak central span
that would need reinforcement before it could safely carry the load the crew needed it to. He and
Kanja worked the reinforcement together through the night the attack came, an engineer's careful
calculation matched against Trinity strength neither one could have supplied alone.

"Your math says this brace holds four tons," Kanja said, driving the reinforcing timber home at the
angle Dol Maren's calculations specified. "I trust your math over my own instinct on a surface
structure. Tunnels I read by feel. This is your kind of work."

"And I trust your hands to seat it exactly where the math needs it seated." Dol Maren checked the
angle against his own sketch, satisfied. "Different craft, same respect for what the structure
actually needs rather than what we'd like it to need."

The attack came before the reinforcement was fully finished — six soldiers with fire oil, moving to
torch the span's dry central timbers before the shipment ever reached it. Obsidian Malice met the
lead soldier's advance at the bridge's near end while Mafesto's frame anchored against the span
itself, holding a load-bearing position no ordinary defender could have sustained without risking
the bridge's own structure; Onyx handled the flanking pair that tried the span's far side, Cadence
Ruin clearing them before either could reach the oil they carried.

The bridge held, the shipment crossed by morning, and Dol Maren's calculated reinforcement — tested
by combat as much as by cargo — proved sound under both kinds of weight it was never separately
designed to survive.

"Half of holding that span was your math," Kanja said afterward, checking the brace one final time.
"I could fight for it. I couldn't have told you it would still be standing afterward without your
numbers behind the fight."

"And half of it was you fighting on top of a bridge I wasn't sure would hold a war as well as it
held a wagon." Dol Maren allowed himself a rare, tired smile. "I think we're calling this one even."
