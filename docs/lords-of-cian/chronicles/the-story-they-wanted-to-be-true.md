# The Story They Wanted to Be True

*Locked canon, Batch 254, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1098`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXVIII, wave 23. Directorate
propaganda fabricates a massacre and blames it on Bane to justify a crackdown; he counters not with
force but by putting the actual evidence in front of independent witnesses.*

---

The pamphlets reached the border district three days before Bane did, and by the time his column
arrived the story had already hardened into something people repeated without needing to be
convinced twice: Bane's fighters had put a village to the sword for refusing to join the rebellion,
forty dead, the Directorate's crackdown that followed framed as protection rather than reprisal.

The village had been burned, and forty people had died in it. Bane's fighters had never been within
thirty miles of the place.

He didn't waste time arguing the pamphlets in front of the people who'd already believed them.
Instead he found three merchants — none of them Rebellion sympathizers, none of them owing him
anything, chosen specifically because their trade routes had carried them through the district both
before the burning and after — and asked them to walk the site themselves. He didn't narrate it to
them. He didn't point out the details that mattered. He let them read the ground the way anyone
trained to notice a burned building's actual cause could read it: the pattern of the fire's origin
points, consistent with torches carried in rather than a defended engagement; the absence of any
spent Rebellion-pattern munitions anywhere on the site; the garrison boot-prints preserved in mud at
the tree line the pamphlets never mentioned.

"I'm not going to tell you what to think of it," he told them once they'd finished. "I'm asking you
to say what you actually saw, to whoever asks you next, and to say nothing about anything you
didn't."

The merchants' own account, told without his presence and carrying none of the weight a Rebellion
fighter's testimony would have, spread through the district's trade routes faster than the original
pamphlets had.

Danne Sok asked him afterward why this one warranted correcting, when the fever-outbreak story that
had been doing the rebellion's own recruiting for months had gone untouched on purpose.

"That story never got anyone killed who wasn't already going to die of the fever," Bane said. "This
one was going to get real people killed by a real crackdown, justified by something we never did.
The difference isn't whether the story helps or hurts me. It's whether it costs someone else
something I could have stopped."

---

*Continuity notes (not narrative): directly extends and contrasts "The Rumor He Never Corrected"
(`MCD-681`, wave 6) by dramatizing the actual principle behind when Bane does versus doesn't correct
a false story -- concrete harm to real people, not damage to his own reputation, is the deciding
factor. Distinct from the tribunal register of "The Council That Asked Him to Speak Plainly"
(`MCD-939`) by being proactive rather than a formal proceeding, and by deliberately routing the
evidence through independent witnesses rather than his own testimony. No new named characters -- the
merchants are unnamed; Danne Sok reused.*
