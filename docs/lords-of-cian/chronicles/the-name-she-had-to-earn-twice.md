# The Name She Had to Earn Twice

*Locked canon, Batch 250, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1086`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LXI, wave 21, first entry in the wave. A newly posted Trust naval liaison refuses to act on the third-generation student's storm call over her lack of standing, and the retired successor deliberately declines to vouch for her -- the doctrine's first legitimacy-of-succession test, distinct from every prior test of the doctrine itself. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The liaison was new to the coast and had never met Sephtis, never met the successor either except by
reputation, and had certainly never met the young woman standing in front of him now with a chart under
one arm and a storm two days out that the almanac in his own trunk said nothing about.

"I don't doubt the school," he said, careful, the way men are careful when they suspect they're about to
insult someone's teacher. "I doubt that you're the one authorized to speak for it."

The third student didn't answer that directly. She looked past him to where the retired successor sat on
the harbor wall, close enough to hear every word, close enough that a single nod from her would have
ended the argument before it began.

The successor said nothing. She looked at the sky instead, the same unhurried way she always had, and let
the silence sit exactly as long as it needed to.

Kanja, standing off to one side in no capacity anyone in the conversation had asked him to fill, understood
what she was doing before the liaison did. She had told him herself, on the night she handed over the
ledger: *I'm teaching myself when to stop being the reason people believe it.* This was that lesson,
applied to someone else's authority instead of her own -- if she vouched for the student now, the student
would never fully own the call that followed. She'd only have borrowed it.

"Then don't ask her to speak for the school," the student said finally, to the liaison, not to the
successor. "Ask the ledger." She held out her own -- three years of calls kept in her own hand, since the
night she'd been handed the running record and started her own beside it, successes and the two honest
misses she'd never once tried to bury among them.

The liaison read it standing on the dock, slowly, weather by weather, while the tide came in around his
boots. He looked up twice -- once at a call he clearly remembered being right about from somewhere else
entirely, and once at one of the misses, circled and dated in her own hand with a note underneath explaining
exactly how she'd been wrong.

"You'd have hidden that one," he said, of the miss. "Most people would."

"Most people don't have to teach the next one how to read it," she said.

He moved the fleet's departure by six hours. The storm arrived four hours after that, exactly where she'd
said, exactly as strong. Nobody was caught in open water.

Afterward, on the wall, the successor finally spoke. "You didn't need me."

"I knew that going in," the student said. "I needed you to know it too, in front of him, without saying
it."

The successor almost smiled at that -- the particular pride of watching a lesson land somewhere she hadn't
had to teach it twice.
