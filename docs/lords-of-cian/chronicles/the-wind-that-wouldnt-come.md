# The Wind That Wouldn't Come

*Locked canon, Batch 258, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1206`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
LXVIII, wave 23. A dead calm as an unbeatable, unhurryable natural condition. Not a territory
Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The calm settled over the fleet on the nineteenth day out from the last port and simply didn't lift —
no storm to navigate, no current to ride, just flat water and dead air stretching to every horizon
with a rescued merchant convoy of six ships trailing behind them, all seven vessels equally becalmed
and equally out of any answer sail alone could give.

Dol Maren read the sky every morning and gave the same report every morning: nothing coming, not
today. Danne Sok's charts showed open water in every direction with no coastline close enough to row
to before the water ran out. It was, Efa Gol observed on the eleventh day of it, the first enemy the
fleet had ever faced that wasn't trying to kill anyone and couldn't be reasoned with, fought, or
outmaneuvered — just waited out.

The merchant crews, unused to the fleet's own discipline, started fraying first. Kanja moved between
all seven ships by launch, not to command the rationing — Efa Gol and the merchant captains had that
in hand — but to be present for it, the same way he'd been present through the fever, through every
hard stretch this fleet had weathered that no gear or Trinity power could shorten. He rowed water
rations personally rather than have it look like scarcity handled from a distance.

On the twenty-second day, a boy on one of the merchant ships, twelve years old and thirsty and
frightened in the specific way only a child stuck somewhere with no visible end can be, asked Kanja
outright if the fleet's legend was even real, if the Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea couldn't even
make the wind blow when they needed it to.

"No," Kanja told him plainly. "I can't make wind. Nobody can. What I can do is make sure nobody on
these seven ships goes without water while we wait for it, and make sure we're still a fleet, not
seven separate ships fending for themselves, when it finally comes."

The wind returned on the twenty-fourth day, no warning, no drama, simply picking up out of the south
the way weather does. Garren Hask logged twenty-four days, seven ships, and not one death from thirst
— the driest entry, he noted, that the ledger had ever needed to hold.
