# The Prediction He Almost Used for Advantage

*Locked canon, Batch 190, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-556`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XVII.
Rebellion era, age 29, the Gale Straits. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material.
Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters beyond the already-locked Sephtis.*

---

Sephtis's prediction placed a coming storm directly over both a Directorate patrol squadron's likely
position and an unrelated civilian fishing fleet working the same waters — a single piece of
foreknowledge that could either trap the squadron in conditions it wouldn't survive, or warn the
fishing fleet in time to reach safe harbor before the storm broke. Reaching both wasn't possible in
the time available. Kanja had to choose which the prediction served.

"The squadron's been running supply for the garrison at Voss Hollow," the crew's own tactician said,
laying out the military case plainly. "Trapping it in that storm ends a supply line we've been trying
to cut for months. The fishing fleet's got no strategic value to either side. Nobody would question
prioritizing the squadron."

"I'd question it. I don't think a storm prediction is a weapon I'm willing to spend on people who
never signed up for this war, even when using it that way would cost me nothing tactically to
justify." Kanja didn't weigh the decision long, the answer clearer to him than the tactician's own
framing suggested it should be. "Warn the fishing fleet. If there's time left after that to warn the
squadron away too, we do that as well. But the fishing fleet doesn't wait on whether military
advantage happens to align with their safety."

There was time for both, in the end — the fishing fleet reached harbor with hours to spare, and the
warning that reached the squadron, delivered anonymously through neutral channels rather than direct
contact, let it withdraw before the worst of the storm without ever engaging the rebel fleet at all.
The supply line continued uninterrupted, the tactical advantage genuinely lost.

"We could have ended that supply line today," the tactician said afterward, not quite objecting, more
processing the cost of the choice. "You gave that up to warn people who were never actually in danger
from us."

"I gave up a tactical advantage to make sure a storm prediction never becomes something civilians
have to fear falling into the wrong priorities. That's worth more to me than one supply line, however
useful cutting it would have been." Kanja watched the fishing fleet's own lanterns settle safely into
harbor. "I'd rather this doctrine be trusted by everyone on this water, not just the people it's
currently fighting for."
