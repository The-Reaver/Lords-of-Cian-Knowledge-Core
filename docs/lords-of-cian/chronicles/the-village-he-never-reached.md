# The Village He Never Reached

*Locked canon, Batch 284, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1504`). Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle CII, wave 34,
closing the wave and this batch's three-wave run. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry
Campaign (MCD-241). Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The trader had walked eleven days out of her way to say something Kanja hadn't asked to hear, arriving
at The Anvil's current anchorage with a bolt of cloth to sell and, once the trade was settled, a
story she seemed almost reluctant to tell at all. Three valleys past the campaign's own furthest
reach, in a village the Rolling Foundry Campaign's route would never come within a season of, the
local smiths had started doing something she said she'd never seen before she'd heard the Lord of
Embers' name mentioned anywhere.

"They don't call it your method," she said, watching apprentices work a shift change she found
plainer than she'd expected. "They don't call it anything. A bad harvest three years back left half
the district's tools broken and no coin to replace them properly, so the two smiths who were left
just... taught everyone who'd stand still long enough. Farmers. Children. A woman who'd never touched
a forge in her life, mending her own plow now. Nobody paid for the lessons. Nobody kept the knowledge
close." She shrugged, the gesture of someone still not quite sure why she'd walked eleven days to
report an ordinary thing. "I heard your reputation on the road and thought you should know there's a
valley living by half of it that never once needed you to get there."

Kanja sat with that longer than the trade itself had taken. "I didn't teach them anything."

"No. Circumstance taught them, same as it taught you, probably, before anyone gave it a name worth
telling a ballad about." She said it plainly, without unkindness. "I just thought you'd want to know
the shape of the thing doesn't need your shape in it. People find their way to open hands when
they're desperate enough and decent enough, whether or not you ever walk through their district."

He didn't send anyone to confirm it, and didn't claim it for the campaign's own ledger the way every
genuine improvement usually earned a line in Garren Hask's tally. It didn't belong there. What he did,
that evening, working alone at a bench three settlements and eleven days from a place he would in
fact never see, was turn the trader's report over the way he turned over any technique he hadn't
tested with his own hands — not doubting it, just sitting with the plain, slightly unsettling comfort
of a method that had apparently gotten there first, on its own, entirely without him.

"Then maybe the campaign was never the cause of any of it," he said to no one in particular, setting
his tools down for the night. "Maybe it was just the loudest example of something people would have
found their own way to eventually, tools or no tools, name or no name."
