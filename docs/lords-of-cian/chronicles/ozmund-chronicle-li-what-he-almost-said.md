# What He Almost Said

*Locked canon, Batch 306, 2026-09-23 (`MCD-1780`). Ozmund Verehimu Character Chronicle series, Chronicle LI, Draconis strand wave 3.*

---

Most of what the General told me about Draconis, he told me as a story about Draconis. This one he told me as a story about himself, and he told it to me only the once, and only after I had known him long enough that I think he trusted me not to make more of it than it was.

It happened, by his account, on an ordinary night some years past the storm and the watch he'd kept outside my — outside his — chamber door, a night with nothing in it worth remembering on its own terms. No threat had reached the household in weeks. No test had presented itself, set or unset. He'd simply found himself awake past the hour sleep should have taken him, turning over, as he told me he sometimes did without meaning to, the whole long list of what Draconis had done for him across the years he'd known him — the road, the wine, the beam, the coastal command let go without a word — and for the first time, lying there in the dark with nothing urgent enough to interrupt the thought, he let himself ask the question he said he'd spent a lifetime not quite asking.

He wondered whether the silence he'd kept was protection at all, or whether he had simply never wanted to find out what Draconis's loyalty looked like once it knew the truth of what it was being spent on.

He told me he'd always had a ready answer, when the question surfaced in passing — that a man doesn't throw himself between a god and a knife, that Draconis needed to believe the danger was real to keep meeting it the way he met it, that the lie served Draconis more than it served him. He'd told himself that so many times, he said, that it had worn smooth as a river stone, easy to hold without feeling its shape anymore. What kept him awake that particular night was noticing, for what he swore to me was the first time in years, that the answer had never once been tested against the alternative. He had never once let himself imagine actually saying it aloud, watching Draconis's face take the weight of it, and finding out whether the man standing on the other side of that sentence was still the same man who'd have thrown himself between the god and the knife in the first place, or someone worse, or someone lesser, or — worst of all, he said, the possibility he'd never let himself finish — someone exactly the same, proving that he could have said it decades sooner and lost nothing at all.

He told me he understood, turning it over that night, that Draconis himself had asked almost the identical question once, about the beam and the horse and the too-quiet fear that never arrived — whether his own not-looking had been wisdom or cowardice, and had never been able to answer it even confessing it aloud twenty years on. The General said it struck him, lying awake with the same unanswered shape in his own chest, that he had let Draconis carry that private uncertainty alone for a decade without once admitting he carried a matching one of his own, on the other side of the exact same silence.

He didn't decide anything that night. That was the part he was careful to tell me plainly, without dressing it up as some quiet resolution reached in the dark — he lay there with the question fully open in front of him, turned it over as long as his body would let him stay awake to turn it, and in the morning rose and dressed and went down to find Draconis already at his post, and said nothing, and let the ordinary business of the day close back over the whole of it the way it always did. He told me that was the honest end of the story, and that he'd never quite forgiven himself for how easy the closing-over had been.

What he did admit to me, the one time he told it, was the part I think cost him the most to say — that some portion of what he called protection, examined that closely and that late at night, looked uncomfortably like appetite. He wanted, he said, to go on being loved by at least one man as nothing more than himself, uncomplicated by density or blood or scar, for as long as that could still be arranged. He didn't know, he told me, whether a man was allowed to call that mercy when part of what it spared was his own exposure along with Draconis's illusions. He said he'd decided, in the years since, not to require himself to answer that question cleanly. Some debts, he told me, you simply keep paying without ever being certain of the sum.
