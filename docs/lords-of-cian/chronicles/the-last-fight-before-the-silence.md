# The Last Fight Before the Silence

*Locked canon, Batch 224, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1014`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle XLIX, wave 17. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The convoy ran under a merchant's flag, but the ledgers Efa Gol lifted off the lead ship told a
truer story: the captives chained below deck were being sold specifically to fund a Trust general's
private suppression campaign against three Rebellion-aligned coastal towns. No armor for this one.
No coat built to answer for him yet — just the same salt-stiffened jacket he'd worn since Ash-Wharf,
and the Trinity.

He didn't hesitate the way later years would teach him to. Mafesto came off his back already
singing, and Obsidian Malice's first discharge took the deck gun before its crew finished loading it.
Onyx of Oblivion followed him up the companionway unleashed — no seal on it yet, nothing holding it
back from whatever it wanted to be — and Cadence Ruin found the rhythm of six men's footing on wet
planking and broke it all at once, and Veil Piercer went through a locked door the way water goes
through a cracked hull.

Efa Gol found him afterward standing over the ledgers, not the bodies, reading the general's own
signature on the bill of sale.

"You could take this straight to Corren," she said. "Put a name on it. Make it a Rebellion victory
instead of a rumor with no owner."

"It's not that kind of fight." He folded the ledger into his coat. "Bane fights generals. This
fights the men who never show up on a battlefield roster." He looked at his own hand, still faintly
lit where Onyx's edge had been a moment before. "It doesn't feel like this will always answer that
fast."

"Why wouldn't it?"

"I don't know yet." He said it plainly, no drama in it, just an honest gap in his own knowledge.
"Something in me says a day's coming where I won't get to swing it like this and call it simple."

Efa Gol didn't have an answer for that, and didn't pretend to. They freed a hundred and six people
off that convoy before dawn, burned the ledgers where the general's own people would find the ash,
and never once had to raise the name Scourge above a whisper to make it land.
