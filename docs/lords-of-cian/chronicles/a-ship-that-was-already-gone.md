# A Ship That Was Already Gone

*Locked canon, Batch 110, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-377`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias
Chronicle I. Rebellion era, weeks after the Siege of the Ghost Harbor (`MCD-235`, age 21), a fresh
naval encounter distinct from the already-locked battle summary. Not a territory Chronicle. New
standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The Trust patrol boat's lookout swore, afterward, in three separate depositions, that he had watched
the flagship for a full ten minutes before reporting it — not because he'd been derelict, but because
he genuinely could not decide whether he was looking at a real vessel or the kind of thing exhausted
sailors saw on long night watches in waters that had recently swallowed six of the Trust's own
warships.

*The Audit* ran without lantern-light, without a visible crew on deck, without so much as a wake that
behaved the way a wake should — Dol Maren's growing obsession with hull-trim had shaved her draft
down to something that barely disturbed the surface at speed, and in the moonless dark she read, at a
half-mile, as exactly nothing at all.

"Report it," the captain finally ordered, once the lookout's hesitation had cost them the distance
to close. "Ghost ship, real ship, I don't care which. We don't let anything unidentified sit that
close to the patrol line."

By the time their own vessel turned to investigate, *The Audit* was gone — not fled, not fighting,
simply absent from the water where she'd been a minute before, as if the report itself had been the
only thing that ever really proved she was there. The patrol found nothing. No wreckage, no wake, no
trace, and orders from higher command the following week that made no mention of the sighting at
all, because nothing about it could be written into a report that wouldn't read as either incompetence
or superstition.

What none of the patrol crew ever learned was that Kanja had watched their approach from *The
Audit*'s own darkened deck the entire ten minutes, motionless at the rail, waiting to see whether the
lookout's doubt would cost them their nerve before it cost him his position. It did. He gave the order
to come about only once the patrol boat's own hesitation had bought enough water between the two
vessels that the turn would read, to any sailor telling the story afterward, as something closer to
vanishing than retreating.

"You let them see you," the helmsman said quietly, once they were well clear. "Why risk that at
all?"

"Because a ghost nobody's ever seen is a rumor. A ghost somebody's crew swears they saw with their
own eyes, and then couldn't find, is a fear the whole fleet carries home with them." Kanja watched
the patrol boat's lantern shrink astern, unhurried. "Ghost Harbor taught the Trust we could beat
their ships. This teaches them we don't have to. Half of winning a war at sea isn't the fighting.
It's making sure every lookout on every hull from here to the capital starts doubting his own eyes
the second the water goes quiet."
