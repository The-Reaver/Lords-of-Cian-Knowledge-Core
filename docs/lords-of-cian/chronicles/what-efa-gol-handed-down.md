# What Efa Gol Handed Down

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Scourge Alias Chronicle, wave 8. Long Mask era,
age 150, V3 gear (Golden Terror era). Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose. No new named characters.*

---

Efa Gol had run decoy and diversion command on every major engagement since Ash-Wharf, a hundred and
twenty-eight years of reading a crowd's panic and turning it into a shape the crew could use, and she
told him plainly, the morning after the Salt Keep's harbor siege, that this had been her last one.

"Not because I can't," she said, before he could ask, because she knew he would. "Because I've started
noticing the half-second where I used to just know which way a crowd was going to break, and now I
have to think about it first. Half a second doesn't sound like much until it's the half-second
somebody needed."

"You've never been wrong that I've seen."

"I've never let you see me be wrong. That's not the same thing." She said it without self-pity, the
way she'd always reported anything, fact stated and set down. "I'm not asking you to talk me out of
it. I'm telling you because the decoy command needs a clean handoff, not a gap nobody noticed until
it cost someone."

He didn't argue the point, which she seemed to have expected and, from the small easing in her
shoulders, appreciated more than an argument would have. "Who takes it?"

"Already chosen. Already trained, longer than you've noticed, because I started this handoff two
years ago without telling you, on purpose, so you'd never have to watch me decide I wasn't sure
anymore in the middle of something that mattered." She allowed herself something close to a smile.
"I wasn't going to let this be a scene. Just a fact, told to you once, at a time I picked."

"A hundred and twenty-eight years."

"A hundred and twenty-eight years of reading fear well enough to spend it on purpose, instead of
letting it spend us." She looked out at the harbor, still smoking faintly from the night before, the
same water she'd stood beside once with a ruined coat and a man who hadn't yet decided what any of
this was going to cost him. "I read that crowd right the first night too, for what it's worth. I just
didn't know yet it was the first of so many."

"It mattered every time since."

"I know." She turned to go, already handing the morning's remaining logistics to hands that weren't
his to manage, exactly as she'd planned. "That's why I'm the one telling you it's done, instead of
leaving you to find out the hard way that it should have been."
