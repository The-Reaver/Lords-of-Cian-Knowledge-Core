# The Trial They Held Without Him

*Locked canon, Batch 254, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1112`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXXXII, wave 28. Direct
extension of the arbiter-council thread -- for the first time Bane deliberately absents himself from
an institutional proceeding he could dominate, letting the council's own process stand on its own.*

---

The council that had ruled on the Directorate's fabricated atrocity claims six weeks earlier was
convening again, this time to try a captured collaborator accused of feeding Directorate patrols the
locations of three safehouses — a case with real evidence, real witnesses, and a verdict that would
matter to people well beyond the room it was decided in.

Corren Halst assumed, packing for it, that Bane would attend. Bane told him otherwise the night
before.

"Last time I was in that room, I watched what happened to the questions people were willing to ask
once I was sitting in it. They asked me things directly instead of asking each other. They deferred to
whatever I said was fair instead of working out what fair meant themselves. The ruling that came out
of it was good. I don't know how much of that was the council's own judgment and how much of it was
mine, borrowed through a room I was standing in."

"You want a worse verdict just to prove they can reach one without you?"

"I want a real one. If it's worse, I'll live with that being what the process actually produces on
its own. If it's the same, then it was never mine to begin with, and I'll know that for certain
instead of guessing at it."

He sent Halst in his place, with instructions to observe only, offer nothing unless directly asked a
factual question about the operation itself, and report the result back without editorializing on
whether it was right.

The council convicted the collaborator on two of the three counts, acquitted on the third for
insufficient evidence — a genuinely mixed verdict, not the clean outcome a room deferring to Bane's own
sense of the case might have produced.

"They argued for four hours on the third count," Halst reported. "Nobody looked to me for a ruling.
They just argued it out themselves until they had an answer they actually believed."

"Then it's real," Bane said. "That's worth more to me than getting the outcome I might have pushed
them toward if I'd been standing in the room."
