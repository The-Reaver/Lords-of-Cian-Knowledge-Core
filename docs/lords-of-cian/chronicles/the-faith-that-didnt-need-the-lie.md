# The Faith That Didn't Need the Lie

*Locked canon, Batch 261, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1300`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LXXXI, wave 27, closing the wave. The priest reconciles faith and the doctrine's mundane finding without losing his congregation. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

Kanja expected to leave the settlement without ever learning how the congregation took the news. He was three days gone before a rider caught up with him, carrying a message from the priest rather than a summons.

*The repair is being redone properly, matched to the original joinery this time. The congregation asked why the crack had appeared after all these centuries, and I told them the truth exactly as you gave it to me — that the original work was extraordinary, and a later, lesser repair had finally worn through. Nobody left over it. If anything, more of them came to see the original stone once they understood what they were actually looking at.*

He read the rest of it twice before folding it away.

*I spent a night deciding whether I could live with what your hand might find under that pillar, and I want you to know what I've decided since: I was afraid the honest answer would cost us something. It cost us a comfortable story we'd told ourselves without ever really examining it. What it gave back was a congregation that now understands, in a way they never fully did before, exactly how remarkable the people who raised this temple actually were — and a repair, going up properly this time, built to last as long as the honest thing it's protecting.*

Kanja carried the letter longer than he usually kept correspondence, turning the priest's own reasoning over the way he might once have turned over a structure's tension signature.

"You look like you're still reading something," the second student said, catching him at it days later.

"I am. Just not with my hands, this time." He passed her the letter. "I've spent years believing the honest answer always costs something, even when it's the right thing to give. This is the first time I've had proof in writing that sometimes it doesn't cost what everyone was afraid it would — it just costs the comfortable version people had mistaken for the truth, and gives back something sturdier once they've had a chance to actually look at what was there the whole time."

"Do you think that's always true? That honesty costs less than people fear?"

"No. I've buried people whose honest answers cost exactly what everyone was afraid they would, and I'm not going to pretend this one letter undoes that." Kanja folded the message away again, careful with it. "But I think I needed this one. A reminder that the doctrine isn't only a thing that tells people what's about to fail. Sometimes it just tells them, plainly, that something's held up longer and better than they ever gave it credit for — and that's worth knowing too, even when nothing was ever actually in danger of falling."
