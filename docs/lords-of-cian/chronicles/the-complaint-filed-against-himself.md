# The Complaint Filed Against Himself

*Locked canon, Batch 257, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1197`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle LXXXVI, wave 29. The workers' council files a formal grievance against Kanja over a unilateral crisis decision -- a real test of whether the institution's authority actually holds against him.*

---

The grievance came through proper channels, which was precisely what made it land as hard as it did. Kanja had ordered a gallery cleared during the vapor-hazard evacuation weeks back without waiting for the council's own emergency protocol to convene, a decision that had saved twenty-one lives but had also, procedurally, ignored the exact chain of authority the council existed to establish.

"You want to file a complaint," he said, reading the formal notice Garren Hask had delivered without comment, "because I moved fast enough to keep people alive."

"We want to know if the council actually means anything," the representative who'd brought it said, not unkindly. "If you can override it whenever the stakes feel high enough to you personally, it's not authority. It's your permission, dressed up as ours."

He could have argued the outcome settled the question -- twenty-one people alive was twenty-one people alive, and no process violation changed that math. He didn't make that argument, because he recognized, turning it over, that it was exactly the kind of self-serving shortcut he'd spent the entire alias refusing to take everywhere else. An institution that bent every time its own founder decided the stakes justified it wasn't actually independent of him at all.

He submitted to the process rather than defending himself outside it, appeared before the full council at the scheduled hearing, and made no attempt to use his own reputation as an argument for leniency. He described the decision plainly, the time pressure that had genuinely existed, and didn't dispute that he'd bypassed the protocol they'd built specifically to prevent unilateral calls in exactly this kind of moment.

The council debated for two full days. The ruling, when it came, wasn't punitive -- they found the underlying decision sound given the actual timeline, but formally reprimanded the bypass itself, and wrote a faster emergency-authorization clause into the protocol so that speed and process wouldn't have to trade against each other next time.

"You could have fought this," Hask said afterward, reading the ruling with him.

"I could have. It would have proven their protocol only applies when I feel like letting it." Kanja set the ruling aside, satisfied in a way that had nothing to do with being vindicated. "This proves something better. That it applies to me exactly the same as everyone else, even when I'm the one who built half of what it's judging."
