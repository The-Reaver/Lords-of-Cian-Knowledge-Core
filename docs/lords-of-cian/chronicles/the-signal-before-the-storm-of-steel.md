# The Signal Before the Storm of Steel

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Crow King Alias Chronicle XXXV, wave 12, second
entry. Rebellion era, roughly three and a half years after the Night of the Crow King (`MCD-236`,
age 23). Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material — a detailed Trinity combat showcase set
up by planned deception rather than forced by its failure. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.
No new named characters.*

---

The engagement at Kessin Ridge was never meant to be won by trickery alone — the position was too
heavily held, the garrison too well dug in, for any false signal to empty it the way the Wetlands or
Coalgate had emptied theirs. This time the deception and the battle were planned together from the
start, one operation with two connected halves, the Hymn-Engine's only job to move the garrison's
reserves somewhere they couldn't reach the actual fight in time.

Three nights of seeded reports convinced the garrison commander that a second, larger force was
massing on his eastern flank, drawing nearly half his reserve strength away from the ridge itself
before a single blade was drawn.

When the real assault came, it came openly — no illusion covering it, no false signature masking its
approach, just Kanja and the Trinity meeting the ridge's remaining defenders directly, Mafesto's
Kinetic Transfer System absorbing and redirecting the momentum of a downhill charge back into the
defenders' own line, Obsidian Malice discharging in short, controlled bursts to break formation
rather than overwhelm it outright, Onyx of Oblivion's Veil Piercer finding the seams in a shield wall
already off-balance from the redirected force breaking against it.

"Half their reserve is two ridgelines away chasing a company that doesn't exist," the crew's own
field commander reported, watching the line buckle under weight it had never been built to hold
alone. "This isn't a rescue from a failed plan. This was always the plan."

It was. The ridge fell in under an hour, the garrison's remaining defenders overwhelmed by a force
they'd been left, deliberately, without the numbers to properly meet — the deception's only purpose
never to avoid the fight at all, but to guarantee the fight, when it came, was one the Trinity could
win cleanly rather than grind through at unnecessary cost.

"People assume the Hymn-Engine exists to avoid battle," Kanja said afterward, surveying a field that
had cost the crew remarkably little for what it had taken. "Sometimes it does. Tonight it existed to
choose exactly which battle actually happened, and make sure it was one we could win without paying
more than we needed to."
