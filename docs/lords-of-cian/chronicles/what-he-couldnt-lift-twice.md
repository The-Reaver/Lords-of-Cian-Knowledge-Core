# What He Couldn't Lift Twice

*Locked canon, Batch 279, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1456`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XCIX, wave 33, closing the wave. The alias's first entry making Kanja himself subject to his own established fatigue rule rather than the one imposing it on others.*

---

His arms told him first, a fine tremor he mistook for cold until he noticed the tunnel around him was warm enough to sweat in. He'd been three days without real sleep, running triage between the frost repairs, the dry well, and a support timber that had started groaning somewhere he hadn't gotten to yet, and it was the fourth crisis in as many days that finally found the seam in him rather than in the rock.

The beam went up on the first lift clean enough. It was the second lift -- shifting it six inches to seat properly against the new post -- that his grip failed on, just for a half-second, just long enough for the beam to drop back half an inch before he caught it again.

"Set it down," Corren Halst said, already at his shoulder, voice flat in the way that meant she wasn't asking.

"I've got it."

"You had it. For half a second there, you didn't." She didn't reach for the beam herself, didn't try to wrestle it from him -- she knew better than that. She simply stood where he could see her and waited, and it was Danne Sok arriving a moment later, reading the same tremor in his forearms that Corren Halst had already caught, who finally said the thing that landed.

"You made a rule after the night crew nearly lost two men to exhaustion nobody caught in time," Danne Sok said. "You wrote it into how this crew runs. You didn't write an exception into it for yourself."

Kanja set the beam down, properly this time, and stood with both hands braced against the post for a moment longer than the work required.

"I know the rule," he said.

"Knowing it and living under it aren't the same thing, and you of all people should know that better than anyone." Corren Halst nodded toward the tunnel mouth. "Three days. You'd have pulled anyone else off rotation after one."

It would have been easy to argue that he wasn't like anyone else on the rotation -- that his own capacity ran further before it broke, that the crises hadn't waited for him to finish resting between them, that someone still needed to be the one holding all four problems in his head at once. None of it would have been dishonest. All of it would have been exactly the kind of exception he'd refused to grant anyone else, on exactly the grounds he'd refused to accept from them.

"You're right," he said instead. "Log it the same as you'd log anyone else. I'm off rotation until the rest catches up to the rule."

He slept nine hours, the longest unbroken stretch since the frost had first started cracking timber, and returned to find the beam properly seated and the support timber's groan already traced and fixed by a crew that hadn't needed him standing over it to manage without him.

"How does it feel," Danne Sok asked, "being the exception you finally didn't make for yourself?"

"Honestly? It feels like I should have written the rule that way from the start." Kanja checked the newly seated beam with both hands, steady now, and found nothing wrong with it. "A rule that only binds everyone but the man who wrote it was never actually a safety rule. It was just an order with better manners."
