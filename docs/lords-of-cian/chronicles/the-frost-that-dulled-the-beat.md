# The Frost That Dulled the Beat

*Locked canon, Batch 282, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1478`). Crow King Alias Chronicle XCIV, wave 32, opening
it. A blizzard's cold numbs the fourth generation's hands mid-operation, degrading the tap-signal
channel for the first time. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The blizzard had been the point. A highland labor camp, three watchtowers, and a storm loud enough
to bury a chant under its own wind -- the fourth generation had planned around the cold, not against
it, counting on the wind to carry a false relief-column report to the nearest tower before the guards
could confirm it by eye. What he hadn't planned for was his own hand.

Two hours into the crossing, tapping the relay pattern against his own forearm the way he'd done a
thousand times, he felt the fourth strike land wrong -- not late, not early, just *thick*, a half-beat
smeared into the one behind it because his fingers had stopped answering him with any precision the
cold hadn't already taken. He tapped it again. It came out worse.

He didn't call for help. He held still a moment, the way the apprentice had taught him to hold still
before deciding anything, and thought about what the pattern was actually for rather than what it was
made of: not the exact rhythm, but the fact that someone thirty yards downwind needed to know, in the
next ten seconds, which of two watchtowers to move past. He simplified everything the cold had ruined
down to the one bit it couldn't ruin -- a single long press instead of three short ones, left tower
instead of right -- and pressed it against the relay runner's shoulder hard enough to feel through two
coats.

It worked. It also nearly didn't, and everyone in the chain knew it by the time the last of the
prisoners were moving free of the camp's fence line under cover of the storm's own noise.

Back at the treeline, Kanja found him still flexing his hand, embarrassed more than hurt. "You held
the line," Kanja said.

"I got lucky the pattern could survive being wrong that badly."

"Then we stop building patterns that can't survive that." Kanja crouched by the fire only long enough
to warm his own hands before he put them back in his gloves. "Voice can fail. Hearing can fail. We
already know that the hard way, both of us. We never once asked what happens when the hands fail too,
because we never needed a signal simple enough to survive numb fingers -- until tonight."

By the time the crew reached the next camp two days later, the fourth generation had already drafted
a second, cruder layer underneath every pattern in current use: one long, one short, meaning nothing
more specific than *danger* and *clear* -- ugly, imprecise, and built to work even through a hand that
could no longer manage anything finer.
