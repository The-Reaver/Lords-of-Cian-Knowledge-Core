They put the survivors in the tannery two streets over, because it was the largest roof still standing that the Trust column hadn't bothered to walk past twice, and because it smelled of something other than smoke, which mattered more than anyone wanted to admit out loud. Daba sat against a vat with Mika asleep in his lap and eleven other children arranged around him like spokes, and did not sleep, and watched a woman he half-recognized from the rent office set up a ledger table out of two barrels and a door.

Her name was Odalys. She had kept the Rookery's rolls for eleven years — who owed what, who'd paid ahead, whose children were whose when the fathers weren't around to say — and she had brought the ledger out with her, he learned later, not because she'd thought to save it but because it had been in her hands when the boots came up the stair and she had simply never put it down. Now she had it open on the door-table under a lamp, and she was doing the only thing she knew how to do with a night like this one, which was writing it down correctly.

"Names," she said, to no one and to everyone, in a voice that had already gone hoarse from repeating it. "I need names before I need anything else from you. Whoever you lost. However you knew them — first name, house-name, what they were called by the little ones if that's all you have. I will not have anyone in this building go into a Trust ledger as a number before they go into mine as a person."

Daba gave her Fennic's name first, and his house-name, and the fact that the younger children called him Uncle Ember because he was always warm even in winter, and Odalys wrote all three lines without once looking up, her hand steady in a way his was not. Then he sat beside her, because it seemed like the only useful thing left to do with his hands, and took names from the others as they came — an old woman missing her whole floor, a boy missing a twin, a man who could only say *the family in 4C, all of them, I don't know if I ever knew all their names* and had to be told, gently, by Odalys, that *the family in 4C* was itself a kind of name and would go in the book exactly that way rather than be left out for want of a better one.

It took until past midnight to run out of survivors willing to speak, and longer than that to run out of names remembered by more than one person, cross-checked so no one was written twice and no one was left off because grief had made two accounts of the same person sound like different people. Somewhere in the small hours a runner came back from what remained of the Rookery's own foundation, where a second team had been counting what the fire had left countable, and gave Odalys a figure in a voice too flat to be anything but exhausted past feeling.

"Eighteen hundred and four," she said, reading it back once to be sure her own hand hadn't slipped, and then again, quieter, as if the second time might make it settle differently in the chest. It didn't. Daba felt it land the way the water in the cistern had landed — total, immediate, no part of him left dry by it — and understood, without yet having words for the understanding, that the number was not going to stay a number. It already had Fennic's face in it, and Uncle Ember's warmth, and the family in 4C who deserved better than an apartment for a name.

Mika stirred in his lap and asked, half-asleep, if the game was still going. He told her yes. He told her it had just changed the rules.
