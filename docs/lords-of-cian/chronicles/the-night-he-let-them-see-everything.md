# The Night He Let Them See Everything

(`MCD-235`). Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material — an inversion of the alias's usual
concealment doctrine. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The village had been burned twice already that year by raiders who'd learned the coast had gone
largely undefended, and its people had reached the specific, brittle state of terror where every dark
shape on the horizon — friend, enemy, or driftwood — read as the third burning finally arriving.

*The Audit* approached at dusk, and for the first time in the fleet's entire operational history, Kanja
gave an order that ran directly against every instinct the reputation had been built on.

"Full lanterns," he said. "Every one we have. Let them see exactly what we are before they have time to
be afraid of what we might be."

Dol Maren hesitated. "We've spent two years teaching people to fear the dark shape they can't identify.
You want to give this village the opposite lesson."

"This village doesn't need another dark shape. They've had two burnings' worth of dark shapes already.
What they need is to watch, in full light, a ship arrive that isn't here to hurt them." Kanja was
already moving to the rail, visible, unhidden, deliberately abandoning every trick that had kept the
fleet a rumor rather than a fact. "The mystery serves us against people trying to hurt others. It
doesn't serve anyone who's already been hurt enough."

They came ashore in daylight the following morning, weapons sheathed, gear stripped down to its most
unthreatening form, and spent three days in full view — helping rebuild what the second burning had
taken, standing watch openly rather than from the concealment the reputation usually relied on,
letting every villager who wanted to see exactly who and what had come to help do so without the fear
that usually accompanied any sighting of the fleet.

"You could have just told us you meant no harm," the village's eldest said, watching the work continue
on the third day. "Instead you let us see everything. Your ship. Your faces. Whatever it is you
actually are."

"Because 'trust me' means nothing to people who've had every reason not to trust anyone claiming to be
here to help. Seeing means something." Kanja set down a beam he'd been carrying, palms raw from three
days of ordinary labor. "The fear we teach the people trying to hurt villages like yours only works
because it's real, earned, specific to them. You've had enough fear. What you needed from us was the
opposite — something plain and visible and boring enough to actually believe."

They left before the fourth day, reputation fully intact for anyone still trying to hurt the coast, and
one small village left with the rare, specific knowledge of exactly what the ghost fleet looked like in
full daylight, unhidden, doing nothing more dramatic than rebuilding a wall.
