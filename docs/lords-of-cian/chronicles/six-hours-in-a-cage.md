# Six Hours in a Cage

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Bane Alias Chronicle XX, second entry in the seventh
wave. Rebellion era, age 19, months after the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Nobody outside the crew's innermost planning knew that Bane let himself be taken at the Corrow
holding station — that the "capture" the Directorate reported with such rare, genuine triumph was a
door he'd chosen to walk through, restraints he'd chosen not to break, a transport crate he'd
chosen to sit inside for six hours while it carried him exactly where he wanted to be taken.

The holding station kept its interrogation logs in a records room two corridors from the transport
bay. That was the whole of the plan. Everything else — the restraints, the six hours, the risk of a
guard deciding to do more than the standing orders permitted a valuable prisoner — was simply the
cost of reaching a room no ordinary approach would have opened.

"You're very calm for a man in a cage," the transport officer said, midway through the third hour,
unsettled by exactly how calm.

"I'm not in the part of this that worries me yet." Bane didn't move against the restraints, didn't
test them, gave the officer nothing to report except stillness. "That part comes later, and it isn't
going to involve you, so I wouldn't spend the next three hours dreading it if I were you."

The officer reported that exchange up the chain that night as evidence of a prisoner either
remarkably disciplined or not actually afraid, and the report sat in a stack of similarly unhelpful
observations while, two corridors away, the records room's night watch found himself persuaded — by
means the official account never fully reconstructed — to leave a door unlatched for the length of
one unsupervised hour. By the time anyone thought to check the transport crate, it held nothing but
cut restraints and a station's worth of interrogation logs missing exactly the pages that mattered.

The Directorate never publicly admitted the capture had been anything but a capture. Internally, the
after-action review split cleanly in two: half the reviewers insisted a six-hour restraint could not
plausibly have been chosen rather than imposed, and half quietly suspected — without evidence they
were willing to put their names to — that they had never held him at all, only been shown, for six
hours, exactly what he wanted them to believe they were holding.

"Six hours," Garren Hask said afterward, tallying the retrieved logs against what the crew had
already known and hadn't been able to prove. "That's a long time to sit still and trust a plan you
didn't fully control the timing of."

"It wasn't the sitting still that was hard." Bane handed over the last of the pages. "It was letting
them believe, for six hours, that they had me. I don't like giving anyone that, even as a lie."
