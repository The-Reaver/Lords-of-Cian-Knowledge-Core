Orsk Dresk's cell had not gone looking for a Weregildd Assessor. They had gone looking for a
recruiter working the Half-Ford road too close to territory 1804 had quietly held for years, the
same kind of boundary violation that had already put the network at arm's-length odds with
Weregildd culling teams more than once before, for reasons that had nothing to do with debt and
everything to do with bloodlines the network had separately sworn to protect.

What they found instead, three weeks into tracing the recruiter's own instructions back through a
chain of intermediaries, was a debt.

Not one debt. Dozens, then hundreds, then a paper trail thick enough that Orsk stopped counting
individual loans and started counting ruined holds instead -- small mercenary houses and struggling
minor estates, each one extended credit at terms that looked generous on the page and worked, in
practice, exactly the way a slow-closing trap works, until the debtor had nothing left to offer the
Writedown Compact but bodies. Every thread led to the same patient, unhurried name behind the paper:
Vex Thurlow.

Daba read the full accounting Orsk sent back and recognized, with the particular discomfort of
recognizing something in an enemy, a method that was not so different in its patience from his own.

"He thinks the slowness is mercy," Daba said to Kether, who had come to review the find with him,
since a target inside the Weregildd's own command structure was not a decision either of them would
make without the other's eyes on it first. "A debt a person takes on willingly, one loan at a time,
feels like a choice to the person taking it. He's built an entire career on the fact that it stops
feeling like a choice long after it's too late to matter."

"So we kill him."

"No." Daba said it plainly, the way he said most things that cost him something to say. "If we kill
him, the Compact appoints a replacement inside a season and loses nothing but a name. If we hand him
to the Trust, the Trust has no institutional interest in prosecuting a man who only ever ruins people
quietly enough not to be their problem. Either way, the paper trail survives him. We need the trail
broken, not the man."

What Daba proposed instead, and what Orsk's cell carried out six weeks later with no fanfare and no
public account of any kind, was handing Vex Thurlow -- alive, unharmed, and stripped of every
document his operation depended on -- to a rival criminal power already hostile to the Writedown
Compact's whole territory. What that power did with him afterward, 1804 never asked and never
recorded. The debts he had written stayed on the books of houses that could not easily unwrite them,
a cost Daba did not pretend had been solved rather than merely redirected.

"It isn't clean," he told Kether afterward, adding no name to any list, because no one had died, and
filing the operation instead under the growing category of things the doctrine answered only
partway. "It never is, with debt. You can break the hand that's writing it. You can't un-sign what's
already been signed."
