# The Council That Asked Him to Speak Plainly

*Locked canon, Batch 215, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-939`). Bane Alias Chronicle LV, wave 19. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The Merrow Compact had stayed neutral through two years of war by never taking a position on
either side, and it kept that neutrality by insisting on a rule the Directorate had learned to
respect and the rebellion had learned to work around: any accusation brought before its cross-
border arbiter council had to be presented by the accuser in person, under questioning, with no
document allowed to stand in for a living witness willing to answer for it.

The accusation this time was against Bane himself — three settlements, all Directorate-aligned,
claiming atrocities in his name that Hask's own careful ledger showed no record of, because they
hadn't happened, or claiming credit for restraint he'd genuinely shown while omitting what had
provoked it. The council's summons didn't accuse him outright. It asked him to come explain
himself, which amounted to the same risk from a man who'd built two years of reputation on never
needing to explain anything to anyone.

He went anyway, unarmed past the chamber door, Onyx left with Halst outside it — the council's rule
applied to everyone equally, and he wasn't going to ask for an exception the accused parties
hadn't been granted either.

Six arbiters, none aligned with either side of the war, asked him questions for the better part of
a day. He answered the ones about what had happened plainly, including the parts that reflected
poorly — the recruit expelled for cruelty at the column's own hands, the promise broken at
Aldren's Reach that had cost four lives, the delay at a prisoner liberation that had cost two more.
He didn't offer those unprompted, and he didn't shade them softer when asked. What he refused,
twice, was to answer for actions the record showed he'd had no part in at all, no matter how the
questioning tried to fold them into a single pattern.

"You're asking me to account for things that never happened so the things that did happen sound
smaller by comparison," he said to the presiding arbiter, the only moment his voice carried any
real edge. "I'll answer for what's true. I won't help you build a version of me that's easier to
either condemn or forgive."

The council's ruling, delivered three days later, credited the ledger over the accusations and
closed the matter formally — not an exoneration exactly, since the Compact never offered those, but
a record, now standing on its own, that outlasted the accusation that had prompted it.
