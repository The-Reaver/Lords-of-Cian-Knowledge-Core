# Lauris Chronicle LXXI: The World Is Below Me

*Locked canon, Batch 300, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1688`). Seventy-first entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, the fourteenth entry of Strand K and the closing entry of this wave's departure
sequence. Set at age ~4,000, in the final weeks and hours before the departure already locked at
`MCD-174` -- the journey to and arrival at the Vask Olmedrin orbital trade-point, the gathering of
roughly 1,200 Karesian women to see her off, and her own final recorded line on departing Kares
Prime, already locked at `MCD-1558` ("The world is below me. It will be below me for the rest of my
life. I will continue."), placed in its full original context for the first time. Ends at the
literal threshold of departure without depicting the journey to Cian itself, which `MCD-175` already
covers directly. Reuses Serath, already locked (`MCD-1636`), now Olmedrin's senior training
authority. No new named characters. No contradictions.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Vask Olmedrin, age ~4,000: "The world is below me. It will be below me for the
rest of my life. I will continue."*

I have chosen to close this entire account of her departure the way she herself closed it — with the
line already preserved elsewhere in this family's record, though I do not think its full weight can
be felt without everything that led to it, which is why I have spent six entries, now, building the
road to this single afternoon.

The journey from Karth-Ven to Olmedrin took the better part of three weeks, most of it on foot and by
the same mountain passages she had walked as a fourteen-year-old girl relocated for training she did
not yet understand she would need. She traveled alone for the first two weeks, by her own choice —
Veska had offered an escort, which Lauris declined, saying only that she wished to see the world she
was leaving one final time at the pace a person actually walks through it, rather than the pace at
which a civilization moves someone it has decided is important. She stopped, her archive records, at
each of the twelve Vasks in turn where the route allowed it, not for formal farewell — those, mostly,
were already spent — but simply to stand for an hour in a place that had mattered to her, and then to
keep walking.

*

Vask Olmedrin itself she had visited only once before in any depth, some twelve hundred years
earlier, in the aftermath of the raid that produced the highest single-engagement density any
Karesian has ever documented. She arrived this time to find the smallest of the twelve surviving
Vasks transformed, for the span of a single day, into something it had likely never been in its
entire operational history: the site of a gathering the Iron-Speakers' own records describe, without
apparent exaggeration, as nearly a tenth of the civilization's remaining population in one place at
one time.

Serath met her at the trade-point's outer gate — the young combatant Lauris had trained at Karth-Ven
some six hundred years before, now the Vask's own senior training authority, standing where her
teacher had once stood to defend the same ground. Their exchange, Lauris's archive notes, was brief,
almost businesslike, which I have come to understand is precisely how the two of them had always
spoken to each other, warmth conducted at low volume across long silences rather than through
declaration. Serath asked only whether she was ready. Lauris's answer, characteristically: *I do not
know that readiness is the correct measure for a thing that has never happened to anyone before. I
know that I have finished everything I set out to finish here. I think that will have to serve in
its place.*

*

I have tried, transcribing the gathering itself, to convey some sense of its scale without
overstating what her own restrained account permits. Roughly twelve hundred women, by the
Iron-Speakers' final count — stonework-guild members from Threnarr, agricultural workers from
Aldreth, combat instructors from Karth-Ven, archivists from Vask-of-Vasks, representatives from
every one of the twelve surviving Vasks that could spare the journey — gathered across Olmedrin's
modest trade-point grounds to witness something none of them, nor any Karesian before them in the
civilization's recorded history, had ever witnessed: a member of their own species, by her own
choice and with her people's own consent after three hundred and eighty years of deliberation,
departing the homeworld for good.

There was no formal ceremony, by Lauris's own account, in the sense the word usually implies. The
Iron-Speaker who had first raised the strongest objection to her departure, three hundred and eighty
years earlier, spoke last among the assembled Council, and her words — preserved by three
independent witnesses closely enough that I trust them as near-exact — are the ones I have chosen to
carry this account toward its close: *We have sent nothing beyond this world in longer than any of us
can measure. We send you now not because we believe you will return, though we hope, without
expectation, that you might. We send you because you are the truest thing this civilization has left
to say about itself, and we find, having argued the question for longer than most of us have been
alive, that we would rather the last thing we ever say be spoken by you, in a language none of us
will ever hear, than never be said at all.*

*

Lauris's own final act on Kares Prime, before she boarded the departing vessel, was to open her
archive one last time — the same archive I have now spent this entire chronicle drawing from — and
write the entry that closes this account, and, in its way, this whole long era of her life. I have
nothing further to add to it. I do not believe it needs anything added.

*The world is below me. It will be below me for the rest of my life. I will continue.*

She kept that promise for approximately two thousand years before making planetfall on Cian, and has
kept it, in every meaningful sense, in every year since.
