# The Song They Sang Without Knowing Whose It Was

*Locked canon, Batch 254, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1108`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXXVIII, wave 26, closing
wave 26. Refugee children turn garbled accounts of Bane into a marching song, unaware the man walking
near the back of the column is its subject -- a quiet, unprompted legend-drift closer.*

---

Nobody had taught the children the song. It had assembled itself the way those things did, out of
half-heard adult conversation and the particular logic children brought to filling in the parts of a
story that didn't make sense to them yet — a rhyme about a man made of shadow and iron who walked
ahead of the frightened and behind the safe, who never slept and never lied and never once raised his
voice above what was needed.

None of the children singing it, strung out along the column's rear where the youngest walked with
the slowest adults, knew that the man three ranks ahead of them, carrying a pack no heavier than
anyone else's, was the one they meant.

Danne Sok noticed it before Bane did, and watched him for a long stretch afterward to see whether it
would bother him — the sort of quiet, unglamorous embarrassment that struck some people harder than
open fear ever had. It didn't seem to. Bane walked the whole distance without turning around, without
correcting a single garbled detail, without doing anything at all to indicate he'd heard it.

"They've got half of it wrong," Danne Sok said eventually, once they'd made camp and the song had
trailed off into ordinary evening noise. "You don't walk behind the safe. You walk wherever the
column needs someone walking."

"It's not wrong. It's just not describing me. It's describing what they needed to believe was walking
with them on a road this long, for children who don't have the years yet to carry the actual shape of
what's protecting them." Bane looked back toward where the singing had come from. "The truth's mine
to carry. That song isn't. It's theirs. It'll do more good staying exactly as wrong as it is than it
would if I fixed it to match me."

"You could tell them. Just once. Let them know."

"And take away the only version of it that actually fits in a child's chest on a long walk, to give
them one that doesn't?" Bane shook his head. "No. Let them have the one that works."
