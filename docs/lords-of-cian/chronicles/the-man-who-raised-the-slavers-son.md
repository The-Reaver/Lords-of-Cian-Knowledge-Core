# The Man Who Raised the Slaver's Son

*Locked canon, Batch 281, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1470`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle XCV, wave 32. Age 120,
V3 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The overseer had died the way most of them died who chose to fight rather than surrender: fast, and
by his own decision, drawing a blade against Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System rather than open the
compound's holds when he was given the chance to. Forty-three captives came out of the ground beneath
his depot before morning. What no one had accounted for, in the count Garren Hask kept that night, was
the boy — six years old, the overseer's own son, asleep in the depot's attached house when his father
died a hundred feet away and no one thought to tell him until the sun was already up.

There was no ledger entry for what happened to the children of the men who ran these places. The crew
had freed captives for ninety-eight years without once needing one, because the question had never
come up in a form that mattered: usually there was somewhere for a slaver's family to go, someone
willing to take them in on the strength of not being the ones who'd done anything. This time there
wasn't. The boy had no mother anyone could find, no other relative within three days' ride, and a
settlement full of newly freed people with every reason in the world not to want him.

Kanja didn't decide it that night, or the next. He spent four days in the settlement under no
disguise at all worth the name, asking the quietest questions he knew how to ask, before an unnamed
fisherman with two grown daughters and a wife who'd been one of the forty-three said, without being
pushed toward it, that the boy could stay with them if no one minded. Kanja never told the fisherman
whose son he was taking in, though the fisherman likely suspected; some things were kinder left
unconfirmed. He left money in the family's care that no one but Garren Hask ever traced back to him,
and he never returned to check how the boy turned out.

He told Efa Gol, on the crossing out, that he didn't know if he'd done right by anyone — not the boy,
not the fisherman's family, not the forty-three who might reasonably ask why a slaver's son got a home
built for him out of the same night that freed them. Efa Gol had no answer that made it simpler. Some
debts, she told him, don't resolve into a number Hask can write down. This one went into the ledger
anyway, its outcome left permanently blank.
