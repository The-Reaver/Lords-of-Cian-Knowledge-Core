# The Season He Couldn't Slow Down

*Locked canon, Batch 256, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1170`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle LXXXVI, wave 29. Institutional friction under genuine scarcity: districts requesting the crew queue faster than they can be processed, forcing a triage the method has never had to practice before.*

---

By the middle of that season, word of the method had spread past anywhere Kanja and Ezio could reach in person. Requests arrived faster than the crew's own pace could answer them — messengers from six different districts inside a single week, each with a case that sounded, on the description alone, as urgent and as worthy as any the crew had ever taken. For two years the method's own limitation had simply been the honest four-day pace of doing each case right. This was a different kind of limit entirely: not how fast any single case could be built, but how many of them could exist in the crew's future at once before something had to be chosen over something else.

Ezio wanted to try answering every request in the order it arrived, a simple, defensible rule that treated every district equally. Kanja rejected it within an hour of watching the messenger pile grow. "Equal order isn't the same as equal fairness. A district that can survive waiting eight weeks and a district that can't don't need the same answer just because they both asked."

What they built, working through the six requests together, was the method's first formal triage — criteria Ezio wrote down explicitly rather than deciding case by case on instinct alone: documented medical urgency first, evidence of active retaliation against testifiers second, scale of the workforce affected third, then simple order of arrival for anything left equally weighted after the first three cuts. It wasn't a system either of them loved. Two of the six districts, judged against the criteria honestly, would wait considerably longer than they'd hoped, for reasons the crew explained to their messengers directly rather than leaving them guessing.

"We're choosing who waits," Ezio said, uncomfortable with the plainness of it. "That's not something the method's ever asked us to do before."

"It's asking us to do it now, whether we like the shape of it or not. Pretending we can serve everyone at once just means we serve whoever shouts loudest, or whoever's messenger happens to reach us first — that's not fairness either, it's an accident wearing fairness's clothes." The criteria held up under real strain across the following months as more requests arrived than the crew could ever fully answer — imperfect, openly acknowledged as imperfect, but consistent, explained honestly to every district it delayed, and revisited whenever a delayed district's own situation worsened enough to move it up the list.

It didn't make Ezio comfortable with the work. What it did, he said months later, looking back over a season that had tested the method's patience more than any single fraud ever had, was keep the crew's own choices as honest as the numbers they demanded from everyone else.
