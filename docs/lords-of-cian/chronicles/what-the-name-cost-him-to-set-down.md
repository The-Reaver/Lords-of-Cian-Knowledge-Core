# What the Name Cost Him to Set Down

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Bane Alias Chronicle XLV, closing the fifteenth wave
and the fifteen-wave arc. Rebellion era, age 20, roughly eighteen months after the Black Trench. Not
a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

He sat alone the night after the Corrow ravine engagement, the column finally asleep, the wounded
stable, the name that had carried him since the fog outside the first ravine wall already beginning,
quietly and without ceremony, to feel like something he was going to have to set down before it set
itself down for him.

It was not a decision made all at once. It had been building since the Undersecretary's folder, since
the officer who never pulled the lever, since a village well and a corrected rumor and a promise he
hadn't kept and a marker Breck had carved without a single word attached to it. Eighteen months of a
name built to mean one specific thing — a threat, a fear, a pivotal piece nobody could buy — carrying,
by the end, a weight the name itself had never been built to hold.

He thought about the Undersecretary's face in the fog, the first time the reputation had done
exactly what he'd needed it to do. He thought about Karrow's Bend, the four dead, the first time the
reputation had cost more than it was worth. He thought about the clerk two folders deep in
contradictions she'd never resolve, and the storyteller who'd known exactly where his own line was,
and Garren Hask's ledger with the wells sitting plainly beside the garrisons, neither one crossed out
to flatter the other.

"You're not sleeping," Hask said, finding him there the way he had before, not counting anything
this time either.

"No. I keep thinking the name's done what it was for." Bane didn't dress the thought up as anything
grander than it was. "It made people afraid enough, early enough, that fewer of us died than would
have otherwise. I don't think it needs to keep meaning that anymore. I think what comes next needs a
different shape, and I don't fully know yet what that shape is."

"You don't have to know yet. Names have found you before without you going looking for them."

"I know. I'm not in a hurry." He looked out at the sleeping column, eighteen months and forty-some
engagements he'd never bothered to count the way Hask counted them. "I just wanted to say it once,
plainly, to someone, before it happens on its own the way these things always seem to. Whatever
comes after this name, I want it built the same way this one ended up built, whether I meant it to
or not — the wells beside the garrisons. Not one without the other."

Hask didn't write that down either. Some things, he had learned by then, were never meant for the
ledger at all — only for the carrying, plain and uncounted, the same way the name itself would be
carried forward, however long it lasted, into whatever came next.
