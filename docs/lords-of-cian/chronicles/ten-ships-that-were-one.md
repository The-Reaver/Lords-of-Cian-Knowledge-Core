# Ten Ships That Were One

*Locked canon, Batch 199, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-773`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle XVIII, wave 6 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Efa Gol had run diversion crowds through a warehouse district and a drowned battlefield's evacuation
lines before anyone asked her to do the same trick with a single ship, and she'd spent two weeks
insisting it couldn't be done before she found a way to do it anyway.

"One hull," she told Kanja, laying out the plan on *The Audit*'s chart table. "Rigged to read as ten
from any distance a raiding captain's going to bother closing to. Extra lantern stations timed to
stagger like separate ships holding formation. Sailcloth cut and hung to break up our silhouette into
shapes that don't match our actual beam. It won't survive a close pass. It doesn't need to. It just
needs to survive the fifteen seconds a raiding captain spends deciding whether tonight's the night he
tests the rumor."

The merchant convoy they were shepherding had six ships and cargo worth more than most of the raiding
fleets working these waters would see in a decade — the kind of prize that made captains gamble against
even a reputation as fearsome as the ghost fleet's, if the reputation was thin enough to call.

It wasn't. Efa Gol's ten ships held position around the convoy's flank all night, lantern patterns
shifting in the staggered rhythm she'd drilled into the skeleton crews running each station, and the
single raiding captain who closed near enough to actually look broke off a half-mile out rather than
commit his three ships against what his own lookouts swore was a full squadron.

"He never got close enough to count masts," Efa Gol said afterward, watching his retreat through a
glass. "Which was the whole point. Close enough to count is close enough to realize it's one hull
doing a great deal of work."

"You gave a merchant convoy the protection of ten ships without spending the crews or the timber for
nine of them," Kanja said. "That's not a trick. That's a resource nobody else has thought to build."

"It's a trick that only works because the rumor's already real. Nobody believes ten ghost ships are
possible unless they already believe one is." She began logging the lantern timing for the next crew
to learn it. "The reputation did the actual work tonight. I just gave it a shape to wear."

The convoy reached port two days later, untouched, its captains never learning how thin the ten-ship
squadron guarding them had actually been — which was, Efa Gol noted in her own log that night, exactly
how a good decoy was supposed to end.
