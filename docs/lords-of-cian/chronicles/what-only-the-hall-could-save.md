# What Only the Hall Could Save

*Locked canon, Batch 251, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1089`). Captain Alias Chronicle LXI, wave 21. Garren
Hask's own mortality becomes real for the first time. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

Years into the peace, with the charter's first three lines quoted more often than remembered and the
fourth still blank under its own dated heading, Garren Hask went down at his own ledger table between
one column and the next. Efa Gol found him — she was always the one who noticed when a man went too
long without being heard from — and by the time Kanja reached the pier, Hask's own crew had already
done everything shipboard medicine knew how to do, which was less than any of them wanted to admit.
Combat medicine stopped a bleed and set a break. It had no answer for a heart that had simply, quietly,
started failing a man who'd never once let anyone see him tire.

"Don't take him aboard anything that moves," Callum Breck said, the first time in years anyone had
heard real fear crack through his ordinary flat calm. "Take him to her."

Sera's hall smelled nothing like the ship — dried herb instead of pitch and salt, quiet instead of
work-shout — and for the first time in his life Kanja walked into it not as a visitor being shown
hospitality but as a man handing over someone he could not save himself. Sera didn't ask him to wait
outside. She put him to useful work instead, holding a basin, counting a pulse aloud so she could keep
both hands free, the way she'd apparently done for every frightened family member who'd ever stood
where he was standing.

"His heart isn't failing because he's old," she said, working without looking up. "It's failing
because he's spent twenty years running himself like a man who thinks stopping is the same thing as
quitting. I can slow this down. I can teach him how to live around it instead of through it. I can't
give him back the years he already spent pretending he didn't need to." She glanced at Kanja once,
direct the way her father was direct. "Neither can you, whatever you're about to offer."

He didn't offer it. He sat with the basin instead and let her work, and understood — watching her
hands move with a certainty nothing aboard any ship of his had ever taught her — exactly what she'd
meant, years before, about being the person the whole coast came to because she was the best at
exactly one thing. Nothing in the Trinity, no gear he'd ever forged or carried, could do what her
plain, unhurried competence did in that room.

Hask woke near dawn, weaker than any of them had ever seen him and furious about it in a way that was,
everyone privately agreed, an excellent sign. "Don't you dare tell the council I nearly died over
ledger arithmetic," he said, and Sera told him, without a flicker of deference to his rank or his
history, that he would follow every instruction she gave him for the next six months or she'd have
Efa Gol sit on him personally.

Kanja found her afterward, cleaning instruments in the pale light, and thanked her the way he'd
learned mattered most — not with anything grand, but by staying long enough to actually be useful
again, carrying water, stacking linen, letting the hall's own rhythm decide what he was for instead of
bringing the ship's rhythm in with him. "The charter's supposed to cut both ways," he said finally,
echoing words that were hers before they were his. "I don't think I understood, until tonight, how
much the crew already depends on the choice you made not to join it."

Sera didn't look up from the basin. "Now you're catching up," she said, and kept working.

*Continuity notes (not narrative): the first entry to make Garren Hask's own aging and mortality
concretely real within the sub-series (previously only foreshadowed in the abstract at "The Promise
for After He's Gone," `MCD-920`), and the first entry to show the crew's shipboard combat medicine
hitting a genuine limit that only Sera's specialized, land-based healer's-hall training can answer --
a direct, concrete payoff of "What Sera Chose Instead" (`MCD-1057`) and "The Charter They Finally
Wrote" (`MCD-1056`), proving the found-family ethos protecting her choice not to join was not a
sentimental gesture but a real, load-bearing dependency running the other direction. The entire scene
stays off any ship under sail, consistent with `MCD-1057`'s closing line that Sera "never once set
foot on a crew ship under sail." No new named characters -- Garren Hask, Efa Gol, Callum Breck, Sera,
and Healer Orenn (referenced, not on-page) all reused. First entry, wave 21.*
