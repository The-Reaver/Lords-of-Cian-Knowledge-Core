# Lauris Chronicle XXXII: We Are the Same. Run.

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1649`). Thirty-second entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, continuing "Strand D" into the transition between the Specialist and
Disillusionment sub-periods. Full-scene treatment of Operation 29 (previously only summarized at
`MCD-187`): the fleeing engineered Karesian subject designated CP-414, who spoke to Lauris in the
engagement's final minutes -- naming additional engineering-tradition facilities in the Korren
Highlands, giving the mispronounced hint of Anu Un Ra's name Lauris would not fully recognize until
Operation 34, and delivering the closing line already locked verbatim at `MCD-187`: "You were not
the first. You will not be the last. We are the same. Run." Dramatizes the rule's own closing claim
that the message "shaped every operation she conducted afterward," and sets up `MCD-188`'s
Operation-30 redirection of her contract acceptances toward the Korren Highlands. Deliberately stops
short of Operation 30's Site K-Theta reconnaissance and Operation 34's later clearance, both
reserved. No new named characters; CP-414 already locked. Zero collisions.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Specialist designation LL-001-S-029: "Four words, and a name I did not yet know
how to hear correctly. I terminated her as contracted, exactly on schedule, precisely as I had
terminated the twins, precisely as I had terminated the seventh subject at Operation 28. I do not
believe, any longer, that precision and mercy are the same thing."*

Operation 29's target, Designation CP-414 in the Directorate's own paperwork, was a fleeing
engineered Karesian subject located after a pursuit of some eleven days through the Korren Highlands'
eastern foothill country — territory Lauris had, by then, begun learning with a thoroughness the
contract itself did not require, though her own private accounting by that point required it of her
without exception. The pursuit's tactical details occupy a brief portion of her archive entry:
efficient tracking, a clean final engagement, no complications the Directorate's own summary would
find worth remarking on.

What occupies the rest of the entry, and what I have chosen to render here at greater length than the
combat itself, is the final several minutes before the terminating strike — minutes during which
CP-414, cornered and, by Lauris's own assessment, fully aware that the engagement's outcome was no
longer contestable, chose to speak rather than continue resisting.

She gave Lauris a location: additional facilities, she said, scattered through the Korren Highlands'
deeper interior, more than the Directorate's own records acknowledged existing. She gave a name, or
what Lauris's archive records as an attempt at one — a mispronunciation close enough to something
that would not resolve into recognizable shape for Lauris for another five operations, not until a
generator's own documentation at a facility she had not yet located would spell it correctly for
her. And she gave, in the last seconds before Lauris's strike, four sentences that Lauris recorded
that same night, in handwriting her archive notes as uncharacteristically unsteady for the only time
in this stretch of her career:

*"You were not the first. You will not be the last. We are the same. Run."*

Lauris terminated her as contracted. She has told me, directly and without the reflexive
deflection she offers most questions about her own interior life, that this is the single act in her
entire Directorate career she has revisited most often, and the one she is least able to resolve into
either regret or justification. CP-414 was, by every operational measure the contract specified, a
hostile subject whose continued existence posed a documented threat; the termination was, by that
measure, correctly executed. CP-414 was also, by her own final words, attempting to communicate
something to Lauris specifically — kinship, warning, an appeal she made no attempt to disguise as a
plea for her own survival — and Lauris ended the conversation in the only way the contract permitted
her to end it.

"I do not know," she wrote, "whether she meant *we are the same subject*, or *we are the same
kind of thing that gets used*. I have considered both readings at length. I no longer believe the
distinction changes what I owe the sentence."

The message did not alter her immediate conduct. She filed the contract as closed, the location
data as actionable intelligence, and continued accepting Directorate work exactly as before. But her
archive from Operation 30 forward shows a deliberate, visible shift in the shape of her contract
acceptances — a beginning, however cautious, of steering her own assignments toward the Korren
Highlands rather than waiting for the Directorate to route them to her, the first occasion in her
entire career on which she used her own standing to choose her targets rather than accept them as
given. I do not think this is coincidence. I think Operation 29 is the operation at which she stopped
being merely disturbed by what she was learning about the apparatus beneath her employer, and began,
quietly and without announcing it to anyone, to act on it.

Her closing note for Operation 29, the shortest and, I believe, the most weighted entry in this
entire stretch of her archive: *She told me to run. I did not run. I turned, instead, toward
whatever she was running from. I record this as the difference between us, though I am no longer
certain it is a difference that favors me.*
