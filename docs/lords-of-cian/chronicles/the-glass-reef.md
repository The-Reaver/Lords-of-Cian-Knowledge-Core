# The Glass Reef

*Locked canon, Batch 259, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1232`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LXVII, wave 23, first
entry. Age 250, V4 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

No chart agreed on what the water did around the black island. Three different Trust surveys marked
the reef three different ways, and all three were wrong, because none of the surveyors had gone in
close enough to see that the reef wasn't coral at all — it was obsidian, decades of undersea
volcanic vents cooling into glass faster than any current could round its edges, a maze of
razor-fine ridges that had opened more than one slaving hull's hull below the waterline and closed
just as many rescue attempts before they started.

The Sovereign Eyes' V4 lenses read it the way the Talisman's Blueprint Eye had always been meant to
be used — not sight exactly, structure, the reef's true geometry laid over his vision in cool
amber overlay, every knife-edge ridge and hollow channel mapped in real time as he swam it, the
kind of reading no chart drawn from a deck above the water could ever have produced. It took him
forty minutes to find the one channel a heavy hull could survive threading, and he found it without
a single cut.

A local diver had been threading that same channel by memory alone for eleven years, ferrying
supplies past the reef the slow way because it was the only way anyone local trusted — and it was
her boat, not his gear, that ended up guiding the captives out afterward, since she knew which of
the channel's turns a small craft could take at speed and which it couldn't, knowledge the overlay
had given him once and she'd been carrying her whole working life. He followed her line back out.
She never asked who he was, and by the time the captives were clear, it hadn't seemed to matter to
either of them that she hadn't.

Ninety-one freed, one glass reef finally charted correctly by someone who'd have no way of
publishing the correction, and a local diver who went back to running the same channel the next
week, the way she always had, only now with one fewer reason to fear what was hiding behind the
black rock at its edge.
