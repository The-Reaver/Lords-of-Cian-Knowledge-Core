# The Last Ship They Let Go

*Locked canon, Batch 258, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1226`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
LXXXVIII, wave 30, first entry in the wave. The fleet releases a captured vessel entirely, choosing
trust over enforcement of the letter of the law. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose.*

---

The smuggler's ship came into the fleet's hands the ordinary way — boarded on suspicion of running
contraband through a blockaded strait, cargo hold searched, manifest checked against Garren Hask's own
records of legitimate trade. What they found wasn't weapons or captives or anything the doctrine had a
clean answer for already: medicine, smuggled specifically to reach a quarantined port the Trust had
cut off from legal supply over an outbreak the Trust itself had mismanaged, sold at cost rather than
profiteered, by a captain who'd broken the blockade knowing full well what capture would cost him.

"By the letter of it, I'm exactly what you've stopped and searched a hundred times," the smuggler
said, not pleading, simply laying out the fact of it. "Illegal cargo, unlicensed route, blockade run.
I won't pretend otherwise."

Kanja read the manifest twice, checked it against the quarantine reports Hask's network had already
confirmed independently, and found nothing in it that didn't match exactly what the man claimed. No
profit beyond fair cost. No cargo beyond what the port actually needed. A blockade that existed, per
the fleet's own long-standing read of the situation, to punish a quarantined population for a crisis
that wasn't their doing.

He didn't disable the ship. Didn't board it further, didn't inspect it for some technicality that
would let the doctrine apply its usual answer. He simply let it go — cargo, ship, and captain
together, no conditions attached, no ledger entry marking it as freed under any standard
restraint-doctrine category the fleet had built up over years of practice.

"You're not even going to make an example of releasing me publicly?" the smuggler asked, plainly
expecting some use to be made of the gesture. "Word of the ghost fleet letting a blockade-runner
through would be worth something to your reputation."

"It's not about what it's worth," Kanja said. "You broke a rule that deserved breaking, to do
something the Trust should have been doing itself. That's not mine to reward or punish. It's just
yours to keep doing, if you're willing to keep risking it."

Garren Hask logged it plainly, as always — not "captured and released," which was the category every
prior entry like it had used, but a category of its own: nothing taken, nothing given, a ship let go
entirely because letting it go was simply the right thing to do.
