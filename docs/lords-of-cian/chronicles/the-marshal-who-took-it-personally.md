# The Marshal Who Took It Personally

*Locked canon, Batch 217, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-952`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle L, wave 17. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Every officer the fleet had humiliated so far had done it institutionally — a task force built by
committee, a blockade squadron assembled from a standing order, a hunter-group designed on paper by
people who never had to answer personally for its failure. The newly-seated Fleet-Marshal of the Trust's
Western Squadron was the first to make it about himself.

He'd lost his previous command at Ghost Harbor, one of the six grounded destroyers whose captain had
been quietly retired afterward while the Marshal, then a junior officer aboard her, had watched the tide
do to his career what the fleet's tactics had done to the ship. Promotion had not softened it. His first
act on taking the Western Squadron was a standing bounty, paid from his own family's holdings rather
than Trust funds, for any intelligence — credible or not — on the ghost fleet's movements.

"He's not fighting a war," Corren Halst reported, laying out what six weeks of careful listening in
Western ports had turned up. "He's fighting a grudge that happens to have a squadron attached to it.
That makes him unpredictable in a way the committees never were."

The first real encounter proved it. The Marshal didn't deploy his squadron in the disciplined
formations Trust doctrine favored — he split it into a dozen fast, independently-roving pairs, each
one ordered to shadow and report rather than engage, a patient net cast wide across water the fleet had
always treated as safely empty. *The Ledger* nearly sailed straight into two of them before Dol Maren's
read of an unfamiliar wake pattern gave the fleet enough warning to slip clear.

"He's not trying to catch us in one fight," Kanja said, once the pairs had been shaken. "He's trying to
learn our patterns the slow way, the way Danne Sok learns theirs. That's not reckless. That's the first
one of them who's actually studied us instead of just hunting us."

They didn't meet in person that season — the Marshal's roving pairs came close twice more and were
outmaneuvered both times, more through Danne Sok's own accumulated chart-work than through any dramatic
escape — but the pattern of it stayed with the crew in a way the earlier, impersonal hunts never had.

"He's not going to stop," Efa Gol said, watching the horizon where the last pair had turned back toward
their own supply line. "A committee gives up when the budget runs out. A grudge doesn't have a budget."

"No," Kanja agreed. "Which means the next time we meet him, it won't be a task force we out-think. It'll
be one man who's spent a year learning exactly how we think, deciding whether that was worth the
learning." He didn't sound worried about it, exactly. He sounded like someone filing away a debt he
already knew would eventually come due.
