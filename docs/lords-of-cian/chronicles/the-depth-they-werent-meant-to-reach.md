# The Depth They Weren't Meant to Reach

*Locked canon, Batch 258, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1220`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
LXXXII, wave 28, first entry in the wave. A fully submerged dive rescue inside a sunk wreck, with no
gear advantage. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The wreck had gone down two days before the fleet found it, a merchant hauler caught on an uncharted
shoal and holed below the waterline fast enough that a full third of her crew never made it to the
boats — and according to the one surviving officer, pulled half-drowned from wreckage still floating,
four more might be alive in an air pocket somewhere in the flooded lower decks, if the pocket itself
hadn't already given out.

No gear in the fleet's loadout was built for what came next. Kanja went down without the Forge-Coat,
without the Boots, stripped to what wouldn't drag him under or catch on the wreck's own tangled
rigging, the Breath Collar's filtration doing nothing for a problem that had nothing to do with air
quality and everything to do with there being no air at all past a certain depth.

He found the pocket by feel more than sight, the wreck's interior black past where any light reached,
following the officer's half-remembered layout of a ship he'd never walked himself. Four people,
exactly as reported, crowded into a shrinking bubble of air trapped against a canted ceiling, two of
them children, all of them past the point of believing rescue was still coming.

There was no combat in it, nothing for Mafesto or Obsidian Malice or Onyx to solve. Just repeated
dives, each one longer than the last, hauling one person at a time back up through a wreck that
shifted and groaned with every pass, air running thinner in the pocket with every trip he made. Pell
Ostra, working from above, rigged a guide-line down through the wreck's collapsed companionway on the
second dive, cutting his search time nearly in half on every trip after.

The fourth trip nearly cost him the last survivor — a debris fall sealing the passage half a second
after he'd cleared it, the man on his shoulder coughing seawater the moment they broke the surface.
Kanja surfaced with nothing left to give the dive after that, and didn't need to; all four were out.

"That's not a fight you can train a Trinity to win," Pell Ostra said afterward, coiling the guide-line.
"That's just lungs and nerve."

"Most of what actually matters usually is," Kanja said.
