# The Cost of Being Corrected

*Locked canon, Batch 149, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-474`). Nyansa Chronicle II. Homage-era Detroit,
Nyansa/Adisa. Territory Chronicle -- Adisa as protagonist, Kanja an unnamed background guest with no
command, credit, or resolution authorship. New standalone material. No new named characters.*

---

The committee member whose unexamined exclusion of night-shift workers Adisa had named aloud, months
before, had genuinely changed his position — the argument hadn't returned to its old unresolved shape,
exactly as "The Long Correction" had promised. What the ability hadn't promised, and what Adisa hadn't
fully anticipated, was what changing that publicly would cost the man among the same peers who'd once
agreed with his old position.

"They've stopped inviting him to the informal planning sessions," a fellow organizer reported,
watching the man's standing within his own faction quietly erode in the months since. "Nobody's said
it's because he changed his mind. Nobody has to say it. Everyone just knows."

Adisa sought the man out directly rather than let the cost go unacknowledged. "I named the
contradiction because it needed naming. I don't think I fully reckoned with what it would mean for
you personally, being the one who couldn't unsee it once it was said."

"I don't regret changing my mind. I was wrong, and staying wrong because the room was comfortable
with it would have been worse." The man didn't perform bitterness he didn't feel, but the real cost
showed plainly enough underneath his steadiness. "I just didn't expect the room to punish the honesty
instead of rewarding it. I thought naming a contradiction out loud was supposed to fix something.
Instead it just moved the cost from the argument onto me."

"The gift only ever promised the contradiction wouldn't return to its old shape. It never promised the
people around you would make that easy to live with." Adisa sat with the weight of that honestly,
without deflecting from his own role in it. "I think I owe you more than I've given you, since this
started — not protection from the cost, because I can't offer that, but at least the honesty that the
cost is real, and that I see it, and that changing your mind was still the right thing even though the
room made you pay for it."

"That's more than most people have offered me since this started." The man's steadiness didn't waver,
but something in it warmed slightly at being seen plainly. "I'll take honesty over comfort. I always
have. I suppose that's rather the whole reason I was capable of hearing the correction in the first
place."

An unnamed man who had walked with the committee member on several of his quieter evenings since,
saying little, was gone by the time the conversation with Adisa concluded.
