# What Held the Bridge Together

*Locked canon, Batch 202, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-874`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XXIX, wave 10 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The pontoon crossing, lashed together from salvaged hulls and fresh-forged chain across a river three
districts needed to reach the campaign's next scheduled stop, was two days from completion when the
Trust detachment arrived to burn it before it could ever bear its first crossing.

Kanja met them on the crossing itself, half-built and unstable underfoot, Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer
System adjusting to a footing that shifted with every wave the river sent through the linked pontoons
— absorbing not just the detachment's strikes but the crossing's own structural give, converting both
into charge rather than losing balance to either.

Obsidian Malice discharged low across the linking chains, a strike calibrated precisely enough to
shatter the detachment's boarding grapples without weakening a single link the crossing itself
depended on. Onyx read the fight across a surface that gave and swayed beneath every combatant equally
— Cadence Ruin tracking six attackers by the pontoons' own shifting rhythm as much as by sight,
finding footing in the instability that the detachment, unfamiliar with the crossing's give, never
managed to match.

By the time the detachment withdrew, not one pontoon had been breached, not one length of chain had
parted, and the crossing's own construction crew — flattened against the far bank through the worst of
it — returned to find two days of work exactly as they'd left it.

"You fought that whole engagement on a surface that wasn't finished being built," the crossing's lead
engineer said, still faintly unsteady from watching it. "I've crossed it a hundred times since we
started and I still don't trust my footing on it completely."

"Neither do I, honestly. That's not something you fight around. It's something you fight with."
Kanja tested one of the linking chains himself, satisfied it hadn't shifted. "The crossing's not weak
because it moves. It's strong because it's built to give a little instead of breaking. Fought that way
too, this once. Worked out about the same either way."
