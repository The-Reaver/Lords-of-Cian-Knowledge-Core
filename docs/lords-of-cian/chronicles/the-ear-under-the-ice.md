# The Ear Under the Ice

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XVI, first entry in
the sixth wave. Rebellion era, age 25. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material — the
doctrine's first cold-climate application. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters.*

---

The river fortress's portcullis chain ran beneath eight inches of solid ice for the last thirty feet
of its span, and the cold had done what four alloy variants and a countermeasure engineering corps
had never managed: it muffled the doctrine almost to nothing. Sound traveled differently through ice
than through open air, arriving thinned and directionless, and the Aegis-Talisman's frequency-
inversion lens, held against the exposed chain links above the waterline, returned a reading too
faint to trust.

Kanja didn't force it. He went to the ice itself.

Obsidian Malice cut a narrow channel down through the frozen surface, careful and slow rather than
explosive, until the lens could be set directly against a submerged link with nothing but frigid
water between contact points. The reading came back clean the moment the ice stopped standing between
the Talisman and the metal — the chain's full tension signature, the counterweight mechanism above the
gatehouse, all of it legible again once the interference was physically removed rather than fought
through.

"You could have broadcast blind," the rebel sapper beside him said, teeth chattering against the wind
coming off the frozen river. "Guessed at the frequency from the muffled reading. Most people would
have."

"Most people would have been wrong by exactly enough to fail and not know why." Kanja kept the lens
steady against the channel wall, patient through the cold his own biology barely registered but the
sapper beside him plainly did. "The doctrine only works because I don't skip the listening when it
gets difficult. Cold doesn't change what the metal's telling me. It just makes me work harder to hear
it clearly."

The chain came down within the hour, the gate's counterweight collapsing inward rather than the ice
itself being touched, and the fortress's garrison found their own defensive water crossing suddenly
undefended without a single structure above the waterline showing visible damage.

"They'll never find the cut," the sapper said, looking back at the narrow channel already beginning
to skin over with new ice. "They'll think the chain just failed."

"Let them think that. A doctrine nobody can trace is worth more than credit for using it well."
