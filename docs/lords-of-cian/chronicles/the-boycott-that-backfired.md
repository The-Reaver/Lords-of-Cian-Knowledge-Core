# The Boycott That Backfired

*Locked canon, Batch 256, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1161`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle LXXVII, wave 26. Workers inspired by the reputation organize their own action independently, without consulting the crew, and it goes wrong before the method can help -- the crew arrives after the fact to document the damage and negotiate reinstatement.*

---

Word of the method had reached the tannery district three towns ahead of the crew itself, the way it increasingly did by then, and a dozen of the district's own workers, certain enough of what Kanja would find and impatient to wait for him to find it, organized a work stoppage on their own timeline rather than the method's — no testimony gathered first, no cross-referenced case built, just a walkout meant to force the owner's hand before anyone had proven anything.

The owner didn't fold the way stalling administrators usually did once a documented case arrived. He locked the gate entirely, dismissed all twelve organizers on the spot for what his own posted rules technically called unauthorized work stoppage, and had two of them removed from company housing within the week — a response the method's usual patient documentation had never given him room to make, because the method's usual patient documentation never handed him a clean pretext to act before the facts were established.

Ezio and Kanja arrived four days later to a district worse off than before anyone had heard the Industrial Myth's name at all: twelve workers dismissed, two families evicted, morale in the yard split between those who blamed the walkout's organizers for moving too fast and those who blamed the owner for using it as cover to purge troublemakers he'd wanted gone regardless.

"They did exactly what we do, just without the part that actually protects anyone," Ezio said, surveying the damage. "The reputation got ahead of the method."

There was no clean finding waiting to be discovered here — no fraud to expose that would simply undo a lawful, if cynical, dismissal for an actual work stoppage. What the crew built instead was the closest thing available: full documentation of the owner's own conduct leading up to the walkout, evidence that the "unauthorized" pretext had been selectively enforced only against the twelve organizers and nobody else who'd ever missed a shift, and a public tally of exactly what the district's grievances had been before anyone ever left the floor — proof the walkout hadn't been baseless, even if it had been premature.

It wasn't enough to reverse every dismissal. Nine of the twelve were reinstated once the selective-enforcement finding made the owner's position legally untenable in front of the Trust magistrate the case eventually reached; three, including both evicted families, weren't, the housing loss compounding the damage past what any finding could undo after the fact.

"We're not fast enough to stop this from happening again somewhere else," Kanja told the district before they left. "The next place that hears our name before it hears from us — they'll move before we can help them move safely. All I can tell people honestly is what happens when the method gets skipped for the reputation. It isn't always this. Sometimes it's worse."

---

*Continuity notes (not narrative): the first entry where the reputation itself, outrunning the method, causes real harm -- distinct from MCD-759's impostor (a grifter using the name to extort) and MCD-760's staged fraud (administrators gaming the method deliberately), since here the workers act in good faith, inspired rather than deceived, and the damage is still real and only partially reversible. A genuine institutional-friction failure state: the crew's own success creates a risk it cannot fully control or arrive in time to prevent. No new named characters; the twelve organizers, the owner, and the district are unnamed. Strictly unarmed and non-combat throughout.*
