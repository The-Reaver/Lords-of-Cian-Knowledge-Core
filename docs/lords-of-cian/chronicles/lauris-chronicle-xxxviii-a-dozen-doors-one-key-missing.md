# Lauris Chronicle XXXVIII: A Dozen Doors, One Key Missing

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1655`). Thirty-eighth entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, part of the wave-one push of Strand L. Advances the Sample K-403 thread
(`MCD-1534`) -- one of roughly a dozen Karesian biological fragments scattered across the Sealbound
Directorate's containment archives, which Lauris delivered into Directorate custody herself during
Operation 4 without disclosing what she recognized it as. Deepens rather than resolves -- she
confirms the fragment has since been administratively reclassified and moved, widening rather than
narrowing the search, and locates partial leads on two further fragments she cannot yet act on. No
new named characters. Narrated by Fermand Aurelias.*

---

*Archive fragment, present-day: "I filed it myself, at Operation 4, before I knew what I was filing.
I have known what it was for the better part of six thousand years. I still do not have it back."*

I had not, before this entry, fully appreciated how long a debt can sit quietly beneath a career
before its owner decides to act on it. Sample K-403 has existed in Lauris's personal archive as an
acknowledged fact -- a fragment of Karesian biological material she herself delivered into the
Directorate's Coastal Containment Office during her second contract, decades before she understood
what the tradition processing such fragments actually was -- for longer than most institutions on
Cian have existed at all. She raised it with me this season not because anything new had forced the
issue, but because the Operation 38 inquiry, which I have recorded elsewhere in this wave, put her in
the habit of asking what "filed" and "processed" and "reported" actually meant in Directorate
practice, rather than accepting the words at face value.

She began where the trail was oldest: the Coastal Containment Office itself, or what remains of an
institution she has not had operational reason to approach directly in some years. Working partly
through Aerelin's network and partly through channels of her own that she declined to describe to me
in detail, she confirmed within a fortnight that the office had undergone at least two administrative
reorganizations since her original delivery, and that Sample K-403's own file had been reclassified
somewhere within the second of these -- moved from active containment inventory to what her contact's
best information described only as a secondary long-term archive, unnamed and unlocated in anything
she was able to recover.

"I expected to find it exactly where I left it," she told me, with what I took to be more frustration
than she generally permits herself to show. "Institutions like that one do not lose things. They
relocate them, reclassify them, and stop telling anyone where. I do not think anyone currently
employed by that office knows K-403 exists, let alone where it currently sits."

The search, in widening to account for the reclassification, surfaced something else she had not
anticipated: partial administrative references to two further fragments among the roughly dozen she
believes exist scattered across the Directorate's various containment archives, both apparently moved
under the same reorganization, both similarly unlocated as a result. She recorded both leads in her
archive with the same discipline she applies to every unresolved thread, and neither, by her own
assessment, is close enough to actionable to justify committing further resources to it at present.

"I could spend a year chasing three fragments across an institution that has spent decades making
itself harder to search," she said. "Or I could accept that the fragments are not going anywhere in
the meantime -- material like that does not decay in storage the way a suspended population might --
and wait until I have better information than a reclassification I only just confirmed."

I asked her, because it seemed the honest question to ask, why the fragments matter enough to pursue
at all, given how many other debts she is already carrying at considerably greater urgency. She did
not answer quickly. "Each one is residue of processing," she said eventually. "A dozen small proofs
of what the tradition did to material taken directly from my homeworld, scattered across an
institution that does not know what it is holding. I do not need them back today. I need to know they
have not been lost entirely, the way K-Theta's cave system nearly was before I found it in time. A
fragment that goes truly missing is a fragment I can no longer account for, and I have spent this
much of my life learning what it costs to lose account of something before I noticed."

She has not resumed active pursuit of any of the three located leads as of this entry, judging the
present information too thin to justify the operational cost of chasing it further. She has, however,
added a standing line to her own archive review, alongside the Drowning Vault and the cave systems,
noting the reclassification and committing to check for further movement at her next scheduled pass
through the region.

Her closing note: *Twelve doors, at least. One I know is not where I left it. Two more I now suspect
are not where they were, either. I do not have the key to any of them yet. I am, at minimum, no
longer pretending the doors have stayed shut on their own.*
