# Five Foremen at One Table

*Locked canon, Batch 194, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-630`). The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XXV, wave 9 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The five foremen had never once agreed to sit in the same room together, not because they disliked
each other, but because each of their districts had spent years competing for the same limited
Compliance attention, and cooperation had always seemed like a way to lose whatever thin advantage
their own district held over the other four.

Kanja called the meeting anyway, in a warehouse loft equidistant from all five sites, and opened it
without ceremony.

"Each of you is keeping your own tally standard," he said. "Which means each of you can be told your
numbers are wrong by an owner who simply points at a different district's different method and calls
it more reasonable. I'd like all five sites using the same standard, cross-checked the same way, so
no owner anywhere on this stretch of canal gets to play one district's numbers against another's
again."

"And if my district's standard is better than the other four?" the Marrow Landing foreman asked,
arms crossed.

"Then it should win on its own merits, in front of all five of you, not stay locked inside one
district where nobody else can verify it." Kanja looked around the table. "I'm not asking anyone to
give up ground. I'm asking everyone to stop letting five separate methods be turned into a weapon
against each of you individually. Standing alone, each of your districts is one voice an owner can
dismiss. Standing on the same numbers, cross-checked by four other foremen who have no reason to lie
for you, you're five voices saying the exact same thing. That's harder to ignore than any one of you
managed alone."

The Ferrymouth foreman, the most openly skeptical of the five, finally spoke. "You're asking us to
trust each other's books. We've spent years not trusting each other's books."

"I know. That's exactly why an owner's been able to play you against each other for years. I'm not
asking you to trust each other out of goodwill. I'm asking you to let three independent clerks
compare all five sets of figures, openly, the same way Silt Row's numbers got compared after a
broken handshake. If your books are honest, the comparison costs you nothing. If someone's books
aren't honest, better all four of the rest of you find out now than after an owner finds out first
and uses it against the whole canal."

It took the better part of the afternoon, and the comparison, when it finally ran, turned up exactly
one real discrepancy — a Ferrymouth foreman's own honest miscount, not malice, corrected in the room
without incident. By evening, the five districts had agreed to the shared standard, the first time in
anyone's memory that the canal's competing sites had acted as anything resembling one body instead
of five separate, exploitable ones.

"That took considerably more patience than a fight would have," Danne Sok observed afterward.

"It took a different kind of patience than a fight takes. I'd rather spend an afternoon convincing
five people who don't trust each other than spend years watching an owner keep exploiting the fact
that they don't."
