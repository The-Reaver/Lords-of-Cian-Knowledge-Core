# The Pact Signed in Salt Water

*Locked canon, Batch 234, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1040`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
LX, wave 20, closing the wave. A sustained diplomatic alliance entry, deepening the foreign-nation
thread first opened in wave 4. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The letter that had once offered safe harbor in good faith (already established, this alias's fourth
wave) turned out not to be the end of that contact but its opening move. Three years on, the same
foreign nation's envoy returned — older, less formal, carrying not another letter but a standing
proposal: a genuine reciprocal arrangement, not a one-time offer of refuge but ongoing joint patrols
against the slaving routes that ran through waters neither power alone could fully cover.

"Safe harbor was easy to offer," the envoy said, laid out across *The Ledger*'s own chart table
beside Garren Hask's open books. "Anyone can say a door is unlocked. What we're proposing costs
something real — our own ships answering to a schedule your fleet keeps, information moving both
directions, and an accounting neither side gets to falsify afterward."

That last condition was the one that mattered to Kanja. "Then Hask keeps the joint ledger," he said.
"Every patrol, every ship stopped, every captive freed, logged the same way regardless of whose flag
did the stopping. If either of us starts padding the numbers, the arrangement ends the day the other
side catches it."

The envoy hadn't expected the condition to come from the smaller fleet. "Most powers negotiating from
strength ask for the credit," she said. "You're asking for an auditor."

"I'm asking for the thing that outlasts either of our reputations," Kanja said. "Reputations drift.
An honest count doesn't, as long as somebody keeps it that way."

The terms took the better part of a week to settle — patrol boundaries, a shared signal system for
distress calls that crossed the maritime line between the two powers' waters, and a standing clause
that either side could suspend the arrangement on a season's notice without it reading as an act of
war. Garren Hask drafted the final ledger format himself, insisting on a single shared book rather
than two separate ones compared afterward, and the envoy signed it aboard *The Ledger*'s own deck,
in salt-stained ink, rather than in any port's formal chamber.

Within the first season under the pact, the joint patrols intercepted four slaving vessels neither
fleet could have reliably caught alone, and Hask's shared book recorded every one of them the same
way — disabled, not sunk; captives freed, not counted as spoils. The envoy, reviewing the book at
season's end, allowed that it was the first accounting she'd ever fully trusted that her own
government hadn't written.
