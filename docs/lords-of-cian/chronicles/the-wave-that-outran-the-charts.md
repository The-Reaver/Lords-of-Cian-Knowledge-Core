# The Wave That Outran the Charts

*Locked canon, Batch 280, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1463`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
XCVII, wave 33, first entry in the wave. A new environmental register: a tsunami strikes a coastal
settlement with almost no warning, distinct from every prior storm-based disaster entry. Not a
territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

There was no storm front, no wind shift, nothing Dol Maren would have recognized as weather at all —
just the tide pulling out further and faster than it should have, exposing a half-mile of seabed that
hadn't seen open air in living memory, and then, twenty minutes later, coming back all at once.

The fleet was two hours out when Kanja felt it first, not through any sense trained for storms but
through Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System reading a pressure wave moving through the water itself,
wrong in a way no squall had ever felt wrong. He didn't wait for confirmation. The Ledger ran flat out
toward the coastline it was already too far from to reach in time to stop what was coming, only in time
to help with what came after.

The wave itself did what it did before anyone could touch it — Kanja watched it break over the harbor
town from half a mile out, helpless in a way the Trinity's every piece of gear had no answer for. What
came after was six hours of pulling people from wreckage that used to be houses, Obsidian Malice's
discharge clearing collapsed timber pinning survivors rather than striking any enemy, Onyx of Oblivion's
Whisper of Shadows finding the muffled sound of breathing under rubble a search party would have walked
past twice.

"There was nothing to fight," Pell Ostra said quietly afterward, hands still raw from clearing debris.
"First time I've ever wanted there to be."

Kanja didn't correct her. Forty-one people came out alive who wouldn't have without the fleet's hands on
the wreckage; nineteen didn't, and no amount of speed or strength changes that arithmetic once the wave
has already broken. Garren Hask logged both numbers plainly, side by side, the way he'd learned to log
every hard thing the fleet couldn't undo.
