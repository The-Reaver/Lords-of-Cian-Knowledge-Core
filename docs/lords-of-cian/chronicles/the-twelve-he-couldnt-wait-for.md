# The Twelve He Couldn't Wait For

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Bane Alias Chronicle XXIX, second entry in the tenth
wave. Rebellion era, age 19, months after the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The intelligence reached the crew four days too late to plan the extraction properly: twelve
captured rebellion sympathizers, held at a Directorate transfer point deep inside secured territory,
scheduled for transport to a labor sentence at dawn on the fifth day — which left Bane a single
night to reach them, or none of them would be reachable again for months, if ever.

A proper operation took weeks. He had one night, three crew, and no time to cut it any closer to
safe.

"We go in with less than half of what we'd normally want," he told the three who'd come with him,
laying out the transfer point's rough shape from a single hurried scouting pass instead of the days
of study an operation like this deserved. "I don't like this. I want that said plainly before
anyone moves. If this goes wrong, it goes wrong because I chose speed over preparation, not because
any of you did anything short of exactly right."

It went as close to wrong as it could without actually failing. The transfer point's second guard
rotation ran twenty minutes off the pattern the scouting pass had recorded, nearly catching Ostra
mid-charge-placement on the outer wall; one of the twelve captives, weakened past the point of quiet
movement, collapsed twice on the way out and had to be carried the last stretch by a crew member who
was supposed to be covering their retreat instead. They cleared the wire with the dawn transport
already visible on the horizon road, all twelve alive, nothing about the extraction resembling the
clean operations the reputation implied.

"That was too close," the crew member who'd carried the collapsed captive said afterward, once they
were clear, voice still unsteady with how close it had actually run. "We don't usually cut it that
thin."

"We don't. I cut it that thin, because the alternative was twelve people gone by morning and no
chance at all instead of a bad one." Bane looked back once at the transfer point already falling
behind them. "I'd make the same call again tonight. I still don't want it to feel like the normal
way we do this. The night we start treating a bad chance like an acceptable one is the night we lose
people who deserved better preparation than I gave them."

All twelve survived the night. None of them ever knew how close the margin had actually run, or how
plainly Bane refused, afterward, to let anyone call it a clean success.
