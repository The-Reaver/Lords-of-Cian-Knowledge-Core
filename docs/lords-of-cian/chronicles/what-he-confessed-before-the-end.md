# What He Confessed Before the End

*Locked canon, Batch 256, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1162`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle LXXVIII, wave 26, closing the wave. An elderly, dying administrator summons the crew to confess decades of theft and make restitution before death; his heirs try to stop it -- a new mortality and redemption register.*

---

The summons came from a sickbed, carried by a servant who wouldn't say what it concerned, only that the old man asked for the crew by name and asked to be seen soon. Ezio nearly turned it away as too strange a request to fit the method's usual shape. Kanja went anyway, and found a man perhaps three weeks from death, by his own physician's honest estimate, who wanted to confess forty years of quietly skimmed wages before he could no longer say the words himself.

It wasn't a case that needed building. He gave Ezio the figures from memory, corrected himself twice where memory failed him, and pointed them to records his own heirs didn't know existed, hidden not to protect the theft from ever being found but, as he put it plainly, because he'd never found the courage while he was well enough to face what admitting it would cost him. Dying, apparently, had loosened something the four decades of ordinary living never had.

His two adult children arrived on the second day of Ezio's audit and tried, with real anger, to stop it. The restitution figure, once fully tallied across four decades, would consume most of what they stood to inherit. They argued the old man wasn't of sound enough mind to authorize it, argued the crew had no standing to act on a deathbed confession nobody had verified, argued — the sharpest version of it — that punishing a dying man for sins he was already atoning for in his own way served no one but strangers who'd never know the difference either way.

Ezio didn't need to argue his soundness of mind; the physician's own assessment, taken independently, confirmed it plainly, and the figures the old man gave matched records Ezio verified against buried company books the heirs hadn't known to hide. What Kanja said to the children wasn't about punishment.

"He's not being punished. He asked for this. The only people it takes anything from are the two of you, and what it takes is money you were never owed in the first place — it was always theirs, just delayed by forty years and about to become permanently unrecoverable the moment he dies with the secret intact."

The restitution was paid from the estate before probate closed, the old man signing the final figure himself four days before his physician's estimate proved accurate almost to the week. He didn't ask for forgiveness from the workers it was owed to, most of whom had died themselves in the decades since, and the crew didn't offer any performance of it on their behalf. What the finding recorded, plainly, was a debt correctly paid to the surviving families of those it had been stolen from, forty years overdue and settled anyway, because a dying man had decided a true number mattered more at the end than it ever had while he still had time to keep hiding it.

"We've never had someone confess without being caught," Ezio said, closing the book on the district for the last time.

"We've never needed to catch this one. He caught himself. Just late."
