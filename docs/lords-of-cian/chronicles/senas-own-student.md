# Sena's Own Student

*Locked canon, Batch 259, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1241`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LXXVI, wave 26, first
entry. Age 235, V3 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Forty-five years after she'd nearly killed an already-surrendered slaving captain out of her own
remembered trauma and sheathed the blade herself instead, Sena was the one doing the talking down.

The newest crew member was younger than Sena had been then, freed off a route two seasons back and
still raw enough that a surrendered guard's open hands hadn't stopped her hand from finding a blade
of her own. Sena reached her before Kanja did, close enough to touch, saying nothing he hadn't once
said to her, in words that had clearly spent forty-five years being turned over and made her own
before they were needed again: *every surrender we honor is someone who never has to be rescued,
because the fight never has to happen for them at all.*

He watched it happen from twenty feet off and didn't move to intervene. There was nothing in it that
needed him. Sena talked the newer woman's hand open the same slow way he'd once talked hers open —
not with force, not with rank, just with the plain mechanism of the doctrine laid out by someone who
had lived the exact same impulse from the inside and knew precisely how much time the guard's life
was actually worth weighing against.

The guard walked away with his hands still raised and his life still his. The newer crew member
didn't sheathe her blade with any grace — her hands shook doing it, the anger in her plainly not
gone, only checked — and Sena stood with her a while after, not explaining further, just present the
way presence had once been enough for her.

Afterward, Garren Hask's ledger recorded the surrender as it always did, one more honored line among
hundreds. What it didn't record, because there was no column built for it, was the second thing
that had happened in the same minute: a doctrine passing itself down one more generation, from the
man who'd first explained it, through the woman he'd once talked down, to whoever she reached next.
