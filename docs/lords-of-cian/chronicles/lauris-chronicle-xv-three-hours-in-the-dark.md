# Lauris Chronicle XV: Three Hours in the Dark

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1632`). Fifteenth entry in Lauris Letitia's own Chronicle
series, the eighth and closing entry of Strand K for this wave, drafted as part of a 50-entry wave
across eight strand-agents under Abad's blanket authorization. Dramatizes directly, for the first
time, the Vask Threnarr mining collapse already locked at `MCD-171` -- her sustained three-hour
Triad-Lock holding an unstable shaft's 2.4-million-metric-ton cumulative pressure-equivalent while
three trapped stonework-guild women free themselves, framed as the civilization's own "first lethal
combat" classification despite the absence of any opponent, and closing on her already-locked archive
line ("The shaft is sealed. The Hold continues.," `MCD-1558`) restored to its full original context.
No new named characters. Full text below.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Vask Threnarr, age approximately 1,247: "Three women below. The shaft moves when I
do not. I have decided it will not move again until they are out."*

I have set down, in the account before this one, the day Lauris did not attend a remembrance and
instead wrote the longest page in her own archive. I set down now, only forty-seven years later by
the Threnarr calendar, the day the same civilization's own ledger of her recorded, for the first
time, an entry the Sister-Hold itself would later classify under a heading I confess I found strange
the first time I read it: her first lethal combat. I want to explain, before the account itself, why
I think the classification is accurate despite what follows containing no opponent at all.

The collapse began without warning, in the deep-mountain galleries beneath Vask Threnarr's own
medical-archival complex, where three women of the Vask's stonework guild had been working a seam
roughly four hundred meters below the surface. I have gathered, from the Threnarr engineering
archive's own after-action account, that the initial failure was geological rather than structural --
a fault line the Vask's surveys had rated stable for eleven thousand years shifting, without
preamble, in the space of a few seconds. The gallery above the three women's position collapsed
inward. The gallery below and around them held, for the moment, in an unstable equilibrium the Vask's
own rescue teams judged, on arrival, too dangerous to enter.

Lauris reached the site within the hour, by what account survives having been at Threnarr on one of
her scheduled visits rather than resident at Karth-Ven that season. She did not wait for the rescue
teams to finish their assessment. She descended alone, into a shaft the Vask's own engineers had just
finished declaring unsafe for any conventional rescue attempt, and positioned herself in Triad-Lock --
the kinetic-anchor stance her instructors at Karth-Ven had spent her first four centuries there
building into her, under Veska's own direct tutelage -- at the precise point in the gallery's geometry
where the unstable mass above the three trapped women would have to pass through her body before it
could reach them.

I want to be careful here, because I think this is the detail that makes the classification make
sense to me now. Lauris was not fighting a person. She was fighting, for three continuous hours, the
mountain's own accumulated weight -- a calculated 2.4 million metric tons of cumulative
pressure-equivalent, absorbed and redirected through a single sustained stance, while the three women
worked, slowly and in the dark, to free themselves from the debris that had trapped them and make
their way, one at a time, past the place where Lauris stood holding a mountain in argument with
itself. It was not a battle against an enemy. It was, in every sense the Karth-Sera curriculum would
later formalize as doctrine, a battle nonetheless -- sustained, total, and, had her attention wavered
for even a portion of a second at any point across those three hours, as lethal to her and to the
three women both as any opponent she would ever face on Cian.

She has told me, since, that she does not remember experiencing the three hours as long. She
remembers the moment the third woman's hand closed around the rescue line rather than the hours that
preceded it, and she remembers, with more precision than the hours themselves, the exact instant she
judged it safe to release the stance and allow the gallery to finish what it had already, for three
hours, been trying to do.

Her own archive entry for the day is four words longer than the fragment I have placed at the head of
this account, and it is the line the Threnarr archive itself would go on to preserve as the Vask's own
official record of the incident, quoted in engineering briefings for centuries after by instructors
who, I suspect, never knew quite how young the woman who wrote it still was by her own civilization's
reckoning: *The shaft is sealed. The Hold continues.*

I have come to think of this line, more than any other in her Kares Prime record, as the truest
statement of what Lauris Letitia has always understood combat to be for. Not conquest. Not even, in
the ordinary sense, survival. A debt, discharged, so that something larger than herself could go on
existing exactly as it had before she was needed.
