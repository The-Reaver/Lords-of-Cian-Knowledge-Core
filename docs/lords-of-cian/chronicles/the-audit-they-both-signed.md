# The Audit They Both Signed

*Locked canon, Batch 277, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1434`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XCV, wave 32.
Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. A new peer-accountability register, distinct from the fraud
(`MCD-1126`), coercion litigation (`MCD-1130`), and Trust-audit (`MCD-1145`) threads already
locked.*

---

The accountant kept books for three of the largest owners on the eastern stretch, and he had never
once claimed the tally method was dishonest. What he claimed, loudly and at length in front of
anyone who'd listen, was that it was sloppy -- a boy's improvisation dressed up as rigor, catching
petty theft while missing the larger arithmetic errors his own trained eye would have found in an
afternoon.

Kanja didn't argue the point. He proposed a test of it instead: both ledgers, the same three sites,
audited side by side over a single week, each man's method checked against the other's findings
rather than against his own claims about himself. The accountant, expecting refusal or bluster,
agreed before he'd fully thought through what agreeing meant.

Garren Hask and the accountant worked the same stacks of tally sheets from opposite ends of a
borrowed table for six days, neither one permitted to see the other's running total until the close
of each day's count. The tally method caught what it had always caught -- three separate cases of
wages quietly shorted, each verified by cross-witness before it went in the book. The accountant's
sharper eye for compound arithmetic caught something neither Hask nor Kanja had -- a shipping
discrepancy running years back, small on any single ledger page and substantial across all of them,
that had nothing to do with wage theft and everything to do with an owner quietly skimming from his
own partners.

Neither man had set out to prove the other wrong on everything. What the week actually produced was
a joint report, signed by both, naming precisely which kind of dishonesty each method was built to
catch and which kind it wasn't. The accountant took the wage-theft findings back to his own clients
as if they were his; Kanja put the shipping discrepancy into the crew's own records without ever
mentioning that his own count had missed it first.

"You could have let him take the credit and said nothing," Danne Sok observed.

"I did let him take the credit." Kanja folded the signed report into Hask's satchel rather than his
own. "I only insisted the two of us tell the truth about which of us actually found what. Those
aren't the same thing."
