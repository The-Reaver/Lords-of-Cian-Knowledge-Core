# The Cost of the Call He Made

*Locked canon, Batch 255, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1136`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle LXXIX, wave 27.
Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. The first entry where the failure is unambiguously Kanja's own
command decision.*

---

The raid on the smugglers' depot had gone wrong before Onyx was ever drawn, and it had gone wrong
because of a choice Kanja made himself, not because any enemy had outfought him or any tactic had
failed against him.

He'd read the depot's guard rotation from two days of watching and concluded, correctly as it turned
out, that the weakest window came an hour before the shift change he'd originally planned around. What
he hadn't accounted for was that the earlier window put his own entry team crossing open ground at a
point where a second, unrostered patrol — a detail he'd have caught with one more day of watching, a
day he'd decided the depot's own growing confidence didn't leave him — happened to be returning early.
A volunteer barely three weeks into the crew, eager past his experience, was on the exposed ground
when the patrol rounded the corner, and Kanja's own signal to advance had put him there a full breath
before backup could close the gap.

He didn't survive it.

Kanja carried him back himself rather than let anyone else do it, and said almost nothing for the two
days that followed. There was no enemy cleverness to study afterward, no gear to improve, no duelist's
unfamiliar style to learn from. There was only the honest fact, laid out with the same discipline the
tally method demanded of every owner it ever checked, that he had chosen to move a day earlier than
his own information warranted, and a volunteer who had trusted that choice had died for it.

"You couldn't have known about the second patrol," Corren Halst said, the closest thing to comfort
anyone offered him.

"I could have known if I'd waited the extra day. I chose not to wait." Kanja didn't accept the
comfort, and didn't ask anyone to stop offering it either. "That's not the same as an enemy beating
me. That's me making a call the information didn't support, because I wanted it done faster than it
needed to be."

He changed nothing about the raid's actual method afterward — the depot was still corrected within
the week, the smuggling still exposed and stopped through documentation rather than force. What
changed was smaller and stayed longer: a standing rule, applied to himself before anyone else, that no
operation moved on a window this thin without a second full day of watching first, regardless of how
confident the target's own routine made him feel. Garren Hask entered the volunteer's name in the
ledger's own casualty column for the first time in months, and Kanja read the entry himself before it
was closed, adding nothing to it and taking nothing away.
