# The Ones Too Small to Dig

*Locked canon, Batch 257, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1180`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle LXIX, wave 23, closing the wave. A child-labor discovery entry -- Kanja extracts illegally employed children from a subcontracted side gallery and forces a hard age floor into the new guild's charter.*

---

The side gallery wasn't on any of the crew's own survey maps, which was the first thing wrong with it. The second was the size of the footprints in the settling dust near its entrance -- too small for any journeyman, too consistent to be a single visitor passing through.

A subcontractor had been running the narrow gallery on a quota basis, paying by the cart rather than the hour, and had quietly discovered that children fit through gaps no adult crew member could without widening them, and cost less to feed than a grown laborer besides. Six of them were down there when Kanja found the gallery, the oldest maybe twelve, moving debris with tools sized for hands twice as large as theirs.

He didn't raise his voice. He got them out first -- carried two himself, walked the rest up slow enough that none of them mistook the pace for danger -- and only turned to the subcontractor once every child was standing in daylight, accounted for, fed.

"The safety training everyone else went through," Kanja said. "None of them had it. None of them could have passed it. That was never an oversight, was it."

"They dig where a grown man can't fit," the subcontractor said, like it settled the matter. "Cheaper, too."

"That's not an argument. That's a confession." Kanja's voice didn't rise even then. "You built a business on the fact that nobody would check a gallery too small for an adult to walk into and see what you were doing in it."

The tradesmen's association the crews had founded without his name on it took the case up formally rather than let it end as one confrontation -- the first real test of whether an institution built to outlast him could actually enforce something against a violation he'd personally uncovered rather than simply approve of what he brought them. They wrote a hard minimum age into the charter that week, backed by inspection rights any member could invoke without his presence required, and barred the subcontractor from every crew under the association's reach.

One of the six, small enough that the tool cart had genuinely dwarfed her, found him before the families collected them. "Am I in trouble?"

"No," Kanja said. "The trouble was never yours to carry. It was his, and now it's the guild's to make sure it stays that way."
