# The Signal Passed Too Many Hands

*Locked canon, Batch 282, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1480`). Crow King Alias Chronicle XCVI, wave 32, closing
it. Five active generations run one operation together for the first time, and the message breaks
somewhere in the middle of the chain. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

It was meant to be simple, which was exactly the problem.

Five people now carried the craft forward at once -- Kanja, the apprentice, the third generation, the
fourth generation, and a fifth barely a season into learning it -- and for the first time all five
were spread across one operation at once, a single message meant to travel from the apprentice at one
end of a valley to the fifth generation at the other, passed hand to hand through the third and fourth
along the way because no one person could carry it the whole distance and still watch their own post.

By the time it reached the fifth generation, *hold until the wagons clear the bridge* had become
*hold until the wagons clear*, four words instead of seven, and he held exactly that long -- long
enough that the wagons cleared the near end of the bridge and stopped, blocking it, while the column
behind them kept closing.

Nothing broke. The fourth generation caught the gap in time, close enough behind the fifth generation
to see the wagons stall and understand instantly what four words could no longer tell anyone. But it
was close, and everyone who'd touched the message on its way through knew exactly how close, because
each of them could point to the word they were fairly sure they'd been the one to drop.

They gathered afterward not to assign blame -- the fourth generation refused to let it become that --
but to look at the actual shape of the failure. "Two of us built this craft on trusting each other's
exact words," the third generation said. "We never built it for four hands passing one sentence
before it lands."

"So we stop passing sentences," the apprentice said. "Anything crossing more than two hands gets
repeated back whole before it moves again. It costs us seconds. It would have cost us the bridge."

Kanja, who had said nothing until then, only nodded. "The craft got old enough to need its own rules
about itself tonight. That's not a failure. That's what growing past three of us was always going to
cost, eventually. We just found out what the bill looked like before it took anything permanent."

growth in scale -- five active practitioners passing one message through more relay links than the
method was ever designed to survive -- distinct from every prior failure in this alias's run, which

rather than the structure of the lineage itself. Extends "The Student's First Failure" (Chronicle

person's mistake, and is resolved with a new standing practice (mandatory repeat-back confirmation
