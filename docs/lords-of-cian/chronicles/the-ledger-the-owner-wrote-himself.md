# The Ledger the Owner Wrote Himself

*Locked canon, Batch 198, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-763`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XXXVIII, wave 13 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The mill owner met them at the district gate before Ezio had so much as unpacked the tally table, a
thick, hand-bound book already under his arm, and the first thing he did was hand it over rather than
wait to be asked for anything.

"I wrote it myself," he said. "Every worker, every year of underpayment I let stand because the
season's numbers looked better that way, every corner I cut and told myself I'd make right later and
didn't. I'm not asking you to compile it. I'm asking you to check it, because I don't trust my own
honesty enough to publish it without someone else confirming I haven't quietly spared myself
somewhere without meaning to."

Ezio had cross-checked plenty of hostile records against production logs by then, and a handful of
cooperative ones besides, but he had never once been handed a confession this complete before a
single question had been asked. He spent two days on it anyway, with the same rigor he'd have used on
the most adversarial ledger in the campaign, because the owner's honesty didn't exempt his numbers
from verification — it only meant the verification, this time, mostly confirmed rather than
corrected what was already there.

"You underclaimed in three places," Ezio told him, at the end of it. "Against yourself. The
production logs show you owe more than you wrote down."

The owner took that harder than he'd taken the prospect of being caught in a lie. "I told myself I
was finally being fair. Even now I couldn't manage it without help."

"That's not a failure. Most men in your position don't write the book at all, let alone underclaim
against their own interest by mistake instead of on purpose." Kanja, who had said little through the
whole exchange, spoke for the first time since arriving. "You did more of this alone than any owner
I've met. Let the corrected number stand. That's the whole point of checking it — not to catch you,
but to make sure what gets published is actually complete."

The finished ledger went to the district's workers within the week, published under the owner's own
name rather than the crew's, the first settlement in the campaign that arrived already agreed upon
before a single worker had been asked to testify at all — trust extended first, and honored, rather
than earned district by district the slow way.

*Continuity notes (not narrative): an inversion of the established hostile-administrator pattern
(`MCD-373`, `MCD-406`) -- a mill owner preemptively compiles and publishes his own honest account
before being asked, going further than merely opening his books, and asks only for verification. The
verification still finds real (self-penalizing) errors, keeping the method's rigor intact even under
full cooperation. No new named characters. Second entry in the Industrial Myth's thirteenth wave.*
