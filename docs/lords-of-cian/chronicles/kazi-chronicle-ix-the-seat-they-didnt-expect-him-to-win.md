# Kazi Chronicle IX: The Seat They Didn't Expect Him to Win

*Locked canon, Batch 289, 2026-09-12 (`MCD-1528`). Fourth entry in Kazi's own Chronicles and the
first with Femi (`PH2-066`) as protagonist, not a Kanja Chronicle. Kanja appears only as a guest,
present but granted no command, no intervention, and no resolution credit, matching the established
convention. Narration is close-third on Femi throughout. Dramatizes the campaign and election
`PH2-066` already states as an accomplished fact, set in the months before Irin ever called a halt
from his own station.*

---

Femi had heard the word before he ever declared for the seat, and he heard it again the morning
after: *the movement's man.* Said flat, in the break-room, by men who had watched Irin's name spread
through the plant like a current through wet ground and had decided, without much discussion between
them, that a trustee seat was no place for whatever that current was. A union local's trustee handled
the pension books, the sick fund, the grievance backlog nobody wanted to read all the way through. It
was not, in the considered opinion of most of the men who'd hold their ballots that year, a place for
a man who spent his lunch breaks arguing that the whole floor was one body.

He did not argue the point. He had learned, watching Irin work and watching Kunle work and watching
Kalamu work, that a man who spent his energy answering a suspicion out loud usually spent it instead
of answering it with something a suspicious man could actually check. So Femi checked things. He sat
through four grievance hearings that spring that had nothing to do with any strike, any halt, any
cause larger than one man's disputed seniority date or another man's denied medical claim, and he
read the fund's own bylaws back to a hearing officer word for word until the officer stopped trying
to talk around them. He did this for men who had never once said Irin's name to him, some of whom
never would.

"You're running a campaign that doesn't mention him at all," Tunji said, watching Femi work through a
stack of case files one evening after the floor had cleared. "Most men would use it. The line's got
real weight behind it now."

"Most men aren't the ones being asked whether they'll use the seat to fight the plant or to fight for
the plant's own workers on the plant's own terms. Those aren't always the same fight, and I'd rather
the men voting on it not have to guess which one they're getting." Femi didn't look up from the page.
"If I win this because they believe I'll read the fund's books straight, I've won something that
holds. If I win it because I borrowed weight I didn't earn myself, I've won a seat I can't actually
use."

The incumbent, a company man in everything but title, ran the kind of campaign such men usually ran
against men like Femi — quiet warnings passed through foremen, a rumor two weeks before the vote that
a win for Femi meant the plant would tie up every fund disbursement in review out of spite. It cost
him votes. It also, in the way such things sometimes did, cost him more than he expected among the
exact men he'd meant to frighten: men who had sat across from Femi in a hearing room and watched him
argue their case on its own facts, with nothing in it for the larger cause at all, and had come away
trusting the specific thing they'd actually seen rather than the general thing they'd been warned
about.

He won by eleven votes, out of just under nine hundred cast — not a landslide, not a mandate anyone
could call overwhelming, a foothold and nothing grander than that. A stranger who had spent the last
three weeks handing out ballots at the count table, careful and unremarkable, said nothing when the
number was read aloud, and was gone from the hall before anyone thought to ask his name.

"Eleven votes," Femi said afterward, turning the number over the way he turned over a disputed
seniority date, looking for what it actually proved rather than what he wanted it to prove. "Not
enough men trust the seat itself yet to call it settled. Enough trust what I did with four hearing
files to give me a chance to prove the rest of it with everything after."
