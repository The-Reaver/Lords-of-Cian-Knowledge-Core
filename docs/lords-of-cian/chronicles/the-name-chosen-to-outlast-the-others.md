# The Name Chosen to Outlast the Others

*Locked canon, Batch 224, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1016`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LI, wave 17. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Valen laid the tally out on the table the way he laid out everything, plainly and without ornament:
Bane's face was known to every Directorate officer who'd survived a siege against him. The Trench
Monarch's name was tied, permanently, to a rebellion that would either win outright or be crushed
within the year, and either way would be remembered as exactly what it was. Neither could carry
forward into whatever came after without carrying the Rebellion's whole shape behind it.

"The Scourge is different," Valen said. "It was never given to you by an enemy classifying a threat
or a crew naming their own captain. It happened by accident, over water, and it's tied to no single
campaign anyone can date precisely. If you need a name that can run for decades without anyone being
able to draw a line back to a rebellion that ended, it's the only one of the three built to survive
its own history."

Efa Gol didn't like it, and said so. "It also means putting the sea between you and everything you're
actually fighting for on land."

"Not everything," Kanja said. "The Trust's slaving economy runs through every port I'd be sailing
past anyway. It's not a retreat from the work. It's a different shape of the same work, stretched out
longer than any of us have planned for yet."

Garren Hask, still young enough then that no one called him elderly, kept the tally book open and
didn't look up from it. "However long you carry it," he said, "somebody's going to keep the numbers.
That much I can promise stays the same, whatever name's on the ledger's cover."

Kanja weighed it the rest of that night — not the tactics, which were already decided, but the
smaller, stranger fact underneath them: that of everything he'd been called so far, the one nobody
had chosen for him was about to become the one he chose for himself, on purpose, to last longer than
any of the others ever would.

"Then it's this one," he said, "for as long as it needs to be."
