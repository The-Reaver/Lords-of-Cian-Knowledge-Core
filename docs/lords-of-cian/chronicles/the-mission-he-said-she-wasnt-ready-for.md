# The Mission He Said She Wasn't Ready For

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Captain Alias Chronicle, wave 13. Rebellion era. Not
a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters (the eager
crew member is deliberately left unnamed).*

---

She'd asked three times in as many weeks to be assigned to the next high-risk extraction, each time
with a case well-argued and a record that, on paper, said she'd earned the chance. Each time, Kanja
told her no.

"I've done everything you've asked of me," she said, the third time, frustration finally breaking
through the patience she'd been holding onto. "I've trained harder than half the people you've
already put on missions like this one."

"You have. That's not in question." He didn't soften the refusal, but didn't dismiss her either. "I
watched you freeze for half a second during the harbor drill last week, when the simulated fire got
close. Half a second isn't a flaw in you — most people freeze the first several times real danger
gets that close. But half a second on a live extraction is the difference between everyone getting
out and someone not getting out, and I'm not willing to find out which on a mission I could have
waited on instead."

"So when do I get to prove it, if you never let me near the thing that would prove it?"

"You prove it on drills that get harder without getting lethal, until the freeze stops happening
there first. That's not me doubting you. That's me refusing to let 'she wanted it badly enough' be
the reason someone doesn't come home. I'd tell any of the crew the same thing, and I have."

She didn't get the assignment. She also didn't leave the crew over it, and over the following
months threw herself into exactly the kind of harder, non-lethal drilling he'd described, until the
half-second freeze stopped showing up at all.

"I still think you were being overly cautious," she told him, months later, the day he finally
approved her for a mission of real consequence.

"Maybe I was. I'd rather be overly cautious with someone's life than find out the hard way I wasn't
cautious enough. You earned this the honest way — not because you wanted it enough to argue me into
it, but because the freeze is actually gone now. That matters more to me than how fast you got
here."
