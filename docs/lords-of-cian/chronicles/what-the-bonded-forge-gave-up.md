# What the Bonded Forge Gave Up

*Locked canon, Batch 262, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1320`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LXXIV, wave
25. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241), a Directorate-aligned foundry
running on indentured labor. A detailed, battle-intense Trinity combat showcase liberating bonded
adult workers. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The foundry ran three shifts around the clock and every worker in it owed a debt nobody had ever
seen the original paper for — a Scrip-Tether arrangement, inherited and compounded past any
honest accounting, that kept grown men and women producing siege iron for the Directorate under
threat of a debt that only ever grew no matter how much they forged against it. Word of it reached
the campaign the way most things did by now, through a Ghost-Lattice relay from a foreman's own
sister three towns over, desperate and specific about numbers: two hundred workers, six armed
overseers, a debt structure built to never actually close.

Kanja went in at the shift change, when the overseers were thinnest on the floor and least
expecting anything to move against a foundry that had never once needed real defending before.
Onyx of Oblivion's Whisper of Shadows crossed the yard before the first overseer's own footsteps
finished echoing off the walls, and Cadence Ruin read the floor's whole layout by the rhythm of
two hundred hammers all going at once, finding every overseer's position inside the noise they
thought was cover for their own movements rather than a map of it.

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System took the one overseer who reached for a worker as a hostage
rather than a fight — grounding the man's own swing back through his own frame before it landed,
a controlled, joint-locking redirection rather than a strike, the difference between ending a
threat and ending a person kept deliberate even in a fight this fast. Obsidian Malice's discharge
cycle answered the remaining overseers as a group, a single coordinated pulse that dropped every
weapon in the room to the floor at once without so much as scorching a worker standing three feet
from any of them — precision the Trinity had built its whole reputation on, spent here to end a
fight before it could become one the workers themselves might get caught inside.

It was over before most of the foundry floor understood what had happened, two hundred workers
standing amid their own unfinished siege iron with no debt left to owe and no overseer left
standing to collect it. Some wept. Some simply sat down where they stood, the particular
exhaustion of relief after a fear held too long to name out loud.

"The debt's paper, not real," Kanja told the foreman's sister, once the floor had settled enough
to hear him. "It was never going to close on its own. It only closes when someone with nothing
to lose decides it does."
