# The Quartermaster's Price

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle LI, wave 17, closing the wave. Rebellion era, during the active Sewer War of Killane (MCD-234, age 20). Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The crew was six days from running out of seasoned timber, and the only legal source left in the
district was the municipal quartermaster's office — a Trust-administered requisition desk that had no
idea it was about to sell half its remaining stock to the men digging tunnels under the garrison it
served.

Kanja filled out the request forms himself, in the same unhurried hand a real brace-setter's foreman
would use, and presented them across the counter without a trace of urgency he didn't want the clerk
to notice.

"Forty beams, rated for wet-gallery bracing, district repair order 's own account." The quartermaster
studied the paperwork longer than the request seemed to warrant. "That's twice what your district's
drawn in any prior month this war."

"Twice the work, since the flood rerouted two passages onto sections that weren't built for the
traffic." Kanja had the load figures ready before the man finished asking, real numbers from real
sections he'd genuinely reinforced. "I can walk you through every beam's intended use if the account
needs it justified line by line. I'd rather over-document a legitimate request than have it questioned
twice."

"It's not the legitimacy I'm questioning. It's the price." The quartermaster set down a second sheet
— a revised municipal rate, nearly double the standing tariff, dated three days prior and clearly not
yet reflected in the district's own budget. "War tax on materials moving through contested ground.
Nothing personal in it. Every requisition in the city's paying it now."

Forty beams at the new rate would have emptied the district's entire legitimate repair fund and left
nothing for the following month — a shortfall that would either expose the resistance's real
consumption rate or force them back onto black-market timber, which came with its own risks Kanja had
spent three weeks avoiding.

"Then let's talk about what forty beams at the old rate would cost you instead," Kanja said, laying
out a second proposal rather than an objection. "My district's crew has cleared two collapsed
passages this month that weren't on any municipal work order — passages your own maintenance division
hasn't had the manpower to reach. I'll trade that labor, documented and verified by whoever you send
to check it, against the difference in tariff. You get cleared ground your own books already needed
cleared. I get timber my district actually needs."

The quartermaster considered it longer than Kanja expected an ordinary bureaucrat to consider anything,
then pulled out a barter-credit form that clearly hadn't been used in months. "Labor-for-materials
trades used to be standard, before the war tax made everyone stop offering them. I can authorize it
if the clearance work checks out."

It checked out three days later, verified by an inspector who found exactly the cleared passages
Kanja had promised and nothing else worth noting. The forty beams reached the tunnel network at the
old rate, the district's books balanced without exposing a shortfall to anyone, and the quartermaster
filed the trade as ordinary municipal thrift rather than anything worth a second look.

"You didn't lie to him once," the crew chief said, when the timber finally arrived. "Just found the
one true trade that got you what a lie would've cost too much to try."

"That's most of what this trade is, if you look at it right." Kanja checked the first beam against
its rated tolerance, satisfied. "Not outsmarting the man across the counter. Just having something
real enough to offer him that neither of us needed to lie to close the deal."
