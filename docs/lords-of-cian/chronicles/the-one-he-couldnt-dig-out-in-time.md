# The One He Couldn't Dig Out in Time

*Locked canon, Batch 195, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-656`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XXI, wave 7 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The partial collapse trapped one man behind eleven feet of settled rubble, and Kanja worked the
face of it for forty minutes with Obsidian Malice and his own two hands, clearing debris in a
sequence careful enough not to bring the rest of the ceiling down on the very man he was trying to
reach.

He was eight feet short when the voice on the other side of the rubble stopped answering.

The last two feet came away clean, careful, exactly as controlled as the first nine — and it made
no difference at all. The man had been dead since somewhere in the first twenty minutes, crushed
before the digging even began in earnest, a fact no amount of skill or care could have changed
once the ceiling gave the way it had.

"You did everything right," the crew chief said, standing beside him in the cleared space where the
rescue had briefly, uselessly, succeeded. "Forty minutes of controlled clearing. Nobody could have
gone faster without bringing the rest down and losing more than just him."

"I know." Kanja didn't move for a while, one hand still resting against the stone he'd just spent
forty minutes reading correctly, uselessly. "That's the part that doesn't help as much as it
should. Knowing I did it right and knowing it still wasn't enough aren't the same feeling, even
though they're supposed to sit next to each other and cancel out."

"What do you tell his crew?"

"The truth. That I got to him too late to matter, not too slow to try. That the collapse killed him
before I ever started digging, and no version of me, Trinity or otherwise, gets to undo a ceiling
that already came down before anyone knew it had." He finally straightened, the stone dust still
on his hands. "I've spent this whole war learning that skill and patience beat almost anything
thrown at me. It's an uglier lesson learning what they don't beat. A man's already gone by the time
you get the chance to try."

The dead man's tools — a set of hand-worn chisels, nothing remarkable — went into Garren Hask's
growing account of the siege's true cost, not its victories, and the crew buried him properly before
resuming work on the same gallery the next morning, the digging continuing exactly as it always had,
now carrying one name it hadn't carried the day before.
