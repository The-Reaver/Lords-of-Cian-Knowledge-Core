# The Man Who Wanted to Fight

*Locked canon, Batch 130, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-438`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle VIII.
Rebellion era, age 19, the Furnace District Strike. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone
material. Kept deliberately unarmed, per this alias's established ethos. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Not every worker on the tally line believed patience was the strategy that would actually win. A
rival organizer — a hauler from a different section of the district, older and harder-used than
most, with three failed strikes of his own history behind him — arrived on the second day with a
following of his own and a plan built on burning the district administrator's own offices rather
than waiting four days for a ledger to finish proving what everyone already knew in their bones.

"Your numbers are good," the man told Kanja, not unkindly, but with the flat certainty of someone
who'd tried patience before and watched it fail. "I've seen good numbers before. I've watched
administrators look at good numbers and decide they'd rather burn the ledger than pay what it says
they owe. Numbers don't stop a man with power from simply refusing to be moved by them. Fire does."

"Fire stops one administrator. It doesn't stop the next one they send to replace him, and it hands
every future organizer a reason to be dismissed before they've said a single word." Kanja didn't
raise his voice to match the man's own rising frustration. "I'm not asking you to trust that patience
works because it's gentle. I'm asking you to trust it because it's the only version of this that
survives past the moment it happens. Burn the offices today, and in a year this becomes a story about
an angry mob instead of a story about workers who were owed something real and proved it beyond
argument."

"And if the numbers don't move him? If we finish your four days and the administrator still refuses?"

"Then I'll stand in front of this district myself and tell every worker here exactly that — that we
tried the version that leaves no room for dismissal, and it still wasn't enough, and now we decide
together what comes next with clear eyes instead of a fire nobody can walk back afterward." Kanja
held the man's gaze without flinching from the weight of the promise. "I'm not asking you to give up
the fight. I'm asking you to let the numbers make the case first, so that whatever comes after is
something nobody can call unreasonable."

The hauler didn't fully believe him — said as much, plainly, before he left — but he held his
following back long enough to let the four days run their course. The strike resolved on the fourth
day exactly as already recorded, the numbers alone proving sufficient, and the fire that never
started stayed exactly that: something that never had to happen.
