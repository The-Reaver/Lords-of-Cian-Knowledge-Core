# Lauris Chronicle LXXXIX: Twelve Names She Never Asked For

*Locked canon, Batch 300, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1706`). Eighty-ninth entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, continuing the second Strand L push and originating a new standing debt. Extends
Operation 13's already-locked aftermath (`MCD-1546`: Captain Drenneth "died during Directorate
processing despite her having delivered him alive," her private assessment that "her own procedural
cleanliness and the Directorate's were not the same standard") to the twelve Iron Veth crew members she
disabled rather than terminated during the same boarding, whose fate in Directorate custody she has
never investigated. Deepens rather than resolves -- the search begins and returns a genuinely mixed,
inconclusive result rather than a clean answer. No new named characters; Captain Drenneth, the Iron
Veth, and Vael Korr-Drennen already locked. Part of the second Lauris 50-Chronicle wave (Chronicles
LX-CIX), drafted under Abad's continued blanket authorization for this run.*

-----

*Archive fragment, present-day: "Six dead by my own hand, at the time, at least a clean accounting. Twelve
disabled and delivered, which I believed was the mercy. I have just learned that mercy and delivery were
never the same promise where the Directorate was concerned. I should have learned this decades ago,
from Drenneth alone."*

I have written, in this series, of Captain Drenneth — the defector Lauris acquired alive aboard the Iron
Veth and delivered, by every term of the contract, successfully, only to learn afterward that he had
died during Directorate processing regardless, a fact she has carried since as evidence that her own
procedural cleanliness and the institution's were never quite the same standard. What I had not
understood, until this account, was that Drenneth's death had never prompted her to ask the same
question about the twelve crew members she disabled rather than killed during the same boarding — men
she struck down, by her own account, specifically to preserve rather than end, and then handed, as
thoroughly as Drenneth himself, to an institution whose account of what happened next she has just told
me, plainly, that she has never once verified.

The question arose, as so many in this wave have, sideways rather than directly — raised in passing
during a conversation with Vael Korr-Drennen about an unrelated registry, when the historian mentioned,
almost as an aside, that Directorate processing records for non-lethal captures from the Established
Hunter period were kept in a separate archive from acquisition contracts proper, and that the two were
almost never cross-referenced by anyone outside the institution's own internal audits. Lauris went still,
by Vael Korr-Drennen's own later description of the moment, in a way the historian had not seen from her
before.

*

"I have spent this season learning that I trusted an institution's paperwork considerably more than I
should have," Lauris told me, once she had explained what the aside had made her realize. "I learned it
first with Sample K-403's register, which told me a fragment was 'disposed of' and meant, I now believe,
only that someone wanted it to read that way. I learned it again, decades ago, with Drenneth himself,
though I did not generalize the lesson at the time the way I should have. I have twelve names — men I
chose, specifically, not to kill — and I have never once asked what the institution did with the mercy
I handed it."

The search, conducted jointly with Vael Korr-Drennen across a week of records considerably harder to
navigate than either woman had expected, produced no single answer. Of the twelve, four records survive
intact and confirm ordinary long-term Directorate detention followed by eventual release under standard
terms — men who, so far as either woman could determine, lived out the consequence of that night in
roughly the shape Lauris had assumed for all twelve at the time. Three records show transfer to a
processing annex whose subsequent history matches, closely enough to trouble both women, the same
"charter quietly dissolved" pattern Vael Korr-Drennen had already traced once before in the Sample
K-403 search. The remaining five records simply stop — no release notation, no death notation, no
transfer, the kind of silence in an old register that Lauris has, by now, learned not to read as an
absence of information rather than a specific, deliberate kind of erasure.

*

"Four accounted for cleanly. Three that resemble a pattern I already distrust. Five that tell me
nothing at all," she said, reviewing the tally with me directly. "I do not know if this means eight men
I disabled rather than killed were, in some fraction of cases, disposed of by the institution anyway,
the way Drenneth was. I know it means I cannot currently rule that out, and that I have carried the
comfortable version of this story — twelve men spared, a clean mercy, a contract closed honorably — for
longer than the evidence, now that I have actually looked at it, appears to support."

She has not decided what, if anything, can still be done for the eight uncertain names, this many
decades after the fact. She has told me only that she intends to keep looking, alongside Vael
Korr-Drennen's own continuing search through the same decommissioned registries already carrying two
other open threads of hers, and that she no longer considers any Directorate captivity record — hers or
anyone else's — settled simply because the paperwork says it is.

Her closing note: *Twelve men. Four accounted for. Three I distrust. Five I cannot yet read at all. I
called this mercy once. I am no longer certain I am entitled to call anything mercy until I know what
became of it.*

I do not know, setting this down, whether the five silent records will ever resolve into anything Lauris
can act on, or whether this is simply one more debt destined to sit, like so many others in this
account, precisely located and permanently unfinished. I know only that a night she once considered among
the cleanest of her Directorate career has become, on closer examination, one more ledger she is no
longer willing to leave unaudited.
