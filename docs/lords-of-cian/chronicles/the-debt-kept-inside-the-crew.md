# The Debt Kept Inside the Crew

*Locked canon, Batch 245, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1072`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
LXII, wave 21. The restraint-over-fear doctrine turned inward, on the fleet's own crew, for the first
time. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

It was Garren Hask who found the discrepancy, the way he found everything — not through suspicion but
through arithmetic. The manifest of goods recovered from a foundering merchant family during a rescue
three weeks prior listed a ring, silver, engraved on the inside band, among the effects the fleet had
promised to see returned once the family reached safe port. The ring wasn't in the crate anymore. Nor
was a modest sum of coin that should have sat beside it.

He didn't accuse anyone. He simply began cross-checking the crate's handling log against who'd stood
watch over it between the rescue and the crate's sealing, the same patient method he'd have used to
trace a discrepancy in an enemy's supply record, until the list of who could have taken it narrowed to
one name — a young rigger, three years aboard, well liked, with no history of anything like it.

The rigger didn't deny it when Kanja asked him directly, alone, away from the rest of the crew. He'd
meant to sell the ring at the next port and send the coin home to a mother who'd been sick since
before he'd shipped out — not greed exactly, or not only greed, but he knew what he'd done and didn't
pretend otherwise.

Word got out anyway, the way it always did aboard a ship this size, and the older hands expected one
of two things: the lash, the traditional answer to theft from a rescued family's own goods, or
banishment at the next port, the fleet's usual answer to anyone who broke faith with what it stood
for. Kanja gave neither. "We tell people who are drowning that stepping onto our deck doesn't mean
stepping into a new kind of danger," he said, to the rigger and to the crew both, gathered on
*The Receipt*'s open deck. "That promise doesn't bend because the danger this time came from inside
the crew instead of outside it. But it also doesn't mean pretending nothing happened."

The rigger returned the ring himself, in person, to the family at their next port of call, and worked
off the value of the coin in labor — logged, by Garren Hask, in the same ledger that tracked disabled
hulls and freed captives, a debt owed and a debt paid the same as any other kind the book recorded.
Efa Gol, who'd argued privately for a harder line, watched the entry go in and said only that she'd
expected the ledger to flinch from writing a crewman's own name beside a theft. It didn't.

Six months on, the rigger was still aboard, trusted again in the ordinary, unremarked way that meant
the trust had actually been rebuilt rather than merely declared. Kanja never brought it up again, and
neither did anyone else — which was, Garren Hask noted privately, closer to the doctrine working than
any speech about it could have been.
