# The Caravan That Didn't Know His Name

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LII, wave 18. Rolling Foundry Campaign, age 27. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

They came down the inland road in a train of a dozen wagons, drovers and traders from far enough
beyond the Trust's reach that the war itself was a rumor to them and the Lord of Embers wasn't even
that. The lead drover addressed the whole of The Anvil's dockside crew as a group, uncertain which
of them was in charge, and settled on Kanja only because he happened to be nearest and carrying
tools.

"We're told there's a forge operation working this coast that trades fair," she said. "Wheel iron,
axle stock, whatever you've got surplus of. We're not particular about who runs it."

"That'd be us." Kanja set his tools down. "What are you offering against it?"

She laid out dyed cloth, preserved foodstuffs, a few crates of goods the campaign had never seen
before, none of it worth more or less to her for who he was. She hadn't asked his name. She wasn't
going to.

They traded through the afternoon on nothing but the goods in front of them — Kanja weighing stock
against her offer the same unhurried way he weighed a reheat, no reputation doing any of the work
either side of the table, the apprentices handling the actual iron while the two of them settled
terms neither needed a war-name to make fair. When it came time to load the wagons, one of the
younger apprentices, still unused to a stranger who plainly didn't know, started to explain who
they'd just traded with.

The drover raised a hand before he got far. "Don't. Whatever the story is, I got a fair trade off a
man who counted the axle stock twice to make sure he wasn't shorting me. That's the whole of what I
need to know before I come back through here next season. Save the rest for someone it'll impress."

"It wouldn't impress you?" Kanja asked, more curious than offended.

"Might. Might not. Point is I already decided whether I trust this floor before I knew there was
anything else to decide about it." She swung up onto the lead wagon's bench. "Most places I trade,
the name comes first and the honesty's supposed to follow from it. Here it ran the other way round.
I'll take that arrangement again, name or no name."

The caravan rolled out toward the inland road as it had arrived, the crew watching it go with
nothing settled between them but a fair trade, cleanly made, that would bring the same wagons back
next season regardless of whatever else anyone chose to tell them in the meantime.
