# The Number She Wouldn't Give

ethos. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

The weaver sat across from Ezio's ledger table with her hands folded in her lap and said nothing
for long enough that he set his pen down.

"I know what you're owed," Kanja said, crouching so he was level with her instead of standing over
the table. "I've seen your loom assignment, your hours, the district's own production count. I
could write the number myself without you saying a word. I'm asking you to say it because it should
be yours to give, not mine to guess."

"If I say it," she said, "you'll ask why it's smaller than what the loom count says I earned."

"Probably."

"And I'd have to tell you the rest of it. Where the difference went." She looked past him, toward
the yard, where another woman was hauling bolts of cloth she had no business lifting alone. "It went
to her. Her boy's been sick two winters running. I've been signing half my wage over to the healer
on her behalf since the first winter, and if I put the true number in your book, everyone in this
district will know exactly how much I gave and exactly how much she needed, and she'll never look at
me the same way again. Charity she can live with quietly. Charity written down in a rebel's ledger
for the district administrators to read — that she can't."

Kanja didn't answer right away. He'd built the method on the premise that a documented truth was
always worth more than a comfortable silence, and he sat with how wrong that premise felt, just
this once, before he spoke again.

"Then I won't write the number," he said. "I'll write that it's larger than stated, and unknown, and
leave it there. The district administrators can argue with a woman who won't give her figure. They
can't argue with a woman who won't give her figure and a rebel who's telling them, in writing, that
he trusts her reasons more than he trusts their curiosity."

She studied him for a moment, the way people did when they expected him to push harder and he
didn't. "You'd leave a hole in your own book."

"It's not a hole. It's the truest entry in it — that some debts are owed by people to each other, not
just by owners to workers, and that not every one of those is mine to record." He nodded toward
Ezio, who had already begun writing without being told what to write. "He'll mark it 'withheld by
the worker's own choice, amount confirmed greater than declared.' That stands on its own. It doesn't
need your friend's name in it to be true."

She gave her number after that — not the real one, but a figure close enough to satisfy the district
count without exposing the difference, and Ezio recorded it exactly as she gave it, no correction
made. It was the first entry in four districts he hadn't quietly adjusted upward, and he never told
her why.
