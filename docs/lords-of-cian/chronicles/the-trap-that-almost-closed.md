# The Trap That Almost Closed

*Locked canon, Batch 123, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-416`). Crow King Alias Chronicle IV, first entry in
the second wave. Rebellion era. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The one soldier who nearly ended the Crow King's entire evasion doctrine was a private nobody had
thought to brief on the Hymn-Engine's sub-bass frequency signature, because privates weren't
generally trusted with classified countermeasure theory — which was precisely why he heard something
none of his superiors' sensor-trained ears had learned to filter out.

"That's not the marsh," he said, to a sergeant too busy watching the instrument readings to listen.
"Listen to it. That's not natural sound. That's people. I grew up near a work-chant district. I know
what three hundred voices trying not to sound like voices sounds like."

The sergeant dismissed it twice before the private, on his own initiative and against explicit
orders, broke from his position to investigate the sound's actual source rather than trust the
instruments that had already been fooled once before. He found the edge of the crew's real movement
column within minutes — no scarecrow trick this time, simply a soldier who'd noticed what a
purpose-built sensor grid had been specifically engineered to miss.

Kanja met him alone, the column still exposed, the evacuation only two-thirds complete.

"You heard something nobody else did," he said, not reaching for a weapon, reading the private's own
genuine uncertainty rather than trained confidence. "That's rare. Most soldiers trust their
instruments over their own ears, even when the instruments have already been wrong once."

"I'm going to report this." The private hadn't drawn his own weapon either, caught somewhere between
duty and the strange, unthreatening calm of the man he'd supposedly caught. "I have to."

"I know. I'm not going to stop you, and I'm not going to ask you not to." Kanja kept his voice level,
buying the last of the evacuation's time with nothing but a conversation. "But by the time your
report reaches anyone who can act on it, this column will already be somewhere your report can't
follow. You found the one flaw in tonight's plan. That's a real, rare skill, and it's not going to
change what happens in the next ten minutes, only what happens the next time someone tries this
exact trick again."

The private filed his report accurately, in full, and it became the single most useful piece of
intelligence the Directorate would gather against the Hymn-Engine doctrine in the entire war — proof,
the crew's own after-action review concluded, that the real vulnerability was never the trick's
mechanics, but the plain, irreducible fact that any deception loud enough to fool machines was still,
eventually, audible to a person who simply knew how to listen.
