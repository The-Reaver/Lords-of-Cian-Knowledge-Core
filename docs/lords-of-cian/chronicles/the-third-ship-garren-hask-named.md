# The Third Ship Garren Hask Named

The keel had been laid in secret over four months, timber and labor scavenged from three separate
sympathetic ports so that no single Trust informant could ever piece together the whole of what was
being built — a third flagship-class vessel, larger than *The Audit* and *The Receipt* both, meant to
carry the fleet's ambitions somewhere neither of the first two ships had been built to reach.

Garren Hask had named both prior flagships without ceremony, the counter's own private habit of
translating what a ship meant into a single word that outlasted every battle it fought — and when the
new hull was finally ready for her name, the whole crew gathered on her deck rather than let the moment
pass the way the first two namings had, quietly, almost as an afterthought.

"You want it to mean something this time," Kanja said, watching him study the ship the way he studied a
ledger, looking for the entry that would balance everything else.

"It's always meant something. First one, *The Audit* — because we were done letting the Trust write
the only account of what we'd taken from them and what we'd give back." Garren Hask ran a hand along
the rail, unhurried. "Second, *The Receipt* — proof, for anyone who doubted the first ship's math, that
the accounting was real and it was ours."

"And this one?"

He was quiet for a long moment, the whole crew waiting without impatience, used by now to the counter's
particular way of arriving at a name only once he was certain it was correct.

"*The Ledger,*" he said finally. "Not the audit that catches what was taken. Not the receipt that
proves it. The whole account — everyone we've freed, everyone we've fed, everyone who's still owed
something the war hasn't paid back yet. This ship carries that. Not just cargo. The whole running
total of what this fleet's actually for."

The crew's cheer at the christening carried further than either of the first two namings had, not
because the ship was larger, though she was, but because three years into a war fought largely in
rumor and shadow, the fleet had grown enough to need a vessel built to hold the full weight of what it
had actually accomplished rather than just the next single engagement.

"Three ships," Kanja said quietly, once the ceremony had wound down and *The Ledger* sat finally
finished at anchor. "You've named every one of them."

"Someone has to keep the account current." Garren Hask allowed himself, briefly, something like pride.
"Ship needs a name that tells the truth about what it's carrying. That's not decoration. That's just
good bookkeeping."
