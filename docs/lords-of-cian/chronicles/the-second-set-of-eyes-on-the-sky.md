# The Second Set of Eyes on the Sky

*Locked canon, Batch 160, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-505`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XII, closing
the fourth wave. Rebellion era, age 29, the Gale Straits. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone
material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters beyond the already-locked
Sephtis.*

---

Sephtis had trained no successor across every engagement the fleet had fought under his predictions
— a deliberate choice, he admitted, born from a quiet fear that his own gift was singular enough that
teaching it to someone else risked producing a false confidence in a method that couldn't actually be
replicated. The events of the fourth wave finally changed his mind.

"I watched a fleet lose six people because I never built any redundancy into what I do," he told
Kanja, requesting a young signal-reader from the crew be assigned to shadow him rather than being
asked. "If something happens to me — an ordinary illness, an unlucky blow, nothing dramatic — this
entire doctrine dies with me. That's not a risk I should have let the fleet carry this long without
saying something."

"What changed your mind, specifically?"

"Watching the margin I set too narrow cost people who trusted the timing completely. I don't know
that a second set of eyes would have caught what I missed. I know a second set of eyes gives this
fleet something to fall back on if I'm ever not here to catch it myself." Sephtis said it without
self-pity, a practical correction rather than a confession of failure. "I should have built this
redundancy years ago. I let my own uncertainty about whether the gift could even be taught stop me
from trying."

The training began within the week — slow, uneven, the young reader's early predictions carrying none
of Sephtis's own precision, but genuine progress visible within months rather than years. Kanja
watched the arrangement develop without interfering, giving Sephtis the same space to teach that he'd
once given every craft he'd personally overseen.

"She won't replace what you do," Kanja observed, watching an early lesson unfold. "I don't think
that's the point, though."

"No. The point was never replacement. The point was making sure this fleet never again depends
entirely on one person's gift holding without fail, forever, with nothing behind it if it doesn't." Sephtis
watched his student work through a forecast with visible, careful concentration. "I should have
understood that a long time before six people had to pay for me not understanding it sooner."
