# The Compass That Pointed Wrong

*Locked canon, Batch 280, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1461`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
XCV, wave 32. A new register: a years-embedded, genuinely willing Directorate agent among the crew is
unmasked not by betrayal or discovery but by his own conscience, refusing a final order rather than
carrying it out. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Efa Gol had trained Fenn herself, four years back — a quiet, competent signalman who'd never given her
a moment's doubt, right up until the morning he was supposed to relay a false bearing that would have
put The Receipt directly into a Directorate ambush lane, and didn't.

"I can't," he told her, standing at the signal post with the flag halfway raised and his hand shaking
on the line. "I've had that order four years. Carry it, wait, carry it, wait. I always told myself I'd
know when the moment was real. It's real now. And I can't."

He told her everything after that, unprompted, faster than she could have gotten it out of him with
threats: recruited young, placed deliberately, patient beyond what any ordinary intelligence operation
usually bothered with — four years of honest work as cover for one order that never came due until it
finally did. He hadn't leaked a single true bearing in all that time. He'd simply waited, the way he'd
been trained to, for a use that turned out to cost more than he had left in him to pay.

Garren Hask wanted him in irons before Efa Gol finished relaying it. Kanja wanted to hear the rest
first.

"Four years is a long compass to hold steady," Kanja said, when Fenn had run out of words. "Long enough
that whatever pointed you here in the first place isn't necessarily what's pointing you now. What do
you actually want?"

"I don't know anymore," Fenn said, and that, more than the confession itself, was what convinced Efa
Gol he was telling the truth.

They put him ashore three ports later, quietly, with passage money and no record of what he'd been —
not punished, not welcomed aboard as one of their own, simply released from an assignment he'd finally
found the nerve to refuse. Garren Hask logged it as a single line: *one compass, recalibrated, returned
to open water.*
