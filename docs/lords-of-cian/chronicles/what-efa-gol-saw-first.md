# What Efa Gol Saw First

*Locked canon, Batch 128, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-433`). Bane Alias Chronicle IX, closing the third wave.
Rebellion era, age 19, shortly after the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone
material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters beyond the already-locked
Efa Gol.*

---

Most of the crew had started treating the name the way the Directorate did — something to be
careful around, even among friends, even backstage where the theatrics weren't for anyone's benefit
but their own morale. Efa Gol had never quite managed the trick of being careful around him, mostly
because she'd known him since before there was a name at all, and old habits didn't bend easily to
new reputations.

"You're doing the thing again," she said, finding him alone after the Kessic Overwatch surrendered,
still in half the armor, staring at nothing in particular. "The thing where you win and then look
like you lost something doing it."

"I didn't lose anything today. The garrison surrendered clean." He didn't look up. "That's the best
outcome available under the circumstances."

"That's not what I asked." Efa Gol sat down across from him, uninvited, the way she'd been sitting
down uninvited since before either of them had titles worth mentioning. "I watched you carry six
wounded men off a field last week when nobody would have blinked if you'd left them. I watched you
leave a captain's crossbow charge unspent today when you could have ended it faster and cleaner. The
rest of the crew's started believing the name the way the enemy does — something that doesn't feel
things the ordinary way anymore. I don't believe that. I've never believed that. I want to know if
you still do."

He was quiet long enough that she nearly repeated the question.

"Some days it's harder to remember than others," he said finally. "The name does what it needs to
do. It ends fights faster than fighting would. But it costs something to be feared correctly, and
some days I can't entirely find the line between playing the part and becoming it. You asking the
question the way you just did — that's the thing that finds the line for me, most days. I don't
think I could do this much longer without someone who still remembers what it looked like before
there was a name to hide behind."

"Then I'll keep asking." She stood, offering a hand to pull him up. "Somebody's got to be the one
who saw it first and isn't afraid to say so out loud. Might as well be me."
