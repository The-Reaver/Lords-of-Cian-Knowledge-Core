# The Tier They Called Reduced

*Locked canon, Batch 278, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1449`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle CI, wave 34. The method's first wage-tier discrimination case, distinct from the earlier testimony-credibility entry for a blind quarry worker (MCD-1154).*

---

The weaving-house's own posted schedule called it the "reduced tier," a fixed lower wage rate applied to every worker missing a hand, an eye, or full use of a leg, regardless of what the loom in front of them actually required. The owner didn't dispute the tier existed. He considered it self-evidently fair — reduced capacity, reduced pay — and had never once measured whether the assumption was true.

Ezio measured it. Six weeks of output records, loom by loom, worker by worker, showed what the owner had never bothered to check: three of the reduced-tier weavers were outproducing able-bodied workers on the same floor, and none were producing meaningfully less than the tier's own stated justification assumed.

"The tier isn't measuring capacity," Ezio told him, laying the output sheets across the owner's own desk. "It's measuring what you assumed capacity would be before anyone checked."

"They're still missing what they're missing," the owner said. "It's not unkind. It's realistic."

"It's realistic about nothing your own looms actually show." Kanja didn't argue the tier's fairness in the abstract. He let the output numbers make the case the way they'd made every other case the method had ever built — the worker with one hand who'd adapted her grip years ago and matched the floor's fastest pace, the man with a stiff leg whose seated work at the loom never touched his legs at all. "Pay for what the loom takes out of a hand, since that's the only thing the wage was ever supposed to measure. Not for an assumption about a body you never actually watched work."

The owner resisted longer over principle than he had over any single figure in the crew's experience — not because the arithmetic was unclear, but because abandoning the tier meant admitting he'd built a rule on nothing but appearance. It was the finding's plain, repeated comparison — three tier workers outproducing floor average, corrected wages applied per output rather than per body — that finally moved him, not any argument about fairness in principle.

"The tier's gone," he said at last, striking the schedule's own line through with his own hand rather than have Ezio do it for him. "I should have measured before I assumed."

"Most people don't," Kanja said. "That's the whole reason the ledger exists — not to punish the assumption, just to show what was true the entire time underneath it."
