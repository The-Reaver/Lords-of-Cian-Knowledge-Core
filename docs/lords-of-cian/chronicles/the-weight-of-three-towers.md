# The Weight of Three Towers

*Locked canon, Batch 197, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-717`). The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XXII, wave 8 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Three siege towers advanced on the redoubt's north wall together, wood-framed and iron-banded, each
one held rigid by rope-and-chain tensioning systems running through their internal scaffolding — a
different structural problem than any Crawler, the tension distributed across timber joints as much
as metal, and moving under its own crew's effort rather than an engine's.

Kanja read the lead tower first, the Aegis-Talisman's lens tracing the rope tension through the iron
bands binding the frame together, and found the same underlying principle he'd spent years applying
to Crawlers: wherever something was held together by force rather than simple weight, that force
had a voice.

The first tower's ropes came apart in a cascading sequence, each band failing into the next as the
resonance found every load-bearing point in turn, the whole structure settling rather than collapsing
outright — deliberately, since a hard collapse would have thrown the crew still climbing its interior
ladders. Mafesto intercepted the second tower's approach directly, the Kinetic Transfer System
absorbing the arrow volley meant to cover it while Obsidian Malice's discharge found the tower's own
iron corner-bracing. The third tower's crew, watching two of their own come apart without a visible
cause, abandoned the approach before Kanja ever turned his attention to it.

Onyx moved through the settling first tower's crew as they climbed free, Cadence Ruin finding the gaps
between men too disoriented by their own structure's failure to coordinate a defense.

"Two towers down, and the third ran," the redoubt's garrison commander said afterward, surveying the
abandoned engine still standing intact at the tree line. "I've never seen a siege broken without a
single arrow loosed at the men crewing it."

"I didn't need the men. I needed the towers to stop being usable, and towers held together by rope and
band tension are no different, underneath the wood dressing, than anything else I've spent years
learning to read. The crew ran because the thing they were standing inside stopped feeling safe to
stand inside. That's not a weapon. That's just honesty about what was always true of it."
