# The Crown He Dug With His Own Hands

*Locked canon, Batch 129, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-434`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle VII, first entry
in the third wave. Rebellion era, age 18, before the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. New
standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The dredge line at Warehouse Twelve ran twelve hours a shift, and nobody on it — not the newest
conscript, not the foreman who'd worked the line for thirty years — expected the boy who'd started
answering to "Trench Monarch" to actually take a shovel and work it alongside them rather than simply
oversee the organizing from a safe distance.

He'd been doing it for three weeks straight by the time anyone worked up the nerve to ask him why.

"You could be planning the next move instead of standing in mud up to your knees," the foreman said,
watching him work a stuck cart free with the same unhurried patience he brought to everything. "Nobody
would think less of you for it. You've earned the right to direct instead of dig."

"I haven't earned anything yet. That's the point." Kanja set his weight into the cart, and it came
free with a wet, grinding release. "I didn't inherit a crown from anybody. Nobody handed me a claim
to this work or a right to speak for the people doing it. If I want either of those things to mean
something, I have to dig for it the same as everyone else standing in this mud — one shovelful at a
time, until the dirt under my nails says I earned whatever they end up calling me."

"That's not how titles usually work."

"Then maybe that's exactly the problem with how titles usually work." He straightened, wiping mud
from his hands onto trousers that had long since stopped being anything but work-stained. "A crown
somebody hands you is a crown somebody else can take back. One you dig out of the ground yourself,
with your own hands, alongside the people who'll actually have to live under whatever you decide to
do with it — that one's harder to take from you. I intend for whatever I end up wearing to be that
kind."

The foreman didn't have an answer for that. He didn't need one. Word of it moved down the line the
way everything did — not as a speech, because there hadn't been one, just a boy still working a
shovel three weeks after anyone would have forgiven him for stopping.
