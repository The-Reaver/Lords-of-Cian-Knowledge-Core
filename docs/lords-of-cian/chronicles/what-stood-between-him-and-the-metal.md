# What Stood Between Him and the Metal

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XX. Rebellion era,
age 25. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material — a tactical/positioning entry rather
than a mechanic-defeating one. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The engineering corps had stopped trying to out-design the doctrine after four failed alloy and
insulation attempts. Their fifth response wasn't technical at all: a screen of loose-stacked rubble
and timber scaffolding built up in front of the true gate mechanism, thick enough that no one could
physically reach the actual chain without first clearing thirty feet of deliberately unstable cover,
under fire, while doing it.

"They've stopped trying to defeat the read," Kanja observed, studying the screen from cover. "They're
just trying to keep me from ever getting close enough to take one."

It changed the shape of the engagement more than any prior countermeasure had. The doctrine needed
contact, or near enough to it, and the rubble screen was built specifically to make contact the
expensive part rather than the diagnosis. Mafesto took point, the Kinetic Transfer System absorbing
the covering fire meant to hold anyone back from the screen, while Onyx cleared a narrow lane through
the loose stone by hand rather than force, careful not to trigger the deliberate instability the
engineers had built into the pile.

It took eleven minutes to close forty feet that would have taken four in open ground. Kanja reached
the true gate with the lens already half-raised, the reading coming back clean the instant contact
was made, the whole elaborate screen rendered irrelevant the moment distance stopped being a
variable.

"Clever," he admitted afterward, looking back at the cleared lane. "They finally understood the
doctrine doesn't care how hard the structure is to reach. It only cares whether I can eventually touch
it. They just made touching it cost more than it used to."

"Will it work again?" Onyx asked, the question carrying its own flat certainty about the answer.

"Once, on someone who hasn't studied what happened here. Not twice. That's true of almost everything
they've tried against this doctrine so far — and it's true of almost everything I've tried against
them too."
