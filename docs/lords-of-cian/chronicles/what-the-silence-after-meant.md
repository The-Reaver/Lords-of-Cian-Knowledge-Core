# What the Silence After Meant

*Locked canon, Batch 254, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1111`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXXXI, wave 27, closing wave
27. A wholly non-combat, humanitarian use of Whisper of Shadows -- escorting a dying informant safely
across contested ground purely so they can deliver one final piece of testimony before they die.*

---

The informant could barely stand on his own by the time word reached Bane that he wanted to talk. Not
about anything urgent — no operation, no tip worth verifying. He'd spent eleven years feeding
fragments of intelligence to whoever would take the risk of carrying them, and the thing eating him
from the inside had finally reached the stage where he had weeks left, maybe less, and one specific
account he wanted told to someone who'd understand what it had cost him, before he ran out of time to
tell it himself.

He couldn't fight if caught. Couldn't run. The territory between his hiding place and the safehouse
where he wanted to say it was thick with Directorate patrols who'd have taken a great deal of interest
in a man who'd spent over a decade feeding them nothing but carefully constructed half-truths.

Bane didn't bring a raiding party. He brought Whisper of Shadows and nothing else that mattered for
this — walked the man across eleven miles of contested ground at a pace an dying body could manage,
past patrol after patrol whose attention slid past two moving shapes the same way it had slid past one
in the depot months before, and reached the safehouse without a single engagement, without a weapon
drawn, without anyone on either side ever knowing they'd passed.

The informant told his account to the two people waiting at the safehouse — not Bane, who stood at
the door rather than the room, deliberately giving him the moment without inserting himself into it —
and died eight days later, in a bed rather than in hiding, having said the thing he'd needed to say.

Danne Sok asked, afterward, why this one had warranted the risk of the crossing at all, when the man
had nothing left to offer the operation.

"He wasn't offering anything to the operation," Bane said. "He was asking for something for himself,
after eleven years of giving everything he had to ours. That's worth eleven miles of walking quietly.
It doesn't have to be worth anything more than that to be worth doing."
