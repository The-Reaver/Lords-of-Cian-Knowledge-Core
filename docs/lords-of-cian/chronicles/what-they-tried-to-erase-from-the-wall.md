# What They Tried to Erase From the Wall

*Locked canon, Batch 286, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1521`). Captain Alias Chronicle CI, wave 34. A detailed full-Trinity combat showcase defending the Pier Nine memorial wall (`MCD-1372`) itself from a targeted attack aimed at erasing the crew's history rather than harming its people.*

---

They came at the deep hour of the night when the wall stood emptiest, six of them, unlisted holdouts from the old Trust hierarchy who had never signed anything and never accepted that the peace applied to them — and they hadn't come for a person at all. They'd come with charges meant for stone: Joran's name, Garren Hask's, Tam Sullen's, every name Mira had ever carved with her own hands, gone in one calculated blast timed for when no watch was posted to see it happen.

Onyx of Oblivion's Whisper of Shadows found them first, the same power that had once picked a raiding party out of a mountain pass before they'd shown themselves at all (`MCD-1367`) — six heartbeats moving with too much deliberate quiet along a stretch of seawall that should have held none. Kanja was there before the first charge was fully seated.

He didn't fight to protect himself. He fought to protect stone, which changed the shape of everything. Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System caught the lead charge's detonation a half-second after it triggered — too late to stop the blast outright, exactly in time to redirect its full force out over open water instead of across the names it had been aimed at, the wall's own surface catching nothing worse than a scatter of harmless spray. Obsidian Malice discharged once, controlled and precise, breaking the second team's footing on the seawall's narrow walk without breaking anything else, denying them the ground to plant a second charge at all. Onyx's Cadence Ruin read the last two through their own rushed, panicked rhythm and put them down disarmed before either could so much as strike a spark.

Callum Breck arrived with the shore watch two minutes later to find it already finished — six holdouts bound and breathing, the wall standing exactly as it had stood that morning, not one name lost. Mira came running not long after, having felt the concussion through the dock boards from half a mile off, and stood a long moment in front of the wall before she trusted it enough to touch it.

"They didn't want to kill anyone tonight," Kanja told her. "They wanted it to be like none of it ever happened at all. Like your father, like Hask, like every name on this wall never cost anyone anything to lose."

"It didn't work," Mira said, running two fingers lightly over her father's carved name, still whole.

"No," Kanja agreed. "It didn't."
