# What They Tried to Erase From the Wall

*Locked canon, Batch 286, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1521`). Captain Alias Chronicle CI, wave 34. A detailed combat showcase defending the Pier Nine memorial wall (`MCD-1372`) itself from a targeted attack aimed at erasing the crew's history rather than harming its people, fought with Kanja's post-Trinity Long Mask-era kit -- the Forge-Coat, Ironfall Boots, Ironhand Gauntlets, Sovereign Eyes, and the Rexmar Machete -- rather than the sealed Trinity. Corrected, Batch 314, 2026-09-28: an earlier draft mistakenly had Kanja fighting with Mafesto, Onyx of Oblivion, and Obsidian Malice, all of which are sealed in the vault for the entire 284-year Long Mask era per `MCD-246`, and this entry is set well within that era; the prose below has been rewritten to use his actual Long Mask-era gear (`ARS-344` through `ARS-356`), his own trained swordsmanship, and his instinctive Rexmar-Mar tactical sense instead. Also corrected Batch 321, 2026-10-02: reworded the Sovereign Eyes' perception function from its amber "glow" (a fear-response signal others see, per `ARS-350`) to its overlay.*

---

They came at the deep hour of the night when the wall stood emptiest, six of them, unlisted holdouts from the old Trust hierarchy who had never signed anything and never accepted that the peace applied to them — and they hadn't come for a person at all. They'd come with charges meant for stone: Joran's name, Garren Hask's, Tam Sullen's, every name Mira had ever carved with her own hands, gone in one calculated blast timed for when no watch was posted to see it happen.

The Sovereign Eyes' overlay found them first — six heartbeats moving with too much deliberate quiet along a stretch of seawall that should have held none, the overlay picking out heat and motion no unaided eye would have caught in that dark. Kanja's own instinct did the rest, the same inherited Rexmar-Mar sense for reading a threat's shape before it fully declared itself that had never needed any blade or armor to work. Kanja was there before the first charge was fully seated.

He didn't fight to protect himself. He fought to protect stone, which changed the shape of everything. The Ironfall Boots found their grounding a half-second after the lead charge triggered — too late to stop the blast outright, exactly in time for Kanja, braced through the Forge-Coat's own reinforced weave, to redirect its full force out over open water instead of across the names it had been aimed at, the wall's own surface catching nothing worse than a scatter of harmless spray. The Ironhand Gauntlets did the rest on the second team: one controlled blow, leverage and trained technique rather than any trick of the metal, breaking the second team's footing on the seawall's narrow walk without breaking anything else, denying them the ground to plant a second charge at all. Kanja's own instinct read the last two through their own rushed, panicked rhythm, and the flat of the Rexmar Machete, wielded through nothing but trained swordsmanship, put them down disarmed before either could so much as strike a spark.

Callum Breck arrived with the shore watch two minutes later to find it already finished — six holdouts bound and breathing, the wall standing exactly as it had stood that morning, not one name lost. Mira came running not long after, having felt the concussion through the dock boards from half a mile off, and stood a long moment in front of the wall before she trusted it enough to touch it.

"They didn't want to kill anyone tonight," Kanja told her. "They wanted it to be like none of it ever happened at all. Like your father, like Hask, like every name on this wall never cost anyone anything to lose."

"It didn't work," Mira said, running two fingers lightly over her father's carved name, still whole.

"No," Kanja agreed. "It didn't."
