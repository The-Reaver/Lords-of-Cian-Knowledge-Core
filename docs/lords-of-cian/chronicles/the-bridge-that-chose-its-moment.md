# The Bridge That Chose Its Moment

*Locked canon, Batch 135, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-452`). The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle VII, first
entry in the third wave. Rebellion era, age 25. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material —
a detailed armor-and-weapon combat showcase per Abad's craft instruction. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The retreating column had a bridge between itself and safety, and a pursuing Directorate cavalry
force closing the distance fast enough that no ordinary rear guard could have bought enough time for
everyone to cross before the pursuit caught the column mid-span.

Kanja took the rear guard alone, letting the column cross first while he held the bridge's near end.

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System read the bridge's own structural tension the moment he set foot on
it — decades of load-bearing stress banked in every support cable, every tensioned span, exactly the
kind of resonance architecture the Aegis-Talisman doctrine had been built to exploit. He let the
pursuing cavalry commit fully onto the span itself, waiting past the point most tacticians would have
judged safe, until the entire pursuing force was committed to the bridge and the last of the retreating
column had cleared the far bank.

Then he listened — not struck immediately, listened, diagnosing the bridge's exact resonant frequency
the way the doctrine demanded before ever releasing a discharge — and Obsidian Malice found it in a
single blow against the near support tower, timed to a precision that collapsed the span's tension
from the pursued end first, giving the cavalry force just enough warning to feel the structure fail
beneath them without enough time to reverse their charge and escape it.

The bridge came down behind the retreating column and ahead of nothing but open water, the pursuing
force stranded on the collapsing far span while Kanja himself, already committed to the near bank
when the collapse began, cleared the falling structure in a single leap Mafesto's stored charge
converted directly into raw output.

"You waited until they were all the way onto it," the column's own rear officer said, watching the
wreckage settle. "A conventional collapse timed any earlier would have only caught the lead riders."

"A resonance collapse isn't like conventional demolition. It's not about timing an explosion. It's
about listening to the structure until it tells you the exact moment collapsing it costs the most to
whoever's standing on it and the least to whoever isn't." Kanja didn't look back at the wreckage.
"The bridge chose its own moment, the way it always does. I just had to be patient enough to let it."
