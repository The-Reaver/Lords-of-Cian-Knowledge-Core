# The Old Hand Who Refused to Stop

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Captain Alias Chronicle, wave 13. Long Mask era. Not
a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters (the veteran
crew member is deliberately left unnamed).*

---

He'd taken a wound at Iron Shallows decades back that never healed quite the way it should have, and
by the time he was well into his later years, the old injury had started costing him speed he used
to have without noticing. Everyone on the crew saw it. He refused to.

"I want the harbor watch shift back," he told Kanja, the same request he'd made and been gently
redirected from twice already. "Not the light duty. The real rotation."

"Your leg isn't what it was. You know that as well as I do."

"I know it. I don't care. I've earned the right to decide what I do with whatever's left of it, and
what's left of it still works well enough for harbor watch. I'm not asking you to protect me from a
decision that's mine to make."

Kanja didn't argue the point further, and didn't overrule him. "Then it's yours. I won't pretend I
like it, and I won't pretend I'm not going to worry every shift you take. But you're not a piece of
gear I get to retire when it stops meeting a standard I set. You're a person, and this is your
choice to make about your own life, even if I'd choose differently for you."

He took the shift, and held it, slower than he'd once been but no less capable of doing the job
right, for another three years before age finally made the decision for him in a way argument never
could. When he did finally step back, it was entirely his own call, made without anyone pushing him
toward it.

"You let him have those three years," Efa Gol said afterward, "when you could have made the safer
call and nobody would have blamed you for it."

"He'd have blamed himself, and rightly. I don't get to decide when someone's life stops belonging to
them just because I'm the one worried about the outcome. Respecting someone's choices sometimes
means respecting a choice you'd never make yourself. That's harder than protecting them. It's also
the only version of respect that actually means anything."
