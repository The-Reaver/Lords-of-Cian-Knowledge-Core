# The Manifest That Never Arrived

The Trust's answer to the five-district agreement, when it finally came, wasn't another company of
soldiers. It was quieter than that, and took nearly two weeks to notice at all: the supply manifests
that fed all five districts' basic provisions began arriving late, then incomplete, then not at all,
rerouted through registries that led nowhere anyone could trace on the district's own maps.

"They're not attacking the sites," Garren Hask said, laying out three weeks of increasingly sparse
delivery records. "They're starving the standard out from underneath us. No dredge line works long on
empty stomachs, standard or no standard."

"Then we need to know where the manifests are actually going, not just where they're supposed to be
going." Kanja was already working through the registries Hask had flagged, the same cross-referencing
habit that had traced the poisoned cistern's delivery weeks before. "A manifest doesn't disappear. It
gets rerouted, and a reroute leaves a record somewhere, even if that record was never meant to be
found by us."

It took four days of tracing false registries back through legitimate ones, cross-checking supplier
names against the five-district clerks' own independent contacts, before the pattern resolved: every
diverted shipment had been rerouted to a single holding depot two districts north, sitting unused,
waiting for the five districts to weaken enough that the standard would collapse on its own without
a single soldier needing to be deployed.

"We could raid the depot," Danne Sok offered. "Take back what's ours."

"We could. I don't think we should, not yet." Kanja studied the full trace a final time. "If we raid
it, it becomes a fight, and a fight is exactly the story they'd want told about us — that the
five-district standard collapsed into the same violence every other arrangement on this canal has
collapsed into eventually. I'd rather expose the reroute publicly, the same way we exposed the
rerouted registry at Silt Row, and let five districts' worth of people see exactly who chose to starve
them rather than negotiate."

They published the full trace instead — depot location, rerouted manifests, the registries used to
hide the diversion — distributed through the same crier network that had carried the five-district
sheet. The depot's own quartermasters, faced with an exposure that made quiet starvation considerably
less quiet, released the withheld shipments within three days, no raid required.

"You could have gotten the supplies back faster with force," Hask said, once the deliveries had
resumed.

"Maybe. But force would have cost us something the manifests alone couldn't replace — the fact that
five districts trust this standard because it's never had to be defended with a blade. I'd rather it
take three extra days and stay that way."
