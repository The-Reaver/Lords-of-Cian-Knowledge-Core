# What They Built After the Rope Broke

*Locked canon, Batch 277, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1438`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XCIX, wave 33,
closing the wave. Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. An accidental workplace death and the systemic
safety reform it produces, distinct from the elder's natural-causes death (`MCD-902`) and the
torture-retaliation death that cracked Kanja's composure (`MCD-901`).*

---

Nobody had done anything wrong, which was, in its own way, the hardest part of it. A hoist rope on
the second canal row's loading dock, inspected on schedule and showing no visible wear, gave out
under an ordinary load and dropped a dredge-cart's full weight onto a man guiding it from below. He
died before anyone could reach him. No owner had cut a corner. No enforcer had swung a fist. The
tally method, built to catch theft and shorted wages, had nothing in it that could have seen this
coming, and Kanja said so plainly at the man's own wake rather than let anyone comfort themselves
with a lie about what the count could and couldn't protect against.

What he did instead took three weeks rather than an afternoon. Dol Maren, already trusted with the
crew's engineering questions, was asked to look at every hoist rope on every site the tally method
touched -- not for wear, which inspection already caught, but for load-rating margins that had never
been standardized district to district, each site's owner having set his own tolerance for how much
weight a rope was trusted to carry before replacement. Maren's answer was blunt, delivered in his
usual number-material-conclusion shape: four of the five districts ran their ropes closer to failure
than the fifth did, for no reason beyond habit and cost, and none of them had ever compared notes.

The reform that followed wasn't dramatic. A single load-rating standard, cross-checked the same way
the tally sheets were cross-checked, adopted district by district over the following month, with
Garren Hask folding the inspection dates into the same ledgers that already tracked wages. It didn't
undo the death it was built in answer to. It meant, as plainly as Kanja put it to the dead man's
widow when he brought her the finished standard rather than only his condolences, that no one else's
family would learn the same thing hers just had, the same way.

"It won't bring him back," she said, not unkindly, turning the pages over in her hands.

"No. It won't." He didn't reach for anything softer to say instead. "It only means the next rope
that could have failed the same way gets replaced before it does, because someone finally checked."
