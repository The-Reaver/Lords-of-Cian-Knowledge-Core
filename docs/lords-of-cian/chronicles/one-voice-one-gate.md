# One Voice, One Gate

*Locked canon, Batch 201, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-835`). The Crow King Alias Chronicle XX, wave 7 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

No Braid, no interleaved lies, no crew of singers holding a resonance for hours across a mile of
marsh — just Kanja, alone, at a single side gate, and one guard who had been standing the same post
long enough to know its rhythms better than he knew his own name.

The hostage held inside the compound was a single person, a courier caught carrying nothing more
incriminating than a list of names the Directorate wanted for questioning, and the operation didn't
call for three hundred voices or six weeks of rehearsal. It called for one man, one door, and a lie
small enough that no sensor grid in the world would ever have been built to catch it.

"Relief's early tonight," Kanja said, stepping into the gate's low lamplight with the unhurried
confidence of someone who belonged exactly where he was standing, no forged papers, no elaborate
cover — just a voice pitched to the exact register of routine authority the guard had spent years
learning to trust without thinking twice.

"Didn't get word of a change." The guard didn't reach for a weapon, more puzzled than suspicious.

"Rotation got moved up after the incident on the south wall. You'll hear the full account at
shift-change." Kanja said it plainly, without elaboration, the way a man repeating something he'd
already heard once and found unremarkable would say it. "Go get some sleep. I've got it from here."

The guard left. No system had been fooled, no network flooded with false reports, no crew of
singers holding a chant for hours — a single true-sounding sentence, delivered with the specific,
unhurried confidence of someone who had nothing to prove, had done all the work three hundred voices
usually did.

"That's the whole operation?" the courier asked afterward, once they were clear, still slightly
disbelieving. "One sentence?"

"Most people think the trick is always complicated because the big ones look complicated from the
outside. The actual craft is knowing exactly how much lie a moment actually needs and never using
one drop more than that." Kanja didn't linger on it further. "Tonight needed one sentence. That's
all I spent."
