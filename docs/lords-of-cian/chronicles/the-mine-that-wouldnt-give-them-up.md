# The Mine That Wouldn't Give Them Up

*Locked canon, Batch 184, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-537`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XIII, first
entry in the fifth wave. Rebellion era, age 20, the Sewer War of Killane. Not a territory Chronicle.
New standalone material — a detailed combined rescue-and-combat showcase per Abad's craft
instruction. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Directorate engineers had deliberately triggered the collapse themselves, trapping a dozen mine
organizers underground and then positioning armed guards at every known access point — not to rescue
anyone, but to ensure nobody else could either, starving the trapped men into surrender or worse while
denying any outside effort the chance to reach them.

Kanja didn't use the known access points.

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System read the mine's full structure from outside, mapping an unmapped
secondary shaft the collapse itself had opened rather than sealed — a route the guards hadn't thought
to cover because it hadn't existed an hour before. Obsidian Malice cleared the shaft's own debris in a
controlled sequence, each discharge checked carefully against the tension-reading before committing to
it, the same patient listening that had read every tunnel in Killane correctly since the first day he
learned to trust stone over assumption.

The guards at the known access points, alerted to movement from a direction they'd never covered,
scrambled to respond — and found Onyx of Oblivion already among them, having doubled back through the
same unmapped route once the trapped miners were clear, Cadence Ruin breaking their hastily
reorganized defense before it could properly form. None of the guards who'd planned to starve twelve
men underground anticipated fighting a rescue that had already succeeded before they knew it was
happening.

"They sealed every way in they knew about," the mine's own foreman said afterward, twelve organizers
alive and clear, the guards disarmed rather than killed. "You found one they didn't know existed."

"The collapse made it, not me. I only had to read what the collapse actually did instead of assuming
it did what the plan said it should." Kanja surveyed the cleared shaft, the same careful listening
that had once cost him dearly now paying for itself in full. "They planned a mine with two entrances
they controlled. The stone itself gave me a third one they never accounted for. I'd rather trust what
the ground actually tells me than what anyone's plan assumes it will."
