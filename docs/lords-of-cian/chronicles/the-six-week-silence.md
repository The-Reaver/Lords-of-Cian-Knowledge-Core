# The Six-Week Silence

*Locked canon, Batch 109, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-374`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle I. Rebellion
era, during the Sewer War of Killane (`MCD-234`, age 20). Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone
material distinct from the already-locked summary. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new
named characters.*

---

Four weeks into the tunnel work, the closest they came to discovery had nothing to do with soldiers.

It was a burst joint in a two-hundred-year-old clay main, the kind of failure any city's
infrastructure produces eventually regardless of who's tunneling nearby, and it happened directly
above the chamber where eleven of Kanja's people were mid-transcription of the Southern District's
Scrip-Registry — three more pages copied that night alone, four months of patient theft nearly
finished. A burst main meant surface crews, and surface crews meant eyes on a stretch of old
sewer nobody official was supposed to be using.

Kanja was the one who went up first, alone, through a service grate barely wide enough for his
shoulders, and found himself standing in a work light's glare in front of two municipal repair
crewmen already elbow-deep in mud, arguing about whether the joint could be patched or needed a full
section replaced.

He didn't fight. He didn't run. He picked up a spare length of clay pipe from the crew's own cart and
turned it over in his hands the way a man who's actually handled pipe before turns it over, checking
the collar seat, running a thumb along the old seal line.

"Your patch is going to fail again in a season," he said, before either crewman had decided what to
make of a stranger climbing out of their own access grate. "This whole run was laid before proper
bell-and-spigot joints were standard down here. You're not looking at a burst. You're looking at a
section that's been failing slowly for years and finally gave. Replace fifteen feet either side of
this joint or you'll be back out here in the cold in four months doing this again."

The older crewman squinted at him, then at the pipe in his hands, then back at him. "You worked
clay main before?"

"Longer ago than I'd like to admit." He set the pipe down, precisely where he'd found it. "Bad night
to be checking a joint alone, though. I'll leave you to it."

He was gone back down the grate before either man thought to ask who he was, or why a stranger
climbing out of a disused service tunnel at midnight happened to know two-hundred-year-old clay
main specifications well enough to diagnose a joint failure at a glance. The crew filed their report
the next morning describing nothing more suspicious than an off-duty tradesman who'd wandered past
and offered free advice — a detail so mundane that nobody flagged it, and the six-week infiltration
continued undiscovered for another two weeks, until the Registry copy was complete and the last
sabotage charge was set.

"You could have just gone back down without talking to them," the crew chief told him afterward, once
they were clear of Killane entirely. "Why risk the conversation at all?"

"Because two confused men filing a report about a stranger who vanished without a word get a
security detail sent to check the tunnel. Two men filing a report about a helpful passerby who told
them their pipe was bad get nothing but a maintenance order." He checked the last page of the
Registry copy against the original, satisfied it matched exactly. "Sometimes the safest thing you can
do in a tunnel full of soldiers looking for you is remind two tired men, for thirty seconds, that
somebody still knows how the work is actually done."
