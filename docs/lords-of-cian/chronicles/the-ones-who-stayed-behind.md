# The Ones Who Stayed Behind

*Locked canon, Batch 258, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1217`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
LXXIX, wave 27, first entry in the wave. A legacy visit to the town the fleet once defended, now
self-sufficient. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Kanja hadn't planned to return to the town at all — the fleet's route simply passed near enough that
Danne Sok mentioned it in passing, the undefended coastal settlement the Trinity had once defended
whole against a raiders' assault years back, and something in him wanted to see what had become of it
since.

What he found wasn't a town waiting to be saved again. A watchtower stood where none had before,
manned in rotating shifts by townsfolk who'd organized their own coastal watch. A modest breakwater,
built with salvaged timber and stone over what must have been two years of steady labor, blunted the
worst of the approach a raiding hull would need to make. And when a genuinely suspicious ship did
drift into sight while Kanja's launch was still a half mile out, the town's own signal system had
every able hand at the shoreline with improvised weapons and a clear evacuation plan for the children
and elderly before the ship even confirmed itself as a threat.

It turned out to be a fishing vessel blown off its course, no threat at all, and the town stood down
as efficiently as it had mustered.

"You didn't need us," Kanja said to the woman who'd organized the watch rotation, once the false
alarm had passed and the town had settled back into its ordinary rhythm.

"We needed you once," she said. "That night taught us what it looked like to actually be defended,
instead of just hoping nobody came. We didn't want to need it a second time." She gestured at the
tower, the breakwater, the drill that had just run without a wasted motion. "This is what we built out
of watching you do it for us."

Kanja stayed one evening, no ceremony, ate with the town rather than being feted by it, and left the
next morning without the fanfare that had marked the night of the original battle. Garren Hask,
logging the visit afterward, found himself writing something the ledger had never quite recorded
before — not a rescue, not a battle, not even really an encounter. Just proof that something the fleet
had done once had kept working long after they'd sailed away.

"That's the best kind of entry," Efa Gol said, reading over his shoulder. "The kind where we're not
even needed anymore."
