# The Magistrate Who Wanted Jurisdiction

*Locked canon, Batch 264, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1376`). Captain Alias Chronicle LXXVI, wave 26. A reconstruction-era magistrate claims formal authority over the crew's informal protection role; resolved through diplomacy, not force.*

---

The new magistrate assigned to the district around Pier Nine wasn't hostile, which made her harder to argue with than an enemy would have been. She was, by her own account, simply doing her job: reconstruction law required any group performing sustained protection or arbitration functions within her jurisdiction to register formally, submit to oversight, and answer to her court rather than its own dispute council when disputes crossed into her district's authority. The crew's own charter, three years old now and treated by everyone aboard as close to sacred, had no clause anywhere addressing what happened when a formal court simply claimed the crew's informal justice as its own to regulate.

Corren Halst wanted to refuse outright. Garren Hask, older and more patient with paperwork than he used to be, wanted to find the compromise before the refusal became a fight nobody needed. Kanja wanted to hear the magistrate out first.

She was not wrong, exactly, about the gap in the law she was trying to close, and she said so plainly across a table at Pier Nine rather than through another unstamped letter. "I'm not trying to end what you do. I'm trying to make sure it doesn't become a problem the day you're not here to run it well. Institutions outlast the people who build them well or badly. I'd rather see this one outlast you well."

It took six meetings, not one, to find language that satisfied her jurisdiction without folding the dispute council under her court's authority — a formal liaison arrangement, the council's own rulings recognized rather than overridden, disputes crossing outside the crew's own membership referred to her bench rather than decided unilaterally. Hask drafted it personally, treating it with the same care he'd given the original charter.

"You spent three years building something you didn't want anyone telling you how to run," the magistrate said, signing the final draft, "and then you spent six meetings actually listening to someone who wanted to tell you exactly that. That's rarer than you'd think."

"The charter's whole point was that it survives being challenged," Kanja said. "This was the first real test of that. I'd rather it holds because it earned the challenge, not because nobody dared bring one."
