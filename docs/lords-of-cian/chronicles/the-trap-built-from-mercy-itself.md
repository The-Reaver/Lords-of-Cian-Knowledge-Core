# The Trap Built From Mercy Itself

*Locked canon, Batch 217, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-954`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle LII, wave 18. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The Fleet-Marshal's second move was not a formation and not a bounty. It was a merchant hull, deliberately
crippled and set adrift in water Danne Sok's charts marked as a route the fleet checked routinely for
exactly this kind of distress — a hull crewed by his own men dressed as merchant sailors, rigged to look
exactly like the kind of wreck the ghost fleet never failed to answer.

Corren Halst caught the first thread of it two days before the fleet would have reached the drift —
a fishing crew's secondhand account of a merchant hull that had put out from a Trust naval yard rather
than a merchant port, loaded with more men than her manifest could plausibly need. He brought it to
Kanja with the plain, uncomfortable shape of what it meant.

"He learned the wrong lesson from watching us," Dol Maren said, once the shape of the trap was laid
out. "He didn't build something to beat our tactics. He built something to beat our decency. He's
betting we'll answer a distress call even knowing it might be exactly this."

"He's not wrong to bet that," Kanja said. "That's the problem with it."

They didn't refuse the bet outright. Refusing it meant the next genuine distress call went unanswered
out of fear it might be another trap, and that cost — a real wreck, real drowning sailors, left to die
because trust had gotten too expensive — was worse than walking in with eyes open. So they answered it,
deliberately, with Mafesto's reading trained on the hull from a mile out and Obsidian Malice's charge
held ready rather than spent.

The ambush sprang the moment *The Audit* closed to boarding range — hidden marines coming up from
below decks in numbers built for a boarding crew, not a merchant complement. It was, briefly, a real
fight, the first time in longer than the crew could easily remember that answering a distress call had
cost them blood rather than saved someone else's. Two hands were wounded before Onyx of Oblivion's
Whisper of Shadows cleared the last of the ambush from the deck.

The Marshal himself wasn't aboard. He'd never intended to be — the trap was information as much as
violence, a chance to watch, through whatever survivors reported back, exactly how the fleet fought when
cornered rather than merely observed. Kanja let the surviving marines go, unarmed, in a launched boat,
rather than hold them, and told them plainly to carry the message back themselves.

"Tell him it worked," he said. "Tell him it cost us two wounded and it'll cost him nothing, because I'm
not going to stop answering distress calls over one trap, and he knows that, and that's exactly why he
built it instead of something that would have actually caught us."

"That's not a victory," Efa Gol said afterward, tending to the wounded herself.

"No. It's the first move in whatever this actually is between him and us. He didn't need to win today.
He needed to prove the mercy was real, so the next trap can be built to actually use it." Kanja looked
back at the empty water where the launched boat had gone. "Which means the answer isn't answering
fewer calls. It's answering them smarter than we did today."
