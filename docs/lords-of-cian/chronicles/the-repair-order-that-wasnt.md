# The Repair Order That Wasn't

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XVI, first entry
in the sixth wave. Rebellion era, age 20, the Sewer War of Killane. Not a territory Chronicle.
Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The order came down through the crew's usual channel of forged paperwork: a municipal maintenance
notice, stamped and dated, directing a repair team to a specific stretch of the eastern gallery
that hadn't been flagged by anyone Kanja's people actually had inside the registrar's office.

He read it twice before he ever set foot near the gallery.

"Somebody wants us in that stretch badly enough to forge a maintenance order for it," he told the
crew chief, turning the page over to check the seal's wax against a genuine one they'd lifted weeks
earlier. "Problem is, whoever wrote this doesn't actually know clay main. Look at the described
fault — a 'settling crack along the crown seam, six feet past the last junction.' That gallery's
lined in fitted stone below the waterline, not clay pipe, and it hasn't had a crown seam to settle
since it was relined eleven years ago. Real municipal engineers don't get their own infrastructure
wrong on paper. Whoever wrote this has only ever seen that gallery from the outside."

The crew chief frowned at the notice. "So it's bait."

"It's bait built by someone who thinks the details don't matter as long as the letterhead's
convincing. They're wrong. The details are the only part that ever mattered." Kanja folded the
forged order and pocketed it rather than burn it — evidence worth keeping. "Send two people to
watch the real approach from a distance. If there's a detachment waiting for a repair crew that
never shows, I want to know how long they're willing to sit there before they decide the trap
failed."

The detachment held its position for six hours before withdrawing, never once aware they'd been
made the moment their own forger chose the wrong fault to describe. No confrontation happened at
all — the trap simply expired, unsprung, its only casualty the false confidence of whoever had
assumed a stolen letterhead was disguise enough.

"They could have just watched the gallery without the fake order," the crew chief said afterward.
"Waited for us to come to them instead of inventing a reason."

"They could have. But a trap built on a lie about the work itself was always going to fail the
moment it met someone who actually knows the work." Kanja handed the forged notice to Garren Hask
for the crew's own records, a small addition to a growing account of enemies who kept
underestimating exactly what kind of knowledge they were up against. "That's the whole advantage.
Not that I'm harder to kill. That I'm harder to fool with a story about pipe that was never
mine to begin with, told by someone who's never once held one."
