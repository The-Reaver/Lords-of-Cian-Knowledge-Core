# The Hand That Stayed Open

*Locked canon, Batch 108, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-372`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle II. Rebellion
era, unarmed throughout -- matching `MCD-244`'s stated ethos. Not a territory Chronicle. New
standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The garrison captain sent to break up the ledger-taking at the weaving district expected a fight,
because every report he'd read described a rebel commander, and rebel commanders carried weapons.
He brought forty men for that reason, expecting the visiting crew to draw arms the moment his column
came through the gate.

No one drew anything. Kanja was sitting on an overturned dye-barrel with a ledger open across his
knees, a line of workers still waiting to give their answers, and he did not stand when the column
arrived.

"You're under arrest for inciting labor unrest," the captain said, weapon drawn, forty more behind
him with theirs half-raised. "Stand up. Slowly."

"I'm not armed." Kanja held the ledger up, both hands visible, the ordinary gesture of a man with
nothing to hide behind. "There's nothing here to arrest me away from except a list of numbers your
own administrators could check themselves, if they wanted to. Go ahead and check while your men
decide what they're going to do with those weapons pointed at an unarmed man in front of four
hundred witnesses."

The captain did not lower his own weapon, but he did not close the distance either. Forty soldiers
stood in a weaving-district yard with their arms leveled at a seated man holding nothing but a
ledger, and the longer the standoff held, the more visibly uncomfortable the front rank became —
because striking an unarmed man for holding a book of numbers, in front of the very workers whose
numbers were in it, was not the kind of order any of them had signed on to carry out without
flinching.

"Stand up," the captain said again, quieter this time.

"I will, when you tell me what charge you're actually here to enforce. Inciting unrest isn't a
number. It's a feeling somebody higher than you had, reading a report they didn't like. I've got
four hundred people behind me whose feelings are written down right here, with figures attached, and
nobody's disputed a single one of them yet. You came here to stop a fight. There isn't one happening.
There's just a book getting fuller while you decide whether you're willing to be the first man in
this yard to draw blood over arithmetic."

The captain held the line for nearly a full minute — long enough for two of his own soldiers, near
the back, to quietly lower their weapons without being ordered to. When he finally holstered his own,
it was not because Kanja had won an argument. It was because the fight the captain had come prepared
for had simply never existed to begin with, and forty armed men standing over one unarmed man with a
ledger had nowhere honest left to point their weapons.

"This isn't over," the captain said, already turning his column back toward the gate.

"It wasn't a fight to begin with. It's still not one now. Come back when you've got a real charge.
I'll still be here. So will the book."
