# The Girl Pier Nine Took In

*Locked canon, Batch 264, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1375`). Captain Alias Chronicle LXXV, wave 25, closing the wave. Mira is warmly folded into the crew's found-family orbit, given the same free choice Sera was given rather than a path chosen for her.*

---

Nobody sat Mira down and decided her future for her, which was, Kanja was careful to make sure, the whole point. In the months after the wall went up, she simply kept coming back to Pier Nine — first to visit the carved name that had been her own small, careful work, then to watch Efa Gol's ledger table because the numbers made a kind of sense grief hadn't, then to sit with Pell Ostra while she worked because Ostra talked to her materials in a way that didn't require Mira to talk back until she was ready to.

Nobody made her a deckhand. Nobody made her anything. When Garren Hask finally raised the question everyone had been quietly circling — whether the crew ought to formally take her in, give her a bunk and a role and a place on the roster — Kanja said no before anyone else could answer, and watched the room brace for an explanation.

"Not because we don't want her. Because every child this crew has ever taken in, we let choose it for herself, in her own time — Sera chose a healer's hall instead of a bunk here, and nobody loved her less for it. Mira gets exactly that same choice, not a smaller one made for her because of what she's already lost." He looked at Mira directly. "You get to decide what you are to us. Crew, if you want that, when you're old enough to mean it. Or just someone the pier belongs to, the way it belongs to anyone whose name is on that wall. Nobody here is going to make that choice smaller than it is by making it for you first."

She didn't answer that day, and nobody pushed her to. What she did instead, over the following year, was simpler than any formal answer could have been: she kept showing up, at her own pace, on her own terms, until "the girl Pier Nine took in" stopped being anyone's careful description and became just what people called her, without anyone remembering exactly when it had stopped needing an explanation.

"She chose us the slow way," Efa Gol said, watching her carry the evening count home from the ledger table like it was already hers to carry. "That's the only way that was ever going to stick."
