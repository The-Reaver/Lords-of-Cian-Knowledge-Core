# The Ledger Keeper's Slowing Hand

*Locked canon, Batch 284, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1502`). Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle C, first entry in
the thirty-fourth wave. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241). Not a
territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Garren Hask noticed it before anyone else did, the way he noticed everything else about a tally
before it became a problem: his own hand, holding the stylus over the campaign ledger, had started
to shake on the smaller figures, the ones-and-twos columns he'd once entered without a second
thought now costing him a careful, deliberate steadiness he hadn't needed a decade earlier.

He said nothing about it for three weeks, correcting the shakier entries in a second pass each
evening when no one was watching, until an apprentice barely older than Hask's own tenure as a
dock-smith had been at its start caught him doing it and asked, plainly, whether something was wrong
with his hand. Hask gave her the same answer he gave everything: the honest one. "Thirty-one years on
the docks before this campaign, and however many since. Hands wear the same way tools do. Longer than
most, if you're careful with them. Not forever."

He brought it to Kanja himself rather than let it be discovered. "I've been the counter since the
founding. I intend to stay the counter as long as the numbers I hand you are numbers you can trust.
The day they aren't, you need to know before I do, not after."

What they built wasn't a replacement — Kanja didn't suggest one, and Hask would have refused it if he
had — but a second set of hands trained alongside his own: a young settlement clerk with a steadier
grip and Hask's own exacting standards drilled into her over months of shared evening tallies,
checking every figure against his before either was entered as final. The campaign's numbers stayed
as clean as they had been from the first ledger entry at the founding. Hask's own hand, watched now
rather than hidden, stayed steady enough for the work that mattered even as the finer columns kept
slipping.

"You could have let this go quietly for years before it caught up with you," Kanja said, watching the
two of them cross-check a week's tallies side by side. "You chose to say something the moment it
started instead."

"The whole method's built on people saying the true thing before it's forced out of them," Hask said,
not looking up from the ledger. "I wasn't going to be the one exception to my own rule, not over
something as small as a hand that shakes a little on the small numbers." He set the stylus down,
satisfied with the entry, and let the clerk make the next one instead.
