# The Elder They Buried at the Canal

*Locked canon, Batch 206, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-902`). The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XLVIII, wave 16. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The old dredge hand had worked the Ferrow Cut canal for longer than anyone still living could
account for exactly — older than the five-district standard, older than the Trench Monarch, older
than most of the piers he'd helped drive into the mud in his younger years. He died the way most
people on that canal died when nothing violent took them first: quietly, in his sleep, in a room
above a tally house he'd watched turned honest in the last year of a very long life.

Nobody sent word to Kanja expecting him to come. It wasn't a wage dispute or a rumor or a threat —
just an old man's death, the kind of ordinary loss the canal absorbed constantly and mostly without
ceremony. He came anyway, walking most of the distance rather than taking the faster barge route,
arriving the evening before the burial with nothing more urgent to offer than his presence.

The burial itself was small — a canal-side plot, a handful of names spoken aloud by whoever had known
him longest, no pamphlet, no crier, nothing that would ever reach Garren Hask's ledger as anything
worth recording. Kanja stood at the back of the gathering for most of it, saying nothing, until one
of the old man's grandchildren, recognizing him, asked if he wanted to speak.

He hadn't prepared anything. What he said was short.

"I didn't know him well. I wish I had. Everyone who did tells me the same thing about him — that he
never once tried to make the work sound like more than it was, and never once let it be less than it
deserved either. That's rarer than it sounds. I've spent the better part of a year trying to get sites
full of strangers to trust a number on a page, and it occurs to me standing here that men like him
were doing the harder version of that same work for decades before I ever picked up a shovel — just
trusting each other, plainly, without needing a tally sheet to make it official." He looked at the
plain marker, no name carved yet, waiting on a stonecutter who'd get to it within the week. "I don't
think there's a version of whatever I end up being remembered for that's worth more than what he
actually was to the people standing here. I just wanted to say that where it could be heard."

He stayed through the night, helping finish the grave marker's foundation stone alongside two of the
dead man's grandsons, work that had nothing to do with any reputation and would never be attributed
to him by anyone outside that canal-side plot.

"Feels like the kind of thing that should go in the ledger," Corren Halst said quietly, on the walk
back.

"Maybe. Or maybe some of it's allowed to just be true without anyone writing it down." Kanja looked
back once at the canal, dark now, the marker barely visible in the last of the light. "I think I'd
rather be the kind of man people remember the way they're going to remember him, than the kind
they write pamphlets about. I don't know if I'll get to choose which one I end up being. But I know
which one I'd pick, if the choice were mine."

Garren Hask's ledger, when Kanja finally mentioned it to him days later, carried no formal entry for
Ferrow Cut's old dredge hand — only a single line, added almost as an afterthought at the bottom of
an unrelated page: *Some debts aren't owed to the ledger. Recorded here anyway, so it isn't only
carried in memory.*
