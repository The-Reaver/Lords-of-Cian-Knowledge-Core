# The Sibling She Waited Thirty Years For

*Locked canon, Batch 259, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1234`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LXIX, wave 23,
closing the wave. Age 150, V3 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose.*

---

Garren Hask's lead had taken thirty-five years to become a certainty. What had started, at age 115,
as a name half-remembered and a route half-guessed had grown slowly through the ledger network's own
patient decades of cross-checked manifests and freed-captive testimony into an actual address: a
smallholding two days inland, a woman in her fifties who had been sold off a different route as a
girl and had spent the decades since building a life so completely her own that she went by a name
nobody in her birth family had ever called her.

The freed captive who'd first asked for this — a woman herself now well into her own middle years,
the searching having outlasted the searcher's youth — made the two-day walk with Garren Hask's
written lead in hand and Kanja walking a half-step behind her, present but silent, the way he'd
learned to be present at things that weren't his to carry.

The woman at the smallholding remembered a sister. She didn't remember this sister — the specific
face, the specific years, ground down by decades into something closer to a shape than a memory —
and the recognition that did come, when it came, arrived slower and stranger than either of them had
pictured it across thirty-five years of hoping. There were tears, but they weren't simple ones.
There was a name, offered and accepted, and an evening spent trying to fit two entirely different
lives lived thirty years apart back into the same family they'd started from, with visible effort on
both sides and no guarantee the effort would hold past the visit.

Garren Hask closed his own copy of the entry that night with more care than most, writing not
*reunited* but *found, and known, imperfectly* — the truest word he had for something that was real
and hard-won and not, by any measure either woman would have used before it happened, exactly the
homecoming either of them had spent thirty-five years imagining.
