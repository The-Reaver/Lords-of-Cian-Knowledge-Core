# The Table Where the Crew Spoke for Themselves

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Captain Alias Chronicle, wave 9. Rebellion era, a
negotiation with an allied settlement. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose. No new named characters (the settlement's council is deliberately left unnamed).*

---

The settlement's council had asked for the meeting expecting to negotiate with Kanja directly — an
alliance for winter harbor rights, in exchange for the crew's protection against raiders working the
coast — and were visibly thrown when he arrived, sat at the far end of the table, and let three
other members of the crew do the actual talking.

"I thought we were meeting with the captain," the council's speaker said, not hostile, just
confused.

"You are. I'm just not doing the talking today." Kanja gestured to Pell Ostra, Danne Sok, and one of
the newer hands who'd grown up in a settlement much like this one and understood its concerns better
than he ever would. "They know what your harbor actually needs better than I do, and they know what
this crew can actually deliver without overpromising. I trust their word as much as my own. More,
on the specifics."

The council took a moment to adjust, then engaged in earnest — Pell Ostra walking through exactly
what mechanical support the crew could offer the settlement's fishing fleet, Danne Sok negotiating
the actual terms of harbor access with a bluntness that cut through two rounds of posturing faster
than diplomacy would have, the newer hand translating the settlement's own unspoken worries about
raiders using language that came from having lived the same fear himself.

By the end, the agreement was better than anyone had expected walking in — and entirely negotiated
without Kanja saying more than a dozen words the whole afternoon.

"You could have gotten a better deal for us faster," the council speaker said afterward, half
observation, half genuine curiosity. "You clearly could have pushed harder."

"Maybe. But then it would have been my deal, made on my authority, and the next time something needs
negotiating, you'd still be waiting for me instead of trusting whoever I send. This way, you've
actually met the people who'll be delivering on it, and they've proven themselves capable of
speaking for the whole crew without me standing over them. That matters more than a slightly better
number today."
