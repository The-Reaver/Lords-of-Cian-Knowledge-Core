# The Defector Who Chose the Name

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Captain Alias Chronicle, wave 8. Rebellion era, the
naval campaigns. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters (the defecting sailor is deliberately left unnamed).*

---

The Trust sailor came across under a truce flag two nights before the fleet he served was due to
engage The Audit in open water, and he didn't ask for amnesty or safe passage inland the way
defectors usually did. He asked to join the crew.

"You understand what you're asking," Corren Halst said, keeping a wary distance despite the flag.
"You'd be fighting your own former fleet within the week."

"I understand it better than you think." The sailor hadn't slept, by the look of him — the decision
had clearly cost him something to reach. "I've served eleven years under officers who'd let half a
crew drown before they'd risk a ship's schedule to pull them out. I heard what your captain did at
Iron Shallows — held position under fire to recover six men who'd already been written off as lost,
cost him the tactical advantage of the whole engagement to do it. My own commander wouldn't have
spent a minute of delay on six men. Yours spent an hour."

"That's the story," Kanja said, arriving in time to hear the last of it. "I'd want you to know the
truth of it before you decide anything on the strength of a story. It wasn't calculated generosity.
It was six of my own people, and I wasn't leaving without them, cost or no cost."

"That's exactly why I'm here," the sailor said. "Not because it was strategic. Because it wasn't,
and you did it anyway."

Kanja didn't decide on the spot. He had him watched for two weeks, tested on small things — honesty
about mistakes, willingness to be corrected, whether the story he'd defected on held up against the
man's actual conduct — the same slow proving every new hand went through, defector or not.

It held. By the end of the month the sailor had a place on the aft watch and, eventually, the same
quiet habit the rest of the crew had of calling Kanja by the one word that had drawn him across in
the first place.

"Strange thing to defect for," Garren Hask said once, genuinely curious. "Most cross over for
safety, or money, or fear of what's coming."

"Some of us cross over because we finally saw proof that the story we wanted to believe was true
somewhere was actually true," the man said. "That's worth more than safety, in the end."
