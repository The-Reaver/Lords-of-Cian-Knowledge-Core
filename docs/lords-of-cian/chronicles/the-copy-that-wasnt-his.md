# The Copy That Wasn't His

*Locked canon, Batch 278, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1445`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XCVII, wave 33, first entry. A dishonest copycat of the method itself, distinct from the earlier impostor who traded on Kanja's own name (MCD-759).*

---

The man calling himself a "tally-taker" in the river districts never once claimed to be the Industrial Myth. That was what made him harder to catch than the impostor who'd worn the name badly years before. He ran the same shape of process — testimony, cross-referencing, a posted finding — and skimmed a tenth of every settlement for his own trouble before anyone thought to ask why an honest method had suddenly started charging a fee.

Word of it reached Ezio as a complaint, not an accusation: three districts writing to ask why the crew's own method had begun taking a cut, certain it was the same operation simply grown less scrupulous with success.

"We've never charged anyone anything," Ezio said. "Whoever this is didn't steal our name. He stole the shape of the work and left the honesty out of it."

Kanja found him in the second of the three districts, mid-testimony, running a passable imitation of the worst-off-first order without understanding why it mattered — starting at the front gate with whoever was easiest to reach rather than the yard's far edge, taking figures at face value with no cross-reference at all, and quietly rounding every settlement down by the exact tenth he'd pocket once it paid out.

"You're not doing it wrong by accident," Kanja said, once the two of them were alone. "You know what the real version looks like. You just left out the part that costs you something."

"It still helps them," the man said, not quite meeting his eyes. "More than nothing."

"It helps them less than they think it does, and it costs them a debt they don't know they're paying you to skip. That's worse than nothing — it's a district that stops trusting any ledger at all the day they finally catch you." Kanja didn't threaten him, didn't take the ledger from his hands. He simply had Ezio's own auditors walk the three districts behind him, publicly reconciling every finding the man had posted against the true figures, the discrepancy laid out plainly enough that no one needed to be told what to think of it.

The man was gone from the river districts within the month, and the three settlements were corrected in full. What stayed with Ezio longer than the fraud itself was how close the imitation had come to looking right from a distance.

"He got the shape of it," Ezio said. "Just not the part that makes the shape mean anything."

"That's the part that was never going to be easy to fake," Kanja said. "Anyone can hold a ledger. Being willing to let it say something you didn't want it to say — that's the whole method. There's no shortcut through that part."
