# The Surrender That Cost No Blood

*Locked canon, Batch 133, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-447`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle VIII. Long Mask era,
age ~165, the Golden Terror period. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The slaving captain had heard every version of the Scourge's reputation long before the black sails
ever crested the horizon toward his own ship, and by the time the flagship drew within hailing
distance, he'd already made a calculation most of his trade never lived long enough to make
correctly: that the reputation alone, unfought, was worth more respected than tested.

He struck his colors before a single shot crossed the water between them.

"I'd heard you don't always give men the chance to surrender," the captain called across the closing
distance, his own crew visibly divided between relief and shame at a fight that wasn't going to
happen. "I'd rather find out that part of the story was exaggerated than prove it true."

"That part depends entirely on what happens in the next few minutes." The Scourge's own crew held
position, weapons ready but not raised, matching their captain's own restraint. "Open your holds. Let
my crew count and release whoever's chained below. Do that without argument, and this ends exactly
the way it's ending right now — no blood, no boarding action, nothing for either crew to carry home
but the story of a captain who chose correctly."

"And if I refuse?"

"Then you'll have your answer about which version of the story was true, and it won't be the version
you were hoping for." The Scourge let the silence that followed do its own work. "I'd rather you never
have to find out. Open the holds."

The captain opened the holds. Forty-one captives came up into open air over the following hour, the
slaving crew watching in a silence that carried more genuine fear than any battle would have — not
of violence that never came, but of a reputation that had turned out, in the one moment that mattered
most to them, to be exactly as absolute as the stories claimed, without ever needing to prove it with
a single blow.

"You could have taken the ship regardless," the captain said afterward, watching his own vessel sail
on, empty of cargo but otherwise untouched. "Sunk it, burned it, made an example the way the stories
say you have before."

"I could have. I didn't need to. You gave me the outcome I actually wanted the moment you opened
those holds — forty-one people free, and a captain who'll tell this story exactly as it happened for
the rest of his life, which does more to end this trade than one more burned ship ever could."
