# The Name That Wasn't on the Wall

*Locked canon, Batch 286, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1522`). Captain Alias Chronicle CII, wave 34, closing the wave. Weeks after the attack on the memorial wall (`MCD-1521`), the crew is asked whether its memory can hold a name from the other side.*

---

She came alone, on foot, three weeks after the attempt on the wall had already been repaired and mostly forgotten by everyone but the people who'd stood watch on it since. She said her grandfather had died at Iron Shallows — not among the causeway's own dead, since the crossing itself had claimed no lives on either side, but in the retreat afterward, in the confusion of an army coming apart, killed by soldiers on his own side for refusing to keep running. She'd only learned that much from a dying aunt's own deathbed account, years after everyone who might have confirmed it firsthand was gone.

"I'm not asking you to say he was a hero," she told Kanja, at the wall itself, where she'd clearly been standing a long while before anyone noticed her. "I don't even know if he believed in what he was fighting for by the end, or just didn't know how to stop. I just know there's a wall here with names on it for people the war cost, and his name isn't anywhere, because he was on the side that was supposed to have been the enemy."

Kanja didn't answer for her. He brought it to the council instead, the way the crew had learned to bring anything that reshaped what an institution meant rather than deciding it alone in a hallway. Efa Gol, whose own pair-partner's death this same war had cost her, sat with it longest before speaking. "I don't think I can carve his name next to Tam Sullen's," she finally said. "That would say something I don't actually believe — that it was all the same loss, wearing different uniforms. It wasn't."

"No," Callum Breck agreed. "But I don't think that's actually what she's asking for either."

It was Mira, still new enough to her observer seat to weigh every word twice before offering it, who found the shape of an answer nobody else had reached for. Not one wall, carrying two incompatible truths at once. A second stone, small, set a respectful distance from the first, plain and unadorned by any crew's own emblem — not for enemies of the crew, and not for the fallen it had chosen to remember as its own, but for something narrower and harder to argue with: those who never chose the side they ended up dying for, and paid for that with their name going nowhere at all.

The woman carved her grandfather's name into it herself, the tools handed to her by the same hands that had cut Joran's and Garren Hask's into the wall a few dozen feet away. Nobody called it forgiveness. Nobody called it equivalence either.

"It's not the same wall," Kanja told her, when she was finished. "It was never going to be. But it's the same seawall holding both of them up."
