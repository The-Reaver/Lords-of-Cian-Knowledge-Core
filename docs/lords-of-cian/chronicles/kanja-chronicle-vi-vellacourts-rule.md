# Kanja Chronicle VI: Vellacourt's Rule

*Locked canon, Batch 358, 2026-10-03 (`MCD-1884`). Sixth entry of the "Kanja version"
Chronicle track, part of the Long Mask extension's first set (the Onyx accounts). Age 48, the
opening act of the Pirate Dawn, some weeks before the Night of Black Sails (`MCD-250`). The
Captain's first kill since the Sovereign Pier: ages 31-47 are kill-free by his own hand (`MCD-1882`
as amended Batch 356). A new, previously undramatized marquee kill (`MCD-1881`): Admiral Teshar
Vellacourt, a private convoy admiral running forced-labor transports under a Sovereign Trust
charter, whose standing practice of flooding his holds when pursued ("Vellacourt's Rule") had made
every pursuit of a slaver a death sentence for the people chained below. The account his surviving
sailing master carries home is why ships later strike to the black sails on sight. Narrated
retrospectively by Onyx of Oblivion as "the blade" (`VB-062`/`VB-063`, `ARS-437`), opening on a
Dark Ledger entry; Onyx was sealed at L9 and was not present. Killing per `CC-161` as amended
Batches 355-356: terms stated once, no death announced, a necessity kill inside an attack. New
named characters: Teshar Vellacourt and Mordane (his sailing master, surname only), both
collision-checked clean against the full ledger and Chronicle corpus. No child-safety issues. Abad's approval: "go."*

---

Dark Ledger. 555,984,000 seconds.

Seventeen years of count stand behind it. The blade logged no kill jolt in all that count. It logged sleep, fevers, the strain of hauling and rowing, wounds that opened and closed. Nothing more.

Then the entry.

A held breath. Long. Longer than lungs of his size should carry. Then cold came all at once and stayed in the skin. A strain in the right hand. The hand closed on something. The something fought to move.

Then one kill jolt.

One.

The heart did not rise for it.

The blade logged it as a signal against the count, with no cause beside it.

The blade records.

• • •

The cause came later, when his hand found the grip again. Scar tissue lies along the inside of the left wrist. The right palm keeps a memory of steel held still. The rest is Mordane's, and every harbor on the Gale Straits has it by heart. The blade has checked his account against the body. It holds.

• • •

Gale Straits. Night. A headland of black stone breaks the wind, and the lee water lies flat as poured lead.

Three ships ride at anchor with lanterns at every rail. The largest rides in the middle. Teshar Vellacourt's ship.

The admiral has never lost a convoy. That is his whole name in the trade. His rank is bought, and the charter came bundled with the rank. Sovereign Trust seal. Forced-labor transport along the southern coast. Debt-bonded workers are carried from the Holds that ruined them to the Holds that want them. Every crate stamped. Every body stamped.

Other cargo rides under the same seal, off any manifest the Trust would sign. The coves along that coast sell on a forty-five-day turn, and Vellacourt's hulls carry for the coves.

His holds are built to seal. The chained sit on one common line, run along the keelson. On the quarterdeck stands a lever of iron, painted the dull red of the rail so no eye stops on it. A rod drops from it through the decks to two sea-cocks below the waterline.

When a ship he cannot outrun closes on him, he pulls it.

The hold floods in minutes. Boarders find a dry deck, a courteous admiral, and no evidence of anything. The charter pays him for cargo lost to piracy.

Per head.

The blade weighs the lever. Rust.

Sailors call it Vellacourt's Rule. Other captains copy it. Nine ships on that coast now carry a red lever on the quarterdeck. Every pursuit of a slaver on that coast ends in a drowned hold. Every drowned hold is logged to the pursuer's account.

The Captain spends a season on it. One question fills the season. How to board a ship nobody can board. How to reach the bottom of a lever before any hand reaches its top.

The answer is water.

• • •

His own ship stands off past the headland, dark hull and plain canvas. Nothing on her a watch would keep in memory.

He goes over her side alone.

The water is cold, and the cold enters through the coat. Forge-Coat, the second one. Treated sea-leather over Dead Drakma wire, the armored collar high at the throat. Nine kilograms dry. More now. Soaked through, it drags at the shoulders like a drowned sailor's arms.

He keeps it on. The deck will want it.

He swims the distance in slow strokes that leave no splash. Then down. Under the keel of Vellacourt's ship, where no watch ever looks.

The breath holds.

Black water. The weight of the hull hangs above him. Barnacle and weed rasp under the palms.

Then a pressure gathers behind the eyes. A cold line draws itself across the dark, and the copper lenses carry it. The hull ghosts out of the black in pale strokes. Keelson. Frames. A rod falling from far above. Two valves seated in the planking. The strokes lie across the dark like a shipwright's chalk on wet slate. The pressure stays. The lines stay.

He finds the first spindle.

A closed valve opens again for any hand that wants it. He leaves it as it sits. He sets the riveted plate of the gauntlet against the spindle and leans. The iron gives slow. The thread shears. The spindle bends past any turning, either way, forever.

Second spindle. Same lean. Same shear.

The lungs complain only near the end.

The blade knows that breath now. The Dark Ledger kept it without a cause. Long. Too long. Iron.

• • •

He climbs the anchor cable. In through the stern hatch of the hold. No one locks that hatch. No one has ever come in that way.

Dark. Bilge stink. Sweat. Old urine. Iron chain on wet wood.

Three hundred and forty people on one line.

They see the eyes first. Two points of amber, low, sliding over the stern sill. The lenses glow in the black. Phosphor. A flaw in the first coating. He keeps the flaw.

Nobody screams. Chained people learn early. A scream moves the line. A moving line pulls every wrist on it.

He crouches. Close enough for the nearest to see a face behind the glow.

"Water's shut," he says. Low. Rough. "Stays shut. Back before light."

He gives no name. He never gives a name in a hold.

He goes up the forward ladder.

• • •

Main deck. Lanterns. Wet planking under lamplight.

Twelve capsules ride the coat's shoulders. Smoke enough to blind the whole waist of the ship. He opens none. A death in smoke becomes rumor. This deck stays lit.

The watch sees him. Soaked coat. Collar up. Amber eyes in the collar's shadow.

The bell. Shouts. The forecastle empties. Forty men. Belaying pins. Boarding axes. A cleaver from the galley.

He stands in the center of the deck.

He does not move.

They come to the edge of the light. They stop there. Forty men. One boarder. The boarder still. No crew of theirs has seen a boarder stand still.

He speaks once. The voice comes out of the collar low and grated. Wrong for a throat. It carries to the bow without rising.

"Sit. You row home."

Nothing more.

The bosun sits first. The hardest man aboard. Pin across his knees. Then the rest. Planking creaks under forty weights settling.

Bosun. Iron.

Mordane tells it after. The voice asks for nothing. It sounds like a thing decided in a room they were never let into. Only the result is left for them to hear.

• • •

Vellacourt comes down from the quarterdeck.

Narrow man. Careful. Well-coated, in his middle years, with a cutlass at the hip worn by a hand that knows it. He takes the ladder without hurry and stops in the light.

He looks at his crew on the planking. He looks at the boarder.

He turns. Climbs the ladder again. Sets his hand on the red lever.

Pulls.

Nothing.

He pulls again. The rod moves. Far below, the bent spindles do not.

"Won't open," the Captain says.

Vellacourt lets go of the lever.

He takes a folded paper from his coat. Comes down the ladder with it. Unfolds it under the lantern. Reads aloud.

The charter. The Trust's seal. The right to carry bonded labor along that coast. The indemnity for cargo lost to piracy. He reads it well, in a clear voice, every clause. He reads it to his crew more than to the boarder. He wants them to carry home an admiral who stood on his own deck with the law in his hand.

The Captain lets him finish.

Vellacourt folds the paper. Puts it away.

"Bosun," he says. "Lamp oil. The hold."

The bosun does not rise.

The admiral takes the lantern down from the mainmast hook himself and walks for the forward hatch. Lantern in the left hand. Open hatch. Three hundred and forty below on one chain. Bilge timber soaked in years of tar and pitch.

The Captain steps into the line between the admiral and the hatch.

Vellacourt sets the lantern on the hatch coaming. Draws.

• • •

The cutlass comes fast. Low. Good line. Thirty years of practice in it.

It hunts the gap between coat cuff and gauntlet plate. The first gauntlets carry riveted plate over the back of the hand and nothing behind the wrist. No brace. Open skin.

Steel opens the inside of the left wrist along the tendon line. Heat. Then wet.

The Captain gives the wrist. The wrist brings the cutlass where the right hand waits.

The right hand closes on the blade's spine. Riveted plate on forged steel. Grind of metal on metal.

The cutlass fights to move. It does not move.

That is the strain. The Dark Ledger has it.

Vellacourt's left hand drops to his sash. A dirk. Out. Up. Driving for the collar seam. For the throat.

The Rexmar Machete is already in the Captain's left hand. Bleeding hand. Brass rivets. Grip worn smooth. A laborer's tool. Older than his name.

One stroke.

Under the ribs. Up. The way a hand that has dressed meat for the pot opens a carcass. Short. Deep. Final.

The dirk falls on the planking. Rings once.

He holds Vellacourt while it ends. Keeps him off his crew. Lays him on the deck.

That is the jolt. One.

The heart stays level. The blade logged that too.

He wipes the machete on the nearest cloth. The nearest cloth is the charter. The seal smears red.

He moves the lantern off the hatch coaming. Sets it on the deck. Far from the oil.

• • •

"Who keeps the log?"

A grey sailor stands up from the planking, weathered, legs unwilling under him. Mordane. Sailing master. Eleven years under Vellacourt. Thirty years in the trade.

The Captain sends the lanterns down the hatch. Mordane counts the hold aloud. All three hundred and forty. Then the crew strikes the chains. Their own hands. Every link.

The Captain stands at the hatch through all of it. A strip of coat lining bound around the left wrist. Blood through the strip. Silent.

When the last link falls, he looks at Mordane.

"Tell it exact."

That is all he says to him.

He goes down into the boats with the first of the freed. He does not come back up.

• • •

The crew rows home. Every man of the forty.

The other two ships of the convoy cut their cables before dawn and run with their holds full. He lets them go. Their captains watched the whole of it from their own rails, in lantern light, with nothing hidden.

Inside a month, both holds empty at a quay. In front of a harbormaster. Freely. By their own captains' order. Both captains tell the harbormaster why.

Mordane tells it in every port on the Straits.

He tells it exact. The lever pulled twice. The paper read aloud. The bosun first to sit. The lamp oil called for. The lantern on the coaming. The cutlass. The dirk. One stroke.

He tells one number more. The sailing master keeps the log. The log copies the filings. Three holds flooded under the Rule. Indemnity claimed per head. Six hundred and twelve heads. Filed in the admiral's own hand. Paid by the Trust.

Mordane draws the lesson himself. The harbors draw it with him. Two ways a night like that can go. Flood the hold, and a lit deck ends like Vellacourt's. Strike, and the crew sits, and the crew rows home.

Sailors laugh at tavern stories of smoke and burning eyes. No one laughs at a sailing master who knows which hand the admiral used on the lever, and how many times. Plainness makes it heavier.

Mordane. Iron.

Inside a season, the red levers come out of the quarterdecks. No one orders it. Shipwrights who fitted them are asked to pull them. Quietly. Paid quietly. Sea-cocks plugged with lead. Nine levers on record. More come out than nine.

Some weeks after, the sails on the Captain's ships go black. The reason is his. The blade does not tell it.

The black crescent comes out of the Straits. The story is already in every harbor. Waiting. No captain needs to verify. They have Mordane. Ships strike on sight.

The legend stands on that deck. One lit deck. One admiral. Forty men rowing home. Told with nothing added.

• • •

The entry is reconciled.

The held breath was the swim beneath the keel. The cold was the water. The strain in the right hand was a cutlass held still in riveted plate. The kill jolt was Teshar Vellacourt. An admiral who never lost a convoy. Six hundred and twelve drowned under the law. Every one filed. Every one paid.

Vellacourt read his paper true. The paper was true. He drowned people under it. Then he walked a lantern to a hatch above three hundred and forty more. He drew on the one man in his way. He drew a second blade for the throat.

Rust.

Mercy is earned. Memory is the receipt.

Vellacourt earned none. Mordane carries the receipt. Six hundred and twelve. Three hundred and forty. Forty men home. One dead.

The cost to the Captain: a scar inside the left wrist. Thirty-two years on, the next gauntlets carry a brace across the wrist. He says the brace has nothing to do with Vellacourt. The blade records what he says.

The heart did not rise. The blade logged it as unmoved. Now the blade knows the season behind it. A whole season spent on hulls, valves, cold water, and the length of one breath. The work was finished before the deck. Nothing was left over to rise.

Five hundred fifty-five million, nine hundred eighty-four thousand seconds. One kill jolt. Cause entered.

The blade records.
