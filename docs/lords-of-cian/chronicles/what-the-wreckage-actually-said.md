# What the Wreckage Actually Said

*Locked canon, Batch 195, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-675`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XL, wave 14 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The collapse killed no one, but it destroyed a week's worth of shoring work and cost the crew a
full day they couldn't spare, and two competing explanations circulated within hours: an honest
structural accident, or sabotage by someone inside the crew who'd had access to the section
overnight.

Kanja read the wreckage itself rather than either story.

"An honest failure breaks in a pattern," he told the crew chief, tracing the fracture lines across
the fallen timber with the same patience he'd once used to diagnose a joint failure on a stranger's
cart. "Wood under genuine overload splinters along the grain, unevenly, because the fibers are
tearing under real stress they weren't built to carry. Look at this brace instead. The break's too
clean, too consistent across three separate timbers that shouldn't have failed the same way at the
same time unless something helped them along."

"You're saying it was sabotage."

"I'm saying the wreckage doesn't read like an accident, whatever it happened to look like from the
outside." He examined a fourth timber, confirming the pattern. "A genuine collapse doesn't fail
four separate supports identically. Someone weakened these deliberately, hours before the section
came down, probably with a blade rather than any tool that would have left different marks."

The investigation that followed, built entirely on what the wreckage itself demonstrated rather than
on suspicion or accusation, eventually identified a laborer recently pressured by threats against
family still living under Trust jurisdiction — not a traitor by inclination, but a man coerced into
a single act he'd clearly hated performing, judging by how amateurish the sabotage actually was
once someone knew to look for it correctly.

"What happens to him?" the crew chief asked, once the truth was established beyond argument.

"Depends what he tells us about who's threatening his family, and whether he's willing to help us
protect them instead of hurting us again." Kanja set the last examined timber down. "I'm not
interested in punishing a man who was cornered into this. I'm interested in the wreckage telling the
truth clearly enough that nobody innocent gets blamed for it in his place, and that starts with
reading what actually happened instead of guessing at who to be angry with first."
