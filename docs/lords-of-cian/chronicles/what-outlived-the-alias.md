# What Outlived the Alias

*Locked canon, Batch 257, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1200`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle LXXXIX, wave 30. A synthesis entry -- the guild, the tradesmen's association, and the workers' council are shown functioning independently years later, with no need of Kanja's presence at all.*

---

He came back to Killane years after the peace settled, unannounced and mostly unrecognized, and spent an afternoon simply walking through what he'd helped build without anyone realizing the Blue-Collar Titan was standing in the room.

The smiths' guild that had once tested him with a warped axle housing was training its fourth generation of journeymen on a certification standard that had absorbed his honest failures alongside his successes, run entirely by masters who'd never met him. The tradesmen's association, founded without his name attached on purpose, had grown into the district's primary voice on construction standards, its founding charter's insistence on carrying no individual's name still intact two generations on. The workers' council, which had once formally reprimanded him for bypassing its own protocol, was mid-session settling a dispute between two crews over resource allocation, its emergency-authorization clause -- written because of that exact reprimand -- cited by name in the proceedings.

None of the three institutions needed him in the room to function. That had always been the stated goal, across dozens of Chronicles spent teaching, sponsoring, submitting to process, declining seats he'd been offered -- but seeing it actually true, from outside, without his own presence propping any part of it up, landed differently than simply believing it would happen eventually.

An elderly journeyman -- the orphaned boy from decades back, master in his own right for years now, silver at his temples -- recognized him near the council chamber's entrance and said nothing to announce it, matching his own quiet the way he'd learned to.

"They don't know you built half of what they're standing on," the journeyman said.

"They don't need to," Kanja said, watching the council session continue without a single head turning toward the doorway. "That was always the point. If they had to know, it would mean it still depended on them knowing."

"Does it bother you? Being forgotten inside your own work?"

Kanja considered the question honestly before he answered it. "It would bother me if it needed me forgotten to survive. This didn't need forgetting. It needed independence. Those aren't the same thing, and I got the one that actually mattered."
