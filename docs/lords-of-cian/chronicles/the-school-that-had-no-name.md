# The School That Had No Name

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Crow King Alias Chronicle XLVIII, wave 16, closing
the wave. Rebellion era, later than the other two entries in this wave. Not a territory Chronicle.
Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters beyond the already-locked apprentice,
unnamed matching established pattern; the twenty scouts are unnamed as a group.*

---

The regional commander's request was plain enough when it finally reached Kanja directly rather than
through a chain of increasingly frustrated intermediaries: twenty of her own scouts, drawn from a unit
that had never worked alongside Kanja's crew and never would on any regular basis, needed to learn
enough of the craft to stop losing people to encirclements the crew's own methods could have avoided.
She didn't want a story about how it was done. She wanted it taught.

"It's never been taught outside people who came up inside the crew," the apprentice said, when Kanja
brought the request to her rather than deciding it alone. "Every one of us — you, me, the ones I've
taught — learned it because we were already standing next to the person who knew it, watching it get
used in operations we were already part of. Twenty strangers who've never run with us at all is a
different thing entirely."

"Is that a reason not to, or just a reason it's going to be harder than anything either of us has done
with it before?"

She considered it honestly before answering. "The second one, I think. I don't actually see why it
has to stay inside people who happened to find their way into this specific crew. That was never a
rule. It was just how it happened to grow, because that's who was standing nearby when I needed
someone standing nearby."

They ran it over six weeks, in a borrowed drill yard rather than a battlefield — the first time either
of them had taught the craft as a deliberate curriculum, with a fixed sequence of lessons, rather than
handing pieces of it to one person at a time as operational need happened to demand. Half the twenty
struggled with the sustained vocal discipline entirely and were reassigned to the signal and
listening components instead; three turned out to have exactly the kind of ear the apprentice herself
had started with, catching patterns after a single demonstration that had taken others weeks to
approximate.

"It doesn't feel like the same thing," the apprentice admitted, midway through, watching one of the
three run a drill correctly on the second attempt. "Teaching one person who already trusts you and
teaching twenty who've been ordered here by someone else entirely."

"It isn't the same thing," Kanja agreed. "It's the same discipline wearing a different shape, same as
it did the first time you taught someone yourself. I don't think it matters that this shape looks more
like an institution than a lineage. I think it matters that it still works, taught this way, to people
who'll carry it into units we'll never personally see operate."

By the close of the sixth week, the regional commander's unit had lost none of its own scouts to an
encirclement in eleven engagements — not because any of the twenty had become what the apprentice was,
but because eleven engagements' worth of patrols had each, independently, mistaken a false signal for
a true one long enough to matter.

No one gave the six weeks a name. The scouts who'd sat through it simply began, without discussion,
teaching pieces of what they'd learned to the scouts who came after them.
