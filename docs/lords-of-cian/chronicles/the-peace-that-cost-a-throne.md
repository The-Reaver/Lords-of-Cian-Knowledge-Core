# The Peace That Cost a Throne

*Locked canon, Batch 258, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1221`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
LXXXIII, wave 28. Kanja declines to use the fleet's reputation to settle a succession dispute. Not a
territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The island principality's council came to the fleet with a request Kanja hadn't heard before in a
decade of doing this work — not rescue, not protection, but a verdict. Two claimants to the small
territory's hereditary seat, both with genuine, tangled claims, had brought their dispute to the brink
of open conflict between their supporters, and a faction on the council wanted the ghost fleet's own
reputation lent to one side simply to end the standoff before it turned violent.

"Your fleet's word carries weight nobody here can argue with," the council's spokesman said. "Back
either claimant publicly, and the other side stands down rather than fight a war they can't win
against your reputation too."

Kanja turned it down flatly, and did it in front of both claimants rather than privately, so neither
could later claim a quiet arrangement had been struck. "I've spent this whole conflict trying to make
sure the Trust doesn't get to decide things for people who never asked them to. I'm not going to turn
around and decide your succession for you just because it's easier than letting your own people work
it out."

"People are going to die if we don't," the spokesman pressed.

"Then let's stop that without deciding who sits on a seat I have no right to weigh in on." Kanja
proposed something else instead — not a verdict, but a mediated, witnessed council where every voice
on the island, not just the two claimants' armed supporters, could speak on who should hold the seat,
with the fleet present only to guarantee nobody used violence to shortcut the process.

It took eleven days. Efa Gol organized the logistics of it, more council than combat, while Garren
Hask kept a plain record of every voice heard, refusing either claimant's supporters any special
weight in how it was logged. The seat went, in the end, to neither original claimant, but to a
compromise figure both sides trusted enough to accept rather than fight over — a result Kanja hadn't
engineered and wouldn't have predicted.

"You could have ended this in a day," the spokesman admitted afterward, "if you'd just picked one of
them."

"I could have," Kanja agreed. "It wouldn't have been your peace, though. It would have been mine, lent
to you. This way, whatever happens next, it's actually yours."
