# The Name They Gave What He Never Named

*Locked canon, Batch 261, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1307`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LXXXVIII, wave 30, first entry. The academic institute formally names the doctrine as a discipline; Kanja declines an honorary role. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The independent academy that had first published the Trust scholar's once-shelved research sent a delegation years later, not with an offer this time, but with a finished thing: a formal charter establishing diagnostic listening as its own recognized field of applied structural science, complete with an agreed name, a course of study, and a founding chair already filled by the scholar himself.

"We'd like your name attached to it," the scholar said, older now, the modest venue that had once published his work having grown into something closer to a proper institution in the years since. "Not as founder. As namesake, if you'll allow it. The field needs a name people can actually teach without it sounding like a rumor."

"What did you settle on?"

"Tension Reading, in the formal charter. Plainly descriptive, nothing dramatic about it — we debated something grander and decided the doctrine's own honesty deserved a name as unadorned as the thing itself." The scholar set the charter down between them. "The honorary chair is a separate offer. A title, a seat on the founding council, nothing requiring your actual presence beyond what you'd choose to give it."

Kanja read the charter without touching it the way he might a structure, listening for what was actually being asked underneath the formal language. A name for the field, plain and honest, matching everything the doctrine had ever claimed to be. An honorary role that cost him nothing and would, he suspected, quietly become something else entirely the moment his name sat attached to an institution rather than simply to the work.

"Name the field whatever serves the teaching best," he said. "I'll not argue with 'Tension Reading' — it's honest, and honest is the only thing I ever asked this to be. The chair, though — I'd rather you leave empty, or fill with someone who'll actually sit in it and do the work of running an academy properly. I've turned down money for this before. I'm not going to accept a title instead, just because a title doesn't come with a price tag attached to see."

"Even an honorary one changes nothing about how the field is taught."

"It changes who gets asked about it, eventually, instead of whoever's actually still doing the work. I've watched a general's honest report get credited to my name more than to the man who wrote it, simply because my name traveled further than his did. I don't want the same thing happening to every honest reader who ever comes after me, footnoted under a chair I never actually sat in." Kanja pushed the charter back across the table, the decision made without much visible weighing of it. "Teach it under whatever name serves it best. Just don't teach it as mine."
