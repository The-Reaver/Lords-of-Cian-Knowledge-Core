# The Boarding in the Blind Dark

*Locked canon, Batch 264, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1374`). Captain Alias Chronicle LXXIV, wave 25. A detailed Long-Mask-era boarding action fought in a moonless storm at sea, fought with the Forge-Coat, Ironfall Boots, Ironhand Gauntlets, Sovereign Eyes, and the Rexmar Machete rather than the Trinity -- the sub-series' first true blind-dark combat showcase. Corrected, Batch 314, 2026-09-28: an earlier draft mistakenly staged this as a full-Trinity showcase (Mafesto, Obsidian Malice, Onyx of Oblivion's Whisper of Shadows), despite this Chronicle's placement well within the 284-year Long Mask era, after Kanja's already-locked age-30 surrender of the Trinity to its sealed vault (`MCD-246`); the text below now uses only the gear and biological traits he actually retains during this era.*

---

There was no moon that night and no lantern light worth risking, not against a slaver vessel running dark specifically to avoid being spotted before it made open water, and Corren Halst's plan depended entirely on getting the Second Chance alongside without a single visible signal passing between the two boarding teams. It was, Pell Ostra said flatly before they went in, the kind of job that separated crews who trained together from crews who'd merely fought together.

They went in on touch and sound alone. Callum Breck ran the approach on hand-signals nobody could see, felt instead through rope-tension code passed boat to boat, a language the crew had built over years for exactly this kind of night. Kanja crossed the gap between hulls guided by nothing more than his own instinctive Rexmar-Mar tactical sense and the Sovereign Eyes' overlay, reading footfall vibration through the deck itself rather than sight, every enemy position mapped before his boots ever found the rail. The Rexmar Machete took the first drawn blade by feel, trained reflex turning aside a lunge he never saw coming by the pressure of displaced air alone; the Forge-Coat and Ironfall Boots, working together, absorbed and grounded the force of the exchange rather than let it carry through -- drilled technique and gear engineering standing in for anything the Trinity used to do without thinking. A single precisely angled strike from the Ironhand Gauntlets, thrown by memory of the hold's layout from the boarding brief rather than any line of sight, sheared the chained hold's locking bar through leverage and trained technique alone -- no discharge of any kind, just plate and muscle -- opening it without touching a single captive chained inside it.

Efa Gol coordinated the whole boarding from the Second Chance's own dark deck by sound alone, counting engagement noises against the boarding roster she'd memorized rather than written down, since no lantern could risk giving away the approach. When she called the hold clear, she called it before anyone topside could confirm it visually — she'd simply counted the right number of freed voices against the right number of chains going slack, and trusted the number.

Dawn found forty-one freed captives blinking against the first real light they'd seen in weeks, and a crew that hadn't lit a single lantern until the fighting was entirely done. "I've fought in worse conditions," Kanja admitted afterward, "but I've never trusted a crew's coordination more than I did tonight, precisely because none of us could see each other doing it."
