# The Duel He Didn't Need Onyx For

*Locked canon, Batch 259, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1244`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LXXIX, wave 27, first
entry. Age 215, V3 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The captain who'd trained a bodyguard specifically to fight the Scourge legend rather than the man
had done her homework well. She'd studied every account of the black-sailed reputation she could
buy or steal, built her champion's whole style around countering a Trinity-armed swordsman's rhythm
— the weighted counters, the reach compensations, the specific defensive geometry a warrior trained
against Onyx of Oblivion's five named techniques would need. It was excellent work. It was also
built to counter a fight he wasn't having.

Forty minutes into the duel, on a ship's deck neither man had left since it started, the temptation
sat exactly where it always sat in a fight this evenly matched and this long: the L9 seal was his
own choice, kept for two hundred years by discipline rather than necessity, and a fight dragging
toward its fortieth minute against an opponent this precisely prepared was the kind of pressure that
made a two-hundred-year-old choice feel, for exactly as long as the thought lasted, negotiable.

He didn't reach for it. What he reached for instead was plainer and slower: the Rexmar Machete,
carried the whole fight as backup rather than primary, and twenty years of the same accumulated
gauntlet-and-footwork technique that had freed two hundred and eleven captives off a slaver galleon
decades before any of this gear existed to help him do it. The bodyguard's whole training had
prepared for a legend. What actually beat him was closer to what had always actually been doing the
work underneath the legend — plain swordsmanship, plain endurance, forty-one minutes instead of
forty, one exchange longer than the other man's preparation had accounted for.

He didn't kill him. The captain's operation folded within the month regardless, its funding having
depended entirely on a champion who could no longer walk without a cane. Garren Hask logged it
plainly: a hard fight, honestly won, the seal exactly where it had been kept for two centuries and
exactly where it stayed.
