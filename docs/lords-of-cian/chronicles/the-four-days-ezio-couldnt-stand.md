# The Four Days Ezio Couldn't Stand

*Locked canon, Batch 256, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1156`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle LXXII, wave 24, closing the wave. The first entry where Ezio is incapacitated and Kanja must run the cross-referencing arithmetic alone, imperfectly, publicly correcting his own error rather than hiding it.*

---

Ezio went down on the second morning of the district, a fall on wet dock planking that broke nothing but wrenched a knee badly enough that standing over a ledger table for the hours the work required simply wasn't possible. He offered, from a cot, to dictate the cross-referencing to Kanja step by step. Kanja refused the offer within the first hour, not out of pride, but because dictated arithmetic through someone else's injury was slower and worse than either of them doing the job properly, and the district's testimony line was already forming outside.

He'd watched Ezio do this work for two years. He understood the principle of it — testimony against production logs, production logs against delivery receipts, discrepancies followed to their source rather than assumed. What he didn't have was Ezio's particular, trained fluency with columns of figures, the instinct for which number in a long tally was the one worth double-checking before it ever reached a conclusion. He was slower by a wide margin, re-adding sums Ezio would have caught at a glance, and by the fourth entry of the morning he'd made a mistake he didn't catch until a worker, politely, pointed out that the total didn't match what he could count on his own fingers from his own pay stubs.

Kanja didn't quietly fix it and move on. He stopped the line, said plainly in front of everyone still waiting that the figure he'd posted an hour earlier was wrong, walked back through the arithmetic out loud so anyone watching could follow exactly where the error had come from — a column he'd added twice instead of once — and posted the corrected number beside the crossed-out original rather than simply replacing it.

"You could've fixed it without saying anything," a worker told him afterward. "Nobody would've known."

"Ezio would've known, eventually, when he checked my work. And you'd have known something was off even if you couldn't say what." He kept working the line himself for the next three days, at roughly half Ezio's usual pace, making two more small errors he caught and corrected the same public way before they reached anyone's final tally. Ezio, back on his feet by the fourth day and looking over what Kanja had produced without him, found the arithmetic sound everywhere it mattered, if considerably less elegant than his own, and told him so.

"You're not as fast as me," Ezio said, checking a column for the third time out of habit rather than doubt.

"I never claimed to be. I claimed the numbers wouldn't get worse because you weren't standing." What the district actually remembered, weeks later, wasn't that Kanja had made mistakes. It was that the one time the method's own engine faltered, it had said so out loud, in front of everyone, rather than let a quiet correction pass as if the mistake had never happened at all.
