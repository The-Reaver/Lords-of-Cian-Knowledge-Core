# What Corren Halst Wouldn't Report

*Locked canon, Batch 258, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1222`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
LXXXIV, wave 28, closing the wave. A character-depth entry testing Corren Halst's own restraint
against leverage he could have used. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose.*

---

Corren Halst found the ledger by accident, tucked in the false bottom of a strongbox aboard a Trust
supply vessel the fleet had disabled and boarded for its cargo manifest alone — a private accounting,
in a Trust quartermaster's own hand, of years of skimmed rations sold on the side while soldiers under
the man's command went hungry on official half-portions. Nothing to do with the ghost fleet at all.
Nothing Kanja had asked him to look for.

He brought it to Kanja anyway, because that was the standard the fleet had built, but he brought it
with a question rather than a recommendation. "This isn't ours to use. It's not about us, it's not
evidence of anything we're accused of, and it's not information we earned honestly — I found it going
through cargo, not investigating anyone."

"What do you want to do with it?"

"Burn it," Halst said. "Or hand it to whoever it'd actually help — the soldiers who went hungry, if
there's a way to get it to them without it costing them more than it costs the quartermaster. Using it
to threaten the Trust, or to buy ourselves something, feels like exactly the kind of leverage Hask's
ledger was built to keep us honest about never taking."

Kanja let him make the call rather than making it for him, the same trust he extended to every crew
member who'd earned it through years of judgment exercised well. Halst chose the second option — a
quiet, untraceable delivery of the evidence to a garrison chaplain known for actually pursuing
corruption complaints rather than burying them, rather than to any authority the fleet could be seen
to be pressuring directly.

Three months later, word reached the fleet that the quartermaster had been quietly relieved and the
ration theft corrected, with no mention anywhere of a ghost fleet's involvement.

"You could have used that to force real concessions out of the Trust," Efa Gol pointed out, once the
outcome was confirmed. "Nobody would have blamed you."

"I'd have blamed myself," Halst said. "The doctrine's not just about what we don't do to enemies. It's
about what we don't do with things that fall into our hands that were never really ours to use."
