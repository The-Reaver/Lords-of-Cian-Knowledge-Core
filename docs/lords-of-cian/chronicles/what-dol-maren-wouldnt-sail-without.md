# What Dol Maren Wouldn't Sail Without

*Locked canon, Batch 261, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1304`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LXXXV, wave 29, first entry. Dol Maren asks Kanja to verify a repaired hull before a dangerous voyage. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

Dol Maren didn't ask for much, and he asked for even less than usual when he finally came to Kanja directly rather than send word through Garren Hask the way he generally routed anything that wasn't purely mechanical. He set down a single sheet with a number on it, a material, and a conclusion — his own fixed reporting format, the one Kanja had heard about secondhand more often than he'd seen it delivered in person.

*Keel repair. Four weeks' work. I trust the joinery. I want your hand on it before we sail regardless.*

"You built it," Kanja said. "You trust the work. What does my hand tell you that yours doesn't already know?"

"Mine tells me what I calculated should hold." Dol Maren's verbal economy didn't stretch further than it needed to, the way it never had. "Yours tells me what actually is holding. Those aren't the same question, and I've spent long enough building things to know the difference costs lives when somebody pretends otherwise."

It wasn't the first time a crew member had asked him to read something with no enemy anywhere in the request — Efa Gol had once brought him a dead partner's keepsake pulley off duty, no stake beyond her own need to know. This felt different. Dol Maren wasn't asking for comfort. He was asking for verification, the same cold, calculation-trusting standard he applied to everything else in his life, extended now to the one thing his own careful work couldn't fully confirm from the inside.

Kanja put his palm to the repaired keel section and read it the way he'd read a thousand structures before it — doubled verification, patient, no urgency forcing the pace. The joinery held true under both passes, a repair as sound as Dol Maren's own reputation suggested it would be.

"It's good work," he said. "Genuinely good. I don't find anything in it I wouldn't trust with my own crew's lives."

"I know it's good work. I built it." Dol Maren studied the confirmation without visible relief, the way a man studies a number that's simply now been checked rather than a fear that's been lifted. "I didn't ask because I doubted my own hands. I asked because trust that isn't verified is just hope wearing a more comfortable name, and I've never once sailed on hope alone if I could help it."

"Then why me? You could have asked any competent inspector."

"Because any competent inspector tells me what the wood looks like. You tell me what the wood is actually *doing*." Dol Maren folded the sheet away, the closest thing to explanation he seemed inclined to offer. "There's a difference, same as there's a difference between a calculation and a fact. I trust calculations. I trust facts more."
