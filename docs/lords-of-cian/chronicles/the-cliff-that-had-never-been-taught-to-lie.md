# The Cliff That Had Never Been Taught to Lie

*Locked canon, Batch 283, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1487`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XCIV, wave 32, first entry. The doctrine's first application to a natural geological formation with no builder, forger, or living growth cycle behind it. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The overhang had stood above the fishing settlement longer than anyone's grandparents could account for, and the tremors of the past month had done nothing to it that the villagers could point to and call proof. No new cracks anyone had seen. No dust shaken loose in daylight. Only a feeling, passed house to house, that the stone above their roofs had started sounding different at night — and a feeling wasn't something the settlement's elder could take to a council and ask for relocation money against.

"I don't fight stone," Kanja said, standing at the base of the cliff with one palm flat against rock that had never once been asked to bear a load on anyone's behalf. "I read tension somebody put into something on purpose — a beam cut to a measurement, a cable drawn to a spec, even a root growing toward a job it means to do. This has never been anyone's on purpose. It's just weather, argued with itself for longer than your whole village has existed."

He listened anyway, because a request was a request and a doctrine that only answered convenient questions wasn't a doctrine worth keeping. What he found had no baseline to measure against — no forger's intended value, no engineer's tolerance sheet, nothing but the accumulated argument of ten thousand tides and as many frosts, layered into the rock in bands he had no name for and no teacher to ask about. He built the baseline himself, on the spot, the only way the doctrine had ever really worked: by listening long enough that the stone's own honest history became the only standard there was.

It took the better part of a day. What he found wasn't a clean answer, and he told the elder so plainly. The bulk of the overhang was sound — centuries sound, likely to outlast every person currently living beneath it. But one seam, low and easy to miss, carried a strain pattern that would not hold forever. Not this season. Not, by his best honest guess, for several years yet. But it would go, someday, on its own schedule, with or without another tremor to blame it on.

"So do we leave," the elder asked, "or do we stay?"

"That's not a question the stone answers. It only told me the truth about itself — not what you should do about it." Kanja looked up at the seam a last time, filing its signature away the way he'd file any live structure's. "Move what you can't afford to lose. Keep living under the rest of it, if that's the choice you'd make anyway. I can tell you what's true. I was never built to tell you what's brave."
