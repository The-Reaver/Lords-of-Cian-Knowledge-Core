# The Second Student

*Locked canon, Batch 197, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-719`); corrected Batch 321, 2026-10-02 (the opening
paragraph rewritten to introduce a genuinely new, distinct second trainee rather than wrongly
re-identifying the first student, `MCD-499`, as the subject of this test; the backstory reference
corrected from "an unarmed overseer" to the actual granary incident). The Iron Bastard Alias
Chronicle XXIV, wave 8 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle.
Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The new trainee had come to Kanja through the first student himself, who'd spent two years watching
her work a dockside crew's rigging before ever recommending her — and she'd spent that same two years
hearing, secondhand, about the night he'd stood beside a Trust granary, owned by a man who'd profited
from his own family's debt, and chosen not to bring it down. The story had stayed with her longer than
any technical lesson he'd actually taught her.

Kanja set her a task without supervision for the first time: a single supply-line gate, isolated,
low-stakes enough that a mistake would cost inconvenience rather than lives, and told her nothing
else.

He watched from a distance, close enough to intervene, far enough not to be a presence the student
could lean on.

She took nearly twice as long as Kanja himself would have — verifying the reading, verifying
it again, tracing the full tension distribution before ever raising a hand toward the structure — and
when the gate finally came down, it came down exactly as intended, no more damage than the task
required, no haste anywhere in it.

"You took your time," Kanja said afterward, not as criticism.

"You told me once that being right nine times out of nine mattered more than being fast. I didn't
have your instincts to fall back on if I rushed and got it wrong. So I didn't rush." She
studied the collapsed gate with something that looked almost like disbelief at her own competence. "I
kept waiting for you to step in."

"I know. That was the point of not doing it." Kanja considered her for a moment. "You didn't just
learn the technique. You learned to be honest with yourself about your own limits while using it,
which is the part I actually couldn't teach you directly. That had to come from you deciding to want
it."

"Does that mean I'm finished learning?"

"It means you're finally ready to keep learning without me standing over you while you do it. Those
aren't the same thing, and I don't think they ever will be, for either of us."
