# The Ledger of the Unclaimed

*Locked canon, Batch 264, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1368`). Captain Alias Chronicle LXVIII, wave 23. A pure humanitarian-logistics entry, no combat: Efa Gol runs a resettlement registry for war orphans.*

---

The forty-one children from the pass were the easy count. Behind them, scattered across three districts still sorting themselves out from the war's end, sat several hundred more with no surviving family able to claim them and no single office tracking who they were, where they'd come from, or who among the scattered relief networks might actually be looking. Efa Gol took one look at the mess of overlapping, half-duplicated lists three different relief offices had produced independently and did the only thing that made sense to her: she started her own ledger, cross-referenced against all three, and refused to stop until every name on every list pointed to exactly one child and not two.

It took her five months, most of it spent not on any ship at all but at a borrowed table in whichever district office would have her, matching handwriting against handwriting, cross-checking a scar description here against a birthmark noted there, catching four cases where the same child had been separately registered as dead by one office and alive by another. She found seventeen genuine family reunions the original offices' overlapping paperwork had simply missed, buried under duplicate entries nobody had thought to compare.

Kanja visited twice, mostly to watch rather than help — this was hers, the way the granary count and the coin ledger had always been hers, and his presence would only have slowed a process that ran on a kind of patient, exacting attention no Trinity gear could substitute for. "You could be doing this from a ship," he said once. "Why here, at a borrowed table, five months running?"

"Because a name that only lives on a ship's ledger is a name that disappears the day the ship sails somewhere else," she said, not looking up from the page. "This has to live somewhere that stays put."

When the final registry was done, she handed a copy to each of the three relief offices and kept none for herself — the crew's own ledger, she said, was for counting what the crew was responsible for, and these children weren't the crew's to claim credit for finding.
