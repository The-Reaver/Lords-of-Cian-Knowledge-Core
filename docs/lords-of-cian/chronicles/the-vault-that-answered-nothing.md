# The Vault That Answered Nothing

Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The Trust records vault had been built, deliberately, by an architect who had studied every account
of the resonance doctrine's prior defeats and designed against all of them at once: iron-banded door,
laminated felt-and-timber lining packed into every wall cavity specifically to absorb vibration before
it could travel, and a frame set in raw stone rather than any structure with its own competing tension
to read against.

The Aegis-Talisman's lens returned nothing usable. Not a false signal, not four alloys pretending to
be one — silence, the felt lining swallowing whatever resonance the iron door carried before it could
propagate far enough to diagnose.

Kanja stood with the lens against the door for the better part of ten minutes before he accepted what
the reading — or the absence of one — was actually telling him.

"It's not lying to me," he said, mostly to himself, mostly for Onyx's benefit. "It's not answering at
all. That's different. A structure that resists the read is still telling me something about itself.
A structure that swallows the question before it reaches the door isn't resisting anything. It's just
built so the door and the wall are never talking to each other in the first place."

He set the Talisman aside.

What followed was forty minutes of plain siege work — Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System converting
sledge blows into stored charge the way it always had, Obsidian Malice's discharge finally splitting
the iron along a mundane stress line no resonance had located, brute mechanical persistence rather
than diagnosis. It worked. It simply worked the way every soldier before the doctrine had always
worked doors, without elegance, without the certainty of a clean read guiding the strike.

"They built a room that doesn't ring," Kanja said afterward, studying the felt lining exposed by the
breach with something closer to respect than frustration. "That's a real answer to the doctrine, not
a trick. If it doesn't carry tension where I can hear it, I can't read what isn't there to read. I
still have hands. I'll use those instead."
