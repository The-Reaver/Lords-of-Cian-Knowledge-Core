# The First Night in the New Coat

*Locked canon, Batch 200, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-811`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle XXVI, wave 9 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The V4 Forge-Coat had spent six years in design and testing specifically to answer what a coiled
Living-Drakma lash had exposed decades earlier, and its first real deployment, against an ordinary
slaving crew that had no idea it was about to become a field test, went nowhere near as cleanly as
the smiths who'd built it had promised it would.

The redesigned grounding weave read the crew's crossbow volley correctly, absorbing it the way its
predecessor always had. It read the ship's own creaking rigging, disturbed by a sudden gust, as a
structural threat instead, and locked half a shoulder panel rigid at exactly the wrong moment, costing
him a full second of mobility he hadn't budgeted for.

"That's new," he muttered, working the panel loose by hand while a boarding pike came in under his
guard, caught instead by the Ironhand Gauntlets on reflex rather than by any advantage the new coat
had actually provided.

The Sovereign Eyes' recalibrated lenses, built to read further and finer than the old amber glow
ever had, overcorrected on the smoke-thick deck and washed the whole scene in a glare bright enough
that he had to blink through afterimages to find the ship's captain at all — a problem the old
lenses, cruder and dimmer, had never once given him in a hundred years of service.

He finished the engagement anyway, the way he'd finished every engagement before this coat existed,
relying more on the Rexmar Machete and plain instinct than on any of the six years of promised
refinement, and stood afterward on a cleared deck taking mental inventory of a garment that had cost
a fortune in Dead Drakma and honest labor and had, on its first real night, been more hindrance than
help twice in the space of one fight.

"They said this one would be better," Efa Gol's successor said, arriving to find him still working
the shoulder panel free.

"It will be. Eventually." He said it without frustration, the same flat cataloguing tone he'd used on
the old coat's failure years before. "Every piece I've ever worn started rough. The V1 coat barely
held together past a boarding party's first swing. This one just has more to get right before it
earns the trust the last one had." He finally freed the panel, testing the joint's new range with
visible skepticism. "Tell the smiths the shoulder locks on rigging vibration it shouldn't read as a
threat. That's the first thing to fix, before I trust it with anything that actually needs speed."

The lesson went into the coat's design notes that same week, quietly, the way every real improvement
in a hundred years of gear had — not from a triumphant unveiling, but from one rough, honestly
reported first night.
