# The First Night in the New Coat

*Locked canon, Batch 200, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-811`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle XXVI, wave 9 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Annals entry. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The Sovereign Eyes' V4 lenses had spent six years in design and testing specifically to answer what a
coiled Living-Drakma lash had exposed decades earlier, and their first real deployment, against an
ordinary slaving crew that had no idea it was about to become a field test, went nowhere near as
cleanly as the smiths who'd built them had promised they would.

The redesigned Voidstone lenses read the crew's own lantern-light and the smoke rolling across the
deck at a resolution the old amber glow had never offered, and overcorrected on the first try,
washing the whole scene in a glare bright enough that he had to blink through afterimages to find the
ship's captain at all — a problem the old lenses, cruder and dimmer, had never once given him in two
hundred years of service.

"That's new," he muttered, narrowing the aperture by hand while a boarding pike came in under his
guard, caught instead by the Ironhand Gauntlets on reflex rather than by anything the new lenses had
actually provided.

He finished the engagement anyway, the way he'd finished every engagement before these lenses
existed, relying more on the Rexmar Machete and plain instinct than on any of the six years of
promised refinement, and stood afterward on a cleared deck taking mental inventory of a system that
had cost a fortune in Voidstone and honest labor and had, on its first real night, been more
hindrance than help.

"They said this one would be better," Efa Gol's successor said, arriving to find him still adjusting
the lens housing.

"It will be. Eventually." He said it without frustration, the same flat cataloguing tone he'd used on
failures before. "Every piece I've ever worn started rough. The first lenses I ever had barely read a
dark room without flaring. These just have more to get right before they earn the trust the last pair
had." He finally settled the aperture, testing the new range with visible skepticism. "Tell the
smiths the glare response is tuned too wide for a smoke-thick deck. That's the first thing to fix,
before I trust it with anything that actually needs speed."

The lesson went into the lenses' design notes that same week, quietly, the way every real improvement
in two hundred years of gear had — not from a triumphant unveiling, but from one rough, honestly
reported first night.
