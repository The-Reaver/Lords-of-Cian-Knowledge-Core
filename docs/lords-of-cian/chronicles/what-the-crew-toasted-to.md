# What the Crew Toasted To

*Locked canon, Batch 138, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-463`). Captain Alias Chronicle IX, closing the third
wave — and closing the third wave for all eleven aliases. Rebellion era. Not a territory Chronicle.
New standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The crew held no formal ceremony for it — nothing so deliberate as an anniversary or a planned
observance — but somewhere across the war's long middle years, a quiet tradition had settled in
without anyone declaring it one: on nights when the fighting allowed it and enough of the crew found
themselves gathered in the same place at once, someone would raise a cup, and the rest would follow,
and the toast was always some variation of the same handful of words.

"To the man who digs his own crown," Corren Halst offered, one such evening, the old dredge-line
phrase still carrying weight decades after Warehouse Twelve.

"To the one who carries the charge for one person same as he would for the whole fleet," Pell Ostra
added, her own memory of a beam and forty seconds still close enough to color the words.

"To Captain," Garren Hask said simply, the word that had outlasted every alias the Directorate ever
bothered classifying, and the whole gathered crew drank to it together, the way they always did.

Kanja, present for this one, didn't interrupt the ritual to correct or deflect it, the way he might
have early in the war. He'd learned, somewhere across the years the crew had spent building this
small unspoken tradition without his direction, that the toast was never really about him specifically
— it was about the crew itself, the thing all of them had built together across every alias, every
battle, every quiet night like this one, that had outlasted whatever the Directorate chose to call
any of it.

"You don't say anything, most nights," Efa Gol observed, watching him simply hold the moment rather
than answer it. "You used to always have something to say back."

"I used to think the moment needed something from me to mean anything. It doesn't. It means something
because you're all still here to raise the cup at all, after everything this war's cost every one of
us." He raised his own, finally, matching the gesture rather than commenting on it. "To all of you.
Every name I've carried only ever meant anything because you were the ones who gave it weight."

The crew drank to that too, and the evening went on, ordinary and unremarkable, exactly the kind of
night none of them would have predicted surviving to see, years before.
