# What Danne Sok Heard Before Anyone Else

*Locked canon, Batch 266, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1394`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XCI, wave 31.
Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. Danne Sok's first dedicated in-era entry within the Trench
Monarch's own run, distinct from his already-locked origin memory (`MCD-530`).*

---

Danne Sok had been standing the same watch for so many months that most of the newer recruits
stopped noticing him the way they stopped noticing the canal's own current — present, useful,
unremarkable. He didn't correct them. He had never once, in all the time since his own freeing,
explained what he was doing when he stood a little apart from a work crew with his attention
angled wrong for the task in front of him, watching something nobody else had thought to watch.

The new man who'd joined the tally-verification line three weeks earlier asked too many questions
in the wrong order. He asked about site schedules before he asked about pay. He asked which
warehouses Kanja visited most often before he'd finished learning where the tools were kept. He
was careful about it, spacing the questions out across different conversations with different
people, so that no single person he spoke to would have heard enough to notice a pattern.

Danne Sok noticed. Not because any one question was wrong, but because he'd spent two years
learning the particular rhythm of a man doing honest work, and this man's rhythm was a half-beat
off from every other new hire who'd ever come through the line hungry and grateful and asking
where he should stand.

He didn't report it to Corren Halst that day, or the next. He watched instead — followed the new
man's movements at the edges of his own duties, noted which sites he lingered near, which evenings
he left early and came back late. On the fifth night, the man met an unfamiliar figure at the edge
of the canal, well outside the lit sections of the site, and handed over a folded sheet of paper
before either of them said a single word loud enough to carry.

Danne Sok took the sheet from the courier's hand an hour later, in the dark, without either man
ever seeing his face clearly enough to describe it afterward. He read it once — a crude but
accurate sketch of the crew's site rotation for the coming week — and burned it over a low flame
before it ever reached whoever had been waiting to receive it.

The informant was gone from the line within two days, quietly, without confrontation or accusation
loud enough for anyone else to connect to anything. Corren Halst asked, once, how Danne Sok had
known. Danne Sok didn't answer with words. He held up two fingers — a full half-beat, the gap
between an honest man's rhythm and a careful one's — and went back to his watch.
