# What Callum Breck Handed Him Instead of Words

*Locked canon, Batch 196, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-707`). Bane Alias Chronicle XLII, wave 14 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Breck had said almost nothing since the Black Trench — the four months of near-total silence, the
slow rebuilding of a voice through purely functional speech, was still ongoing this deep into the
Bane era, long before Ghost Harbor would eventually pull a full sentence back out of him. Most of the
column had learned to work around it, to read what he meant through the work of his hands instead of
through anything he said.

Karrow's Bend still weighed on Bane weeks after the broken promise there, more visibly than he let
most of the column see, and it was Breck — silent, watching, the way he watched everything since Nev
Torr — who noticed it first and did something about it in the only register still fully available to
him.

He found Bane alone one evening, said nothing at all, and simply set down, without comment, a small
carved marker — the kind the column used, when they used any marker at all, to note a promise kept
rather than a grave. He'd made it himself, quietly, evidently over several evenings, and it carried
no words, no explanation, nothing but the plain fact of its own careful construction.

Bane understood it anyway. "You're telling me the promise I broke isn't the only promise I've ever
kept."

Breck didn't answer in words — didn't have the words readily available yet to answer with — but he
nodded, once, and the nod carried the specific weight of someone who had lost far more than a broken
promise and understood, better than almost anyone in the column, the difference between a mistake and
a pattern.

"Thank you." Bane turned the marker over once in his hands, no words carved into it, none needed.
"I don't think anyone else would have known to say this without saying it."

Breck stayed a while longer, still silent, his presence itself doing the rest of the work his voice
couldn't yet do — and left before either of them needed to turn the moment into anything larger than
it already was. The marker went into the same place Garren Hask kept the things that weren't meant
for the ledger, plain and uncounted, exactly where Breck had meant it to go.
