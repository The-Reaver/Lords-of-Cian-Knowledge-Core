# The Diplomat Who Came to Negotiate a Surrender

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XXXVII, wave 12.
Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The Trust diplomat arrived under a parley flag, alone, unarmed, requesting not a battle but a formal
conversation — a proposal, she explained once seated across from him, to recognize the entire coastal
stretch the Rolling Foundry Campaign had already rebuilt as a self-governing territorial concession,
outside Trust jurisdiction, in exchange for the campaign's own commitment to halt its advance at the
current line.

"You're offering to hand over ground we've already taken, in exchange for us agreeing to stop taking
more of it," Kanja said, turning the proposal over without dismissing it outright. "That's a fairly
generous read of who's negotiating from strength here."

"It's a practical one. Garrison command has run the numbers on continuing to contest this coastline
and doesn't like what they show." She said it plainly, without the posturing he'd expected from a
Trust envoy. "You keep everything you've rebuilt, formally, permanently. We stop bleeding resources
trying to retake it. Nobody else has to die proving a point both sides already understand."

He didn't answer immediately, weighing what the offer would actually cost against what it would
actually secure — a hundred and twenty thousand people's rebuilt homes, made permanent rather than
provisional, against a rebellion that still had further coastline left to reach.

"I'll take the recognition for what's already been rebuilt. I won't take the line as a stopping
point." He was direct about it, matching her own plainness. "The districts already reclaimed get
whatever protection your government's willing to put in writing. The ones ahead of us don't get
written off in exchange for it. I'm not selling anyone's future to secure someone else's past."

She hadn't expected the counter, and took the better part of an hour weighing it before finally
conceding the point had a logic her own superiors would likely resent but couldn't easily refute.

"That's a harder bargain than the one I brought."

"It's the only bargain I'm actually willing to sign." Kanja extended nothing more than his own word,
no ceremony attached to it. "You can take back a partial agreement or no agreement at all. I'm not
short on rebuilding to do either way."

The recognition, narrower than the diplomat's original offer but real, was ratified within the month
— the campaign's already-reclaimed coastline formally secured, its advance continuing unimpeded beyond
it.
