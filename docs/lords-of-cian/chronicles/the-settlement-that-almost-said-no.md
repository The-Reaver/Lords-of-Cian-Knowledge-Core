# The Settlement That Almost Said No

*Locked canon, Batch 261, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1294`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LXXV, wave 25, closing the wave. A settlement council debates banning the doctrine after the rogue former student's scandal. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

Word of the extortion reached the settlement that had once sent twelve volunteers to be trained before Kanja's own account of confronting it did, and by the time he arrived to explain himself in person, the council had already drafted a motion to bar the doctrine from being taught within their walls ever again.

"One of ours did this," the council elder said, not as accusation exactly, more as a wound still being assessed for depth. "Trained here, certified here, sent home with our own trust behind him. We're asking ourselves now whether we ever had any business teaching this at all."

"You have every right to ask that," Kanja said. "I've been asking myself the same question since I found him."

"Then convince us of the answer, if you have one, because right now the motion has enough support to pass."

He didn't argue that the man had been an aberration, didn't minimize what had happened the way the doctrine itself had always refused to minimize a genuine failure. "Nine of your twelve passed that trial. One of the nine turned what he learned into something ugly. That's a real cost, and I'm not going to stand here and tell you it isn't. But eight others are still out there doing exactly what the training was meant to produce — reading honestly, teaching honestly, refusing every temptation that's come their way since. The doctrine didn't fail here. A man who understood it completely chose to betray it anyway, and no teaching on earth fully closes that door for every single person who ever walks through it."

"That's a fine distinction to make from where you're standing. It's harder to make from where the dockmaster he threatened was standing."

"I know it is. I went to that dockmaster myself before I came here. I didn't ask him to forgive the doctrine for what was done to him — I asked whether he'd rather live somewhere the method was never taught at all, or somewhere it was taught honestly and, once, went wrong in one man's hands out of nine. He didn't have a clean answer for me either. I don't think there is one."

The council's vote split closer than the elder had predicted going in — not a clean victory for either side, but enough to defeat the ban by a narrow enough margin that everyone in the room understood it as a warning rather than an endorsement.

"You've bought yourself continued trust," the elder said afterward. "Not forgiveness. Not certainty. Just enough doubt in the other direction to keep the door open a little longer."

"That's more than I came here expecting," Kanja said. "I've built this whole doctrine on the idea that an honest account outlasts whoever tries to bury it. I'd rather earn that trust back slowly, one honest account at a time, than have you hand it to me today because I made a good argument. The next one of your nine who proves the training took will do more to answer this than anything I could say standing here."
