# The Isle That Stopped Needing Him

*Locked canon, Batch 200, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-814`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle XXIX, wave 10 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The island had been a slaving waypoint once, a rocky stop where transport ships took on water and
sold what they'd chained on the mainland to whichever buyer met them there, and he had cleared it
personally, decades past, in an engagement short enough that the crew's own records gave it barely a
line.

He returned only because a chart update suggested the waypoint might have been reoccupied, and found
instead a working fishing settlement of perhaps four hundred people, its harbor busy with ordinary
trade, its market square selling fish and rope and nothing that had ever once been a person.

An old woman running a dockside stall recognized nothing about him — the Forge-Coat stayed folded
below deck, and he walked the harbor in the same plain traveler's coat the settlement's own founders
would have worn — but she was happy enough to talk to a stranger about the island's history when he
asked.

"Slavers had this rock for longer than anyone alive remembers exactly," she said, gutting fish with
the same unhurried rhythm she likely used for every stranger's question. "Then one night, a very long
time ago now, somebody burned the holding pens and broke every lock on the dock, and just — left. No
speech, no flag planted, nothing. My grandmother's grandmother was one of the ones set free that
night. She always said the strangest part wasn't the rescue. It was that nobody ever came back to
check on what they'd done with it."

"What did they do with it?"

"Built this." She gestured at the harbor without much ceremony, a woman describing her own home
rather than a legend. "Took about sixty years to stop looking over our shoulders for the ships to
come back. Longer than that to stop telling the story like it needed telling at all. These days it's
just where we're from."

He bought fish he didn't need and asked no more questions, satisfied in a way he hadn't quite
expected to be — not the satisfaction of a fight won, but the quieter, stranger kind that came from
finding a place that had, entirely on its own, finished the work a single night's rescue had only
started.

He left before anyone connected the plain traveler asking careful questions to anything the old
stories described, and the chart update, when the crew filed it that season, carried a single new
annotation in a hand only Garren Hask would have recognized: *no further attention required — this
one held.*
