# The Burn He Didn't Let Them See

*Locked canon, Batch 219, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-974`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LIV, wave 18. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

It was an ordinary accident, the kind the floor had absorbed a hundred times without anyone
remarking on it — a green billet, quenched too soon by an apprentice still learning to read the
color, throwing a spray of scale that caught Kanja across the forearm before he could pull clear.
He finished the heat he was working before he looked at it.

The senior smith caught the wince he thought he'd hidden well enough, three stations down, and
crossed the floor without being asked. "Let me see it."

"It's nothing that needs the floor to stop for."

"I didn't ask whether the floor needs to stop. I asked to see it." She took his arm before he
answered, turned it to the light, and went quiet in a way that told him more than any word would
have. The burn ran deeper than a spray of scale usually left, a bad angle and a bad moment
compounding into something that wanted real care rather than a wrapped cloth and a return to the
anvil.

"You've been running this floor eleven hours a day for six weeks straight," she said. "You'd have
caught that billet clean on a normal day. You didn't catch it clean today. That's not the billet's
fault."

"The apprentices are watching."

"The apprentices are always watching. That's rather the point of you being here at all, isn't it —
what they learn from watching." She didn't let go of his arm. "What exactly do you imagine they
learn, watching you hide a burn that needs binding because you'd rather they see nothing ever
touches you? I've spent this whole campaign telling them the floor judges the work, not the person.
Man in front of them right now is asking to be judged like he's not a person at all."

He let her bind it, finally, sitting at the edge of the floor rather than at the forge, the
apprentices' work continuing around him under her direction while he sat still for the first time
in longer than he could immediately place. It felt stranger than the burn did.

"This is not a story that needs telling past this floor," he said.

"It's not a story I'm interested in telling. It's a lesson I'm interested in them learning, and
they're learning it right now, watching you sit still and let someone else do the work of taking
care of you for a quarter of an hour." She finished the binding and sat back. "That's not weakness
they just watched. That's the actual thing worth copying. Took you a burn to teach it, but you got
there."
