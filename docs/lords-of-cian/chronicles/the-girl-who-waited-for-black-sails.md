# The Girl Who Waited for Black Sails

*Locked canon, Batch 121, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-412`). Sovereign Ghost Alias Chronicle VI, closing the
second wave. Rebellion era. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

She was seven the night her family's fishing boat lost its rudder in open water with a Trust
requisition vessel closing to seize the catch as unlicensed cargo, and she remembered, decades
later, more clearly than almost anything else from her childhood, the exact moment black sails
appeared on the horizon and her father stopped shouting instructions and simply went still.

"He wasn't scared anymore," she told her own grandchildren, the story worn smooth by dozens of
retellings over the years. "He'd been terrified for twenty minutes, and the second those sails
showed up, something in him just — settled. I didn't understand it yet. I was seven. I thought maybe
the ghost ship was going to be worse than the Trust boat. I found out different fast."

The Sovereign Ghost's fleet never boarded her family's boat at all. It simply positioned itself
between the drifting vessel and the requisition ship, close enough to be unmistakably recognized,
and held position without a shot fired until the requisition captain reconsidered his evening's
priorities and withdrew rather than test whether an unlicensed fishing catch was worth the
confrontation.

"That's it," she'd say, whenever someone expected a more dramatic ending. "No battle. No boarding. He
just stood there being exactly what everyone already believed he was, and that was enough to end it
without anyone needing to fight. I used to think that was a disappointing story to tell. Took me
years to understand it was actually the whole point — the fear had already done its work by the time
the sails showed up. He didn't need to do anything else."

Her family's boat was towed to safe harbor by two of the fleet's smaller vessels, still without a
single word exchanged between the families and the crew that saved them, and she spent every
storm-threatened night for the rest of her childhood watching the horizon out of habit, half-hoping,
half-expecting, to see black sails again.

"I never did see them a second time," she told her grandchildren, decades after the war had ended and
the ghost fleet had long since passed into pure legend. "Didn't need to. Once was enough to spend the
rest of my life understanding what my father understood in that one second his shoulders finally
dropped — that some fears, once you've actually needed them and had them answered, never quite
finish teaching you what safety is supposed to feel like."
