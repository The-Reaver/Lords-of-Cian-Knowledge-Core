# Two Apprentices, Two Roads

*Locked canon, Batch 202, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-875`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XXX, wave 10 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

They had stood at the same forge station for the better part of a week, two apprentices from the same
settlement, taught the same hinge lesson by the same patient hand, and had walked, afterward, in
almost entirely opposite directions.

One became a smith — a competent, unremarkable one, by her own description, running a small forge in
the settlement she'd grown up in, teaching the reheat's patience to whoever wandered in wanting to
learn it, exactly the way she'd been taught it. The other joined a settlement militia within the year,
trading the hinge lesson for drill formations, eventually rising to captain a defense company that had
fought, more than once, to protect a forge much like the one his old station-mate now ran.

They met again, by chance, a decade later, at a militia muster held in the smith's own settlement.

"You went the opposite way from me," she said, only half teasing, watching him inspect a company's
worth of weapons her own forge had made. "Same week, same teacher, same lesson. I don't know how we
ended up doing such different things with it."

"Not that different, if you think about it." He turned one of her blades over, checking the temper
along its edge with a professional's eye. "You build the things worth protecting. I protect the things
worth building. Neither one works without the other. I think that might've been the actual lesson,
under the one about hinges. Never said it that way, but I don't think he needed to."

She hadn't thought of it that way before, and found, turning it over, that it fit better than any
other explanation she'd come up with in ten years of wondering why the same six days had sent them so
far apart.

"Building and protecting. Feels like it should be one thing, said like that."

"Maybe it always was. We just each picked up half of it." He handed the blade back to her, satisfied
with its edge. "Doesn't feel like two roads anymore, does it. Feels like the same one, walked from two
different ends."
