# The Trial of the Falling Roof

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LIV, wave 18, closing the wave. Rebellion era. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The market roof had come down two days after Kanja had passed through the district on unrelated
business, killing no one but crushing three merchants' stalls beneath it — and by the time he heard of
it, half the district had already decided the Iron Bastard's mere presence nearby was reason enough to
name him the cause.

He came back to answer the accusation in person rather than let it stand unaddressed, which the
market's own convened tribunal hadn't expected of a man they'd been prepared to try in absence.

"I never touched that roof," he said, standing before three district elders and a crowd that hadn't
yet decided whether to believe him. "I'll prove it the only way I know how — by telling you exactly
what actually brought it down, in front of all of you, rather than asking you to simply take my word."

He read the collapsed bracing where it lay, the tribunal and half the crowd watching a technique none
of them had ever seen used as testimony rather than a weapon.

"Here," he said, tracing a joint that had failed along an old repair rather than anywhere near where
his own prior passage through the district had taken him. "This seam was patched badly eleven months
ago, tensioned wrong from the start. It's been failing slowly since before I ever set foot in this
district this season. What brought it down wasn't anything I did. It was a repair that was never sound
to begin with, finally giving out on its own schedule."

One of the elders pressed him on it — asked whether a man capable of collapsing a bridge from a
distance couldn't just as easily have caused this from one, unseen, days removed.

"I could show you every reading I've ever taken this season, and you'd find none of them anywhere near
this roof," Kanja said. "But I understand why watching a man read a structure's death like a physician
reading a wound doesn't automatically make you trust the diagnosis over the diagnostician. All I can
offer is the same honesty I'd give the structure itself — and let the seam speak for what actually
happened, since it has no reason to lie for me."

The tribunal ruled in his favor by the end of the day, the original repair crew identified and held to
account instead. Kanja left before any thanks could be organized, uneasy in a way the actual danger of
the accusation hadn't quite accounted for.

"I've defended the doctrine to councils before," he told Danne Sok on the road out. "I hadn't yet had
to defend myself with it. It answered for me cleanly enough. I don't think I want to need it to again."
