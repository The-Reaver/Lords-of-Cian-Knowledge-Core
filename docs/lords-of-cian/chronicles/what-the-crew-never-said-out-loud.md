# What the Crew Never Said Out Loud

*Locked canon, Batch 260, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1267`). Crow King Alias Chronicle LXXV, wave 25, closing
it. Callum Breck reflects on witnessing Kanja's own vulnerability across the hunter's trap and the
blast, and the trust it built rather than eroded. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

Callum Breck hadn't been on either operation -- not the near-miss with the false signal built to fool
Kanja himself, not the bridge where the blast took his hearing for two days -- but he heard both
accounts within the same week, told by people who had been there, and found himself turning the same
thought over for longer than he expected to.

He knew what it was to lose his own voice. Four months of it, once, after the Black Trench took
Nev Torr and left him with nothing worth saying. He'd rebuilt it slow and functional, word by word,
and it had never once occurred to him that Kanja -- Captain, the man the whole crew had built half
their trust around -- could be made just as silent, just as deaf, just as capable of nearly
believing a lie built specifically for him.

"Some of the newer hands are unsettled by it," he told Garren Hask, turning the account over the way
he still turned over hard news before he spoke it aloud to anyone else. "They want to believe he
can't be fooled. Can't be hurt. It's easier to follow a man like that."

"And you?"

"I think it's the opposite. I think I trust him more, not less, knowing the trap almost worked and
the crew caught it instead of him. That's not a weaker Captain. That's a crew doing exactly what
he's spent years building us to do." He thought of his own four silent months, the crew that had
carried him through them without ever once demanding he speak before he was ready. "He built
something that doesn't need him to be unbreakable. I don't think most men in his position would want
that to be true. I think he does."

Neither of them said it as praise, exactly, or as reassurance for the newer hands who still wanted
their Captain untouchable. It was just something true, passed quietly between two men who'd both, in
their own ways, needed the rest of the crew to carry something they couldn't carry alone -- and
found, each time, that it held.
