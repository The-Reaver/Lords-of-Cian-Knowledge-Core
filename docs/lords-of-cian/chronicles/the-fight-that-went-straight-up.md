# The Fight That Went Straight Up

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Bane Alias Chronicle XXXVIII, second entry in the
thirteenth wave. Rebellion era, age 19-20, roughly a year after the Black Trench. Not a territory
Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The siege towers at Marrenfeld Keep climbed sixty feet of sheer curtain wall, and the Directorate
garrison holding the battlements had built their entire defense around the assumption that height
itself would do most of the work an ordinary fight left to skill — that anyone forced to fight
upward, off balance, without solid footing beneath them, would lose to gravity long before they lost
to a blade.

Bane went up the lead tower himself, Onyx of Oblivion drawn before the platform ever reached the
wall, and the engagement that followed ran unlike anything the column had fought on open ground.

There was no room on the battlement's narrow walkway for Mafesto's full kinetic-transfer footwork,
no clean ground to root a grounded strike through — every exchange happened on a walkway three feet
wide with a sixty-foot drop on one side and a wall of spears on the other, momentum and balance
mattering more than raw strength, a fight decided by who could stay upright on a surface built to
throw an attacker off it. Onyx of Oblivion's Whisper of Shadows let him read a spear thrust's true
line a half-second before it committed, the margin he needed on ground that offered no margin of its
own; Veil Piercer found the gap in a shield wall too tightly packed for the wall's own defenders to
properly turn and face him. He cleared the first thirty feet of battlement in the time it took the
second tower to reach the wall at all, fighting a fight built entirely around not falling as much as
around not being struck.

"You fought that whole stretch like the ground wasn't there," one of the crew who'd followed up the
second tower said afterward, still catching his breath from the climb. "None of your usual footwork
holds on a walkway that width."

"It doesn't. I had to fight without most of what usually does the work for me." Bane checked his
own footing on the now-cleared battlement, sixty feet of drop still close enough to feel. "Height
changes everything about a fight that ground makes easy to ignore. I'd rather learn that on a wall we
won than find it out for the first time on one we didn't."

Marrenfeld Keep fell before the second tower's crew had finished the climb, the garrison broken
along a walkway that had been built, deliberately, to make height into an advantage no attacker
could overcome — and hadn't accounted for an attacker who simply refused to let height decide the
fight for him.
