# The Magistrate Who Spent His Name

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XXV, wave 8.
Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The district magistrate had been collecting an extra tithe for four months by the time word of it
reached the campaign's own route — a modest surcharge on every household, levied, he told them,
"toward the Lord of Embers' rebuilding fund," with no fund anyone could point to and no rebuilding
that had ever actually followed the collection.

Kanja arrived at the district's central square unannounced, the magistrate's own collectors still
mid-round with their ledgers, and did not raise his voice once through the entire confrontation that
followed.

"You've been spending a name I never gave you permission to spend," he said, in front of the same
crowd the magistrate had been collecting from. "I'd like to know where four months of that tithe
actually went, since it plainly never came anywhere near a forge."

"I assumed the campaign would want the district's support formalized," the magistrate said, already
calculating how much of the truth the crowd's presence would force out of him. "A structured
contribution, rather than relying on—"

"Rather than relying on people giving because they wanted to, instead of because you told them my name
required it of them." Kanja let the square's own silence do most of the remaining work. "I don't take
tithes. I've never taken a tithe anywhere on this entire campaign. Every rebuild this district's ever
seen from us, we paid for out of what we already had, not out of what you collected in my name without
asking me."

The magistrate had no accounting that survived the crowd's own scrutiny once it was invited to look.
The four months' collection, what remained of it, was returned household by household before the
campaign moved on — not by Kanja's own hand, but by the district's residents themselves, once they
understood exactly whose authority had actually been standing behind the collector at their door.

"You could have just taken his head for it," one onlooker said afterward, still processing how quietly
the whole reckoning had happened. "Would've been faster."

"Would've made the lesson about me instead of about what he did." Kanja watched the last household
receive its return. "I don't need every wrong done in my name answered with a bigger wrong done in my
own. The name only means something if I'm careful who gets to spend it, and how."
