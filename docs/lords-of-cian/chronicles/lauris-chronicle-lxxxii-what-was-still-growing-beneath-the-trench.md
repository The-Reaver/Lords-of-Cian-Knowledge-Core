# Lauris Chronicle LXXXII: What Was Still Growing Beneath the Trench

*Locked canon, Batch 300, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1699`). Eighty-second entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, a Strand D entry giving full-scene treatment to Operation 27 (previously only
summarized at `MCD-185`, with tactical precedent already dramatized at `MCD-1545` for Operations 11
and 21), continuing the Specialist sub-period. Dramatizes the location and destruction of a
Tide-Wraith breeding substrate in the Voskharen Trench -- roughly 14 adults, ~400 juveniles, and the
generative substrate itself -- and the revelation that the species was engineered rather than
naturally evolved, an Ionic-Rite-derived production line believed, at the time, biologically
extinguished on Cian. Deliberately leaves that belief exactly as uncertain as `MCD-185` itself leaves
it, without asserting or foreshadowing Operation 39's later disproof (reserved, outside this entry's
scope). No new named characters. Zero collisions.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Specialist designation LL-001-S-027: "Two of these, alone, taught me how to kill
one. Neither taught me there were four hundred more, still becoming, in a chamber the first two had
been bred specifically to guard the entrance to."*

Operation 27 found her returning to water she already knew. The Voskharen Trench had given her two
Tide-Wraiths before this contract ever reached her — a juvenile at Operation 11, an adult eighty
months later at Operation 21 — and the tactical grammar she had built against both, walking the
trench floor beneath a hardened Directorate vessel deployed as bait, severing the jaw at its first
tooth-ring during the creature's closing strike before pursuing it down its own spine to end the
engagement at the motor ganglion, had by Operation 27 become something closer to a settled discipline
than an improvised response.

What brought her back was not, this time, a single hostile sighting. Directorate coastal survey had
noted, across the preceding eighteen months, three further Tide-Wraith encounters in the same general
stretch of trench, none matching the size or maturity of either creature she had already terminated —
juveniles, by every report, and more of them than two isolated engagements ought reasonably to have
produced. Lauris has told me that the pattern struck her, reading the survey reports before departure,
in a way it evidently had not struck the Directorate's own analysts: two Tide-Wraiths, killed eighty
months apart, does not explain a rising population of juveniles unless something continues to produce
them.

She spent eleven days locating the source rather than hunting individual sightings, working the
trench's deeper contours with the same underwater-walking method her earlier engagements had already
proven, until she found it: a cavern system roughly four hundred meters below the trench's working
depth, its entrance guarded by fourteen fully grown adults arranged in a pattern too deliberate to
read as ordinary predator behavior, and beyond them, in a flooded gallery she has described to me as
unlike anything her prior two encounters had prepared her for, a generative substrate — pulsing,
organic, unmistakably artificial in its regularity — surrounded by roughly four hundred juveniles at
every visible stage of development, from newly hatched to nearly mature.

*

The fourteen adults she engaged first, over the better part of two days, applying the same
jaw-severing, spine-pursuit technique that had ended their two predecessors, scaled now across
fourteen simultaneous, coordinated opponents rather than one isolated engagement at a time — the
hardest sustained combat, by her own later assessment, that any single species had yet asked of her
career. She lost no engagements and sustained, by her own accounting, nothing worse than bruising and
a torn shoulder ligament that healed within the week. The four hundred juveniles she cleared in
considerably less time, most too undeveloped to offer real resistance, a task her archive records
with the same clinical brevity she gives most necessary but joyless work.

What occupied her afterward, once the chamber was cleared and the generative substrate itself stood
exposed before her, was the confirmation she had suspected since counting the survey's rising juvenile
sightings on the ride out: the substrate was not a natural breeding ground. Its structure, once she
examined it directly, matched the same Ionic-Rite-derived architecture she had by then catalogued at
a dozen unrelated sites — not a species that had evolved to thrive in the Voskharen Trench, but a
production line, built and seeded there deliberately, generating Tide-Wraiths the way a resonance node
at Settlement K-447 had once generated hostile subjects from murdered bodies. The two isolated
creatures that had taught her the tactics were never, she realized, isolated at all. They had been
outriders of a nursery she had simply not yet found.

She destroyed the substrate itself outright, the same departure from mere clearance she had begun
practicing since Velaris, judging correctly that a generative architecture left standing would
eventually produce a fourth encounter no survey report would predict in time. Her report to the
Directorate described the site's neutralization accurately as to method and outcome, and named the
species, as her contract required, biologically extinguished on Cian as of that clearance — a
conclusion she recorded honestly, by every measure available to her that day, and one her own archive
never claims to have been certain of rather than merely, at the time, unable to disprove.

Her closing note for Operation 27: *I killed the two that taught me how before I ever knew they were
guarding something. I have learned, by now, to ask what a lone hostile is standing in front of before
I congratulate myself on having ended it. This is the fourth time that question has proven worth
asking. I doubt it will be the last.*
