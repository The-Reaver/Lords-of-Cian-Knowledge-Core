# The Charge She Measured Twice

*Locked canon, Batch 246, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1076`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LXIII, wave 21,
closing the wave. A quieter spotlight entry centering Pell Ostra, the crew's demolitions and
chemistry specialist, for the first time in the Scourge sub-series. Not a territory Chronicle.
Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The warehouse shared its landward wall with a nursery — not a euphemism, an actual nursery, thirty-odd
children of dockworkers minded there through the day while their parents worked the very piers the
slaving ring operated behind. Whoever had built against that wall a generation ago had no way of
knowing what would eventually be stored on the other side of it. That didn't change what a breach
charge sized for the warehouse's reinforced stone would do to a wall never built to survive one.

Pell Ostra had been given four hours. She used most of them lying on her back under the warehouse's
foundation line with a hand-lamp and a listening rod, mapping the wall's actual thickness rather than
trusting the survey someone else had drawn from outside it — old mortar reads differently than new,
and this wall, she found, had been repaired unevenly across its life, thicker in places the original
plans didn't show.

She talked to the charge the whole time she built it, the way she always did, the way the crew had
long since stopped finding strange. Not commands. Requests. *I need you to open here and stop there.
I'm not asking you to be gentle, I'm asking you to be exact.* She split it into two linked charges
instead of one — a smaller lead charge to crack the mortar seam she'd found, a larger one timed a
half-second behind it to follow the crack rather than force a new one — because a single charge sized
to guarantee the breach would have guaranteed the crack ran the other way too, toward thirty sleeping
children instead of away from them.

When the moment came, it worked exactly the way she'd built it to. The warehouse wall opened along the
seam she'd mapped, clean, fast, in the half-second window the raid needed. The nursery wall, four feet
away, didn't so much as crack its plaster.

Afterward, on the walk back to the ship, Kanja asked her the question he'd never quite found the
occasion to ask in sixteen decades of working beside her. "You could have used less and been safer.
Smaller charge, slower breach, less risk to the wall."

"Slower breach means longer for someone inside to reach for a hostage," she said. "I wasn't choosing
between safe and dangerous. I was choosing exactly how much force, in exactly what shape, and trusting
myself to have measured it right the first time so I didn't need a second guess partway through."

He thought about that longer than the answer seemed to require. "That's most of what I do too," he
said eventually. "Just louder."

She didn't laugh, exactly, but something in her face allowed the joke to have landed. Later that
season, a young apprentice — new to the crew, still learning to read old mortar from new by ear
alone — watched her measure a charge twice before setting it once, and asked why. "Because the wall
only tells you the truth if you ask it twice," Pell Ostra said, "and I only get to be wrong about that
once."
