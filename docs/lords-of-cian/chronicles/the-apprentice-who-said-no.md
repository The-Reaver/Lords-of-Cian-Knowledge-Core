# The Apprentice Who Said No

*Locked canon, Batch 257, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1195`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle LXXXIV, wave 28, closing the wave. The orphaned boy from "The Boy Who Wanted the Blade Instead" (MCD-653), now a master, corrects Kanja's own proposed shoring method -- the taught lineage comes full circle.*

---

The journeyman from the supply caravan two years back had settled into the crew properly now, his own reputation for careful, unhurried work spreading past the orphan he'd once been teaching into something closer to a name of his own among the diggers who worked beside him. So when Kanja proposed a shoring method for a difficult junction and the journeyman said, flatly, that it was wrong, half the gallery went quiet waiting to see what happened next.

"It'll hold," Kanja said, not defensive, just certain, the way he'd been certain about a hundred similar calls before.

"It'll hold for now." The journeyman didn't back down, spreading his own sketch beside Kanja's. "You're solving for the load it's carrying today. I've watched this exact junction three times since I started here. The load shifts in about four months, once the gallery past it opens fully. Your method doesn't account for that. Mine does."

It wasn't insubordination and it wasn't the boy Kanja remembered redirecting away from vengeance all those years ago. It was a tradesman with his own accumulated field experience, watching a pattern repeat across years Kanja himself hadn't been present for every stage of, arriving at a correction built from evidence Kanja simply didn't have.

He studied the journeyman's sketch a long moment, checking it against what he knew and finding, plainly, that it was better.

"You're right." He said it without hesitation, the same way he'd once told a guild master his own reheat had been rushed. "Not just close. Actually right. Your method goes in instead of mine."

The gallery, which had been braced for a confrontation, watched something quieter happen instead -- the man who'd taught this exact trade to an orphaned, furious child years ago accepting correction from the tradesman that child had become, without a trace of wounded pride in it.

"You taught me the work was the thing that mattered," the journeyman said afterward, still working through something that looked like disbelief at how easily it had gone. "Guess I didn't expect you to actually mean it applied to you too, once I was good enough to prove you wrong."

"That was always the whole point," Kanja said. "I didn't teach you the trade so you'd agree with me forever. I taught it to you so you'd eventually know it better than I do."
