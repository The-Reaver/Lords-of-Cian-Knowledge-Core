# What the Crossfire Left Behind

*Locked canon, Batch 254, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1096`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXVI, wave 22, closing wave
22. A liberation raid succeeds by every operational metric except one -- a stray discharge kills a
child no one knew was hiding in the building. Genuine, unresolved civilian-cost grief register.*

---

By every count that mattered to the operation, the raid was clean. The garrison fell in nine minutes.
Every captive walked out under their own power. Not one Rebellion fighter took a wound worth
reporting.

The supply shed at the garrison's east wall wasn't on any manifest Bane's people had for the site. A
nine-year-old boy had been hiding in it for two days, since before the raid was ever planned, and a
single deflected round from the fighting outside found its way through the shed's thin wall in the
first minute of the assault.

His mother found Bane an hour later, once the garrison was secured and the captives were being
counted out into the open. She didn't ask who fired the shot. She'd already been told no one could
say for certain, that it had come from the general exchange and not from any one identifiable weapon,
and she didn't want that answer repeated to her again.

"You're going to tell me it was clean," she said. "Everyone keeps telling me it was clean."

"It was clean by every measure except the one that matters to you. I won't pretend those are the same
thing." Bane didn't sit, didn't reach for her, didn't offer the shape of comfort that would have let
either of them look away from it faster. "I didn't know he was there. That's true and it isn't an
answer to anything you're owed."

Corren Halst moved to say something about the cost of war, the arithmetic of nine minutes against a
garrison that would have taken far longer and cost far more if fought any other way, and Bane cut him
off with a look before a word of it reached her.

"Don't," he said, quiet enough that it wasn't for her at all. "Not on my behalf. It's mine to carry,
not something to be explained away for me."

He didn't move the column that day, or the next. He stayed for the burial, stood at the back of it
without being asked and without asking to be included, and left only once it was done — carrying
nothing that looked, from the outside, any different than it had before.
