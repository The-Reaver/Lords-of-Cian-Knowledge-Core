# What the Fog Remembers

*Draft only — not yet locked, pending Abad's approval. Bane Alias Chronicle IV, first entry in
Bane's second three-Chronicle wave. Rebellion era, within the "Bane" window, a new engagement in
terrain deliberately echoing the Black Trench's own chemical fog and confined ground (`MCD-232`).
A detailed showcase of the full Trinity -- Mafesto, Obsidian Malice, Onyx -- in the same kind of
environment that first proved them as a combined system. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone
material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The gorge the second Suppression detachment chose was not the Black Trench, but its engineers had
studied the Black Trench closely enough to try to reproduce its exact conditions on purpose —
narrow walls, a single sealed approach, and canisters of the same chemical fog compound that had
once been meant to disorient a lone man in a ravine he'd sealed himself into. Their theory, argued
up three levels of Directorate command before it was approved, held that Bane's Black Trench victory
had depended on conditions he chose. Force the same conditions on him without the choice, and the
advantage would reverse.

The fog rolled in on schedule. Mafesto came online before it reached him, the Kinetic Transfer
System already primed against blind, close-quarters contact — not sight-dependent, reading impact
and pressure through the exoskeleton's own plating rather than a soldier's eyes, which meant the fog
that blinded the detachment's own forward line left him unaffected in the one sense that actually
mattered. The first three strikes that found him in the white haze were absorbed and converted
before their owners had finished committing to the swing.

Obsidian Malice discharged twice in quick succession once the stored charge peaked — not blind
strikes but precisely placed ones, each landing exactly where a moment before an attacker's own
labored breathing had given away a position the fog should have hidden. Onyx moved through the haze
between them, Whisper of Shadows doing in this fog exactly what it had once done in an ordinary
night's darkness, dissolving the detachment's confidence that concealment cut both ways. Cadence
Ruin found and collapsed two more coordinated strikes before they landed, the same rhythm-reading
gift that had unmade sixteen Compliance soldiers years earlier now unmaking a detachment that had
rehearsed this exact ambush for six weeks.

The detachment broke within eleven minutes, less than an eighth of the Black Trench's original nine
hours, because this time there had been no need to seal anyone in — the fog that was meant to be a
weapon turned, the way it always eventually did against him, into simply more information he could
use and they couldn't.

"You copied the wrong half of it," the surviving detachment commander said afterward, disarmed and
kneeling in fog that was already beginning to clear. "You copied the fog. You copied the walls. You
never once asked why any of it worked for him and not for anyone else who's ever tried to use
confined ground and bad visibility as a weapon. It was never the conditions. It's that he doesn't
need his eyes to know where you are, and everyone you sent in here still did."
