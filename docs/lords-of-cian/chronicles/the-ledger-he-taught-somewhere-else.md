# The Ledger He Taught Somewhere Else

*Locked canon, Batch 277, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1435`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XCVI, wave 32,
closing the wave. Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. A successful independent replication of the tally
method, deliberately contrasting the failed imitation at `MCD-947`.*

---

She had been one of the first fourteen names on Garren Hask's own attendance sheet the season the
tally-verification classes formalized at Warehouse Twelve -- a rope-splicer from the second canal
row who asked more questions in her first week than anyone else asked in a month, not because she
doubted the method but because she meant to actually understand it rather than merely copy its
motions.

Eighteen months later she came to Kanja with a folded letter rather than a question: a cousin's
husband ran a granary two districts north, in a town the tally method had never reached and never
would through Kanja's own hands, since the districts he already held claimed every hour he had to
give them. She wanted to go, to build the same cross-witnessed count from nothing in a place that
had never heard the name Trench Monarch and didn't need to.

"You understand it well enough that it doesn't need my name attached to work," Kanja told her,
which was as close to a blessing as he offered anyone. "Take Hask's method, not mine. He built the
verification discipline; I only insisted it existed."

She left with a copy of the attendance ledger's own training notes, no crew, no escort, and no
promise that it would work the way it had worked under his own name in his own five districts. The
letter that reached Warehouse Twelve four months later reported a granary tally corrected without
a single confrontation, the discipline she'd carried north taking root on its own terms rather than
as an imported curiosity -- proof, Hask noted in his own hand at the bottom of the page, that the
method had finally outgrown needing either of them to hold it up.

Where the distant imitation the year before had ruined an honest owner through the method's form
without its rigor, this one succeeded precisely because she'd taken the rigor and left the form
behind entirely -- no chalked name, no reputation to invoke, only the discipline itself, carried
somewhere it had never needed to be defended.
