# The Wedding He Was Asked to Witness

*Locked canon, Batch 212, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-919`). Captain Alias Chronicle XLVII, wave 16. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Two of the crew — a rigger who'd signed on during the second year and a cook's mate who'd joined
after Iron Shallows — asked him to stand as witness at their wedding, which was, by the crew's own
loose custom, the person who signed the union ledger and said the words that made it official under
crew law rather than any Trust magistrate's. Nobody had ever asked him before. He said yes before he
'd finished hearing the whole request, which made both of them laugh.

"You don't even know what day yet," the rigger said.

"Doesn't matter. Tell me where to stand."

The wedding itself happened on the aft deck at low tide, strung with borrowed lanterns and more
flowers than the ship had any business carrying, and for one full evening nobody aboard treated him
as anything but the person holding the ledger and, later, dancing badly with Efa Gol while Pell
Ostra kept time on an overturned crate because the ship carried no proper drum. Callum Breck, three
cups into something Garren Hask had been saving for an occasion exactly like this one, stood up
uninvited to deliver a toast that ran twice as long as anyone expected and made the bride cry twice.

When it came time for the actual ceremony, Kanja read the union words plainly, the way the crew's
custom asked for — no embellishment, no title invoked, nothing that made it about him at all — and
then added one line that wasn't in the custom, because he meant it and thought it belonged there
anyway.

"I've watched what this crew builds out of nothing more than showing up for each other, over and
over, on the worst days it's ever had. Whatever the two of you build from here, you're building it
in good company. That's not nothing."

He signed the ledger last, under both their names, in the space the custom left for a witness rather
than an officiant — a small, deliberate distinction the crew had settled on years back, so that no
wedding aboard would ever read, even in the smallest formal sense, as belonging to him rather than to
the two people actually marrying.

Later, well past the point where any of it needed his presence to keep going, he sat at the edge of
the deck with his boots off, watching the dancing continue without him, and found he had nothing he
needed to be doing instead. Efa Gol dropped down beside him, breathless from the dancing, and didn't
say anything for a while.

"You look like a man who doesn't know what to do with an evening that isn't a crisis," she said
eventually.

"I'm working on it."

"Good," she said. "Keep working on it. We could all stand a few more evenings like this one."
