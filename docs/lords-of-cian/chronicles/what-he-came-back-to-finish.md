# What He Came Back to Finish

*Locked canon, Batch 262, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1329`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LXXXIII, wave
28. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241), directly following "The Weeks
the Method Stood Alone" (MCD-1328). A detailed, battle-intense Trinity combat showcase fought under
real, still-healing physical limitation. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose.*

---

He wasn't fully healed when the message came — a coordinated Directorate strike massing against
three settlements the campaign had just left, timed, the intelligence suggested, specifically for
a window when word of his own injury had reached ears it shouldn't have. Someone had decided the
Lord of Embers compromised was an opportunity worth moving on quickly, before the healing finished.

"You're not at full strength," the senior smith's successor said, watching him test his own ribs
against a training strike and fail to fully hide the wince it cost him. "Whoever's coming knows
that. It's why they're coming now."

"Then I fight like a man who isn't at full strength," Kanja said. "That's not the same as fighting
like a man who's lost."

He went in differently than any prior showcase — Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System doing more of
the defensive work than usual, grounding blows he'd have simply absorbed and answered at full
health, buying his own healing ribs distance rather than risking a direct exchange his body wasn't
ready to sustain. Onyx of Oblivion carried the fight's actual weight in his place: Whisper of
Shadows closing gaps he'd have closed on raw speed before, Veil Piercer finding openings efficiently
enough that a diminished, careful Kanja never needed to press an advantage his own body couldn't
fully back. Obsidian Malice's discharge cycle ran at a controlled, conservative intensity throughout,
every pulse placed rather than spent freely, a fighter managing a resource he knew wasn't fully
restocked yet.

It was, by his own later admission, the most tactically careful fight of the entire tour — no
flourish, no unnecessary risk, every exchange calculated to end things a beat sooner than a fully
healthy engagement would have needed to, because a beat longer was a beat his ribs genuinely
couldn't afford. The three settlements held. The Directorate force, expecting a compromised target
to be an easy one, found instead a fighter who'd simply traded raw capability for a discipline his
full health had never forced him to develop before.

"You fought smarter hurt than most people fight healthy," the successor said afterward, checking
his ribs with real concern rather than professional distance.

"I didn't have a choice," Kanja said. "Hurt takes the reckless option off the table. Turns out
that's not always a loss."
