# The School Sephtis Never Meant to Start

*Locked canon, Batch 220, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-978`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XLIX, wave 17. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

It started, as the successor liked to point out, entirely by accident. Sephtis had never intended to
teach more than one person. Then a second young signalman had watched her working the relay charts
long enough to start asking real questions instead of idle ones, and Sephtis, too old by then to
pretend he minded the company, had answered them.

By the time anyone thought to call it a school, it already had four students crowded onto the same
stretch of deck rail Sephtis had once used to teach one.

"You never planned this," the successor said, watching him correct a boy's reading of a cloud bank
with the same patience he'd once spent on her alone.

"I planned to teach you," Sephtis said. "The rest is your doing more than mine. They came because
they saw what you built out of it, not because I went looking for more hands to fill."

The four students were not equal in gift, and Sephtis made no pretense that they were. One read wind
faster than anyone he'd trained. Another was slow, careful, and rarely wrong once she committed. A
third had a sailor's instinct but no patience for the mathematics underneath it, and struggled visibly
with every lesson that asked him to explain why he was right rather than simply be right. The fourth
was the successor's own choice, brought in over Sephtis's mild objection that she was too young — a
decision the successor made unilaterally, the first real exercise of authority in the craft that
wasn't simply following where Sephtis pointed.

"You're building something bigger than either of us taught for," Sephtis told her, one evening, watching
the four of them argue over a reading none of them fully agreed on. "I taught one person because one
person was what I had room in me to teach. This is different. This is going to outlive both of us
whether we plan for that or not."

"Is that a warning?"

"It's an observation," Sephtis said. "Warnings are for things that might not happen. This already is
happening. The only choice left is whether it happens carefully."

She looked at the four young readers, none of them her yet, all of them already something the fleet
would come to rely on in ways neither she nor Sephtis could fully predict.

"Then we do it carefully," she said.
