# The Smith Who Built Their Weapons

*Locked canon, Batch 189, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-553`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XVII.
Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone
material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The Directorate smith captured during the fifth site's defense had spent years forging the same
siege engines and armor plating that had killed rebel fighters throughout the campaign — a skilled
craftsman, not a soldier, who'd built weapons for whichever side paid him without much thought for
what they were used for. His own crew expected Kanja to punish him accordingly.

He offered the man work instead.

"You built the engines that broke through Site Two's own defenses," the campaign's senior smith said,
watching the offer with open disbelief. "Men died to what his hands made. Most of this crew would
understand if you punished him for that."

"I understand the anger. I don't think punishing him actually metabolizes what happened the way this
campaign has metabolized everything else thrown against it." Kanja studied the captured smith, weighing
the decision carefully rather than reflexively. "He built weapons because that's the work he was
given and paid for, not out of any particular loyalty to what they were used for. I'd rather turn that
same skill toward this campaign than destroy it out of anger at what it was pointed at before."

The smith, given the choice rather than forced into it, accepted — genuinely uncertain whether to
trust an offer that ran so contrary to what he'd expected from a captured enemy's fate, and genuinely
unwilling to test whether the alternative might be worse.

"Why would you trust the same hands that built what killed your own people?"

"Because skill isn't loyalty, and I'd rather metabolize your skill into something this campaign
actually needs than waste it proving a point about punishment that helps nobody who's already dead."
Kanja set him to work alongside the campaign's own smiths, watched rather than restrained. "You'll
earn trust the same way anyone here does — through the work itself, not through anything I decide
about you today. That's the only kind of trust that's ever actually meant anything in this forge."

The smith worked the remainder of the campaign without incident, his own considerable skill turned
toward defenses rather than the siege engines he'd once built — a conversion the campaign's own
skeptics watched closely and, over months, came to accept as exactly the kind of transformation the
Lord of Embers had always claimed the alias was actually for.
