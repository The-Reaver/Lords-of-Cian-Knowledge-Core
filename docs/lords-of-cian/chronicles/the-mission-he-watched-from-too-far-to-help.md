# The Mission He Watched From Too Far to Help

*Locked canon, Batch 261, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1289`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LXX, wave 24, first entry. The third-generation apprentice's first solo mission, with Kanja too distant to intervene. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

Word reached him three days after it was already decided: the third generation — the second student's own apprentice, old enough now that the word "child" no longer fit her any better than it had once stopped fitting the second student herself — had taken a solo commission alone, a granary roof in a district two weeks' travel from wherever Kanja happened to be standing at the time.

He hadn't sent her. He hadn't known until it was already finished.

"She didn't ask permission," the second student said, when he finally caught up to the district weeks later, something between pride and residual alarm still visible in how she told it. "She heard the settlement's request before it ever reached me, and she went. I only found out when they sent word of thanks addressed to both of us."

"You weren't there."

"I was three districts away. She knew that. She went anyway." The second student's voice carried an old echo Kanja recognized from somewhere further back than either of them — his own voice, once, watching her work a supply-line gate from a distance close enough to intervene, far enough not to be a presence she could lean on. "I taught her everything I know to teach. I didn't teach her to wait for me before using it. I'm not sure that was ever something I could have taught, even if I'd tried."

The read itself, by every account gathered afterward, had been correct — patient, doubly verified, the discharge controlled enough that the granary's grain stores survived the collapse of its failing roof supports without a single sack lost. No misdiagnosis. No injury. A clean job, exactly the kind Kanja himself might have signed off on if he'd been standing close enough to watch it happen.

He hadn't been. That was the part that sat strangely.

"I used to stand close enough to step in," he told the second student, turning the account over once more. "Close enough that failing wouldn't have cost more than the lesson. She didn't have that. She had two weeks of distance and a settlement that needed the granary fixed regardless of whether either of us thought she was ready."

"Does that frighten you?"

"It should, probably. Mostly it tells me something I hadn't fully let myself believe until just now — the doctrine doesn't actually need me standing close enough to catch it anymore. It needs someone who learned it honestly enough to trust their own read without me there to confirm it." Kanja considered the distance he hadn't been able to close in time to matter. "I spent years being the only proof this could be taught safely. She just proved it can be trusted a full generation away from anyone who could have stopped a mistake in time. That's not a small thing to have happened without me in the room."
