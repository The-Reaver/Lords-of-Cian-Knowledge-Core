# Aztlán Chronicle III: The First Time He Actually Listened

*Locked canon, Batch 172, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-519`). Homage-era Los Angeles, Aztlán/Ollin. Territory
Chronicle — Ollin as protagonist, Kanja an unnamed background guest with no command, credit, or
resolution authorship. Set some years after Iya's already-locked walkout (`MCD-359`). New standalone
material. No new named characters.*

---

A new grievance surfaced within the organization years after Iya's departure — a younger woman
raising nearly the same complaint Iya once had, credit for organizing labor flowing upward to Ollin
while the actual work stayed uncredited in the hands that did it. The old pattern stood ready to repeat
itself exactly as it once had.

This time, Ollin didn't defer it.

"I heard this exact complaint once before, and I let it go six meetings before it cost me people I
never got back," he said, before she'd finished raising it a first time, cutting off his own old
instinct to minimize rather than address. "I'm not doing that again. Tell me plainly what needs to
change, and I'll actually act on it this time instead of nodding along until it's too late."

She hadn't expected the immediate acknowledgment, hesitating before continuing. "The organizing work
this quarter — nearly all of it was done by women in the group, coordinated by us, and every public
account of it still credits you as though you'd run it personally."

"Then the public account is wrong, and I should have caught that before you had to be the one to tell
me." Ollin didn't defend the oversight, didn't ask for time to think it over. "I'll correct it this
week, publicly, and I'd like your input on how the organizing gets credited going forward so this
doesn't quietly slide back to the old pattern once the immediate discomfort passes."

The correction ran within days, credit reassigned publicly and plainly — a small act compared to what
Iya's departure had once cost the organization, but a genuinely different response than the one that
had produced that loss in the first place.

"I heard what happened before I came here," the younger woman admitted afterward, some of her own
guardedness easing. "I wasn't sure you'd actually respond differently this time."

"I wasn't entirely sure either, until I actually did it. Knowing you should change and actually
changing turned out to be two different things, harder than I expected even with the lesson already
paid for once." Ollin watched her rejoin the organizing work, credited properly this time. "'The
Formation' still holds the same way it always has. I don't think that was ever really the problem. I
think the problem was always me, and I'd rather prove that's changed than just say it has."

An unnamed man near the organization's edge, present for the exchange, left without comment once the
correction had been made.
