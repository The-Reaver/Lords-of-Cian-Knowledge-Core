# The Nights Toran Commanded Alone

*Locked canon, Batch 265, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1393`). Bane Alias Chronicle XCIII, wave 31, closing
wave 31. Direct payoff to wave 23's "The Lieutenant Corren Halst Chose" (`MCD-1099`) and wave 21's
"The Weight Maret Vos Chose to Carry" (`MCD-1061`): Toran runs a full operation with neither Corren
Halst nor Bane present at all, the first fully unsupervised test of the crew's trained discipline.*

---

The request came from Toran himself, which was the part Corren Halst kept turning over afterward.
Not a plan he'd been handed to execute. A plan he'd built, for an extraction three days' hard travel
from wherever the main column happened to be standing, and a request that nobody senior to him come
along to watch it run.

"If I can only do it right with you or him standing behind me," Toran said, "then you haven't
actually taught me anything. You've taught me to need you there."

Bane let him go. Corren Halst didn't like it and said so, plainly, the way he said everything he
didn't like — but he didn't override it, and that was its own answer about how much of the training
had actually taken.

Three nights passed with no word, which was expected; the column Toran led carried no fast way to
send one back. Bane spent them the way he spent every stretch he couldn't control the outcome of —
working, mostly, letting the not-knowing sit where it sat instead of chasing it.

Toran's own standing order held the whole three nights without him needing to invoke it by name:
no plan ran past the fourth sleepless stretch without a second set of eyes reviewing it, exactly as
Bane had made it apply to himself first, three waves back, after his own judgment slipped once and
nearly cost thirty harvesters their lives. Toran built that second set of eyes into his own command
the same way — rotated it through two junior scouts he trusted enough to argue with him, and let
one argument on the second night talk him out of a route that would have run the column directly
into a patrol he hadn't accounted for.

He came back on the fourth day with everyone he'd left with, plus the nineteen people the extraction
had been for, and no story that made for a dramatic telling — which was, Bane told him, closer to
the actual measure of success than anything with a better shape to it would have been.

"You made the call on the second night without either of us," Bane said. "That's the part I wanted
to see. Not whether you could follow a plan under pressure. Whether you could throw one out when it
stopped being the right plan, with nobody there to tell you it was safe to."

"I almost sent a runner back for you anyway. Just to be sure."

"What stopped you?"

Toran considered the honest answer instead of the easy one. "I already knew what you'd have said.
I've been in enough rooms with you to know how you'd have weighed it. Sending for you would've just
been asking you to confirm a decision I'd already made."

Bane didn't answer right away. It was, without either of them quite naming it, the same shape of
thing the presence itself was built from — a decision already reached somewhere the other person
wasn't standing, carried back to them only once the result was certain. Watching it live in someone
he'd trained, rather than in himself, was its own kind of answer to a question he hadn't realized he
was still asking.

"That's not a smaller thing than following orders well," he said finally. "It's the whole point of
training anyone in the first place."
