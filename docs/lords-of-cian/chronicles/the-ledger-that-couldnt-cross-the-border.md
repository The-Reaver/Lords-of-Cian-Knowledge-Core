# The Ledger That Couldn't Cross the Border

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle I, wave 11.
Rebellion era (age 19-21), unarmed and non-combat throughout, matching the alias's established
ethos. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The quarrying operation straddled the seam between Sovereign Trust territory and the Shattered
Kingdoms' fractured border holds, and its owner had built his whole labor arrangement around exactly
that seam, paying workers across the line in whichever jurisdiction's wage floor was lower that
season.

The Trust magistrate Ezio brought the finished ledger to read it carefully, checked the operation's
registered address twice, and pushed it back across the table without opening the binding past the
first page.

"This concerns workers employed on the Shattered Kingdoms side of the line for the majority of the
tally period," she said. "That's not a matter for this office."

The nearest Shattered Kingdoms border hold, when a runner carried the same ledger three days
further, gave the opposite answer with equal certainty — the operation's ownership and its
administrative registration both sat on the Trust side, whatever side of an unmarked quarry line the
labor happened to occur on, and so the matter was, by their reading, not theirs either.

"Both offices agree the debt is real," Kanja said, once the second refusal came back. "Neither one
will claim it's theirs to collect."

"That's not a hole in the ledger." Ezio didn't reach to close the binding, kept it open on the table
between them as if closing it would concede something neither of them meant to concede. "It's a hole
in who's willing to be responsible for anything that happens on a line nobody agrees is really
there. The quarry owner built his whole arrangement around exactly this gap, and he's not wrong that
it works — a debt that's real on paper and unclaimed by anyone with the authority to enforce it is
still, in every practical sense, unpaid."

They did not force a resolution neither jurisdiction would grant. The ledger stood — complete,
cross-checked, undisputed by either office that had read it — a documented account with nowhere left
to be presented that would act on it, filed instead against the day some future authority finally
claimed the ground it described.

"So it just sits there," a quarry hand said, when Ezio told him honestly what had happened to the
account of his own six years' unpaid wages. "True and useless both."

"True, and not yet actionable," Ezio said. "I won't pretend those are the same thing. They're not."
