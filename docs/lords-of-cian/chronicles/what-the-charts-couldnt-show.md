# What the Charts Couldn't Show

*Locked canon, Batch 258, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1204`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
LXVI, wave 22, closing the wave. First contact beyond the edge of every known chart. Not a territory
Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Danne Sok's charts ran out three days past the Sovereign Trust's own farthest patrol line, the paper
simply going blank where the coastline should have continued, and for the first time in years of
charting the fleet's escape routes he had nothing left to draw from. "Past here," he told Kanja,
"I'm guessing same as you."

They found the islands on the fourth day past the edge of the known chart — a scattered chain of
them, terraced and cultivated in ways that owed nothing to Trust engineering or Rexmar tradition,
worked by people who came out to meet the three strange flagships in narrow outrigger craft faster
and more maneuverable than anything in the fleet's own hulls. No hostility in it, but no welcome
either — a careful, watchful distance kept exactly the same on both sides.

It was Dol Maren who found the way in, not through language, which neither side had, but through the
hulls themselves. He went out in a launch alone, unarmed, and spent an afternoon simply examining one
of the outrigger craft's lashings and joinery with the same open curiosity he brought to every hull
he'd ever studied, and the island's own boat-builders — recognizing a fellow tradesman's genuine
interest when they saw it, regardless of what flag or fear he might otherwise have carried — brought
him ashore to see how it was built.

By evening the fleet had traded iron tools for fresh water and fruit neither ship's stores had
carried in months, no coin changing hands, no terms negotiated beyond what two crews of shipbuilders
could work out with their hands. Kanja stayed aboard throughout, deliberately, letting Maren's craft
do the work reputation and fear never could have done this far past where the ghost fleet's name
meant anything at all.

"They don't know who we are," Efa Gol said, watching the trade wind down as the sun set. "First time
in years nobody on the other side of an exchange knows what ship this is."

"Good," Kanja said. "Let's see what gets built when nobody's afraid of us first."

Garren Hask logged the exchange as he logged everything — no names for the island or its people,
since none had been offered or asked for, just the fact of it: contact made, trust built through
craft rather than fear, three days past the edge of every chart the fleet had ever carried.
