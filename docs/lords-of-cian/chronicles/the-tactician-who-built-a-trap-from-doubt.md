# The Tactician Who Built a Trap From Doubt

*Locked canon, Batch 134, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-450`). The Crow King Alias Chronicle VIII. Rebellion
era, age 23. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose. No new named characters.*

---

The Directorate tactician assigned to counter the Crow King had studied every prior encounter closely
enough to conclude that attacking the deception itself was a losing strategy — every trap built to
catch the Hymn-Engine's tricks had already failed once, and a second attempt built the same way would
likely fail the same manner. He chose a different target instead: not the signals, but the crew's own
trust in them.

False orders, forged in a style close enough to Kanja's own crew protocols to pass casual inspection,
began reaching individual squads within the rebel network — orders that contradicted each other,
sent minor units to positions that made no tactical sense, seeded just enough confusion that by the
time the real operation began, several of Kanja's own people had started quietly wondering whether
the signals reaching them were genuinely his at all.

"Half the crew doesn't know which orders to trust anymore," the crew's own communications officer
told him, three days into the confusion, visibly rattled by a problem no amount of tactical skill
could solve directly. "He's not trying to beat the Hymn-Engine. He's trying to make us stop trusting
each other so the Hymn-Engine doesn't matter."

"Then we give them something to trust that doesn't run through a signal at all." Kanja didn't
counter-forge orders of his own, didn't escalate the deception war on the tactician's chosen ground.
Instead, he moved in person, squad to squad, delivering the real operational plan face to face,
voice to voice, in a manner no forged order could replicate — slower than a signal, but unforgeable
in a way nothing sent at a distance could be.

The tactician's confusion campaign collapsed within a week, not because the Hymn-Engine had
out-deceived him, but because Kanja had refused to fight the battle on the ground the man had chosen
at all. "You built a trap that only worked as long as trust traveled the same way information did,"
Kanja told the crew afterward. "The moment trust travels a different way — face to face, in person,
unforgeable — the trap stops mattering. That's not a trick. That's just refusing to let someone else
choose the terms of the fight."
