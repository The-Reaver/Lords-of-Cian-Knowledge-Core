# The Third Voice She Never Expected

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Crow King Alias Chronicle XXXIX, wave 13, closing
entry. Rebellion era, roughly four years after the Night of the Crow King (`MCD-236`, age 23). Not a

The apprentice's own student — steadier now, months past the misjudged timing that had once nearly
cost an operation its margin — came to her the way she had once come to Kanja, asking to be taught
the craft properly rather than simply deployed as a skilled hand within it, and the request left the
apprentice quietly unsettled in a way she hadn't expected.

"I don't know that I'm ready to teach someone else," she admitted, to Kanja rather than to the
student directly. "I only just finished learning it myself, or so it feels most days."

"You've been carrying real operational weight for over a year. You corrected a cryptographer's own
breakthrough. You held a live improvisation together under conditions no rehearsal prepared you
for. I don't think readiness is the actual question here." Kanja said it without pushing her toward
an answer either way. "The question is whether you want to be the reason the craft keeps growing past
what either of us built alone, the same way I once had to decide whether I wanted that for you."

She said yes, eventually, the same quiet uncertainty in it that Kanja remembered from his own first
lesson with her years before — and began teaching not by repeating what she'd been taught word for
word, but by testing which parts of it had actually become hers enough to pass on in her own voice.

The new student's first lesson looked almost nothing like the apprentice's own first lesson had, built
around a different set of examples, a different pace, a different way of framing the discomfort of
choosing silence over cleverness — the same underlying discipline, worn into a shape that belonged
now to a third person entirely, three full steps removed from wherever the craft had actually
started.

"It doesn't feel like teaching what I was taught," the apprentice said afterward, still working
through the strangeness of it. "It feels like something new, built out of what I was taught."

"That's what it's supposed to feel like, if it's actually alive," Kanja told her. "I stopped being
able to predict exactly what the craft would become the moment I first handed it to you. I imagine
you're about to lose that same certainty yourself, watching what she does with it next."
