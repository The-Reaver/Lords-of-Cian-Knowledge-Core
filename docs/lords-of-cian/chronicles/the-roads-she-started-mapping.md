# The Roads She Started Mapping

*Locked canon, Batch 200, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-803`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle XVIII, wave 6 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Twenty-four years after a caravan road three weeks from the sea had surrendered forty laborers into
open air, one of them — a girl of nine then, a woman past thirty now — had spent most of that time
turning a single afternoon's rescue into a trade of her own.

She found him at a coastal waystation he hadn't chosen for any reason beyond a night's rest between
engagements, and she didn't ask for anything before she opened the satchel she'd carried the last
two hundred miles specifically to show him.

"Maps," she said, spreading them across the waystation's one usable table. "Every inland route I
could confirm still moves people in chains, and every safe crossing I could find that doesn't. I've
been walking them myself for eleven years, checking which wells are trustworthy, which villages will
feed a stranger without asking questions they shouldn't ask. I wasn't going to wait for someone else
to follow the chains the way you did for me."

He studied the marks a long moment before he answered. "You did this from one afternoon."

"I did this from being nine years old on a road I thought I was going to die on, and then not dying
on it." She didn't dress the memory up with more feeling than that; she'd clearly told it plainly
before, to herself if no one else. "I don't need you to remember me. I need you to tell me if these
maps are wrong anywhere, because I can't verify the northern routes myself — too far from anywhere
I can walk in a season."

He corrected two crossings and confirmed the rest, and didn't offer to walk any of it with her.

"That's not what I came for," she said, reading the unasked question anyway. "You following every
chain in the world isn't a plan. It's just you, doing what you can reach. I'm building something that
doesn't need you to reach it at all — routes people can use whether or not the Scourge ever hears
about their particular road."

"Good." He rolled the corrected maps back into her satchel himself, careful with the ink. "That's the
part of this that was always supposed to outlast me."

She left before dawn, maps recopied and one route wiser, and the waystation's keeper — who hadn't
caught the stranger's name and wouldn't have believed it if he had — remembered for years afterward
only that a woman with dust-worn boots had spent one evening arguing cartography with a quiet
traveler, and had walked away smiling like she'd won something larger than the argument itself.
