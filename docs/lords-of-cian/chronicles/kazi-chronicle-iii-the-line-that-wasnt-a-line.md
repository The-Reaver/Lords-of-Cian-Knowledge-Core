# Kazi Chronicle III: The Line That Wasn't a Line

*Locked canon, Batch 176, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-523`). Homage-era Detroit, Kazi/Irin. Territory
Chronicle — Irin as protagonist, Kanja an unnamed background guest with no command, credit, or
resolution authorship. New standalone material. No new named characters.*

---

The dockworkers who loaded and moved freight through Kazi's own district worked nothing resembling an
assembly line — scattered gangs, individually assigned, structurally interdependent only in the loose
sense that one crew's delay backed up every crew behind it. When a dispute over loading quotas reached
crisis, Irin wondered aloud, for the first time, whether "The Line Stops" reached workers bound
together this loosely at all.

It did.

The call went out from the district's central loading yard, and every dock gang, scattered across
piers that had no literal chain connecting them the way an assembly line's belt did, felt the same
called halt arrive simultaneously — not through any mechanical connection, but through the genuine
structural interdependence of freight that had to move through all of them in sequence to reach
anywhere at all.

"I wasn't certain this would work," Irin admitted afterward, watching the halt hold across every pier
without a single gang breaking early. "The factory line binds people through a belt they can see and
touch. This is different — freight moving through hands that never meet, on a schedule none of them
control directly."

"Different shape, same binding, it turns out." His own lieutenant, Kunle, had studied the mechanism
closely enough to offer a theory. "I don't think the gift cares whether the chain is a belt or a
shipping schedule. I think it cares whether the work is genuinely interdependent — whether one link's
halt actually matters to the others, structurally, not just in spirit."

"That's a wider reach than I'd assumed I had." Irin considered the implication carefully, the
district's entire freight network suddenly a single body in a way he hadn't previously understood it
to be. "I've been thinking of this gift as belonging to factory floors specifically. I think it
actually belongs to anything built the way labor itself is built — dependent, link to link, whether
anyone ever drew the chain out where it could be seen."

The quota dispute resolved within days, the district's freight network having proven, for the first
time, that its own scattered structure was as genuinely unified as any single factory floor — bound
by the same underlying interdependence, however differently it was shaped on the surface.
