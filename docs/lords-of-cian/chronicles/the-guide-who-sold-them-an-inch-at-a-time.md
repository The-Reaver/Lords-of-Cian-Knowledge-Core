# The Guide Who Sold Them an Inch at a Time

*Locked canon, Batch 265, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1392`). Bane Alias Chronicle XCII, wave 31. First
trusted-insider betrayal entry -- a coerced, months-long guide who has fed the Directorate small,
seemingly harmless details is discovered from inside the column rather than exposed by an outside
enemy.*

---

Efa Gol was the one who noticed the pattern first, because noticing patterns in other people's
movements was the whole of what she did. Three ambushes in two months, each one narrowly avoided,
each one narrowly avoided at a spot the column had only decided to pass through the day before.
Luck doesn't repeat that specifically. Something else does.

It came back to the guide. Four months with the column by then, trusted with river crossings and
game trails and every shortcut that had saved the column real time, a man nobody had thought twice
about because thinking twice about him had simply never come up.

Bane didn't confront him with accusation. He sat across from him the way he sat across from anyone
he needed the truth from, and let the silence do what it always did — not a threat, just the plain
sense that whatever was going to be said had already been understood before the man opened his
mouth.

It broke him faster than it had ever broken anyone Bane could remember, and not the way it usually
broke people. Not the slow unraveling of someone caught in a lie they'd built their identity around.
Relief. The guide's shoulders came down like a weight he'd been asking someone to take off him for
months and nobody had offered.

"They have my sister," he said. "Since before you ever hired me. Small things, at first — where you
were headed, nothing that could get anyone killed, I told myself that every time. Then it stopped
being small and I didn't know how to tell you without telling you everything, and once I didn't tell
you the first time, every day after made the next day harder."

Bane let him finish before he answered, which was its own kind of answer.

"You've been paying a debt you never agreed to owe, on a schedule someone else set," he said. "That
doesn't make what happened to those columns not real. Two men died at the second ambush who
wouldn't have if you'd come to me the first week instead of the fourth month. I'm not going to tell
you that cost isn't real, because it is, and you're going to carry it." He paused. "But the way
you're carrying it right now — waiting for me to decide what happens to you — that's not the debt
that matters most tonight. Where is she."

It wasn't forgiveness in the shape anyone expects forgiveness to take. It was an extraction plan,
drawn up before sunrise, aimed at a coercion the guide had spent four months believing was a wall
with no other side.

Corren Halst asked him afterward, once the guide had gone to sit with what he'd said and what was
now, tentatively, going to be done about it: "You could have ended that a dozen different ways. Most
of them faster."

"Fear built that leak," Bane said. "I wasn't going to patch it with more fear. He didn't sell us out
because he stopped caring what happened to us. He sold us out because someone made caring about her
cost more than he could afford, and kept raising the price every month he paid it instead of asking
for help." He looked toward where the man sat. "The debt's still real. So is the reason he owed it.
Both things are true, and only one of them was ever his fault."

guide is coerced through a held hostage rather than acting from malice or ideology, extending the
sub-series' established preference for nuanced rather than clean-villain resolutions. VB-060's
"Already-Finished Negotiation" presence trait is shown producing relief rather than fear or
capitulation for the first time, against someone already broken by a burden rather than someone
resisting or defeated. Resolution addresses the coercion's root cause (an extraction, not
punishment) rather than retribution, consistent with the amnesty-doctrine and mercy precedents
already locked elsewhere in the run. No new named characters -- the guide and his sister are unnamed;
Efa Gol and Corren Halst reused.*
