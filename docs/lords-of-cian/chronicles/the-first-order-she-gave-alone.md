# The First Order She Gave Alone

*Locked canon, Batch 286, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1517`). Captain Alias Chronicle XCVII, wave 33. Danne Sok's daughter, grown since enlisting (`MCD-1002`), leads her first independent operation, mirroring Corren Halst's own growth a generation later.*

---

Kanja stayed away on purpose, the way he'd learned to across the six-week absence and every shorter one since (`MCD-1385`, `MCD-1364`). Danne Sok's daughter didn't know that when she took the assignment; she only knew that the smuggling skiffs working the delta's back channels needed intercepting before the season's grain shipment reached them, and that Corren Halst, newly free of the chair's own daily weight, had looked at the roster and put her name at the top of it without asking anyone's permission first.

"You've run half of every operation you've ever been part of," Halst told her, when she asked why. "You've just never had to be the one nobody was quietly backstopping. Time you found out you don't need it."

She planned it the way she'd watched Halst plan things for years — three crews, staggered timing, a fallback route memorized before the primary one was ever walked. It went wrong exactly once, in the way plans always went wrong exactly once: the lead skiff broke from the expected channel a full quarter-hour early, catching her second crew still moving into position.

For one long breath she felt the old instinct — look for Kanja, look for Halst, look for anyone whose judgment outranked her own by right of having been doing this longer. Neither was anywhere near the delta. Her father, working the third crew, was watching her face for the decision, not making it for her.

She made it. Pulled the second crew back rather than force the timing, let the lead skiff run its early course into open water where a single boat alone was no threat to anyone, and took the two remaining skiffs clean at the fallback point twenty minutes later exactly as the contingency had always intended. No shots fired on either side. Every sack of grain accounted for by nightfall.

Danne Sok found her afterward, coiling a line she didn't strictly need to coil, the way people did when their hands needed something to do while the rest of them caught up to what had just happened. "You didn't wait for someone to tell you it was the right call," he said.

"There wasn't anyone to wait for," she said. "Turns out that's not the same as being alone in it. It just means the call was actually mine."

When Kanja returned three days later and heard the account secondhand, from her father and not from her, he understood it had gone exactly the way it was supposed to.
