# Air Enough for Six

*Locked canon, Batch 199, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-775`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle XX, wave 7 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The merchant hull had gone down two hours before *The Audit* found her, bow-first onto a sandbar
shallow enough to keep her from disappearing entirely — which was the only reason the six sailors
trapped in her aft hold were still alive, breathing an air pocket that was shrinking by degrees every
time the tide shifted her weight.

"No enemy here," Kanja said, already stripping to the waist. "Just water and time."

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System, built for absorbing and redirecting force, did nothing useful
against a hull that needed cutting, not striking — so the work went to hands and to Obsidian Malice's
edge, used not to discharge but simply as the sharpest tool aboard, opening a hole in the collapsed
aft bulkhead wide enough for a man to pass through without widening it so far the whole compartment
flooded before anyone could get out.

The first pass found nothing but dark water and the sound, faint and rhythmic, of someone striking the
hull from the inside to prove they were still alive to hear it answered.

"They're past the second bulkhead," Kanja surfaced to report, breath ragged. "Air pocket's smaller
than we thought. I need another hand who can hold a breath and not panic in the dark."

Dol Maren went in on the second pass, reading the hull's own groaning stress the way he read every
hull, calling out which sections were still sound enough to cut and which would come apart if pressed —
the same expertise that kept ships whole under him, now used to keep one from finishing the job of
sinking on the men still trapped inside it.

They brought the six sailors out one at a time over the better part of an hour, the last two coughing
water and shaking in a hold that had, by the final extraction, narrowed to a pocket barely large enough
for a single desperate breath.

"You're not Trust," one of the rescued sailors managed, once he'd stopped retching seawater onto the
deck. "You're the ghost fleet. You're supposed to be the thing that sinks ships like mine."

"We're supposed to be a lot of things nobody ever checked in person." Kanja wrapped a blanket around
the man's shoulders and moved on to the next. "Tonight we're just the thing that happened to be close
enough when your hull needed six extra hands more than it needed a reason to say no."

No weapon fired that night, no reputation deployed, no fear taught to anyone — only the specific,
unglamorous work of counting breaths and cutting careful holes until every name on the manifest matched
a living sailor standing on *The Audit*'s deck.
