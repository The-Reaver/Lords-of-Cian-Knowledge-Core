# The Weight of a False Ledger

*Locked canon, Batch 195, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-654`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XIX, wave 7 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The shoring timber kept coming up short.

Not missing — the requisitions all matched the quartermaster's own tally sheet, delivery for
delivery — but short in a way that only showed up if you weighed a bundle instead of counting it,
and weighed it against what a bundle of that grade actually ought to weigh rather than what the
paper said it should.

Kanja weighed one himself, on a borrowed scale, the night he started to suspect the numbers.

"This is green-cut," he told the crew chief, hefting the bundle. "Not seasoned the way the tally
claims. Green timber weighs different, holds different, and fails different under load than what
we've been charged for. Someone's substituting cheaper, unseasoned stock and pocketing the
difference between what we're paying for and what we're actually getting."

"The quartermaster's one of ours. Vetted twice."

"Vetted for loyalty, maybe. Nobody vetted him for honesty about a shoring order, because nobody
thought to ask a man moving lumber whether he was skimming it." Kanja set the bundle down and
started sorting the stockpile by hand, separating seasoned from green with nothing but weight and
grain to guide him. "I'm not accusing him of working for the other side. I think he's just found a
profit in a war nobody's watching closely enough to notice, and I'd rather correct that quietly
than let it cost someone their life the day a green timber gives out under a load it was never
rated for."

The confrontation, when it came, wasn't a fight. Kanja laid the sorted stockpile out in two piles —
what had been paid for, and what had actually been delivered — and let the quartermaster do his own
arithmetic in front of it.

"I can make it right," the man said, quieter than his usual bluster. "Replace the difference out of
my own take, no one else needs to know."

"Everyone needs to know, actually. Not to shame you — to make sure the next man handling this
crew's materials knows somebody's checking the weight, not just the paper." Kanja didn't raise his
voice through any of it. "You're not the first person to think a war's chaos means nobody's
counting. You won't be the last. I'd just rather you be the one who learns it costs you a
correction instead of costing someone else a collapsed tunnel."

The full shipment of seasoned timber arrived within the week, and the crew's shoring never failed
again for want of proper stock — a quiet correction that never made it into any account of the
siege except the crew's own, and Garren Hask's private tally, kept for exactly this kind of thing.
