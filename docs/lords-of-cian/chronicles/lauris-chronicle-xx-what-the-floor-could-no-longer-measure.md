# Lauris Chronicle XX: What the Floor Could No Longer Measure

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1637`). Twentieth entry in Lauris Letitia's own Chronicle
series, the seventh entry of Strand K (Kares Prime / deep past). Set roughly 250 years after Chronicle
XIX (`MCD-1636`), reusing Serath (now a mature Olmedrin combatant) and Veska Karth-Ven. Dramatizes a
second training engagement from the Long Operational Period (`MCD-1555`) that functions as a
deliberate hinge toward `MCD-174`: Vask Karth-Ven's central training floor, calibrated continuously
for twenty-eight thousand years before Lauris's own arrival (`MCD-1554`), is shown for the first time
genuinely failing to produce a usable reading of her combat-progression ceiling during a formal
reassessment -- foreshadowing, without asserting, the "exhausted what the civilization's surviving
infrastructure could still offer her" language `MCD-174` uses of her eventual decision to depart. No
new named characters. Full text below.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Vask Karth-Ven, age ~3,150: "The floor read me accurately for two thousand nine
hundred years. Today it stopped partway through the fourth hour and reported a number it could not
have meant. I do not think the floor is wrong. I think I have simply become a question it was not
built to answer."*

I want to be careful, setting this entry down, not to overstate what she herself records with
characteristic restraint. This was not, by any account she has given me, a crisis. It was a
reassessment -- routine, in the sense that Karth-Ven's Iron-Speakers periodically requested one of
every combatant whose profile the training floor tracked, and unremarkable in every respect except
its conclusion.

Serath was present for it, which is the detail that makes the entry worth transcribing in full rather
than summarizing. She had, by this point, spent some two hundred and fifty years since her own
training under Lauris concluded, and had become, in the intervening time, Olmedrin's own senior
training authority -- teaching a discipline descended directly, by her own account elsewhere in
Lauris's archive, from the adapted Karth-Sera principles Lauris had built for her. She had returned
to Karth-Ven that season for an unrelated matter, and Veska, aware of the history between them,
invited her to observe the reassessment rather than simply hear of its result secondhand.

*

The floor itself -- sixty meters of continuously calibrated high-gravity-forged Drakma, the same
platform that had, over Lauris's own training, developed a more detailed combat-reading of her than
of any other Karesian in the Vask's recorded history -- was built to track density output across a
sustained engagement and translate it into a figure the Iron-Speakers could compare, generation to
generation, against every combatant who had ever trained on it. For two thousand nine hundred years,
by Lauris's own account, it had done exactly that for her, tracking her rise from a fourteen-year-old
testing at double cohort ceiling through every subsequent recalibration with what she describes as
total fidelity.

On this occasion, past the fourth hour of a sustained engagement against three rotating opponents --
a protocol designed to simulate duration rather than any genuine threat, since no living opponent on
Kares Prime could sustain the pace required to test her honestly by that point in her career -- the
floor's own instrumentation began returning readings Veska's own senior technicians could not
reconcile with each other. Not an equipment failure, by their own diagnosis; the floor continued
functioning correctly for every other purpose it was asked to serve that same day. It had simply
reached, in Lauris's own words, the edge of what twenty-eight thousand years of Karesian engineering
had ever anticipated needing to measure, and it had nothing further to offer past that edge except
noise.

*

I have asked Lauris, more than once, whether this troubled her at the time. She has told me, each
time, that it did not -- that she has never particularly needed external instrumentation to know her
own condition, and that the floor's failure to keep pace with her told her considerably less about
herself than it told her about the floor. What she does record, in the entry's closing lines, is
something else entirely, and I believe it is the true reason this account survives in her archive at
all:

*Serath asked me, afterward, what it felt like to be a question the Vask could no longer answer. I
told her I did not yet know. I have thought of little else since. I have spent nearly thirteen
hundred years assuming that whatever this world could not yet give me, it would eventually give me
in time -- more training, more calibration, more years on this floor or one like it. I am beginning
to suspect, watching the floor fail today for the first time in its recorded history, that some
questions are not answered by more time on the same floor. I do not know yet what the alternative
is. I record only that I have begun, for the first time, to wonder whether one exists.*

I have transcribed this entry many times since I first found it, and I confess I did not fully
understand, on first reading, how much weight it was quietly carrying. She was not, at age three
thousand one hundred and fifty, deciding to leave Kares Prime. That decision, as I understand the
record, was still some two hundred and fifty years distant. But I believe this is the entry in which
the question first entered her archive in a form she could no longer dismiss as an idle one -- not
whether the civilization that raised her still needed her, which it plainly did, but whether it still
had anything left to give her in return. Serath, for her part, is recorded as having said nothing
further that day. Lauris notes only that she stayed at Karth-Ven three additional days after the
reassessment, training on the same floor regardless, as though giving it one more chance to surprise
her.
