# Three Generations Under the Same Debt

*Locked canon, Batch 256, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1151`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle LXVII, wave 23. The method's first inherited-debt-bondage case, tracing a single fraudulent loan across three generations of the same family, resolved by voiding the debt as illegitimate rather than merely recalculating it.*

---

Three men from the same family testified on the same afternoon, in the same tent, about the same debt: a grandfather too old now to work the quarry he'd been bound to for fifty years, his son working it beside him, his grandson — fourteen, already logged on the crew's books — starting the same rotation his elders had never finished. The original loan, according to the estate's own records, had been struck two generations back: a small advance against wages, repayable at a modest rate, that should have cleared itself inside four years by any honest accounting.

It hadn't cleared in fifty. Every generation, the estate's ledger showed a fresh charge added before the old one closed — tool rental, lodging fees, a mandatory "training levy" charged to each new family member as he came of working age, each charge compounding against a principal that, on the numbers alone, should have been settled before the grandfather turned thirty.

Ezio worked backward from the original loan document rather than forward from the current balance, which was the only way to see what the estate's own paperwork was built to hide: every one of the added charges across fifty years traced to the same clause, reworded slightly each generation but functionally identical — a debt that renewed itself against the next family member the moment the current one showed signs of nearing the end of it. Three generations of a single family had never once been free of a loan that, run honestly against the estate's own interest terms, should have closed before the grandfather's own children were born.

"This isn't three debts," Ezio said, laying fifty years of entries end to end. "It's one debt that was never allowed to end."

Kanja didn't ask the estate to recalculate a fairer figure. He had Ezio write the finding plainly: the original loan, honestly amortized against its own stated terms, closed in year four; everything charged against the family in the forty-six years since was fabricated against a debt that no longer existed to attach to. The whole construction — not a balance owed, not a balance forgiven, but a debt voided as never legitimately outstanding past its fourth year — released grandfather, son, and grandson from the estate's books in the same afternoon, the fourteen-year-old's name struck from the rotation before he'd worked a single week of it.

The grandfather didn't celebrate the way Ezio half-expected. He sat with the finding a long time, reading a document he couldn't fully read for himself, and asked only one question: whether it meant his grandson would grow up owing nothing to anyone for a mistake made before his father was born. Told yes, he folded the paper carefully, the way a man folds something he intends to keep rather than something he intends to use.

Resolved by voiding illegitimate compounding rather than recalculating a fairer balance, a new remedy category. No new named characters; the grandfather, son, and grandson are unnamed and one-scene.
