# The Ninth Who Passed

*Locked canon, Batch 261, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1292`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LXXIII, wave 25, first entry. Discovery that one of the cohort's nine graduates has turned the doctrine to extortion. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The report reached Kanja secondhand, the way the worst ones usually did: a distant settlement, three days beyond the district where the cohort of twelve had trained, paying a man to "protect" its granary and dockside stores from structural collapse — collapse he himself was quietly threatening to cause if the payments stopped.

He recognized the technique described before he recognized the name. Doubled verification, applied correctly. A discharge controlled enough to bring down exactly what was threatened and nothing more, a precision that only came from someone who'd genuinely learned the doctrine rather than faked it the way the freelance mercenaries once had. The report named one of the nine who'd passed the cohort's final trial — sent home, at the time, with real certification and Kanja's own honest confidence that the training had taken.

"I trained him myself," Kanja said, when the second student brought the report to him directly rather than let a rider carry it. "One of four in my own group of the twelve. He passed clean. No hesitation I remember, no warning sign I failed to catch."

"Warning signs aren't always there to catch," the second student said. "You taught us that yourself, with your first student's own granary. Sometimes the temptation only shows up once nobody's standing close enough to notice it."

"That's not the same as this. He told me the truth about his temptation before he acted on it. This man's been acting on his for months, by the sound of it, and telling no one." Kanja set the report down, feeling something colder than anger settle into the decision he already knew he'd have to make. "I built a doctrine on the idea that an honest account outlasts whoever tries to bury it. He's been using the exact method I taught him to make sure his own account never gets told at all."

"What do you intend to do?"

"Go there. Not to punish him for being taught by me — that's not a debt he owes for existing. To find out whether he still remembers a single word of what the technique was actually for, or whether he's forgotten it so completely that the only thing left of my teaching is the parts that make him dangerous." Kanja folded the report and set it inside his coat rather than leave it behind. "I've spent this whole doctrine's life defending it against people who never understood it. I don't know yet which is worse — that, or someone who understood it completely and chose this anyway."
