# The Apprentice Who Became the Guildmaster

*Locked canon, Batch 202, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-872`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XXVII, wave 9 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

She had been one of sixty unarmed apprentices on the beach the day the raiders came for the forge and
found the fight kept deliberately away from them instead — twelve years old then, too young to
understand at the time how close the morning had come to going differently. By the time a delegation
of younger smiths sought her out, decades later, to settle a dispute over guild admissions, she was
the guildmaster of a territory that had, within living memory, refused to admit smiths on the same
exclusionary terms The Anvil had once openly defied.

"They tell me you trained aboard the original Rolling Foundry campaign," the youngest of the
petitioners said, half in awe, half testing whether the story was even true. "That you actually worked
a floor next to him."

"I did. For about eighteen months, on and off, whenever the barge came through." She didn't dwell on
it, turning back to the dispute the petitioners had actually come to settle — a talented smith denied
guild standing over a technicality that concealed an older prejudice underneath it. "He never taught
me anything mystical. Taught me to true a hinge, taught me patience with a reheat, taught me a floor
judges work by the work and nothing else. I spent the rest of my life making sure that lesson didn't
die with the campaign that taught it to me."

She ruled in the disputed smith's favor before the day was through, citing nothing but the work itself,
the same standard she'd watched applied on The Anvil's own floor decades earlier to a woman the
conventional guilds of her own era had refused outright.

"Some of the older masters won't like this ruling," one of her own officers warned her.

"They're welcome to their opinion." She said it without heat, recognizing, only as the words left her,
exactly whose voice she was echoing. "I didn't build this guild's standards on exclusions I hadn't
examined. Someone taught me that, a long time ago, on a floor that isn't there anymore. Seemed worth
keeping."
