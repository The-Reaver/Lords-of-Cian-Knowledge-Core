# The Blow Danne Sok Took Without a Word

*Locked canon, Batch 194, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-641`). The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XXXVI, wave 12 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Danne Sok had never been a man of many words, even before Kanja freed him — a fact the crew had long
since stopped remarking on, the way you stop remarking on weather that never changes. What he did
instead of speaking, more often than anyone gave him credit for noticing, was position himself.

The crowd at the Marrow Landing reading had grown larger than usual, the five-district figures drawing
onlookers from districts that hadn't signed the agreement at all, and it was in that larger, less
predictable crowd that a man with a grievance nobody had flagged in advance — a foreman displaced by
the new standard, quietly furious about it for weeks — pushed forward with a length of dock chain
already swinging before anyone called out a warning.

Sok took it instead. Not because he'd been asked to, not because anyone had assigned him to watch that
particular angle of the crowd, but because some quiet calculation, made without a word to anyone,
had already placed him half a step ahead of where the chain would land if it reached its intended
target.

It caught him across the shoulder, hard enough to drop him, and by the time Kanja turned toward the
sound the man had already been disarmed by two other hands and the moment, from the crowd's
perspective, was mostly already over.

"You didn't say anything," Kanja said afterward, kneeling beside him while someone checked the
shoulder for a break. "You just moved."

Sok didn't offer an explanation, not because he didn't have one, but because explaining it would have
required more words than the act itself had. He shrugged with his good shoulder instead, wincing
slightly at even that much movement.

"You could have called out. Warned me. Let someone else take it who saw it coming with more time to
brace."

Still no answer, only a look that carried, as clearly as any sentence could have, something closer to
*there wasn't time, and it didn't need discussing.*

Kanja let it go, understanding, without needing it spelled out, that some kinds of loyalty didn't
travel through speech at all — that the freed man who had never fully explained why the silence after
his own liberation had lasted as long as it had, communicated instead through exactly this: a body
placed where a blow was going to land, decided and executed faster than any warning could have been
spoken.

The shoulder healed in three weeks. Sok never spoke of the chain again, not that year, not for years
after, and Kanja never asked him to.
