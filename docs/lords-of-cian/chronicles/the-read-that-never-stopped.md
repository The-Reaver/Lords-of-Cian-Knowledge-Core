# The Read That Never Stopped

*Locked canon, Batch 261, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1305`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LXXXVI, wave 29. A storm forces the doctrine's first sustained, continuous live-monitoring application. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The storm found them nine days into the voyage, past the point of any safe harbor, and it found the repaired keel exactly where Dol Maren had feared a storm eventually would — under load it hadn't yet been tested against, the confirmed-sound joinery from the dock now working under a punishment no single verification pass had accounted for.

Every prior read of the doctrine's run had been a moment: a diagnosis, a discharge, an answer arrived at and acted on. Nothing about the technique had ever needed to persist past the instant it confirmed a tension. Tonight, with the ship pitching hard enough that a single read taken now would be stale before the next wave finished hitting the hull, Kanja found himself doing something the doctrine had never asked of him before — holding the read open, continuously, hour after hour, tracking the keel's tension as a living, shifting number rather than a fixed one he could confirm once and set aside.

It cost him in a way discharge never had. Doubled verification was a discipline of patience between two distinct passes; this was patience stretched across an entire night with no pause between passes at all, his palm against the hull's interior brace for hours at a stretch, the strain of sustained attention settling into his shoulders the way it never did during even the longest single engagement.

"Tell me the number," Dol Maren said, twice an hour, never more urgency in the request than the words themselves carried.

"Holding. Climbing, but holding." Each answer came slightly different, the actual figures shifting as the storm worsened, and each time Kanja gave the honest one rather than round it toward comfort. Near dawn, the number climbed past what he'd have called safe on any static reading, and he said so plainly, giving Dol Maren the chance to make the call rather than making it silently himself.

"Turn her bow-on to the swell," Dol Maren said, already moving before Kanja finished the sentence. "Less lateral strain on the keel section. Costs us three hours of heading. Costs us the ship if we don't."

The adjustment held the number where it needed to stay for the remaining four hours until the storm finally broke. Kanja didn't let the read go until the sea itself had calmed enough that the number stopped moving on its own — the first time the doctrine had ever demanded that kind of sustained, unbroken attention rather than a decisive instant of diagnosis and response.

"You held that the whole night," Dol Maren said, once it was over, something that might have been the nearest thing to genuine surprise Kanja had yet seen cross his face. "I didn't know the doctrine could work that way."

"Neither did I, until tonight," Kanja said. "I've spent years building this around a single true answer, arrived at carefully and acted on once. Tonight the truth wouldn't hold still long enough for that. I had to learn, in real time, how to keep listening instead of just listening once."
