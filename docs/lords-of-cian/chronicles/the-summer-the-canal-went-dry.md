# The Summer the Canal Went Dry

*Locked canon, Batch 255, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1129`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle LXXII, wave 24,
closing the wave. Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. A drought environmental register, the first
without water anywhere in it.*

---

The canal that had drowned two hundred soldiers the year before was, by midsummer, a cracked silt
bed a determined child could walk across without wetting a boot.

Nothing about the district's dispute work had ever assumed a dry canal. The tally method's usual
rhythms — barges moving goods on schedule, workers crossing by plank-bridge, owners moving cargo by
water because the water was simply there — all of it depended on a canal that carried weight, and
this one, for the first time anyone at Warehouse Twelve could remember, carried none. Wagons crawled
along the exposed banks instead, choking the narrow dirt tracks with traffic the roads had never been
built for, and an owner three districts over saw in the wagon bottleneck an opportunity the water had
never given him: goods that used to reach the district by barge, transparent to anyone watching the
canal, now moved by wagon, out of sight, along routes nobody had ever needed to document before.

Dol Maren's seventeen bridges stood over nothing. The plank spans that had once been the district's
most contested crossings — worth fighting over, worth an ambush, worth the "Line That Wouldn't Break"
kind of defense — became, overnight, irrelevant pieces of engineering spanning dry silt. What mattered
instead was the wagon track itself, and who controlled which stretch of it, and whether a load
claimed for one district actually arrived there or quietly diverted to a buyer the cracked canal bed
made suddenly much harder to catch doing it.

Garren Hask adapted the tally method before anyone told him to. Wagon manifests replaced barge
schedules; wheel-rut counts on the dirt tracks, cross-checked against declared cargo weights, became
a rough but workable substitute for the water traffic the method had always quietly assumed would be
there to watch. It caught the diverting owner within two weeks — not through any power of Onyx's, not
through a duel or a raid, but through the same patient documentation discipline the method had always
relied on, applied to dust instead of water.

"Everything we built assumed the canal," Kanja said, standing on the cracked bed itself, silt
crumbling under his boots where water had run for as long as the district had existed.

"Everything we built assumed a lot of things," Hask said. "Doesn't mean it breaks when one of them
stops being true. Just means somebody has to notice fast enough."

The rains returned before autumn, and the canal filled again over the course of a single wet week,
Bridge Eleven and its sixteen fellow spans returning to relevance as abruptly as they'd lost it. The
wagon-manifest method Hask had improvised during the dry months stayed in the ledger anyway, folded
into the standard practice for whichever season came next.

*Continuity notes (not narrative): the first dedicated drought/heat environmental register in the
sub-series, deliberately inverting the water-and-flood motif that dominates nearly every prior
Trench Monarch entry (the founding Dredge-Line Ambush's flood, `MCD-900`'s storm, `MCD-1125`'s
spring flood, `MCD-631`'s poisoned water, `MCD-625`'s flooded darkness). Reuses Dol Maren's
established bridge-building work (`MCD-1123`) by rendering it temporarily irrelevant rather than
repeating it, and shows Garren Hask's method adapting to a changed environment without any Onyx
application or combat. No new named characters -- the diverting owner is unnamed. Closes the Trench
Monarch's twenty-fourth wave (with "What the Blade Remembered Before Him," `MCD-1127`, and "The Man
They Punished for Standing Near Him," `MCD-1128`).*
