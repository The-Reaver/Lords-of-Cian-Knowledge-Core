# The Final Burning

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XLVI, wave 15.
Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign's final weeks. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated
in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The last punitive strike attempted against the campaign came in the tour's final weeks, ordered by a
garrison commander who had studied every prior burning across the campaign's full length and concluded,
correctly this time, that no single tactic remained available to him that hadn't already failed once
before. He burned the district anyway, less from any real expectation of success than from an order he
couldn't afford to refuse.

Kanja arrived to find a district already organizing itself before the crew had finished mooring — work
parties forming from muscle memory built across eighteen months of watching the method work elsewhere,
residents who had never personally rebuilt anything themselves stepping into roles they'd only ever
seen demonstrated in other settlements' accounts.

"You barely had to do anything," the district's own headwoman observed, watching the framework rise
with a speed that owed almost nothing to the crew's direct labor. "Half of this was already moving
before you even reached the dock."

"That's not an accident, and it's not really about me anymore, if it ever fully was." Kanja walked the
rebuild's perimeter, correcting almost nothing, the district's own instincts proving sound without his
intervention. "Eighteen months ago, the first burning on this campaign needed me standing at the center
of every decision. This one barely needed me at all. That's not a smaller victory. It's the actual
one."

The rebuild finished in six days — the fastest of the entire campaign, achieved with the least direct
involvement from the crew of any site the tour had reached. The garrison commander's own report, filed
afterward, would be the campaign's last recorded acknowledgment from the Trust side of a tactic it had
never once found an answer to.

"Eighteen months, and they never adjusted the strategy at all," Callum Breck said, reading the report
over Kanja's shoulder. "Just kept doing the same thing and hoping the outcome would eventually change."

"Maybe they couldn't adjust it. Burning was the whole tool they had." Kanja set the report down,
watching the district's own six-day rebuild stand finished against the tide line. "We spent eighteen
months proving it was never going to be enough. Feels strange, watching the very last one land exactly
as little as the very first one eventually did."
