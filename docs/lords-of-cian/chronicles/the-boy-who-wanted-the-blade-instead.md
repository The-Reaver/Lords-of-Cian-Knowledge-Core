# The Boy Who Wanted the Blade Instead

*Locked canon, Batch 195, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-653`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XVIII, wave 6 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The boy was fourteen, an orphan of the Southern District raids, and he had attached himself to the
crew three weeks earlier asking for exactly one thing: to be taught how to kill the soldiers who'd
killed his father.

Kanja put a length of pipe fitting in his hands instead.

"That's not what I asked for," the boy said, turning the fitting over with obvious contempt.

"I know. It's what I'm offering." Kanja crouched to his level, the way a man crouches to speak with
someone rather than down at them. "You want to hurt the people who hurt your father. I understand
that better than you think I do. But hate makes a man fast for a season and useless for a
lifetime, and I'm not interested in handing a fourteen-year-old a blade so he can die trying to
prove something a length of pipe could prove better and safer."

"You fight. Everyone says you're the strongest thing down here."

"I fight when there's no other way through. Most days there is one, and it looks like this." He
tapped the fitting. "You want to hurt them. Fine — help me take away everything they need to keep
hurting people like your father. Every tunnel we shore holds up a roof over people they'd rather
see buried. Every joint you learn to read right is one more thing they can't use against us because
we understood it first. That's not smaller than killing them. It just doesn't get you killed doing
it, and it actually adds up to something when the war's over and you're still here to see it."

The boy didn't answer for a long moment, turning the fitting in his hands the way he'd watched
Kanja turn one, weeks earlier, in front of two confused municipal repairmen.

"Teach me the joints, then," he said finally. "But if it comes to a blade—"

"If it comes to a blade, you'll have earned the right to hold one properly, because you'll have
learned first what it actually costs to build something instead of only what it costs to break
one." Kanja handed him a second fitting, matched to the first. "That's the only order I'll teach it
in. Building first. Everything else, if it has to happen at all, comes after."

By the end of the six weeks, the boy could set a shoring brace correctly on his own, had never once
raised a blade in anger, and had started, quietly, calling the pipe fittings "the boring work that
matters" — a phrase the rest of the crew adopted without ever quite tracing it back to him.
