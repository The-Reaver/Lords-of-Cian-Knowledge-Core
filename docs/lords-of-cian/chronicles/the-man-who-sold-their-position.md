# The Man Who Sold Their Position

*Locked canon, Batch 258, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1203`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
LXV, wave 22. A betrayal-from-within entry testing the restraint doctrine against coercion rather
than an external enemy. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Danne Sok found the pattern before anyone else did — Trust patrols intercepting the fleet's supply
runs three times running, each time at a point on the route that only existed in his own charts and
the handful of men who carried them. He brought it to Kanja quietly, the way he brought every
uncomfortable finding, laid out plainly with no accusation attached yet, just the shape of what the
numbers showed.

The trail led to a young hand aboard *The Ledger* — a cargo-runner who'd joined two years back,
reliable, unremarkable, and terrified in a way that had nothing to do with getting caught. Kanja
found him alone before anyone else could and asked him directly rather than through anyone else, and
the man broke in under a minute: a Trust intelligence officer had his mother and two younger sisters
in a debtor's hold in Jicome, and had made the arrangement plain — the fleet's positions, or his
family stayed locked up until they died there.

Callum Breck, who'd learned four months of silence over one death and understood exactly what it cost
a man to be the reason someone else didn't come home, was the one who argued hardest against the
crew's first instinct, which was to put the informant off the ship at the next port and be done with
him. "He didn't sell us for coin," Breck said. "He sold us because somebody put a knife to the only
thing he had left. That's not the same thing."

Kanja didn't debate it. He took *The Ledger* to Jicome himself, quietly, and did what the fleet had
never openly done before — bought three lives out of a debtor's hold with coin rather than force, no
reputation, no fear, just payment made and a family walked out the door before the Trust officer
running the arrangement even knew the ghost fleet had noticed him.

The informant stayed aboard. Garren Hask logged the debt paid and the position compromised in the
same entry, side by side, exactly as it had happened — not softened, not hidden, just recorded true.
"You'll answer for what you gave them," Kanja told the man, once his family was safe belowdecks.
"Not by leaving. By making sure it never happens again, and by helping us close the gap you opened."
