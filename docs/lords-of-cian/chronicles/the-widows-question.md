# The Widow's Question

*Locked canon, Batch 258, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1216`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
LXXVIII, wave 26, closing the wave. A war widow confronts Kanja directly over a death from the
alias's own first reputation failure. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose.*

---

She found Kanja in a market square rather than aboard any ship, which told him before she said a word
that she'd gone to some trouble to arrange the meeting rather than simply happen across him — a woman
perhaps thirty, plainly dressed, with the specific tired steadiness of someone who had done all her
crying already and had questions left instead.

"My husband served aboard the *Vigilant Crown*," she said. "The one that fought you off the Sovereign
Coast — the captain who didn't believe in ghosts and made you prove it real." A real fight, years
back, where the reputation had failed to end things bloodlessly, before doctrine and legend had
hardened into the near-unbroken record the fleet carried now. "He died in that fight. I want to know
why it had to be a fight at all, when every other ship you've met since got to walk away whole."

Kanja didn't reach for an excuse, and didn't reach for the doctrine either, though it would have been
easy enough to point at every rescue since as evidence the restraint was real. "Your husband's captain
chose to fight rather than let the reputation decide it for him. That was his right. I gave him every
chance to stand down that I've given every ship since, and he used every one of those chances to close
the distance instead. I didn't want that fight. I didn't avoid it either, when it came, and a man died
in it who might not have if I'd been slower, or worse at what I do, or willing to just let his ship
board mine and take what they wanted."

"That's not an apology."

"No," he agreed. "I don't think an apology is what you actually came here for. You came to see if I'd
tell you the truth instead of a story that made it easier to hear. He died fighting a battle his own
captain chose. I don't get to unmake that by being sorry about it now."

She stood with that a long moment, and something in her shoulders came down half an inch, not
forgiveness exactly, but the specific relief of having finally gotten a straight answer instead of a
legend's comfortable version. "Garren Hask's ledger," she said. "Does it have his name in it?"

"It does. Every name, every ship, exactly as it happened."

"Good," she said. "That's more than the Trust ever gave me."
