# Lauris Chronicle XLIV: The Terms She Still Controls

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1661`). Forty-fourth entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, the sixth entry of Strand L. Advances a relational/trust debt with
Kareth-Vassen Aerelin (already locked: `MCD-191`, `MCD-194`, `MCD-197`) rather than a facility or
population debt: Aerelin, through Sephtis's standing broker channel, asks Lauris for personal rather
than institutional help, testing the condition Lauris negotiated at her recruitment that her
relationship with Aerelin's network "stays on terms she controls rather than the movement's central
command" (`MCD-194`). No new named characters; Aerelin and Sephtis already locked. Deepens rather
than resolves -- the operative's situation and whether Lauris acts remain open at the entry's close.
Abad's blanket authorization: "lock it, continue uninterrupted, test and push to main."*

-----

*Archive fragment, present-day: "Aerelin has never asked me for anything she did not need. I have
never once made her ask twice. This is the first time I have made her wait for an answer at all."*

Sephtis's archive room is not a place I am often invited into, and I want to note, before this
account proceeds, that my presence for what follows was itself something of an exception — granted, I
believe, because Lauris judged the matter significant enough to want a record of it kept, even one she
would later review before allowing it into this series.

The message came through the channel Sephtis has held, personally rather than as a Lords of Cian
command function, since before Lauris ever arrived at the movement's headquarters: the sole route by
which Aerelin's autonomous Kareth War-Order network reaches her, established at Lauris's own
recruitment as one of three conditions negotiated without modification — that her relationship with
Aerelin and the network stays on terms she controls, not the movement's. In twenty decades of Era G, by
Sephtis's own account, Aerelin had used the channel sparingly, and never before for a request framed,
explicitly, as personal rather than operational.

The request itself: one of the roughly eighteen autonomous operatives Lauris's Operation 35 had once
helped expand the network to, working now for years under a cover identity within a Sovereign Trust
administrative office, had begun showing the specific, accumulating signs of a cover under strain —
small procedural errors, a pattern Aerelin's own analysis flagged as consistent with fatigue rather than
compromise, but not yet distinguishable, at this distance, from the earlier stage of an exposure neither
of them could afford to misjudge. Aerelin did not ask the Lords of Cian for an extraction team. She
asked Lauris, specifically, as the one person whose help would not require explaining to anyone else why
it was needed.

*

Lauris did not answer that evening, which I understand, from what Sephtis later told me of Aerelin's own
long acquaintance with her, to be itself unusual. She has told me, since, that the delay was not
hesitation about whether to help — she considers Aerelin's trust, built across roughly two centuries
now of a relationship that predates every other bond she holds on Cian except Sephtis's own, a debt she
takes as seriously as any operational obligation she carries. The delay was about the shape the help
should take, and about a tension she was honest with me in naming directly: that helping personally, as
Aerelin's terms require, means not bringing the matter to Ezio, to Kanja, or to anyone else in the Lords
of Cian's own command structure who might, with entirely good intentions, want to fold a fraying
operative's extraction into a larger, more resourced operation than a single autonomous asset was ever
meant to need.

"She did not ask me to keep it from them," Lauris told me, when I asked directly whether the secrecy
troubled her. "She asked me to handle it myself. Those are not quite the same request, though I
understand why they might look identical from where you are standing. I have kept things from this
crew before when keeping them was the price of a trust I valued more than the crew's completeness. I am
not certain, this time, that the price is the same."

*

What she has decided, as of this account, is to go — alone, on her own timeline, without briefing Ezio
or informing the wider crew of Aerelin's existence at all, exactly as the standing arrangement requires
— and to assess the operative's situation in person before deciding whether the extraction Aerelin fears
may soon be necessary is, in fact, necessary yet. She has not decided what she will do if it is. She has
not decided, either, whether a genuinely dangerous extraction would finally be the circumstance that
justifies breaking, for the first time in two centuries, the terms she herself negotiated and has never
once needed to test against a case this uncertain.

Her closing note in the archive she keeps of this exchange, brief even by her habitual standard: *Aerelin
asked me to hold this alone. I will hold it alone for now. I do not yet know whether alone will still be
the right size for it by the time I arrive.*

I do not know, setting this down, whether the operative's cover will hold, or whether Lauris will find,
on reaching the administrative office in question, a situation smaller or considerably larger than
Aerelin's own distant analysis suggested. I know only that a debt built, deliberately, on the terms
Lauris herself insisted on at the very start of her life on Cian is, for the first time I have recorded,
genuinely being tested by the weight of what it might now require her to carry entirely alone.
