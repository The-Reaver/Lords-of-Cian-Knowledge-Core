# What Garren Hask Refused to Write Down

*Locked canon, Batch 254, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1117`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXXXVII, wave 29, closing
wave 29. Direct payoff to "The Ones Who Stayed Behind to Lie" and companion piece to "The Numbers
Bane Left Behind" -- Garren Hask leaves the rear guard's cost out of the crew's own ledger entirely,
a deliberate first refusal.*

---

Garren Hask tallied everything. It was the thing the column had come to count on him for as much as
any ship he'd named or any counting he'd ever done at a dredge line — garrisons stood down, wells
rebuilt, rumors corrected, every number that told the honest shape of what the Bane era had actually
cost and actually bought, kept in his own running ledger since before most of the crew had a name for
what they were part of.

He didn't write down the three who'd stayed behind at the fire line.

Bane noticed the gap himself, going through the tally a week later, and asked him about it plainly —
not as an accusation, just the specific quiet he used when something didn't add up the way he
expected it to.

"You've got everyone else. You don't have them."

"No." Garren Hask didn't reach for the ledger to correct it. "I thought about it for two days before
I decided that on purpose. Everything else in that book gets a number because a number is how I keep
this crew honest about what it's actually doing out here — how many wells, how many freed, how many
lost, so nobody gets to tell themselves a smaller story than the true one. But those three didn't die
doing something I can reduce to a line in a column of figures next to a garrison count. They died
giving something nobody asked them to give, on their own terms, and if I put a number next to that,
it becomes a cost like any other cost in the book. It isn't. It's owed to the people who paid it, not
to the ledger, and writing it down the way I write everything else down would make it smaller than it
actually was."

"The ledger's the only record most of this will have."

"Then let the record be honest about what it can't hold instead of pretending it holds everything.
I'll tell anyone who asks exactly what happened and exactly who they were. I'm just not going to
shrink it down to fit next to a well count." Garren Hask closed the ledger. "Some numbers are owed to
the people who paid them. Not to a book."
