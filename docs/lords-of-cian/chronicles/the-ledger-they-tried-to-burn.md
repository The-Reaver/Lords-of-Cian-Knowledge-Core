# The Ledger They Tried to Burn

*Locked canon, Batch 119, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-404`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle IV, first entry
in the second wave. Rebellion era, unarmed throughout, matching `MCD-244`'s ethos. Not a territory
Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters.*

---

The district administrator who ordered the raid on the crew's recording tent understood, correctly,
that the actual threat was never the visiting crew's numbers — it was the ledger itself, the single
physical document accumulating four days of sworn testimony into something no denial could survive
once it reached the capital. Destroy the ledger, and the testimony became forty-eight hours of
unrecorded hearsay.

The soldiers found the tent, and the ledger sitting open on its table, and burned both before anyone
could stop them.

What they did not find, because Ezio had insisted on it from the first hour of the first day, were
the seven separate copies already dispersed among seven different, ordinary-looking workers scattered
across the district — each carrying an identical transcription, none of them looking like anything
worth searching, updated every evening precisely so that no single point of failure could erase four
days of sworn testimony.

"You burned a decoy," Kanja told the administrator, once the raid's aftermath had settled and the
crew's actual ledger — reassembled from all seven copies, cross-checked against each other for
consistency — was back in Ezio's hands. "Not deliberately. You just burned the one you happened to
find, and there were six more you never looked for, because you assumed a threat this dangerous had
to have an obvious, singular form."

"That's not how proof is supposed to work," the administrator said, still visibly shaken by how
completely the raid had failed to achieve anything. "One document. One record. You keep one copy of
something this serious, and you protect it. That's the whole discipline of record-keeping."

"That's the discipline of an institution that's never had to survive someone trying to burn its
records." Ezio held up the reassembled ledger, its testimony intact down to the page. "We didn't
invent anything clever. We just assumed, correctly, that you'd eventually try exactly what you tried,
and built the method around surviving it before you ever got the chance."

The administrator's own superiors, informed weeks later that the raid he'd personally ordered had
not only failed but had generated its own documented incident — one more entry in a ledger that now
included an account of an armed raid against unarmed testimony-gatherers — ended his career over the
decision faster than the original wage-theft exposure ever would have.
