# The Grandson Who Came to Warn Him

*Locked canon, Batch 281, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1474`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle XCIX, wave 33, closing
the wave. Age 230, V3 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Sixty years is a long time for a warning to travel down a family, and longer still for it to arrive
sincere rather than merely convenient. The young man who found Efa Gol's contact on a trading dock and
asked, carefully, to pass word to the Scourge himself, was the grandson of a slaver who'd converted his
whole operation to legitimate trade under collapsing economics decades before the young man was even
born — a story he'd grown up hearing not as shame kept quiet but as the reason his family had anything
honest left to hand down at all.

What he'd come to say was this: two of his grandfather's former rivals, men who'd never converted and
never forgiven the ones who had, were planning something against the crew directly — a false rendezvous
signal, borrowed from a captured decoy code years out of date, meant to draw a raiding party into a
prepared ambush at a cove the grandson knew by name because his own family had once used it for
entirely different cargo. He'd come three days by cart and boat to say it in person rather than trust
it to any message that could be intercepted or doubted.

Kanja heard him out without confirming anything about who he was speaking to, the way the reputation
always demanded, and asked only one question in return: why come at all, when nothing obligated him
to. The young man's answer was plain enough that it needed no persona to receive it. His family had
been given a chance to be something other than what they'd been, once, by a man who never came back to
check whether they'd taken it. Passing on a warning cost him nothing next to that.

The ambush, when the crew quietly avoided it three weeks later, never became a fight at all — the
rendezvous simply wasn't kept, and the two rivals waited in an empty cove for a raid that had already
rerouted around them. No one but Efa Gol's people ever knew precisely why. Garren Hask's ledger logged
it plainly: a debt paid forward, sixty years and one generation removed from the man who'd first
incurred it, closed by someone who'd never met either side of it directly.
