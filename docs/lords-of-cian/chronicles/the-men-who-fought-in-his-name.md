# The Men Who Fought in His Name

*Locked canon, Batch 255, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1134`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle LXXVII, wave 26.
Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. A group forms in his name and escalates toward violence he never
sanctioned.*

---

They called themselves the Dredge Line's Answer, a dozen men and women from a neighboring district
who had watched the Trench Monarch's reputation grow from a distance and decided, without ever
approaching Warehouse Twelve to ask, that they understood exactly what it authorized them to do. Where
Kanja's crew exposed theft through documentation and forced restitution through proof, the Answer
began with the same grievances — real, verifiable wage theft, the kind Hask's method would have
caught cleanly given time — and skipped straight to burning a granary storehouse belonging to the
worst offender, in the name of the Trench Monarch, before anyone had checked a single figure.

Nobody died in the fire. That was, Kanja understood when the news reached him, closer to luck than to
restraint on the Answer's part.

"They think they're doing what you do," Hask said, laying out the account of it. "Faster. Without the
paperwork."

"They're not doing what I do. They're doing what's easy to imagine I do, from far enough away that
the actual work never showed." Kanja read the account twice, the same granular attention he gave a
disputed ledger. "The name isn't the method. The name is what people remember after the method
already worked. If they start burning storehouses under it, the name stops meaning anything except
fear, and fear doesn't build anything that lasts."

He went to the neighboring district himself, without Onyx drawn, and found the Answer's twelve
members considerably less certain of themselves in his actual presence than their chosen name had
suggested from a distance. He didn't threaten them. He laid out, plainly, everything the tally method
had achieved specifically because it never burned anything down first — every case closed on proof
rather than fear, every owner who'd paid because the figures left him no honest argument rather than
because he'd been terrorized into it.

"You want my name to mean something," he told them. "It already does, because of the part you skipped
— the part where nobody can say we lied, or stole, or burned something that didn't need burning. You
want to help. Do the part that's actually hard. Check the figures. Prove the theft. Make it so
airtight the owner can't argue his way out of it."

Not all twelve accepted the correction. Four left the district entirely rather than trade the
satisfaction of the granary fire for the slower discipline of documentation. The remaining eight spent
the following weeks under Hask's own reluctant tutelage, learning the method properly, the storehouse
fire's owner receiving, eventually, a legitimate restitution figure alongside a public acknowledgment
that the fire itself had never been sanctioned by anyone it claimed to honor.
