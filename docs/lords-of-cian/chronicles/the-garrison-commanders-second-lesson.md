# The Garrison Commander's Second Lesson

*Locked canon, Batch 262, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1325`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LXXIX, first
entry in the twenty-seventh wave. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241),
months after "What the Enemy Learned to Rebuild" (MCD-922). A rematch entry with a recurring
Directorate antagonist. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Nineteen days, the garrison commander had managed once, rebuilding his own outpost using methods
lifted from a tour he'd studied rather than fought — a real feat, by any honest measure, and one
that had bought him a promotion and a second, larger garrison to prove it wasn't a fluke.

It took him fourteen days the second time, and he made a point of sending word ahead of the
campaign's own arrival to say so, an open challenge rather than a boast: come see for yourself
whether the lesson held twice.

Kanja came, because the commander had asked in good faith and answering that kind of request
mattered more than the schedule cost. Fourteen days was real. The rebuilt garrison stood solid,
its workforce organized along lines that genuinely mirrored the open method's own logic — shared
tools, cross-trained crews, apprentices learning alongside veterans rather than beneath them.

"You didn't just copy the speed," Kanja said, walking the finished structure with something close
to respect in it. "You copied why it works. Most people who steal the method stop at the parts
they can see."

"I had fourteen days to think about the parts I couldn't see the first time," the commander said.
"Nineteen days taught me the mechanics. This time I wanted to understand the principle underneath
them." He hesitated, then said the rest of it plainly, the way a man says something he's been
rehearsing. "I still serve the Trust. I'm not renouncing that. But I don't think what I took from
your method and what I serve are as compatible as I once assumed they were, and I don't have an
answer yet for what I do about that."

"I'm not going to pretend that's a small thing to sit with," Kanja said. "Most people who get that
far don't say it out loud to the person who caused it."

He left the commander with the same offer he'd left him with the first time — the method itself,
freely given, no strings on how it got used — and no promise about what came of a man clearly
working through a loyalty the campaign hadn't set out to unsettle in the first place. Whatever the
commander decided, it wasn't Kanja's decision to make for him.
