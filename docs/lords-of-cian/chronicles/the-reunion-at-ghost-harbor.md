# The Reunion at Ghost Harbor

*Locked canon, Batch 258, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1225`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
LXXXVII, wave 29, closing the wave. An unorganized, large-scale reunion of people the fleet has
rescued across its history. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Nobody organized it, which was the part Garren Hask kept returning to afterward as he tried to log it
properly — no summons went out, no single announcement, just word passed slowly over months between
people who'd once been strangers to each other and shared nothing but the fact that this fleet had, at
some point across years of scattered encounters, changed what happened to them.

They gathered at Ghost Harbor itself, the site of the battle that had given the fleet its
alias-defining moment, because it was the one place enough of them already knew by name and
reputation to agree on. The freed captives from the slaver's manifest. The fishing family whose
daughter had waited for black sails. The reformed captain, cleared of a false accusation, bringing his
own wife. The town that had built its own watchtower and breakwater. Survivors of the wreck pulled
from a sunken hold. Dozens more, then hundreds, arriving over three days by whatever transport they
could arrange, until the harbor held more small boats than the fleet's own three flagships combined.

Kanja hadn't expected it and hadn't asked for it, and said as much plainly to the crowd once it became
clear no amount of quiet departure was going to spare him the occasion. "I don't know most of your
names," he admitted. "Hask does — every one of them's in his ledger somewhere. I just did what the
doctrine asked of me each time, one encounter at a time. I never once expected it would add up to
this."

"That's exactly why we came," the girl who'd once waited for black sails said — grown now, no longer
waiting for anything. "You didn't do it for the credit. Doesn't mean we don't get to give it anyway."

There was no battle to fight, no rescue to perform, nothing for the Trinity or the doctrine to prove
itself against. Just three days of a harbor full of people the fleet had touched in passing, each with
their own small piece of the larger record Garren Hask had spent years keeping — and for once, he
closed the entry not with facts alone, but with a count: four hundred and eleven names present, by his
own careful tally, every one of them free because of something this crew had once chosen to do rather
than not do.
