# The Season They Ran It Without Him

*Locked canon, Batch 263, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1362`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LXXXIX, wave 30. Kanja is away on other business for a full storm season; the school's now fully self-organizing institution handles every call, rescue, and evacuation of the season without him present at all -- the first time in the sub-series' entire history.*

---

It wasn't planned as a test, though it ended up functioning as one. Kanja's other business kept him away from the coast entirely for the better part of a storm season -- the longest stretch, by the ledger's own accounting, he had ever gone without setting foot on the harbor wall since Sephtis first began training a successor decades earlier.

The school didn't slow down in his absence. It didn't, by any measure the returning record could find, do anything differently at all.

Three fleet-scale calls went out that season, all correct. A merchant convoy was rerouted twice on the dual-tradition sailor's own cross-checked reading, saving both ships from weather neither fleet's older instinct alone would have caught in time. A minor coastal evacuation -- nothing at Ghost Harbor's scale, but real lives moved from a threatened fishing hamlet all the same -- ran entirely under the fourth-generation apprentice's own direction, his first true solo command of an evacuation rather than a reading. The council that had begun meeting without being summoned convened four times that season, twice for ordinary comparison and twice for genuine disputes, resolving each one through the same disagreement protocol the successor and Sephtis had first established decades earlier.

Not one entry in the season's ledger mentioned Kanja at all. Not as a protector. Not as an unnamed guest. Not as a witness to anything. For the first time in this alias's own recorded history, an entire season had simply happened without him.

The student found this fact almost impossible to explain when he finally returned, not because it needed defending, but because none of them had thought to remark on his absence while it was happening. "We didn't miss you," she said, and then, seeing how that landed, added, "not because it didn't matter that you were gone. Because there was nothing in the season that needed you specifically to happen the way it needed to happen."

Kanja read through the season's own record slowly, every call correct, every rescue clean, every dispute resolved through a protocol built before most of the people who'd used it that season had even joined the school. He felt something he hadn't quite expected to feel, reading it -- not displacement, not the particular ache of being unneeded that some of his other aliases might have made of the same fact.

"This is the whole point," he said finally. "I think I've understood it in pieces for a long time and never seen it laid out whole like this. Every other alias I wear exists because I'm the one who has to be there. This one was always building toward the opposite."

"You're still welcome," the student said. "You've never once been in the way."

"I know," he said. "That's not the same as being needed. I think this might be the first alias I've worn where those two things finally came apart clean."
