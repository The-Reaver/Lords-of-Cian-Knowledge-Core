# The Crown That Wasn't His

*Locked canon, Batch 201, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-834`). The Crow King Alias Chronicle XIX, wave 7 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The first village Kanja heard about had lost its grain stores to armed men wearing crude
black-feather crowns and invoking his name before they burned the granary anyway, a detail that
made no sense against a method built entirely around avoiding violence — and by the time the third
village's account reached him, carrying the same crown, the same invoked name, and the same senseless
cruelty, it was clear the problem wasn't a misunderstanding.

Someone had taken the symbol and was wearing it to rob people.

"They're not even pretending to fight the Directorate," the crew's own scout reported, having
traced the pattern to a small bandit company operating two provinces from anywhere Kanja's people
had ever worked. "They're using your name because it buys them a night's head start before anyone
thinks to chase. Villages are too frightened of the real Crow King to report it quickly."

"Then we make sure the name stops buying them anything." Kanja didn't send a warning or a
proclamation — words alone would have meant nothing against a crown already proven to terrify on
sight. Instead, three nights later, the bandit company's own leader woke to find every one of his
company's stolen crowns removed from their sleeping quarters overnight and left, without a mark of
violence anywhere, arranged in a neat row outside the camp's own gate, alongside a full and exact
accounting of everything they'd stolen and from whom.

"No one was touched," the bandit leader would tell the next village he tried to threaten, visibly
unsettled in a way armed resistance had never managed to leave him. "He just took the crowns back
like they were never ours to wear, and left a debt we now have to pay every family on that list, or
he'll be back for more than crowns."

The company disbanded within the month, paying restitution under threat of nothing but the
certainty that whoever had reclaimed the crowns without a sound could reclaim considerably more.

"You didn't hunt them," the scout said afterward, still working out the method. "You didn't fight
them. You just took the thing they were stealing back."

"The crown was never really mine to lose," Kanja said. "It's a story people tell each other about
what happened at the Wetlands. I can't stop someone from wearing a story badly. I can make sure
wearing it badly costs more than it's worth."
