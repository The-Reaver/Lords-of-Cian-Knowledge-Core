# The Ambush Meant for the Hearing

*Locked canon, Batch 194, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-646`). The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XLI, wave 14 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Whoever had failed to convict him at Canal House had not given up on removing him entirely. The
attempt came three streets from the tribunal building, in the crowded evening market that separated
Canal House from the canal-side road home — a setting chosen, Kanja understood within the first
several seconds, specifically because a blade drawn there risked the market's own crowded stalls and
bystanders more than any prior ambush had accounted for.

Two came from the crowd itself, indistinguishable from market-goers until the moment they weren't.
Onyx met the first strike a half-beat before it should have been possible to react to at all, Cadence
Ruin collapsing a rhythm built to look like an accidental jostle right up until the knife came free —
but the parry, executed in a space this crowded, sent the deflected blade skittering within inches of
a fruit-seller's stall rather than into open ground.

"Move them back," Kanja said, not to his own crew but to the crowd itself, loud enough to carry over
the market's noise, buying himself room with words before the fight forced it with steel. Most
scattered. Some didn't move fast enough.

The second attacker used the hesitation, pressing in low, and Kanja found himself fighting not just
the blade in front of him but the crowded space itself — a duel that Onyx's gifts, built for reading
intention and finding seams, had to be applied with a restraint no open-ground fight had ever
required, every parry calculated as much for where the deflected strike would land as for whether it
landed at all.

Whisper of Shadows caught the third attacker, positioned in the crowd itself rather than approaching
openly, an ordinary bystander's stillness that had nothing ordinary behind it — the gift un-hiding
intention where eyes alone would have seen only another face in a busy market.

It ended in under two minutes, all three down, no bystander struck by blade or deflected blow, though
one elderly stall-keeper had to be helped up after being knocked aside in the scramble to clear space.

"That's the first time you've had to think about the fight and the crowd at the same time," Corren
Halst said afterward, once the market had begun settling back into its ordinary noise.

"It is. I don't want it to be the last time I think about both, even when there isn't a crowd this
size to force the question." Kanja checked on the stall-keeper himself before anything else, satisfied
the man was shaken but unhurt. "A duel in open ground only costs what the duel itself costs. A duel in
a market costs whatever gets caught in it that never agreed to be part of the fight at all. I'd rather
carry that calculation into every fight from now on, not just the ones crowded enough to force it."
