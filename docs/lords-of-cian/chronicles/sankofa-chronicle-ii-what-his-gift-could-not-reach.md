# Sankofa Chronicle II: What His Gift Could Not Reach

*DRAFT -- pending Abad's approval, not yet locked canon. Batch 97 candidate (`MCD-360`
candidate). Second entry in Sankofa's own Chronicles, its protagonist Baálé (`PH2-021`), not a
Kanja Chronicle. Kanja appears only as a guest, present but granted no command, no intervention, and
no resolution credit, matching the established convention. Narration is close-third on Baálé
throughout, not Onyx of Oblivion. Directly dramatizes `PH2-021`'s own stated vulnerability, left as
live, unresolved tension at the close of Sankofa Chronicle I (`MCD-346`): "a faceless, coordinated
threat... is exactly what his gift cannot reach." Brings back Kojo, left open as a minor recurring
figure by Chronicle I. Deliberately does not restage the already-locked COINTELPRO backstory event
itself, matching that event's own backstory-only status, and deliberately does not resolve who is
behind the new threat -- consistent with `PH2-021`'s own framing of a conspiracy that "never shows
its face."*

---

The letter reached Kojo three months after he'd sworn himself to Baálé, and it was written well
enough that a lesser man than Kojo would have believed every word of it before he'd finished the
second paragraph.

It claimed to carry Baálé's own hand, though it did not, and it said, in words shaped carefully
enough to sound like the man who might have written them, that the old western-block crew Kojo had
once run with — men who had fought at his side for years before that one bad afternoon in the
street — were marked now, quietly, to be dealt with the way Baálé dealt with anyone who'd once
raised a hand against him and hadn't yet proven himself worth keeping. It asked nothing of Kojo. It
simply told him, plainly, what was coming, and let the rest do its own work.

Kojo did not sleep that night. He read the letter eleven times, by his own later count, weighing
every phrase in it against three months of a loyalty he had not fully understood the shape of until
he found himself, near dawn, standing outside Baálé's own door with the letter still in his hand,
having made a choice he would only later understand was the entire point of the exercise, whichever
way he'd gone.

"Someone wants me to believe you'd do this," Kojo said, when Baálé opened the door himself. "I
almost did. I want you to know that plainly, because I think it matters more than pretending I
never doubted it at all."

Baálé took the letter and read it once, and something in his face did a thing the stranger — who
had been in the doorway of the house across the narrow street for most of that night, unremarked,
the way he tended to be — recognized without needing to be told what it was. Not surprise. Dread,
old and specific, arriving exactly on schedule.

"I know this hand," Baálé said, quietly. "Not the hand that wrote it. The hand behind the hand.
I've seen letters shaped exactly this way before, aimed at exactly this kind of gap, timed to land
on exactly the night a man's most likely to believe the worst thing anyone's ever told him about the
one person he's chosen to trust completely." He set the letter down on the table between them,
careful not to crumple it, the way a man handles something he intends to keep as evidence rather
than burn out of anger. "It's the same shape as the letters that nearly got me and Kra killed before
either of you were old enough to know my name. I never learned who held the pen that time either."

"You're certain it wasn't you."

"I'm certain. But certainty from me isn't the thing that matters here, and I think you already know
that, or you wouldn't be standing at my door at this hour with the letter still in your hand instead
of already gone." Baálé looked at him a long moment. "You could have believed it. You had every
reason to — I gave you nothing three months ago but a fight you survived and a loyalty you've never
had cause to fully test since. This is the first real test of it, and you passed it by walking here
instead of walking away, which is a thing I couldn't have made you do even if I'd stood in front of
you and asked."

"That's the part that frightens me most about it, if I'm honest." Kojo looked at the letter between
them. "You beat me once, face to face, and something in me answered that the way it answers
anything I've ever fully respected. But whoever wrote this didn't need to beat me at all. They just
needed a piece of paper and enough patience to wait for the right night."

"That's exactly the shape of it. That's exactly what beat me the first time, before it ever came to
a fight at all — the fight was almost the mercy, at the end of it, because a fight was a thing my
gift could actually meet." Baálé folded the letter once, carefully, and set it aside rather than
destroying it. "I can turn any man who raises a hand against me into someone bound to serve me for
the rest of his life. I have never once, not in all the years I've carried this thing, found a way
to turn a piece of paper into anything at all. Whoever's behind this knows that better than I do.
They've known it longer than I have."

The stranger crossed the narrow street only once the door had begun to close, and only to leave, on
the step, a second letter — one Kojo did not recognize, addressed to no one, unsigned, found
discarded three streets over that same night by a hand that had simply been walking, the way it
tended to, and had thought it worth someone's while to see.

Baálé read it after Kojo had gone. It named no one. It answered nothing. It was, as near as he could
tell, only ever meant to be found, a second thread from the same hand, left somewhere it was certain
to be noticed by someone who would bring it straight to his door — proof, if he'd needed any, that
whoever was behind this was not finished, and did not particularly care whether he knew they weren't.

"You could have told me who left this," Baálé said, once the stranger was inside.

"I don't know who left it. I only found it." The stranger looked at the second letter without
touching it again. "You didn't need me to tell you what it means. You already know the shape of it
better than anyone left alive."

Baálé did not sleep that night either. He sat with both letters on the table in front of him, a
threat with no face and no hand he could confront it with, and understood, the way he had understood
it once before at a much higher cost, that some things a man survives are never actually defeated —
only outlasted, one letter, one night, one man who chooses to knock instead of run, at a time.

*Continuity notes (not narrative): Baálé is the protagonist and viewpoint character throughout;
Kanja is the unnamed guest, present across the narrow street for most of the night and the one who
finds and delivers the second letter, without taking command, credit, or resolution authorship --
he explicitly does not identify or investigate the letters' author, and the threat itself is
deliberately left unresolved and unidentified at the close. This directly dramatizes `PH2-021`'s own
stated vulnerability for the first time: "The Turn" requires a direct, face-to-face attack to
convert, and is stated to be explicitly useless against "a conspiracy that never shows its face" --
this Chronicle shows exactly that gap in operation, with the threat resolved (for this one instance)
not by Baálé's ability at all but by Kojo's own choice to bring the forged letter directly to Baálé
rather than act on it alone, an ordinary act of trust standing in for the ability's own missing
reach. Kojo (introduced in Sankofa Chronicle I, `MCD-346`, left open as a minor recurring figure) is
brought back as the target of the new threat, given real interiority and doubt rather than being
reduced to a simple loyalty test. Deliberately does not restage the already-locked COINTELPRO
backstory event itself, which per `PH2-021` remains backstory-only; the new letters are a distinct,
newly invented instance of the same shape of threat, not the same historical event. Deliberately
leaves the letters' true author unidentified and the threat unresolved, consistent with `PH2-021`'s
own framing and with the pattern established across other Chronicles of leaving genuine hooks open
rather than closing every thread. No new named characters are introduced; no new proper nouns
requiring a collision check. This Chronicle slots into no existing MCD- rule -- original homage-era
material set in Sankofa itself, and is the sixth territory (after Xaragua, Areíto, Guanín, Uhuru,
and Aztlán) to receive a second Chronicle entry.*
