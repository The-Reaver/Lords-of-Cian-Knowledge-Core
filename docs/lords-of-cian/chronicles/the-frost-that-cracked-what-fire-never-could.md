# The Frost That Cracked What Fire Never Could

*Locked canon, Batch 279, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1451`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XCIV, wave 32, opening the wave. The alias's first winter/frost-heave hazard, distinct from every prior heat-, water-, and vapor-based crisis register.*

---

The cracking started as a sound Corren Halst almost mistook for gunfire -- a sharp, percussive snap somewhere in the rock overhead, followed by a second, then a third, spaced out like something counting.

"That's not a strike," she said, already moving crews back from the gallery ceiling. "That's timber."

Kanja pressed his palm to the nearest shoring post and felt it -- not the slow give of rot or the sudden lurch of overload, but a rhythm, tight and mean, wood fibers seizing as the hard frost above ground worked its way down through fissures that had stayed dry all autumn. Water in the stone had frozen, expanded, and was now thawing in the brief warmth of midday only to freeze again by nightfall, and every cycle was driving a wedge a little deeper into wood that had held through combat, through flooding, through fire, and had never once been built to survive being frozen and thawed forty times in a season.

"Fire we can fight," he said. "Water we can move. This is neither. This is the ground itself changing its mind twice a day."

He worked the length of the gallery with both hands flat against post after post, and it took him longer than any hazard he'd read before to understand what he was actually feeling -- not a single failure point but a whole network of small, patient stresses building in timber that looked, to the eye, exactly as sound as it had a month before. Mafesto's kinetic transfer had never been asked to track something this slow. He had to still his own breathing and hold his palms in place through three full freeze-thaw cycles, mapping which posts groaned and which held silent, before he could tell Corren Halst with any confidence which twelve needed replacing before the week was out and which forty could wait.

"You're reading the whole winter," she said, watching him work. "Not the crack. The season."

"The crack's just where the season finally said something out loud." He marked the twelve posts with chalk, then walked the rest of the gallery a second time, checking his own read against itself before he trusted it enough to pull crews off other work to act on it. "Fire tells you it's coming. Frost doesn't say anything until it's already said everything."

They replaced the twelve posts over two bitter nights, packing the new joints with a mortar mix Kanja adjusted twice to account for the coming freeze rather than the one already past, and by the time the following week's cold snap hit, the gallery held without a sound.

Corren Halst found him afterward, breath fogging in the tunnel mouth, looking back at the work with the particular satisfaction of a problem that had never needed a fight at all.

"You didn't swing anything the whole week," she said.

"Some of the worst things down here never give you anything to swing at." Kanja flexed fingers still stiff from three nights of held-still listening. "This one just wanted someone patient enough to notice it happening slow instead of waiting for it to happen fast."
