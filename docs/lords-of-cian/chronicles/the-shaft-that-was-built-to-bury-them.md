# The Shaft That Was Built to Bury Them

*Locked canon, Batch 283, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1491`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XCVIII, wave 33. A detailed, battle-intense full-Trinity combat showcase in a confined underground mine shaft, the doctrine's first application in that environment. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The charge that brought down the mine's only entrance wasn't meant to kill the eleven workers and three soldiers trapped past it — not quickly, anyway. It was meant to leave them exactly where they were, in the dark, with air enough for perhaps two days and rescue enough for none, while whoever had planted it walked away having spent almost nothing to win a fight without ever showing a blade. Kanja reached the collapsed entrance within the hour, and the doctrine had never once been asked to work in a space this narrow, this dark, or this unwilling to let a sound travel further than an arm's length before the rock swallowed it.

"There's still someone down there," he said, one hand already flat against the rubble, filtering the shaft's own groaning settle from anything that might be a living voice underneath it. "And there's still someone up here who didn't leave when the job was done."

The ambush came from a side tunnel the collapse hadn't sealed, three men who'd stayed behind to make certain no one dug the entrance open again. Onyx's Whisper of Shadows put Kanja behind the first before he'd finished raising his own light, Cadence Ruin's rhythm-break took the second off his feet mid-swing in a passage too tight for a full stance, and Soulbound Edge finished what needed finishing without a wasted motion in a space where a wasted motion could bring the whole side tunnel down on both of them. Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System absorbed a swung pick meant for Kanja's skull and fed the impact straight back into the tunnel wall rather than his own frame, cracking rock instead of bone.

The harder work started after. Every remaining support timber down that shaft had shifted from the original collapse, some sound, some a single vibration away from bringing the rest down on the trapped workers' heads, and there was no clean read from a distance in a space this confined — every signal bounced, doubled, lied by echo alone. Kanja worked it timber by timber, hand to wood, doubled verification confirmed twice on every single one before he trusted it, Obsidian Malice's discharge used only twice in the entire descent, each time to relieve a specific overstrained beam rather than clear a path by force. It took most of the night. Every worker came out breathing.

"You could have gone faster," the mine foreman said afterward, hollow-voiced with relief. "Torn straight through."

"Faster gets people out. It also gets people crushed, in a shaft that's already decided it wants to come down. I wasn't going to bet eleven lives on being in a hurry."
