# The Girl Who Grew Up Visiting the Ship

*Locked canon, Batch 222, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1002`). Captain Alias Chronicle LV, wave 19. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

She was six the first time she met him, on a low stool in her mother's one-room house, asking
whether he was afraid of the dark. She was eleven the next time the ship's repair schedule lined up
with a visit, tall enough now to look less impressed by his height and more interested in the plating
scars along Mafesto's housing, which she asked to touch and was, after a pause, allowed to.

By thirteen she'd started coming to the docks herself instead of waiting for her father to bring her,
walking the two hours alone over her mother's mild objection, and the crew had stopped treating it as
unusual. She had a favorite spot on the aft rail where she did her lessons while the hull crews
worked, and a running list of questions for Kanja that had shifted, gradually, from whether he'd ever
cried to what it actually felt like to hold a burning building's worth of weight without being
crushed by it, to whether he thought she was too young to learn to splice line the way the old rigger
used to teach.

"You're not too young," he told her, the year she turned fourteen. "You're the right age to decide
you want to know something and go find someone who'll teach you properly instead of waiting to be
handed it."

"Is that what you did?"

"More or less. Mostly I didn't have a choice about the timing."

She thought about that for a while, coiling a practice line the way Corren Halst had shown her that
morning. "My father says you're different around me than around the crew. Softer."

"Your father notices more than he lets on."

"Is it because I'm a child?"

"No," he said, and meant it plainly enough that she believed him. "It's because you've never once
needed anything from me except honest answers. Everyone else on this ship needs something from me
eventually — a decision, an order, a rescue. You just show up with questions. That's a rarer thing
than you'd think, and I don't take it for granted."

At sixteen, she asked to sign on properly, the way any recruit her age might, and Kanja gave her the
same answer he gave everyone: that trust wasn't handed out for having grown up nearby, that she'd
start exactly where any stranger started and prove herself the same slow way. She took the answer
without complaint — she'd have been disappointed, she admitted later, if he'd given her anything
easier.

Danne Sok watched all of it happen across a full decade without ever quite commenting on it, until the
day she formally signed the crew ledger under her own name instead of his. "You could have made it
easier for her," he said. "Nobody would have blamed you."

"She wouldn't have wanted it easier. Neither would you, if you're honest."

Danne Sok didn't argue the point, because he knew it was true, and because watching her earn the same
slow trust he had, rather than inherit it, was its own kind of proof that nothing about this crew had
changed just because he had.
