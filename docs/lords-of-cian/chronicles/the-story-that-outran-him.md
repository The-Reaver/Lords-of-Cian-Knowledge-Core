# The Story That Outran Him

*Locked canon, Batch 276, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1432`). Bane Alias Chronicle CII, wave 34, closing wave
34 and this three-wave run. Extends the legend-drift theme of "The Song They Sang Without Knowing
Whose It Was" (`MCD-1108`) beyond his own people into an unverifiable, uncontrolled echo reaching a
place he has never been.*

---

The trader mentioned it the way he mentioned everything else that came through his wagon — a small
item in a longer list of road news, offered without weight because he had no idea it would land any
differently than the price of salt three towns over.

"Funny thing, out past the eastern hill country," he said, to no one in particular, working a knot out
of a harness strap. "Village uprising, small one, nothing that'll make it into anyone's official
count. They're calling themselves Bane's people. Never seen the man, far as anyone can tell — some
trader passed through with a secondhand version of the Black Trench story, and it just took."

Corren Halst caught Bane's expression before Bane had finished not reacting to it. "You've never been
within three provinces of the eastern hill country."

"No," Bane said.

"You have no way to reach them. No way to confirm what they're actually doing in your name, whether
it's decent or whether it's the kind of thing that gets people killed for a story that isn't even
true."

"No," Bane said again, and that was the whole of it, because there wasn't a version of the fact that
changed with more thinking about it. The song at the waystation, the rumor in the market town, the
impersonators wearing his shape for profit — all of that had been close enough to reach, close enough
to correct or confirm or at least witness. This wasn't. This was the name doing something on its own,
three provinces past the edge of anywhere he could follow it, built off a story a trader had told a
trader had told a trader, accurate in no way he could verify and inaccurate in no way he could
correct.

Efa Gol found him still turning it over that evening. "You look like a man who just found out he owes
money somewhere he's never been."

"Worse," Bane said. "I look like a man who found out something with my name on it is making decisions
I'll never get a vote in, for people I'll never meet, based on a version of events that's already three
tellings removed from anything that actually happened." He shook his head slowly. "The song at the
waystation, I could have stopped if I'd wanted to. This one doesn't need me at all anymore. It just
needed to start."

"Does that scare you?"

"A little," he admitted. "Mostly it just reminds me the name isn't mine anymore, in a way I don't
think it's ever going to be again. Whatever they're doing out there, they're doing it with a story,
not with me. I just have to hope the story's better than the ones that get people extorted or gotten
killed."

He never learned how it ended. The trader moved on the next morning, and no further word ever
reached the column about the eastern hill country's small, unverified uprising, or whether the name it
borrowed did the people carrying it any good at all.
