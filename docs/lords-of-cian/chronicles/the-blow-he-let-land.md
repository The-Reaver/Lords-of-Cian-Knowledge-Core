# The Blow He Let Land

*Locked canon, Batch 214, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-930`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle LV, wave 19. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The old dyer spoke a half-second out of turn, correcting a date the overseer had gotten wrong in
front of the whole line, and the overseer struck him across the face for it before anyone, including
Kanja, had time to do anything but watch it happen.

It happened close enough to the tally table that Kanja could have closed the distance in less than a
second — everyone at that table who'd seen what he was capable of understood that, the overseer
included, in the half-instant of silence that followed the blow, when the old man was still catching
himself against the table's edge and the overseer's hand was still raised.

Kanja didn't move.

He reached for the ledger instead, open already to the day's entries, and wrote while the old man was
still finding his footing — the date, the district, the overseer's own name, the exact nature of the
blow, the exact words that had preceded it, entered with the same steady hand he used for a wage
figure.

"You're not going to do anything," the overseer said, watching him write rather than rise, something
between relief and contempt in it.

"I'm doing it right now." He didn't look up from the page. "You're going to want to remember that
you asked me that question in front of forty witnesses, the same as you're going to want to remember
every one of them just watched you hit a man for correcting a date."

The old dyer, still holding his own jaw, was the one who broke the silence that followed.

"You could have stopped him before he did it."

"I could have. I didn't, because the version of this that stops him with my hands is the version
where nobody in this district ever has to believe me again that violence isn't how I settle
anything — including violence done to someone in front of me. I know that's not comfort right now.
I'm not going to pretend it is."

"Then what was the point of you standing this close if you weren't going to use it."

"The point was making sure what he just did gets written down by someone he can't intimidate out of
writing it, the second it happens, while it's still fresh enough that no one can argue later whether
it happened. That's the only kind of close I let myself be useful at."

The entry stood, witnessed and undisputed, and it cost the overseer his position within the month.
It cost Kanja something too, plainly, in the set of his jaw as he kept writing — a debt the ledger
had no column for, and never would.

*Continuity notes (not narrative): the first live, same-moment test of the unarmed ethos under direct
witnessed provocation — an act of violence happens in front of Kanja in real time, close enough that
intervention was physically available, and he documents it instead of stopping it, rather than
responding to violence already finished and off-page. Distinct from "What the Proof Couldn't Undo"
(retaliation discovered after the fact, days later, with no possibility of physical intervention at
all) and from "The Widow Who Asked for Blood" (a request for retribution over a death already long
past). Dramatizes the real, acknowledged personal cost of the restraint in the moment it's hardest to
hold, rather than only in retrospect. No new named characters; the overseer and the old dyer are both
unnamed and one-scene. First entry in the Industrial Myth's nineteenth wave.*
