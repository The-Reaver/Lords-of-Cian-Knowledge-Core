# Lauris Chronicle XIX: The One She Asked For

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1636`). Nineteenth entry in Lauris Letitia's own Chronicle
series, the sixth entry of Strand K (Kares Prime / deep past). Dramatizes the first of the four
training engagements Lauris requested herself during the Long Operational Period (`MCD-1555`), serving
as primary opponent for a promising combatant from another Vask as she began to feel responsibility
for developing the next generation's capability. Introduces one new minor named character, Serath, a
young combatant from Vask Olmedrin (already-locked location, MCD-173) -- collision-checked clean
against the full live ledger (zero prior hits for "Serath"). Reuses Veska Karth-Ven, the already-locked
instructor quoted at MCD-1558, as Karth-Ven's senior training authority. Full text below.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Vask Karth-Ven, age ~2,900: "Veska asked whether I wanted a student. I said I did
not know how to want one, only how to be asked for by one. She said that would do. I did not
understand the distinction until some years later. I record it now because I think I finally do."*

The Olmedrin defense was some hundred years behind her by the time this account begins, and the
reputation it had earned her -- eight hours alone against four hundred raiders, the highest
single-engagement density any Karesian has ever documented -- had, by her own wry note, produced an
unintended consequence she had not anticipated when she declined Olmedrin's formal acknowledgment at
the time. Young combatants from across the surviving Vasks had begun asking, through their own
Iron-Speakers, whether the woman who had held Olmedrin alone might one day take a student.

She had refused, quietly, for nearly a century. Veska Karth-Ven -- the same instructor who had once
observed, watching a twenty-year-old Lauris train without visible fatigue, that the child would
continue indefinitely if not stopped -- was the one who finally asked the question in a form Lauris
could not deflect: not whether she wanted a student, but whether she had noticed that Karth-Ven's own
training floor, calibrated for twenty-eight thousand years to read Karesian combat capability, had
begun producing readings for one particular candidate that neither Veska nor anyone else at Karth-Ven
knew how to interpret.

*

Serath arrived from Olmedrin at seventeen, already carrying, by Karth-Ven's own assessment, a
density reading well outside cohort norms for her age -- not approaching Lauris's own, which no one
on Kares Prime approached, but sufficiently unusual that Olmedrin's own instructors had exhausted
what they knew how to teach her. Lauris's archive entry describes their first session in terms I
recognize, having watched her assess opponents on Cian: not as an evaluation of raw capability, which
she notes plainly was already evident, but as an attempt to determine whether Serath understood her
own body's signals or was simply, as most young combatants are, pushing through them without reading
them.

She was not, by Lauris's account, impressed by the first session, though she is careful to record
this without cruelty. Serath fought at full effort from the opening exchange, exhausted herself
within eleven minutes, and lost badly to an opponent who had not yet raised her own density past a
fraction of its ceiling. Lauris ended the session there, deliberately, rather than press the
advantage further, and told her -- the line preserved in the archive exactly -- *You fought as though
the fight would be short. I have never once fought as though a fight would be short, because I have
never known one to stay that way if I did not let it. Come back tomorrow having thought about why
that might matter.*

*

What follows, across the some six years Serath trained under her at Karth-Ven, is the closest thing
I have found in the Kares Prime archive to a sustained record of Lauris actually building the
discipline Tiramen had once built for her alone -- adapting, in real time and without any curriculum
to draw from beyond the Karth-Sera's own three founding principles, a version of Continuous
Engagement and Density-Progressive Combat scaled to a student who would never reach Lauris's own
ceiling but who could, Lauris judged, reach one considerably past anything Olmedrin's own instructors
had believed possible. She trained Serath not to front-load her strength, as her instinct had first
demanded, but to read her own rising capacity across a fight's duration and to trust that duration
rather than fear it -- the same foundational logic, Lauris notes explicitly in one entry, that Tiramen
had built the Karth-Sera around for her, applied now, for the first time, by her own hand to someone
else's body rather than her own.

She records, at the close of Serath's second year, a single line that I have returned to often since
first transcribing it: *I do not know if I am teaching her to survive what I survived, or simply
teaching her because I was, myself, once someone's account to preserve, and I find I understand the
impulse to preserve one in return better than I expected to.*

I do not believe, reading this entry against everything else I know of her, that she had fully
recognized yet what this responsibility would eventually become to her -- a pattern she would return
to, deliberately, three more times across the remainder of the Long Operational Period, each time
seeking out a Vask's most promising and most overlooked combatant rather than waiting to be asked.
But I think the recognition was already, at this early point, closer than she knew.
