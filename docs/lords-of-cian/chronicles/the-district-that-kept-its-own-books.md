# The District That Kept Its Own Books

*Locked canon, Batch 204, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-896`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XLVIII, wave 16. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The quarry district asked a harder question than most, on the crew's last scheduled day there, and
it came from a woman who'd spent three weeks watching Ezio work rather than lining up to testify
herself.

"When you leave tomorrow, does the book leave with you?"

"It goes into the district ledger with everything else. The debt stays recorded whether we're here
or not."

"That's not what I'm asking. I'm asking who catches it when the overseer starts shorting the next
crew of haulers the week after you're gone, and none of us know how to prove it the way you just
did."

Kanja didn't have an answer ready, and he said so, which surprised her more than a confident one
would have. He spent that evening turning the question over with Ezio rather than dismissing it —
whether the method the crew had spent two years refining was something that only worked in their own
hands, or something that could be handed to a district outright and trusted to hold.

They stayed four extra days to find out. Ezio ran the same corrective drills he'd once used to test a
single apprentice's instincts, except across a chosen dozen from the quarry's own workforce — cross-
checking testimony against production counts, spotting entries too modest to be honest, learning to
read a fabricated counter-tally for the small inconsistencies that gave it away. Most of them were
slower at it than Ezio had been at their stage, and several were faster, and by the fourth day the
dozen had audited an entire week's fresh testimony on their own, with Ezio correcting only two entries
out of thirty-one.

"That's not a bad number," Kanja said, reviewing the tally with him that night. "That's a district
that doesn't need us here anymore."

"They'll still get it wrong sometimes. We got it wrong plenty of times before we got it right."

"Getting it wrong sometimes and having nobody watching at all aren't the same problem. This solves
the second one."

He left the quarry district's ledger in the dozen's own keeping the next morning, along with a
standing instruction rather than a promise to return: any entry they couldn't resolve among
themselves, any counter-tally too tangled to untangle with what Ezio had taught them, could be
carried to the next district the crew reached, however far that turned out to be. The woman who'd
asked the question watched them go with the district's book in her own hands rather than in Ezio's.

"You didn't answer whether the book leaves with you," she said, at the gate.

"It doesn't. Not this time. That was always supposed to be the point — not that we keep the books
forever, but that eventually nobody needs us to."

*Continuity notes (not narrative): scales the knowledge-transfer register introduced in "The Boy Who
Tried to Take Ezio's Place" from a single apprentice scribe to an entire district workforce (a dozen
trained auditors), establishing that districts can be left self-sustaining rather than dependent on
the crew's continued presence or return — a deliberate answer to the sustainability question the
campaign's rapid multi-district pace had left implicit until now. Distinct from "The Last District
Before the Strike" (Kanja stepping back within one district, immediately before the strike itself, to
let two already-capable local leaders take over a campaign already largely complete) in that this
entry is set well earlier, addresses an entire new cohort trained from a standing start, and frames
the practice as a repeatable standing policy going forward rather than a single handoff. No new named
characters. Third and closing entry in the Industrial Myth's sixteenth wave.*
