# The Second Time the Colonel Came

*Locked canon, Batch 254, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1106`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXXVI, wave 26. Colonel
Serrin Draeth returns, having drilled himself to resist the "Already-Finished Negotiation" presence
trait (VB-060) by refusing to enter Bane's presence at all -- the countermeasure itself becomes the
tell. Thread advances, not resolved.*

---

Draeth didn't come himself this time. He sent a subordinate lieutenant to deliver terms — a proposed
prisoner exchange, reasonable on its face, routed through an intermediary specifically so that Draeth
never had to stand in a room with Bane at all.

Bane understood what it was before the lieutenant finished the second sentence.

"He worked out he couldn't win a conversation with me in person," Bane said, once the lieutenant had
gone to relay terms back. "So he's decided not to have one. That's not strategy. That's a man who
knows exactly what happens to him when he's actually standing here, and has built his entire approach
around never finding out again."

Corren Halst hadn't been in the room for the exchange the first time — had only heard about it
secondhand — and asked what, precisely, Draeth was so determined to avoid.

"He wants a fight he can win by being right. Everyone who's ever come at me wanting that already knows,
somewhere under the wanting, that it isn't there to be won. Being in the same room makes that
impossible to keep pretending otherwise. So he's stopped putting himself in the room."

The exchange itself went through without incident — the terms were genuine, the prisoners real, and
Bane honored them exactly as offered, refusing to let Draeth's absence become an excuse to punish the
lieutenant caught delivering someone else's grudge. But the pattern was its own information, more
useful than anything Draeth could have said in person: a man that specifically afraid of a feeling had
already conceded the confrontation he still believed, on paper, that he was managing to avoid.

"He'll come himself eventually," Bane told Halst, watching the lieutenant's party withdraw. "A man
who has to work this hard to stay away from something isn't actually staying away from it. He's just
delaying the part where he finds out again."
