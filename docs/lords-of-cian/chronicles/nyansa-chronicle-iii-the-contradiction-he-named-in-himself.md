# Nyansa Chronicle III: The Contradiction He Named in Himself

*Locked canon, Batch 179, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-526`). Homage-era Detroit, Nyansa/Adisa. Territory
Chronicle — Adisa as protagonist, Kanja an unnamed background guest with no command, credit, or
resolution authorship. New standalone material. No new named characters.*

---

Adisa had spent years naming other people's unexamined contradictions aloud, watching the technique
work its slow, permanent correction on committees, factions, and individuals alike. He had never once
turned it on himself until a younger organizer, in the middle of an ordinary strategy session, pointed
out that an early position of his — one he'd quietly stopped defending years ago without ever formally
retracting it — still sat, uncorrected, in the movement's own written record.

"You've never actually said you were wrong about it," she said, not unkindly, simply observing the
same gap he'd spent his career pointing out in everyone else. "You just stopped mentioning it. That's
not the same as naming the contradiction the way you've taught the rest of us to."

Adisa sat with that longer than he expected to need.

"You're right. I've built an entire practice around the idea that a contradiction quietly abandoned
still poisons the argument underneath it, even after everyone's stopped repeating it out loud. I
never once applied that standard to my own record." He didn't defend the old position, didn't
minimize the gap between what he'd taught and what he'd actually done. "I think I assumed the
technique was something I gave to others, not something I owed myself the same honesty about."

He named it publicly within the week — the old position stated plainly, the reasoning that had once
supported it, and the specific way his thinking had since moved past it, filed into the movement's own
record with the same rigor he demanded of every correction he'd ever handed someone else.

"That couldn't have been comfortable," the younger organizer said afterward, watching the correction
settle into the record alongside his name.

"It wasn't. I don't think it's supposed to be, for anyone, including me. That was always rather the
point of the technique — the discomfort is what makes the correction real instead of quiet and
unaccountable." Adisa reviewed the finished record, the gap finally closed. "I'm grateful you were the
one who noticed. I don't think I'd have caught it myself, not because I couldn't have, but because it's
considerably harder to point the technique at your own reflection than at someone else's."

An unnamed man who had sat through the original strategy session without comment left once the
correction had been formally filed.
