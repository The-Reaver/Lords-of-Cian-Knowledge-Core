# The Numbers Bane Left Behind

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Bane Alias Chronicle XXX, closing the tenth wave.
Rebellion era, age 19-20, roughly a year after the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated
in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters beyond the already-locked Garren Hask.*

---

Garren Hask kept a private ledger no Directorate file would ever match for accuracy, and on the
evening the crew's talk first turned, however lightly, toward the alias's name beginning to feel
smaller than what the man wearing it had actually become, he sat down and finished the accounting
the way he finished every tally — plainly, in his own hand, nothing rounded off or softened for
effect.

Garrisons stood down rather than fought: eleven. Operations run on intelligence later proven
unreliable, corrected after the fact rather than repeated: one, with the correction still holding a
year on. False accounts publicly disowned rather than left to work in the crew's favor: one. Days
spent on labor with no combat attached to it at all — wells reset, roofs rebuilt, nothing that would
ever make a story: five, that he knew of, likely more he didn't. Captives extracted on margins
thinner than any operation should have run: twelve, alive, against odds Hask had privately given
worse than even.

"It doesn't balance the way a ledger's supposed to balance," Hask said, showing him the tally
unprompted, the way he occasionally did when a season's accounting felt like it was owed to someone
besides the page it was written on. "Some columns you'd want higher. Some you'd want lower. I don't
know that any ledger of a person ever actually balances clean."

"It's not supposed to. If it balanced clean, I'd worry I was rounding something off to make it look
better than it was." Bane read the tally slowly, the corrected intelligence entry, the returned
gatepost rumor, the five unglamorous days at Marrow's Edge sitting plainly beside eleven stood-down
garrisons as if they weighed the same, which, in the only ledger that mattered to him, they nearly
did. "The name's going to keep changing. It already has, more than once, more than people watching
from outside probably realize. I don't know what I'll be called a year from now. I know I want
whoever's counting it, when the counting's done, to be able to write it exactly like this — plainly,
nothing softened, the wells beside the garrisons and neither one crossed out to flatter the other."

"I intend to. However many names it takes." Hask closed the ledger, the accounting for now complete,
the accounting for whatever came next already waiting on the pages after it. "That's the part of the
job nobody ever has to ask me twice about."
