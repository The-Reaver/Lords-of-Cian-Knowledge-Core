# What the Third Generation Chose to Keep

*Locked canon, Batch 260, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1276`). Crow King Alias Chronicle LXXXIV, wave 28,
closing it. The third generation, old enough now to consider stepping back from front-line
operations, decides what role she'll keep. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

She was not old the way Voris had been old when he finally stepped back -- this world's long years
made "old enough to consider it" a different arithmetic entirely -- but she had been running
operations long enough, and training enough students of her own, to feel the same pull Voris had
once described from the other side of the conflict: the sense of having studied a thing long enough
to understand it without needing to keep chasing its every new edge personally.

"I'm not stopping," she told Kanja, when she finally said the thing she'd been turning over for
months. "I don't think I could, and I don't think I want to. But I want to be honest about what I'm
actually still for. I'm not the fastest read in the field anymore -- your fourth-generation student is,
some days, and I taught him half of what lets him be. I think what I'm actually for now is judgment.
Deciding who learns next. Deciding what the craft is allowed to become."

"That's not a smaller role," Kanja said. "Voris told his own second something like this, near the
end of his own chasing -- that understanding a thing completely was never really the point, only
building something that could keep growing past whoever understood it first. I think that's what
you're describing wanting to be now. Not the edge of the craft. The judgment underneath it."

She kept training in the field, some, because she still wanted to and because the discipline of doing
it herself was part of what let her judge it honestly in others. But the center of what she did shifted
from that day forward, quietly, toward exactly what she'd named: watching, deciding, weighing who was
ready and who wasn't, the way Kanja had once weighed it for her alone, before there had been anyone
else to weigh it for.

"I used to think I'd know I was ready to step back when I stopped being useful in the field," she
said. "I don't think that's it at all. I think I'll know when the judgment matters more than the
field does. I think that's already true. I just needed to say it out loud to believe it."
