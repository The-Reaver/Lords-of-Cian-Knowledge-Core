# What the Second Sky-Reader Doubted After

*Locked canon, Batch 208, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-908`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XLVIII, wave 16. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The two damaged hulls were repaired within the week. The successor did not recover as quickly.

She had spent years building a reputation on calls that were, without exception, honestly hers —
earned the slow way, the same way Sephtis had earned his, through visible willingness to be wrong in
front of people whose lives depended on the reading. This was different. She hadn't been wrong. She
had been used, and for three days afterward she found she could not fully trust the difference between
those two things in her own head.

"I keep going back through the relay chain," she told Sephtis, the old man retired but still near
enough to be asked. "Looking for what I should have caught. And every time I find nothing, because
there was nothing to find — the signals were clean. Which means I can't even learn from it the way I
learned from the miss. I don't know what to watch for differently next time. I just know I have to
watch for something I couldn't see before."

"Then that's what you build," Sephtis said. "Not a correction. A discipline. When I missed by two
hours, the water taught me something about how I read it wrong. This isn't the water lying to you.
It's a person, and people don't hold still to be studied the way weather does. You won't fix this by
reading harder. You'll fix it by building a second kind of watching — not for the sky, but for
whoever might be standing between you and it."

She didn't answer right away. When she did, it wasn't relief in her voice, only a decision made.

"Then I'll build that too," she said. "Same as you built the first one. Slowly, and by getting it
wrong in front of people before I get it right."

Within the month, every relay station in her network carried a second, independent verification
line — redundant, deliberately inconvenient, built specifically to catch a false confirmation rather
than a natural miss. She called it nothing grand. She called it, simply, the second watch.
