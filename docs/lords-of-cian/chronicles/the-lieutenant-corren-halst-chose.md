# The Lieutenant Corren Halst Chose

*Locked canon, Batch 254, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1099`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXIX, wave 23, closing wave
23. Corren Halst personally trains a new second-tier field commander, extending the crew's
operational discipline beyond the founding circle for the first time.*

---

Toran had been with the column eight months, long enough to stop flinching at Obsidian Malice's
discharge and to earn a reputation as the kind of fighter who thought two moves ahead of the one he
was making. Corren Halst had been watching him the whole time, and when he finally pulled Toran aside
it wasn't to praise him.

"You're going to plan and run a raid on your own," Halst told him. "Not because you're ready. Because
you won't be certain you're ready until you've done it once, and I'd rather that first time happen
with me still able to catch what you miss."

Toran planned it well — a granary resupply interception, low-risk, well-scouted, the kind of
operation Bane's own standing order existed to protect against being run carelessly. He'd been awake
three nights running getting the details right, and when Halst asked, the way Halst always asked now,
whether a second set of eyes had reviewed the plan before it went to the column, Toran said yes
before he'd actually done it.

Halst caught the lie inside a minute of looking at the plan himself — not from what was wrong with
it, but from the specific kind of confidence in it that only showed up in work nobody else had
touched. He didn't report it. He handed it back and told Toran to bring it to Bane directly, tonight,
properly, and to say out loud why he'd almost skipped the step meant to catch exactly the kind of
blind spot exhaustion built.

"I didn't want it to look like I couldn't do it myself," Toran admitted, later, once Bane had found
the one real gap in the plan — a resupply timing window that assumed a garrison patrol pattern three
days stale.

"Nobody who's ever used that rule needed it because they couldn't do the job," Bane said. "I made it
because I couldn't see my own blind spot the night I needed it caught. You're not proving anything by
skipping the part that exists because even I couldn't skip it."

The raid ran two days later, corrected timing and all, and came off clean. Halst didn't take credit
for it in front of the column. He told Bane privately, afterward, that the real test hadn't been
whether Toran could plan a raid — it was whether the rule from the worst night of Bane's own judgment
would survive being taught to someone who hadn't been there to learn why it mattered the hard way. It
had.
