# The Night the Coast Held

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
XXVI. Rebellion era, age 21, some months after Ghost Harbor (`MCD-235`). Not a territory Chronicle.
New standalone material — a detailed combined Trinity showcase defending civilians from a non-Trust
raiding fleet. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The raiders weren't Trust and weren't Directorate — an opportunist fleet out of the lawless waters
beyond either power's reach, drawn to a coastal town by the simple, ugly arithmetic of a settlement
left undefended while every able hand it had was already fighting somewhere else in the war.

*The Audit* reached the bay an hour before the raiding fleet did, warned by a fishing boat that had
outrun the danger by minutes and nothing more.

"Six ships," Dol Maren reported, glass fixed on the horizon. "Against a town with no walls and no
militia worth the name."

"Then we're the militia tonight." Kanja was already moving toward the gear racks. "Full deployment.
This isn't a lesson for the Trust. This is just people who need someone standing between them and six
ships that want to hurt them."

The engagement that followed ran the length of the night, fought close to shore in water shallow
enough to limit maneuver and force every advantage down to raw combined skill. Mafesto's Kinetic
Transfer System converted the raiders' own opening broadside into stored charge that powered three
consecutive strikes against their lead vessel's hull. Onyx's Veil Piercer found the weak seam in a
second ship's rigging in the confusion of a boarding rush, dropping her mainmast across her own deck.
Obsidian Malice discharged twice in close combat aboard a third vessel, clearing a boarding party that
had already reached the town's own fishing docks before the war club's held charge scattered them back
into the water.

The fighting never crossed onto the shore itself — every engagement fought deliberately in the water
between the raiders and the town, the town's own people watching from rooftops and hillsides as six
ships that had come to take everything they had were, one by one, disabled, boarded, or driven off
before a single raider set foot on their beach.

By dawn four of the six raiding vessels had struck their colors and two had fled, and the town's own
militia — such as it was, a dozen fishermen with boat hooks and nerve — had never needed to fire a
shot.

"You didn't have to come," the town's eldest fisherman said, finding Kanja still checking the gear for
damage as the sun came up. "We're nobody. No garrison, no strategic value, nothing the war actually
needs from us."

"That's exactly why we came." Kanja looked past him, at the town still standing, untouched, exactly as
it had been the evening before. "The war doesn't need you. That's not the same as you not mattering."
