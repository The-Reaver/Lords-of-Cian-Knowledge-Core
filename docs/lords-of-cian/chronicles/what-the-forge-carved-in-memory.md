# What the Forge Carved in Memory

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XLIX, wave 17. Rolling Foundry Campaign, age 27, aboard/around The Anvil. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. Reuses the campaign's recurring senior smith; no new named characters.*

---

The fever took eleven days to kill him, and none of them had anything to do with the Trust, or a
raid, or a burning. Toma had been the quietest of the sixty apprentices, a boy who kept his own
counsel and did clean, unremarkable work, and then he had a cough that didn't clear, and then he
didn't wake up one morning, and there was no enemy anywhere in it for anyone to answer to.

"There's nothing to rebuild," the senior smith said, standing over the cold half of the second
anvil that had been his station. "That's the part nobody prepares you for. Every other loss on this
tour, we've had something to point the work at. A wall. A district. A district's worth of anger.
This one, the work's got nowhere to go."

Kanja didn't argue the point. He'd spent eleven days at the boy's side between shifts, changing
water, saying little, and none of it had bought back a single hour. "It doesn't," he agreed. "I'm
not going to pretend otherwise to make today easier."

The other apprentices had gathered without being called, the way people do when a working group
loses one of its own, standing at the edges of the floor in a silence that had nothing productive
in it. Someone had already banked the forge low rather than let it run through the vigil.

"He liked the small work," a girl near the front said, not quite to anyone. "Hinges. Latches. The
stuff nobody notices unless it breaks."

Kanja crossed to the cold forge and built the fire back up himself, slow, without asking anyone to
help him do it. When it was hot enough he took a scrap of good iron and worked it under the
apprentices' watching, unhurried, no urgency in it at all — the opposite register of every rebuild
the campaign had ever run. What came off the anvil after an hour was a small iron hinge, plain,
perfectly true, the kind of unremarkable object a boy who liked hinges would have been proud to
have made himself.

"I can't give him back," he said, setting it in the senior smith's hands rather than keep it in his
own. "I can build the kind of thing he'd have built, and let it sit somewhere his name gets said
over it. That's the whole of what this floor has to offer against a fever. It was never built to
answer this kind. Doesn't mean we don't still answer, best we can."

They mounted the hinge on the floor's own tool rack, unremarkable among the other hardware to
anyone who didn't know its story, exactly as Toma himself would have wanted it — nothing announced,
nothing burned into the metal to mark it. The senior smith touched it once on her way past, and
didn't stop walking.

"Eighteen months of raids and burnings and embargoes," she said later, low, mostly to herself. "And
the thing that finally showed me the floor's got a limit was a cough."

"It always had one," Kanja said. "We just hadn't met it yet."
