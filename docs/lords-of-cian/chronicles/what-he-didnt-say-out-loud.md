# What He Didn't Say Out Loud

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Bane Alias Chronicle XXVIII, first entry in the tenth
wave. Rebellion era, age 19, months after the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters beyond the already-locked Garren Hask.*

---

Garren Hask found him awake well past the camp's last watch change, sitting apart from the banked
fires with no task in front of him, which was unusual enough on its own that Hask nearly turned back
rather than intrude on it.

"You're not counting anything," Hask said instead, settling nearby without quite asking permission.
"That's usually how I know something's actually wrong. You count when you're thinking something
through. You're not counting."

Bane didn't answer right away. When he did, it wasn't the voice that met garrisons, or the voice
that met recruiters, or even the plainer voice the crew mostly knew — something quieter than all of
those, closer to the nineteen years underneath the reputation than anything he generally let surface
where it could be seen.

"I keep waiting for the day the numbers stop bothering me the way the Kessarine crossing did. The
day carrying somebody's death starts costing less because I've carried enough of them." He didn't
look at Hask while he said it. "It hasn't happened yet. I used to think it would, that there'd be
some point where the ledger got long enough that one more entry stopped landing the same way the
first ones did. It doesn't work that way. Every one still lands exactly like the first one did."

"Would you want it to stop landing that way? If you could choose."

"No." That came fast, with none of the hesitation the rest of it had carried. "No, I wouldn't. The
day it stops landing is the day I've become the thing the Directorate's already decided I am in
their files — something that doesn't count the cost anymore, just the outcome. I'd rather carry
every single one of them exactly this heavy for the rest of however long this takes than trade that
for it getting easier."

"Nobody sees this part of you. The version that meets the garrisons doesn't carry it like this. Or
doesn't show it, at least."

"That version can't afford to show it. This one's the truth underneath the version, and I'd rather
keep at least one person knowing the difference exists." Bane finally looked over at him, something
almost like gratitude in it, unshowy and brief. "Don't put this in the ledger, Hask. Some of it isn't
meant to be counted. Some of it's just meant to be carried."

Hask didn't write it down. He never had to be told twice about the things that weren't meant for the
tally.
