*Archive fragment, present-day, undated by her own habit: "Ozmund did not ask me to stay. I stayed
because the night was clear and I like the weight of Cian's air at that hour. He noticed eventually
that I was not going to leave. I think he found that more restful than anything I could have said to
him."*

I set this account apart from the others I have kept of her present-day life, because it concerns a
tie that has never been close in the way her bonds with Sephtis or Valen or Ezio are close, and I
think the account is more honest for admitting that plainly rather than dressing it up as more than
it is. Lauris and Ozmund are not intimate in any sense either of them would claim. What passed
between them on the rampart that night was smaller than intimacy, and I have come to believe it was
not smaller in the way that matters.

He had not sought company. I do not think he sought anything at all beyond the air itself, which is
not unusual for a man carrying a scar that most of his House would call an inheritance and most of
his conscience would call a debt. He had walked the eastern rampart alone for perhaps twenty
minutes before he noticed her at the far end of it, seated on the stone with her boots crossed at
the ankle, unmoving in a way that suggested she had been there considerably longer than he had.

"You did not need to keep this watch," he said. It was, I noted, an observation rather than a
dismissal.

"I am not keeping it for you," she said. "I like this hour. The gravity reads differently once the
heat leaves the stone. I could explain the mechanism if you wanted it explained."

"I do not want it explained."

"Then I will simply enjoy it, and you may sit or not, as you prefer."

He sat. I do not think either of them intended the exchange to mean anything in particular, and I
think that absence of intention is precisely what let it mean something after all.

They spoke very little across the better part of an hour, which will not surprise anyone who has
read what I have already written of her closest companionships — she keeps low conversational
volume across long duration with the people she trusts most, and I have come to suspect Ozmund
falls closer to that category than either of them would readily admit aloud. Lilith joined some time
later, without announcement, and took the space on his other side the way a person does who has done
this before and expects to do it again. I record what little was actually said, because I believe
the little is the whole of it.

"I do not always know whether the crown I refused was mine to refuse," Ozmund said, at length, to no
one in particular, in the flat register I have learned means he is saying a true thing rather than a
comfortable one. "There are days I refuse it again privately, as though the first refusal did not
hold."

Lilith did not answer that. I do not believe it was hers to answer.

"It held," Lauris said. Nothing more. She did not argue the point or build a case for it. She simply
stated it the way she states most things she has already worked out privately and sees no reason to
dress further.

He did not thank her for it, and I do not think either of them would have found a thank-you
appropriate to the size of what had actually passed between them. He sat a while longer with the
matter, visibly turning it over, and I watched something in him settle by degrees rather than all at
once — not resolved, I want to be clear, since I do not believe doubt of his particular shape
resolves in a single evening or from a single flat sentence spoken by a woman who is not given to
comfort for its own sake. But quieter. Held differently than it had been held an hour before.

Lauris left before either of them did, without ceremony, the way she arrives at most things. I asked
her afterward, since I make a habit of asking rather than assuming, whether she had gone to the
rampart that night intending to find him there. She told me she had not, and that she did not think
it would have mattered to the outcome if she had. "I go where the night is good," she said. "That I
was there when he needed someone to simply agree with him, once, plainly, was not planning. It was
only ever luck, and I do not think less of the evening for admitting that."

I have written before that she carries joy the way the other leads of this crew carry their own
particular weights, and I stand by the description. I want to add, having watched this, that the
carrying does not always announce itself as anything grander than a woman willing to sit on cold
stone at an hour when sitting there serves no purpose but her own contentment, and who happens,
without trying especially hard, to leave the people near her lighter than she found them.
