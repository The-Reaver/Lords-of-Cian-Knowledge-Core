# The Second Burning

*Locked canon, Batch 114, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-389`). Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle I. Rebellion era,
weeks after the Free Quarter's fourteen-day rebuilding (`MCD-241`, age 27). Not a territory
Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters.*

---

The garrison prefect who ordered the second burning had studied the first one exhaustively, and had
concluded, with the particular confidence of a man who believes he has finally found the flaw in an
opponent's method, that the fourteen-day rebuild had only worked because the Trust's own abandoned
incendiary equipment had been left behind for salvage. This time, he ordered every scrap of
equipment recovered before withdrawal. No Dead Drakma left behind. No raw material handed, even by
accident, to the district he was punishing.

He burned the district's eastern quarter down to bare foundation stone, confirmed the salvage teams
had missed nothing, and reported the operation complete.

Kanja arrived four days later to find not equipment left behind, but 6,000 fighters who had already
begun stripping the district's own undamaged western half — walls, fixtures, anything load-bearing
that could be repurposed — under his crew's direction, trading a smaller intact footprint for a
faster rebuild of the burned half, using nothing the Trust had failed to recover because none of it
had ever been needed to begin with.

"You gave us the wrong lesson to guard against," he told the district's assembled residents, watching
the first new support beams go up against the ash-blackened foundation stones. "The Trust thinks it
solved a supply problem. It never was one. Burn a district and you don't take away my material. You
take away the argument that we can't rebuild faster than you can destroy. I don't need salvage to win
that argument. I need people who've decided punishment isn't going to be the last word, and this
district's got six thousand of them standing right here."

The rebuild took eleven days — three faster than the first, the western quarter's willing
self-demolition providing cleaner material than salvaged incendiary equipment ever had. The prefect's
report, filed afterward, would describe the outcome in a single line that outlived his career far
longer than the burning itself: *material denial achieved; strategic objective not achieved; unknown
variable identified as the population itself.*

"He thought the ash was the weapon," one of the six thousand said, watching new roof timbers go up
over what had been rubble eleven days earlier. "The ash was never the weapon. We were. He just
finally figured out you can't confiscate that."
