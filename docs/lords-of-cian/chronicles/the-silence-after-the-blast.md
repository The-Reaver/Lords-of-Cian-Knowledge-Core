# The Silence After the Blast

*Locked canon, Batch 260, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1266`). Crow King Alias Chronicle LXXIV, wave 25. An
explosion temporarily deafens Kanja mid-operation, and the lineage carries the deception without his
hearing for the first time. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The charge wasn't meant for him. It was a Directorate demolition set to collapse a bridge span behind
a retreating patrol, and Kanja was close enough when it went that the world afterward arrived to him
as pressure and light with almost no sound in it at all -- a ringing wall where the operation's
half-sung, half-signaled coordination had been a moment before.

He couldn't hear the Braid's own patterns anymore, couldn't tell true rhythm from false, couldn't
even hear his own voice well enough to trust throwing a signal of his own. For the length of the
operation's most dangerous stretch, the man who had built the craft was effectively cut out of it.

The lineage didn't stop. The fourth generation's tap-signal relay -- built years before from a throat
wound, never abandoned once the voice healed -- carried through his palm against Kanja's own arm,
translating the operation's unfolding shape into pressure he could feel without hearing a word of it.
The apprentice ran the vocal half of the Braid herself, without him, for the first time in the
craft's history. Between them, deafened and mute to the operation's own language, Kanja was walked
through the crossing by touch alone, trusting a relay he couldn't verify with his own ears.

It worked. The patrol got clear, the bridge came down behind them on schedule, and Kanja's hearing
came back gradually over the following two days, muffled and uneven before it settled.

"You ran an operation with me in the middle of it and I couldn't have told you a single true thing
that happened until it was over," he said afterward.

"You didn't need to," the apprentice said. "That's the whole design, if you think about it. We built
every backup channel assuming one day the primary one would fail. We just never expected it to fail
on you."

"I'm glad it did, in a strange way," Kanja said. "It's the first time I've had to actually trust the
craft the way everyone else has to trust it -- without being able to check it myself."
