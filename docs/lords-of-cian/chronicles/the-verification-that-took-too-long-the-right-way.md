# The Verification That Took Too Long the Right Way

*Locked canon, Batch 254, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1109`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXXIX, wave 27. Direct
companion piece to "What the Delay Cost" -- this time the same standing caution catches a fabricated
trap before the column walks into it, completing the trade-off the earlier entry left one-sided.*

---

The tip came from a source who'd never been wrong before: a garrison guard supposedly ready to open a
side gate for a prisoner extraction, timed and detailed with the kind of specificity that made most of
the column want to move on it that same night.

Bane didn't move on it that same night. He held the operation the standing extra day the way he'd
held every prisoner-liberation tip since the one that had cost two lives to an informant's timing,
months before, and sent a single runner ahead instead of the column.

The runner came back with what verification always either confirmed or didn't: the guard was real,
the gate was real, and the timing was a lie. Directorate counterintelligence had turned the source
two weeks earlier and fed the detail specifically to see whether it would draw Bane's column into a
kill zone staged on the approach road.

"If we'd moved last night," Corren Halst said, once the runner's report had been laid out in full,
"we'd have walked the whole column into that road in the dark."

"We would have." Bane didn't say it like a victory. "The day cost us nothing this time. Last time it
cost two people who didn't have a day to spare. I'm not going to pretend this makes up for that, or
that the caution's suddenly free just because it worked out clean tonight."

"It's still the right call."

"It's still the right call on average. That's not the same as free, and it's not the same as always
right in the specific moment it's being made. Some nights it saves everyone. Some nights it costs the
two people who needed the hour we spent checking instead of moving. Both of those are true about the
same rule. I'd rather live with both than pretend only one of them is real."

The extraction ran two nights later, along a different route entirely, and pulled eleven prisoners
clear without a shot fired.
