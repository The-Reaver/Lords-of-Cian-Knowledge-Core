# The Silence Where the Talisman Should Have Answered

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LII, wave 18. Rebellion era. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The Aegis-Talisman had taken the glancing edge of a strike meant for Kanja's shoulder two engagements
earlier — a hairline crack through its inner lens, nothing that had stopped it from working, until the
moment it did.

He reached toward the siege ramp's bracing the way he had a hundred times before and the frequency
simply didn't return. No false signal, no interference, no muddled noise to work through — the
Talisman's answering hum cut out mid-reach and didn't come back, the crack finally running its full
length through the mechanism that had carried every reading since the doctrine's first day.

"That's never happened," he said, more to himself than to anyone near him, turning the dead lens over
once before setting it aside rather than wasting time on a repair the moment didn't allow.

The ramp still needed reading. The assault behind him still needed the answer in minutes, not the days
a proper Talisman repair would cost.

He put his palm flat against the bracing instead, the way he had against the dam months earlier, against
the anchor chain in the river, against a hundred structures before he'd ever had an artifact to lend the
listening any reach at all — and made himself trust that the skill had always been the part that mattered,
the Talisman only ever the amplifier standing between his own hands and a reading he'd learned to take
long before anyone forged it for him.

It was slower. It required standing close enough to be seen, holding contact long enough that a sentry
nearly caught him at it, reading through touch alone instead of a broadcast that could reach a structure
from cover. But the signature came through — the same tension, the same weak seam, arrived at without
the Talisman's help at all.

Obsidian Malice's discharge went into the seam by hand-guided contact rather than a clean broadcast
lock, cruder than the Talisman-assisted version but no less accurate. The ramp came down.

"You could have waited for a repair," Danne Sok said afterward, examining the cracked lens with the
same care she gave any piece of the Trinity's gear.

"I could have," Kanja said. "I wanted to know first whether I still could, without it." He turned the
dead Talisman over once more before handing it off for real repair. "It slowed me down. It made me
stand closer than I wanted to. But it didn't take the doctrine away from me — it just took away the
part that was never actually the doctrine to begin with."
