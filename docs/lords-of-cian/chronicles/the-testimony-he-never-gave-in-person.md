# The Testimony He Never Gave in Person

*Locked canon, Batch 224, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1018`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LIII, wave 18. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The prize court needed more than freed captives could give it. A syndicate captain, taken alive off
a raided depot months earlier, had hired advocates skilled enough to argue that no crime had occurred
without a sworn account of what the Scourge himself had witnessed aboard that ship — and no ordinary
witness could testify to a boarding action from the deck of a vessel that had, by the time the court
convened, already sailed on to three other rescues in two other waters.

Garren Hask carried the sealed account into the courtroom instead, in the Scourge's own words,
transcribed and countersigned the night of the raid and never altered since.

"The court will note," the presiding magistrate said, turning the document over, "that the deponent
is not present, cannot be cross-examined, and cannot be compelled to appear."

"The court will also note," Hask said, "that everything sworn to in that account has already been
independently corroborated by forty-one freed captives, three of whom are standing in your gallery
right now and none of whom have ever met each other outside that hold."

The magistrate read the account twice before admitting it, less because the law required it than
because the corroboration made refusing it look like exactly what it would have been — a court
choosing not to hear the truth because of who happened to deliver it.

The syndicate captain was convicted on the strength of testimony from a man the court never saw.

"He'll want to know it held," Hask told the freed captives afterward, closing his own ledger over the
day's entry.

"Tell him," one of them said, "that it held because of what we said, not what he did."

Hask wrote that down too, word for word, before he left the building.
