# What the Hounds Heard

*Locked canon, Batch 201, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-847`). The Crow King Alias Chronicle XXXII, wave 11 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Every calculation behind the Hymn-Engine had been built around human ears — trained or untrained,
suspicious or complacent, but always human, responding to frequencies and patterns shaped by years of
study into exactly what a person's hearing would accept as natural. Nobody in the crew had spent any
real thought on the garrison's tracking hounds until the operation was already three hours deep and
the animals began, without explanation any sentry could offer, to bark and strain toward a stretch of
open ground the false signal had specifically been built to make appear empty.

"They're not fooled," the apprentice said, watching the hounds through the dim, growing more
concerned by the minute. "Whatever we're doing to the sensors and the sentries, it isn't working on
them at all."

It wasn't working because it had never been built to. The Hymn-Engine's frequencies sat comfortably
within the range human hearing found unremarkable — that was the entire design. A hound's hearing
reached well past it, into a register the crew's own careful calibration had never needed to
consider, and the animals were responding to something genuinely present in the false signal that no
person nearby could hear at all.

Kanja didn't try to fix the frequency mid-operation — there wasn't time, and no version rebuilt on
the spot would hold the timing the rest of the plan depended on. Instead, the crew's own handler,
already positioned nearby for an unrelated contingency, used a scent-masking technique meant for an
entirely different purpose to blunt the hounds' focus just enough that their handlers dismissed the
straining as ordinary restlessness rather than a genuine alert.

The operation held, barely, on a solution that had nothing to do with the Hymn-Engine at all.

"We build for what people hear," Kanja said afterward, reviewing the near-failure with real
attention. "Every calibration, every frequency choice, every test — all of it assumes a human ear at
the other end. I've never once accounted for anything listening that isn't one."

"So the craft has a blind spot that isn't an enemy's cleverness at all," the apprentice said. "It's
just an animal doing exactly what its own ears were built to do."

"A significant one. We fix the frequency range for anywhere hounds might be present, starting
tonight. It's a strange thing, being reminded the craft's biggest vulnerability isn't a person who
outthought it. It's a dog that was never trying to."
