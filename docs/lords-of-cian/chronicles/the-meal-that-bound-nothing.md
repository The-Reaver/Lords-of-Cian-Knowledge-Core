# The Meal That Bound Nothing

*Locked canon, Batch 148, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-473`). Hekalu Chronicle II. Homage-era Detroit,
Hekalu/Adom. Territory Chronicle -- Adom as protagonist, Kanja an unnamed background guest with no
command, credit, or resolution authorship. New standalone material. No new named characters.*

---

A rival cooperative's own enforcer sat at Adom's table on his boss's direct order, sent specifically
to gather what he could while appearing to accept the same hospitality that had, months before, turned
an unrelated rival owner into an unprompted ally. The instructions had been explicit: eat, listen, say
little, report back everything.

Nothing happened.

"The Common Table" moved through the room the way it always did with a genuine guest — warmth, easy
conversation, the particular unguarded quality Adom's hospitality reliably produced — but it produced
none of it in the enforcer specifically, no matter how long the meal ran or how much of it he
outwardly consumed. He left at the evening's end exactly as guarded as he'd arrived, his report to his
boss thorough and entirely unaffected by anything the table was meant to do.

"I don't understand," Adom admitted afterward, genuinely puzzled by the failure. "I've seen the gift
work on far more hostile guests than him. He ate the same food, sat at the same table, stayed the same
length of time as anyone it's ever worked on."

"Maybe that's not what it actually needs," his own second observed, working through the puzzle
alongside him. "Everyone it's worked on chose, themselves, to sit down — even the rival owner who came
in suspicious. He decided, on his own, to accept the meal. This one didn't decide anything. He was
sent. The choosing was never his."

Adom sat with that a long moment, turning it over against every prior case he could recall. "I told
someone once, a while back, that a man had sat at this table without meaning to be there and left
entirely unchanged by it. I always assumed that meant something about him personally. I think I
finally understand it meant something about the table instead. It was never really about the meal, or
even about who's sitting. It's about whether the person sitting is actually choosing this, of their
own will, or just occupying the chair someone else put them in."

"So the gift can't be tricked into working on someone who isn't free to accept it."

"No. And I don't think I'd want it to be able to. A binding built on a coerced choice wouldn't be
worth having in the first place." Adom watched the empty chair the enforcer had vacated. "The table
only ever offers what it's always offered. Whether it takes root was never mine to force. That's
exactly why it means something when it does."

An unnamed man near the room's edge, present for the entire meal, said nothing throughout and left
without being noticed.
