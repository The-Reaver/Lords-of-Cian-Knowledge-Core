# The Council That Built Itself

*Locked canon, Batch 257, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1183`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle LXXII, wave 24, closing the wave. Workers formally establish a self-governing council with real voting authority over conditions; Kanja declines a seat, synthesizing the safety-training, guild, and wage-dispute threads into a lasting institution.*

---

It started as a suggestion box outside the gallery Garren Hask used for his ledgers and became, within two months, something closer to a government. The safety training every recruit went through before touching a tool. The tradesmen's association that had grown out of a siege and carried no one's name. The wage schedule rewritten after forty-one laborers put down their picks and were heard. Three separate reforms that had each solved their own crisis in isolation were, someone eventually pointed out, the working parts of an institution nobody had actually built on purpose.

So they built it on purpose. Elected representatives from every crew, standing authority over conditions, wages, and safety enforcement, with real voting power rather than advisory suggestions a foreman could ignore. They offered Kanja a permanent seat, unanimous on the first vote, before the council had even finished naming itself.

He turned it down in the same meeting.

"You built this to survive without needing to ask me anything," he said. "A seat with my name on it defeats the entire point before the ink's dry."

"You built half of what we're formalizing," one representative said. "The training. The wage review. That's yours as much as ours."

"I built pieces of it because pieces were on fire at the time. What you're building now is supposed to outlast the fires." He looked around the room -- crew leaders, a former quartermaster's clerk, the mucker who'd asked the hard question during the wage dispute, none of them there because of him specifically. "If it only works while I'm standing in it, it was never actually an institution. It was just me, wearing a bigger hat."

They seated him as an advisor instead, present when asked, absent otherwise, with no vote and no veto. He attended exactly one more meeting that month, uninvited, silent the whole time, just to confirm for himself that the room functioned the same with him saying nothing as it had without him in it at all.

It did.
