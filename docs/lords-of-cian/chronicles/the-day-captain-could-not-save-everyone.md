# The Day Captain Could Not Save Everyone

*Locked canon, Batch 127, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-428`). Captain Alias Chronicle IV, first entry in the
second wave. Rebellion era. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The extraction went as well as any extraction could that still ended with three names added to
Garren Hask's ledger, and the crew had never once, in the alias's short history, watched Kanja fail
to bring everyone home before.

"It wasn't your fault," the extraction team's senior member said, hours later, watching him sit alone
with the day's casualty report longer than the paperwork itself required. "The collapse wasn't
something anyone could have predicted. You got thirty-one people clear. Three didn't make it. That's
not a failure. That's the actual math of this kind of work."

"I know the math." Kanja didn't look up from the report. "I've known the math since the Black Trench.
Knowing it doesn't make three names easier to write down, and it shouldn't. The day it gets easier is
the day I stop being worth the name people started calling me instead of the one the Directorate
gave me."

Word of the loss moved through the crew the way it always did — quietly, without official
announcement, three names finding their way into conversations that trailed off whenever he entered
a room, the particular careful silence crews extend to a leader they're worried might be
questioning himself in ways he won't say aloud.

It was Callum Breck, four months into his own rebuilt voice, who finally said what the silence
wouldn't.

"You didn't lose them because you're not good enough at this," Breck said, choosing each word with
the same deliberate care that had marked every sentence since the Black Trench. "You lost them
because sometimes the ground gives out and no amount of skill changes physics. I know that math too.
I've known it since Nev." He didn't elaborate further, didn't need to. "The crew doesn't call you
Captain because you're perfect. We call you that because when it goes wrong, you're the one who sits
with the report instead of looking away from it. That's not nothing. That's actually the whole
reason the word still means something to any of us."

Kanja said nothing for a long moment, the weight of the day settling differently once someone who'd
survived his own version of exactly this had said it plainly. "Thank you for that."

"Don't thank me. Just keep sitting with the reports." Breck turned to leave, then paused at the
doorway. "That's the job. Nobody who's good at this job gets to stop finding it hard. The day you
stop finding it hard is the day the rest of us should start worrying."
