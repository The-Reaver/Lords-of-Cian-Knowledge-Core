# The Heat That Came Up From Below

*Locked canon, Batch 257, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1181`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle LXX, wave 24, opening the wave. The alias's first geothermal/extreme-heat hazard -- an unexpected earth-heat vent, distinct from every prior fire register.*

---

Nobody had lit anything. That was what made the heat wrong before anyone understood what was causing it -- a gallery deep in the western workings climbing past anything a torch or a forge fire could explain, walls warm to the touch a full body-length from any known heat source, the air itself starting to shimmer the way it did over open flame with no flame anywhere in sight.

"This isn't your fire trick again," Danne Sok said, already sweating through a shirt that had been dry twenty minutes earlier.

"No." Kanja pressed a palm flat against the tunnel floor and held it there longer than comfort allowed. "This is coming up, not in. Something down there's venting heat the earth's been sitting on a long time, and we dug straight into the path of it."

It wasn't sabotage. It wasn't accident in the usual sense either -- just the unlucky intersection of a new excavation line and a geothermal pocket nobody had mapped because nobody had ever dug this deep in this particular direction before. Extinguishing it wasn't an option; there was nothing burning to put out. Venting it the way he'd vented smoke or displaced heavier-than-air gas wouldn't work either, since the heat itself wasn't contained in a pocket that could be drained -- it was radiating steadily upward through solid rock from somewhere below anyone could safely reach.

Mafesto's kinetic transfer read the heat gradient the way it had read pressure and density before, mapping where the temperature climbed fastest and where it leveled, and Kanja used that map to reroute the entire excavation line around the vent rather than through it, sacrificing three days of planned tunnel and accepting a longer route in exchange for a safe one. Obsidian Malice, driven in short controlled strikes along the new path's rock face, cut faster than the delay should have cost them, buying most of the lost time back through raw efficiency rather than a shortcut through danger.

By the time the rerouted gallery broke through to the planned junction, the crew had lost a day and a half instead of three, and left a permanent warning marker on the old line for anyone who dug this deep again.

"We could've pushed through faster," Sok said, looking back at the abandoned original path. "Just not safely."

"Faster and safely aren't always the same question," Kanja said. "Down here, if you're not asking both at once, you're not actually asking either one."
