# The Camp Fed by No One's Orders

*Locked canon, Batch 259, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1230`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LXV, wave 22. Age
176, V3 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The port refused them before the ship had even finished mooring. Sixty-three freed captives, and a
harbor-master who wouldn't let a single one of them past the breakwater — not out of cruelty, he
kept saying, but because a fever had gone through the hold on the crossing and killed four of them
before it burned itself out, and his own town had buried enough people to fever in living memory to
know exactly what an uncontrolled landing could cost it. He wasn't wrong. That was the difficulty of
it.

There was no enemy to fight here. No garrison to break, no captain to disarm, nothing the coat or
the gauntlets or six years of documented reputation could solve by being more frightening than the
problem. So the crew built a camp instead, on a spit of empty coastline a mile outside the harbor's
own jurisdiction, using canvas and lumber that had been cargo an hour before and shelter an hour
after.

The Breath Collar earned its place in the loadout properly for the first time in years, doing
exactly the unglamorous work its designers had built it for: its filtration membrane, tuned against
airborne contaminants rather than combat gas, let him move between the sick and the well inside the
camp for six straight days without carrying anything between them, checking fevers by hand and
breath sound without becoming a second vector himself. It wasn't dramatic. It was the difference
between a quarantine that held and one that didn't.

By the eighth day the fever had run its course with no further deaths, and the harbor-master — who
had sent food twice under cover of darkness, publicly still refusing entry and privately unable to
let sixty-three people starve a mile from his own walls — opened the gate himself, no ceremony,
already turning to the next problem before anyone could thank him for it. Kanja never told him who
had built the camp. It hadn't needed his name to work; it had needed six days of nobody getting
worse.
