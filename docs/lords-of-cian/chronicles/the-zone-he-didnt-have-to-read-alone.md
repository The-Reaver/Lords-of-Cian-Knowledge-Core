# The Zone He Didn't Have to Read Alone

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LV, wave 19. Rebellion era. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The harbor works sprawled across more structure than Kanja had ever been asked to read in a single
operation — six piers, three cargo cranes, a breakwater, and a garrison seawall, all needing diagnosis
before dawn if the liberation was to move ships out ahead of the tide.

He'd have taken it alone a year earlier, zone by zone, trusting speed over rest. This time he didn't
have to.

The defected Trust engineer arrived first, listening rods already in hand, and the second student —
the one who'd worked a solo operation clean months before — arrived not long after, both of them
waiting on him to simply assign ground rather than direct every reading personally.

"I've never divided it before," Kanja admitted, studying the harbor's full layout with all three of
them present at once. "Every structure this size, I've read myself, start to end, because there wasn't
anyone else I trusted to read it the way it needed reading."

"There is now," the engineer said, without any particular weight on it, the way a fact gets stated once
it's simply become true.

He gave the student the piers, familiar territory close to what she'd already proven she could carry
alone. He gave the engineer the cranes, structures closer to the Trust schematics the engineer still
half-thought in. He took the breakwater and the seawall himself, the two largest and least forgiving
reads, not because he doubted the others but because some habits took longer to set down than others.

Three readings converged inside two hours instead of the single night the work would have cost one
listener alone. The piers came free without a single miscall. The cranes, read exactly to the engineer's
old Trust training, gave up their weak points faster than Kanja could have found them cold. The
breakwater and seawall, his own to carry, held no surprises at all.

Ships cleared the harbor before the tide turned, the garrison waking to find half its own works
already disabled and no visible line of attack to answer.

"You didn't need to be everywhere tonight," the student said afterward, watching him check her piers
anyway, out of habit more than doubt.

"No," Kanja admitted. "I didn't. I think that might be the first time this doctrine has ever felt like
it belonged to more hands than mine while I was still standing in the middle of it." He looked out
across six piers no longer needing him at all. "I built this because I didn't trust anyone else to hear
what I heard. I hadn't planned on how strange it would feel, being right that eventually they would."
