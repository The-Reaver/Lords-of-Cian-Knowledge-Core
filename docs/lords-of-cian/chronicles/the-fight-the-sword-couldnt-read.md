# The Fight the Sword Couldn't Read

*Locked canon, Batch 118, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-402`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle V. Rebellion
era, within the alias's early window (age 18), a solo-blade engagement showing a genuine limit of
Onyx of Oblivion for the first time. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated
in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The duelist the canal-district gambling houses sent to test him fought with no rhythm at all, and it
took Kanja most of the exchange to understand that this was the entire strategy.

Cadence Ruin found nothing to collapse, because the duelist never committed to a pattern long enough
to establish one — every strike arriving at a deliberately random interval, sometimes rushed,
sometimes held for an uncomfortably long pause, timed not against Kanja's own reactions but against
a private, arrhythmic count the duelist alone seemed to be keeping. Veil Piercer found no gap to
open, because the duelist carried no shield and no formation to see through, only an unpredictable
body moving through space in a way that defeated pattern-recognition by simply refusing to form one.

"You're not fighting me," Kanja said, three minutes in, already breathing harder than the fight's
pace should have demanded. "You're fighting the sword's own instincts."

"I'm fighting the only way I've ever found that works against it." The duelist didn't stop moving,
didn't settle into any stance long enough to be read. "Word travels. Your blade reads rhythm,
timing, coordination — the shape of an attack before it lands. So I decided years ago never to have
a shape at all. Doesn't make me better than you. Makes me the one fight your sword can't solve for,
because there's nothing in what I'm doing for it to solve."

The bout ran nearly twenty minutes, longer than any prior engagement the alias had produced, before
Kanja finally won it — not through Onyx's gifts, which found nothing reliable to exploit the entire
fight, but through plain accumulated fatigue: the duelist's arrhythmic method, whatever advantage it
bought against a sword built to read pattern, cost enormous physical effort to sustain, and simple
endurance eventually settled what technique couldn't.

"You beat me on legs, not blade," the duelist said afterward, winded and disarmed but oddly
satisfied. "That's the most honest loss I've had in three years of doing this. Most men who fight me
never even understand why they're losing. You worked it out in the first three minutes and still
had to out-last me instead of out-thinking me. I'll take that."

Kanja said little on the walk back, turning the fight over privately — the first time since
recovering Onyx at seventeen that the blade's own gifts had offered him nothing decisive, and the
first real evidence that whatever the sword could do, it was not, in fact, an answer to everything.
