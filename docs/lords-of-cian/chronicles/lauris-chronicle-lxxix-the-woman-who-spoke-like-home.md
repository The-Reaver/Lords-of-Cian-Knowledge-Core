# Lauris Chronicle LXXIX: The Woman Who Spoke Like Home

*Locked canon, Batch 300, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1696`). Seventy-ninth entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, a Strand D (Sealbound Directorate years) entry giving full-scene treatment to
Operation 18, the Verith engagement (previously only summarized at `MCD-180`), in the closing
stretch of the Established Hunter period. Dramatizes the contract as a direct consequence of what
Operation 13 had already taught her (`MCD-1642`, Chronicle XXV) about what "delivered alive" can
quietly become once a subject leaves her own hands, and the moment `MCD-180` already locks as "the
first engineered Karesian Lauris encountered alive, and the moment she stopped considering herself
the last of her kind on Cian." The deferred conversation with Val Mirel Kareth that `MCD-180` reserves
stays reserved here -- referenced only as a debt not yet called in, never dramatized. No new named
characters; Verith already locked. Zero collisions.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Established Hunter designation LL-001-S-018: "She spoke to me in a tongue I had
only ever heard read aloud from Threnarr transcripts by women three thousand years dead. I answered
her in the same tongue, because it seemed the least dishonest thing I could do before I ended her.
I do not call what followed mercy. I have not yet found the correct word."*

Operation 18 reached her filed as a straightforward pursuit contract, the kind her career had by
then made almost administrative in its familiarity: a Cian-born-presenting woman, alias Verith,
wanted for a string of violent confrontations with Directorate enforcement along the Korren
territories' southern approach, capability rated far beyond what her file's stated origin could
plausibly explain. Lauris has told me, more than once, that she nearly declined to read the briefing
in full before departure, expecting one more variation on a pattern her earliest contracts had
already taught her to anticipate. She read it in full anyway. She has also told me this is the
single habit of hers I should most credit for what she found.

She located Verith within nine days, in a fishing hamlet on the Voskharen approach, and observed for
two more before making contact — a delay her archive records, as it so often does by this point in
her career, as deliberate rather than cautious. What she watched, across those two days, unsettled a
category of assumption she had not realized she was still carrying: Verith's stance-work carried
cooperative-era Karesian combat forms, precise past what any secondhand transmission could plausibly
produce, and once, overheard through a shuttered window arguing with a fish-buyer over weight, Verith
lapsed without apparent awareness into a phrase of spontaneous cooperative-era Karesian vocabulary —
not recited, not performed, but spoken the way a person speaks a language they learned before they
learned to notice they were speaking it.

I want to record, because I believe Lauris would want it recorded honestly, that she did not
approach the engagement itself with anything resembling detachment. She has admitted to me that she
delayed a further half-day beyond what tactical necessity required, turning over what she had heard,
because the alternative explanation — that this was simply a woman who happened to carry old words —
had already, by then, stopped being credible to her.

The engagement, when it came, was neither long nor especially difficult by the standard her career
had set. Verith fought with real capability, Karesian-class density readings her own instrumentation
placed near six thousand times baseline at rest and better than double that under sustained output,
but capability without seasoning, the particular unevenness Lauris has described to me as belonging
to a body that has never had the centuries her own training demanded of it. She closed the distance
with the Aristocrat rather than the Spine of Dagon, judging the engagement one that called for
precision over weight, and ended it in under a minute.

What she did afterward is the part of the account I have asked her to walk me through more than
once, because I do not believe I have ever heard her describe a decision with less certainty attached
to it. The contract's terms permitted acquisition. Nothing in the brief required a killing strike.
And five operations before this one, at a naval captain's quarters aboard the *Iron Veth*, she had
delivered a man alive exactly as a contract specified, only to learn, weeks later and by her own
asking rather than the Directorate's telling, that alive had not meant what she assumed it meant once
he left her hands. She has told me that Drenneth's name came to her, unbidden, in the seconds before
she decided what to do with Verith — not as a memory she reached for, but as one that arrived on its
own and would not leave until she had answered it.

She chose the killing strike. She has recorded, in the entry she has since shown me in full, that
she does not believe this was mercy in any sense the word ordinarily carries, and does not believe it
was cruelty either. It was, she wrote, the only choice available to her that kept the ending inside
her own accounting rather than handing it to an institution that had already once shown her it did
not keep the same books she did.

*

Her post-engagement examination told her the rest, in fragments her own archive has since spent
considerable effort assembling into a coherent account: Verith was not Cian-born at all, but an
engineered Karesian, raised across roughly thirty-seven years within an undisclosed containment
program built from cooperative-era genetic source material — and raised, given the depth and fluency
of what Lauris had overheard, by instructors with living access to cooperative-era Karesian language
and discipline, not merely archival fragments of either. Somewhere, held by the same apparatus whose
edges she had been tracing since Operation 3, cooperative-era Karesian culture had not simply been
excavated. It had been, in some diminished but genuine form, kept alive and passed forward.

I have asked her whether Operation 18 changed how she understood her own singularity — the fact,
carried since Kares Prime's own fall and her own strange conception from it, that she had believed
herself, in some private and never fully spoken register, the last surviving thing of her particular
kind. She has told me only this, and I record it exactly: "I had believed I was the only ledger left
open. Verith proved there was at least one more page, kept by someone else's hand, in someone else's
book. I have not yet decided whether that makes the account less lonely or simply larger."

The question of whether she was right to end it as she did, she has deferred — deliberately,
explicitly, to a conversation with Val Mirel Kareth that has not yet occurred, and that she has told
me she does not intend to force before its own time arrives. Her closing note for Operation 18, the
shortest entry from this stretch of her archive that I have been permitted to read in full: *I am no
longer the last of my kind on Cian. I do not yet know what I am instead. I have filed the question
where I file everything I am not ready to answer: open, and mine alone to carry until I am.*
