# The Command Vehicle in the Formation

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LI, wave 17, closing the wave. Rebellion era. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Eight Crawlers held the defensive ring around the supply depot, identical in silhouette, identical in
plating from any distance a scout could safely hold — and somewhere among the eight sat the command
vehicle coordinating all of them, the one machine whose loss would scatter the rest without a battle
at all.

Kanja had disabled Crawlers by the dozen by now, sequentially, one after another, verifying each in
turn. This was the first time the answer wasn't disabling all eight at once but finding the one that
mattered among seven decoys built to look exactly like it.

"They all read the same on a passing glance," he said, moving along the ring's outer edge out of sight
of the gunners, Mafesto's listening awareness spread wide across the formation. "Every plate seam, every
axle tension, close enough to fool an eye. But a command vehicle carries more than the others — more
communication gear, more bracing to hold the extra weight steady, and bracing has its own signature no
amount of matching paint changes."

The seventh Crawler read almost identically to the other six. The fourth read wrong in a way too small
to notice without already knowing to look — a slightly heavier tension along its rear axle mounts,
consistent with additional unseen cargo lashed inside rather than the standard plating load the other
seven carried.

"That one," he said, and didn't touch the other seven at all.

Obsidian Malice's discharge went into the fourth Crawler alone, a single precise strike disabling its
drive train without a sound loud enough to alert the rest of the ring. Command orders stopped reaching
the other seven within minutes; without a coordinating voice, the formation broke apart into individual
crews making individual, uncertain decisions, and the depot's garrison walked out through gaps that had
been a defensive ring an hour before.

"You could have taken all eight," a fighter said afterward, watching seven undamaged Crawlers sit
abandoned where their crews had left them. "Why just the one?"

"Because eight readings costs eight times the risk of being seen, and this ring only ever needed one
thing removed to stop being a ring at all," Kanja said. "I've spent this whole doctrine learning to
tell a true signature from a false one. This was the first time the question wasn't true or false — it
was which true signature actually mattered, hiding in plain sight among seven that didn't."
