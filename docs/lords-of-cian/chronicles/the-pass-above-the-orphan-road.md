# The Pass Above the Orphan Road

*Locked canon, Batch 264, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1367`). Captain Alias Chronicle LXVII, wave 23. A detailed Long-Mask-era gear showcase defending a war-orphan resettlement caravan on a mountain pass. Corrected Batch 314, 2026-09-28: an earlier draft of this entry mistakenly used Trinity-era gear (Mafesto, Obsidian Malice, and Onyx of Oblivion's Whisper of Shadows) despite this Chronicle's placement deep in the 284-year Long Mask, well after the Trinity's already-locked age-30 vault surrender (`MCD-246`) -- corrected below to the Forge-Coat/Ironfall Boots, Ironhand Gauntlets, Sovereign Eyes, Smoke System, and the Rexmar Machete, per `ARS-344` through `ARS-356`.*

---

The resettlement office had no ships and no soldiers to spare, only a list of forty-one children with nowhere left to walk but a single overland pass through terrain none of them had ever seen, and a rumor that the same opportunist bands who'd hit the Pier Nine granary run were working the high road now, further from any coin the Trust could offer to stop them. Kanja took the job before Efa Gol had finished reading the letter aloud.

The pass was narrow, switchbacked, and cold in a way none of the coastal engagements had ever been, wind cutting sideways off exposed rock with nothing but a thin caravan line of children and handlers strung along it for half a mile. Corren Halst walked the column's spine, keeping the pace even so no single stretch was ever undefended for long. Pell Ostra had rigged nothing explosive this time — the risk of rockfall onto the column itself ruled it out — and instead laid quiet deadfall traps along the only two approach lines a raiding party could use without exposing themselves on open scree.

They came from above at the pass's narrowest point, eleven men working ropes down a face they'd clearly scouted in advance. Kanja read the rock face before the first rope even went taut — the same instinctive Rexmar-Mar sense for terrain and intent that had carried him through every engagement long before any gear existed to help it, sharpened now by the Sovereign Eyes' low, patient glow picking out handholds and breath-sound against wind that should have hidden everything. The Forge-Coat's plating and the Ironfall Boots' grounded, impact-sole stance let him catch the first man's dropped grapple and throw the force back up the line through his own braced weight, snapping two ropes clean before their owners had descended twenty feet; one leveraged strike from the Ironhand Gauntlets — trained technique through riveted plate, nothing more — sheared rock low and angled, sending a controlled slide across the only approach still open rather than into the column itself. Kanja closed the last three personally, the Rexmar Machete doing the rest: an excellent blade in a swordsman's hands, footwork built for balance on a slope no ordinary fighter could hold, every strike disarming, the Smoke System's low haze covering his approach so none of the three saw him coming until it was already over.

Not one child on that caravan saw more than distant shapes moving fast against grey stone. They reached the resettlement house by dusk, cold and tired and entirely unaware how close the pass had come to going differently, and Corren Halst logged the engagement afterward under a line of her own: eleven turned back, zero children touched, the only number that had ever mattered to this particular job.
