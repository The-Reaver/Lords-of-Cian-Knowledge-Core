# The Pass Above the Orphan Road

*Locked canon, Batch 264, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1367`). Captain Alias Chronicle LXVII, wave 23. A detailed full-Trinity mountain-pass combat showcase defending a war-orphan resettlement caravan.*

---

The resettlement office had no ships and no soldiers to spare, only a list of forty-one children with nowhere left to walk but a single overland pass through terrain none of them had ever seen, and a rumor that the same opportunist bands who'd hit the Pier Nine granary run were working the high road now, further from any coin the Trust could offer to stop them. Kanja took the job before Efa Gol had finished reading the letter aloud.

The pass was narrow, switchbacked, and cold in a way none of the coastal engagements had ever been, wind cutting sideways off exposed rock with nothing but a thin caravan line of children and handlers strung along it for half a mile. Corren Halst walked the column's spine, keeping the pace even so no single stretch was ever undefended for long. Pell Ostra had rigged nothing explosive this time — the risk of rockfall onto the column itself ruled it out — and instead laid quiet deadfall traps along the only two approach lines a raiding party could use without exposing themselves on open scree.

They came from above at the pass's narrowest point, eleven men working ropes down a face they'd clearly scouted in advance. Onyx's Whisper of Shadows read the rock face itself before the first rope even went taut, picking out handholds and breath-sound against wind that should have hidden everything. Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System caught the first man's dropped grapple and threw its own momentum back up the line, snapping two ropes clean before their owners had descended twenty feet; Obsidian Malice discharged once, low, angled to shear rock into a controlled slide across the only approach still open rather than into the column itself. Onyx closed the last three personally, blade-work built for balance on a slope no ordinary fighter could hold, every strike disarming.

Not one child on that caravan saw more than distant shapes moving fast against grey stone. They reached the resettlement house by dusk, cold and tired and entirely unaware how close the pass had come to going differently, and Corren Halst logged the engagement afterward under a line of her own: eleven turned back, zero children touched, the only number that had ever mattered to this particular job.
