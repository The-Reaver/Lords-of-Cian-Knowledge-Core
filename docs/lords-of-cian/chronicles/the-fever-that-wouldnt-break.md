# The Fever That Wouldn't Break

*Locked canon, Batch 216, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-942`). The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XLIX, wave 17. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The fever came the way canal fevers usually came — through the drinking cisterns at Halworth Reach,
after a week of standing water nobody had gotten around to draining. It took eleven workers off their
feet within three days. It took Kanja on the fourth.

He tried to work through the first day of it, the way he tried to work through everything, and made
it as far as insisting the afternoon's tally count still needed checking before Corren Halst physically
took the ledger out of his hands and told him, without much patience left for argument, that a man who
couldn't stand upright wasn't going to be the one catching arithmetic errors.

By the second day he couldn't have argued if he'd wanted to. The fever ran hot enough that Danne Sok
sat up through two full nights changing the cloths on his forehead, and Maret Vos, who had never once
in his life nursed anyone, learned in the space of an afternoon how to make a broth that would stay
down. Nobody outside the immediate crew was told what was actually wrong. As far as the five districts
knew, the Trench Monarch was simply occupied elsewhere for a stretch.

It was the longest he had gone, since Callum Breck first chalked a name onto a captured officer's
forehead, without doing anything that could be attributed to that name at all.

On the fifth night, lucid again if not yet strong, he asked Halst what had happened at Halworth Reach
while he was down. Halst told him the eleven other cases were resting, that two of the worst had come
close to real danger before turning the corner, and that nobody had thought to boil the cistern water
first because nobody up the chain had ever told them to.

"That's not a fever problem," Kanja said, hoarse. "That's a habit nobody's ever had a reason to build."

He was on his feet within the week, weaker than he wanted to admit, and the first thing he did wasn't
correct a tally or negotiate a wage. He walked Halworth Reach cistern by cistern with two workers who
knew the water system better than he did, and by the end of the day there was a standing rule, posted
on a board by the largest cistern in a hand plain enough for anyone to read: water drawn for drinking
gets boiled first, no exceptions, no matter whose shift it falls on.

"You nearly died of something a boiled pot would've stopped," Vos told him afterward, not unkindly.

"So did eleven other people, and nobody wrote a pamphlet about any of them either." Kanja looked at
the posted rule, still a little unsteady on his feet. "I don't need the sword for this one. I don't
need the reputation for it. I just needed to have been sick enough, once, to actually go look at why."

The rule outlived the fever season by years. Garren Hask, when he heard the full account later, added
one line to the ledger that had nothing to do with wages: *Halworth Reach, cistern policy, adopted
after the alias nearly lost its own man to something no enemy caused. Recorded because it worked, and
because it almost didn't have the chance to.*
