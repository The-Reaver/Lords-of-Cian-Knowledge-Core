# The Officer Who Refused to Sail Into It

*Locked canon, Batch 192, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-585`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XL, wave 14 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The dissent came from inside Kanja's own command structure this time, not from an enemy or a wavering
ally — a veteran naval officer, long-serving and well respected among the crew, who refused a direct
order to sail a squadron into a storm-timed approach he considered reckless regardless of whatever
forecast backed it.

"I've served on this water for twenty years," he said, in front of enough of the crew that the
challenge couldn't be quietly resolved. "I've seen forecasters wrong before. I've seen good ships lost
trusting a chart over their own captain's eyes. I won't sail my crew into a storm on another man's
say-so, no matter how many times he's been right before."

"You're not required to trust the forecast," Kanja said. "You're required to follow the order, or to
refuse it formally and accept what that costs you. Those are different things, and I'd rather you pick
one honestly than dress a refusal up as caution."

The officer refused it formally. Kanja reassigned the approach to a different squadron rather than
force the issue, and the reassigned squadron completed the storm-timed maneuver successfully, exactly
as forecast.

It would have ended there, a straightforward matter of insubordination handled without incident,
except that three days later a genuine local hazard — a shoal the forecast hadn't and couldn't have
accounted for, invisible to Sephtis's method entirely — nearly cost the reassigned squadron a ship the
officer's own decades of hands-on knowledge of that exact stretch of coast would have flagged
immediately.

"I was still wrong to refuse the order," the officer said afterward, once the near-miss had been
avoided through luck rather than judgment. "But I wasn't wrong that the forecast doesn't see
everything. I'd like the chance to prove that's worth something, instead of just being remembered as
the man who doubted you."
