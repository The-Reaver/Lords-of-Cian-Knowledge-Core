# The Captains Who Chose a Side

*Locked canon, Batch 185, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-540`). The Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias
Chronicle XIII, first entry in the fifth wave. Rebellion era, age 21, Ghost Harbor. Not a territory
Chronicle. New standalone material — a detailed, large-scale coalition naval battle per Abad's craft
instruction. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Three Trust-commissioned captains, ordered to intercept the ghost fleet as part of a larger combined
operation against a Shattered Kingdoms raiding squadron, made their own decision mid-engagement once
they saw which side was actually protecting merchant shipping and which was using the chaos to loot
it — breaking from their own orders to fight alongside the fleet they'd been sent to hunt.

What followed was the largest combined naval engagement of Kanja's Rebellion-era career.

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System coordinated the flagship's own maneuvers with three unfamiliar
captains' vessels in real time, absorbing the raiding squadron's opening volleys while reading each
defecting ship's own capabilities well enough to direct them into positions that amplified rather
than duplicated the flagship's strengths. Obsidian Malice discharged twice against the raiders' own
lead vessels, disabling rather than sinking, while Onyx of Oblivion led a boarding action against the
squadron's command ship, Cadence Ruin and Whisper of Shadows clearing a path the three allied
captains' own crews finished securing.

The raiding squadron broke within two hours, six vessels captured, the merchant convoy it had been
hunting entirely unharmed — a combined force of four captains and crews who'd never trained together,
coordinated entirely through the storm of the engagement itself rather than any shared command
structure built in advance.

"We were ordered to hunt you," one of the defecting captains said afterward, watching his own crew
work alongside the ghost fleet's without friction. "I don't regret disobeying that order. I regret
that it took watching you in action to understand which side actually deserved it."

"You made the call that mattered when it mattered. That's worth more than regret over how long it
took to get there." Kanja extended no judgment for the delay, only genuine welcome for the choice
itself. "The Trust gave you orders. The water gave you the truth. I'm glad you chose to trust the
water."

The three captains' own reports back to the Trust, filed honestly despite the personal risk, became
the first formal crack in official denial of the fleet's actual conduct — three officers' own
testimony carrying weight no rumor ever could.
