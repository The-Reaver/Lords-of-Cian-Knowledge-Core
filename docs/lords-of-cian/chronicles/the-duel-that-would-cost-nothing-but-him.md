# The Duel That Would Cost Nothing But Him

*Locked canon, Batch 276, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1425`). Bane Alias Chronicle XCV, wave 32. First deliberate
full-Trinity stand-down: a garrison commander's wager duel fought entirely with a plain blade, no
Onyx, Mafesto, or Obsidian Malice engaged, distinct from the peer-level duel of "The Fight He
Couldn't Walk Away From" (`MCD-476`).*

---

The commander sent the offer under a truce flag three days into a siege he already knew he'd lose:
single combat, himself against Bane, on open ground between the lines, weapons only, no tricks. If
the commander won, the column withdrew and the siege ended. If he lost, the garrison surrendered
whole, every soldier walking out alive.

"He knows he's beaten," Corren Halst said. "This is him choosing how many of his men die finding that
out."

"Then let him choose it cheap," Bane said, and sent back one condition of his own: no gear beyond the
blade at his hip. Onyx stayed sheathed and silent. Mafesto and Obsidian Malice stayed with the column.
Whatever happened on that ground would happen to him alone, on nothing but what Valen Sinisterblade
had spent years putting into his hands before any of the rest of it existed.

The commander was good — trained young, disciplined, patient in a way most of Bane's opponents
weren't, since most of them had never had reason to test a blade against him without also testing
whatever else came with the name. He opened cautious, reading for an opening that wasn't there yet,
and Bane gave him nothing to read: no myth, no reputation, just a stance and a breath and the
specific quiet Valen had spent a year drilling into him before he ever went into the Rebellion at all.

The fight ran long. Neither man rushed it. The commander's footwork was clean enough to hold even
ground for the better part of twenty minutes, testing angles, retreating when a line closed too
tight, conserving. Bane matched him breath for breath rather than force for force — not because he
couldn't have ended it faster with power in reserve, but because that reserve wasn't on the table
today, and the commander deserved a fight decided by what was actually there. It came down, in the
end, to who tired first and who noticed it soonest: the commander's guard dropped a fraction on his
back leg in the twenty-third minute, an old fatigue tell, and Bane's blade was at his throat before
the man had finished correcting it.

He didn't take the kill. He hadn't planned to.

"You held your ground a long time," Bane told him, lowering the blade. "That's not nothing. It's why
your men walk out standing instead of carried."

The commander, breathing hard, looked at the sheathed sword at Bane's hip rather than at his face.
"They tell stories about what you carry. Nobody tells stories about how you actually fight."

"They should," Bane said. "It's the only part of any of it that was ever mine to begin with."

The garrison marched out under its own colors an hour later, weapons surrendered, no further blood
spent on either side.
