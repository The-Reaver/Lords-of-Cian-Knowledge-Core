# The Engineer Who Learned to Read Weather Like Ground

*Locked canon, Batch 192, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-584`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XXXIX, wave 13 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The siege engineer who had questioned Sephtis's hedged confidence before the crossing sought him out
afterward, not to apologize for the doubt, but to ask a genuine professional's question.

"I spend my life reading ground," he said. "Where it'll hold, where it won't, where a foundation looks
solid and isn't. I never thought to read weather the same way until I watched that river do to a
garrison's earthworks what I've spent twenty years doing to walls with a battering ram. How much of
what you do is actually the same skill I already have, pointed at the sky instead of the dirt?"

"More than either of us probably realized before today," Sephtis admitted. "I read patterns building
over time and try to say what they'll become. You read a foundation's weight and stress and say where
it'll fail first. Different material. Might be the same underlying habit of mind."

They spent the following weeks comparing notes in a way neither had approached forecasting or
engineering before — Sephtis learning to think about a coming storm's force the way the engineer
thought about a wall's load-bearing limits, the engineer learning to factor weather timing into siege
planning as deliberately as he already factored ground composition.

"I'm not going to become a sky-reader," the engineer said, at the end of it. "That's not what this
was. But I don't think I'll ever plan a siege again without asking what the sky's going to do to the
ground I'm planning to fight on. You taught me that's not a separate question from the one I already
know how to ask."
