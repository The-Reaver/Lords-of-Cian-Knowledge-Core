# The Storm That Never Came

*Locked canon, Batch 263, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1346`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LXXIII, wave 25, first entry in the wave. The senior third-generation student calls a major storm and moves three fleets to avoid it; the storm veers and dissipates, the doctrine's most expensive false positive yet.*

---

Every sign had been there -- pressure dropping the way it dropped before the worst systems, the specific gull-tightness the northern pilot's own tradition had taught the school to weigh seriously, a cross-check from the rival fleet's own forecaster that agreed independently before either side compared notes. Three fleets moved on the call: a merchant convoy rerouted a full week's sailing out of its way, a fishing fleet held in harbor through what should have been its best week of the season, and a Trust naval exercise cancelled outright.

The storm never formed. Whatever had been building offshore broke apart against a cold current nobody's model had accounted for, and the sky that was supposed to be unsurvivable by evening was, by evening, ordinary and clear.

The cost wasn't abstract. The merchant convoy's rerouting cost real coin and a missed delivery window that broke two contracts. The fishing fleet's lost week meant a genuinely leaner season for harbors that had trusted the call without hesitation. The cancelled exercise cost the Trust nothing but pride, which turned out to matter more than the coin, because pride was what the complaints were actually made of.

The student didn't defend the call the way her instinct wanted to. She logged it plainly, the same afternoon the sky cleared, in the same hand and the same format as every hit the ledger had ever recorded -- date, conditions, the specific readings that had informed it, and beneath all of it, underlined the way Sephtis himself had always underlined his own honest misses: *Called correctly on available signal. Signal proved incomplete. Cost: real, not hypothetical. Correction: the cold-current interaction is not yet in the model. It is now.*

"You could soften that," the retired successor said, reading it over her shoulder.

"I could," the student said. "Sephtis never did. I'm not starting now, on the one that actually costs something."

Kanja found her afterward on the same harbor wall the retired successor so often chose, and didn't offer comfort so much as company. "This is the expensive one," he said. "Not the storm you missed. The one you called and it didn't come."

"I know which one costs more," she said. "A miss where I say nothing happens, people forgive fast. A miss where I move three fleets and nothing happens -- that one, they remember at every dock for a decade." She looked at the ledger page, still open, ink still drying. "Good. Let them remember it. The remembering is what makes the next call worth something when it matters."
