# What Held the Causeway

*Locked canon, Batch 263, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1353`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LXXX, wave 27. A detailed Long Mask-era gear showcase defending Ghost Harbor's main evacuation causeway from storm-surge collapse while it is still crowded with fleeing civilians. Corrected Batch 321, 2026-10-02: an earlier draft staged this as a full-Trinity showcase, despite this Chronicle's placement well within the 284-year Long Mask era, after Kanja's already-locked age-30 surrender of the Trinity to its sealed vault (`MCD-246`); replaced with his correct Long-Mask-era kit (`ARS-344` through `ARS-356`, plus the Rexmar Machete). Also corrects a mischaracterization that attributed Obsidian Malice to Onyx of Oblivion -- it is a war club (`ARS-030`) and Kanja's own weapon, not one of Onyx's named powers.*

---

The causeway was never built for a storm this size, and everyone who'd helped design the evacuation route had known it going in -- it was simply the fastest way off the harbor's lowest district, and the fastest way had to be good enough, because the alternative was leaving that district for last.

It held for two days of building surge exactly as the older engineering promised it would. On the third day, an hour before the last scheduled crossing, a support pylon on the causeway's seaward side gave way under a wave taller than the structure's own designers had planned for, and the whole span began to list with several hundred people still crossing it.

Kanja was already moving before the list became visible to anyone on the causeway itself, the student's own reading having flagged the exact hour the surge would peak against the structure's known tolerances. The Forge-Coat and Ironfall Boots took the pylon's own failure and redirected the load sideways into the causeway's still-sound supports, buying the span minutes it had no engineering right to have. The Ironhand Gauntlets struck beneath the listing section, not to break anything but to displace enough water to ease the pressure driving the collapse, a controlled venting rather than a strike meant to harm. The Smoke System carried Kanja the length of the crossing twice in the time it took the crowd to understand what was happening, guiding the last stragglers toward the solid half of the span while his own instinctive Rexmar-Mar tactical sense timed each surge's own brief lull to move the most vulnerable -- children, the injured, an old man who'd refused to leave without his whole family -- across in the safest possible window.

The causeway lost its seaward half entirely within the hour, collapsing clean into the harbor once the last crossing had cleared it. Not a single person on it when it fell.

"You held it just long enough," the harbor magistrate said afterward, watching the wreckage from solid ground, "and not a moment past what it needed."

"That's the whole trick," Kanja said. "Not holding forever. Holding exactly as long as the people on it needed it to." He looked at the reading the student had made three days earlier, the one that had given the city its warning in the first place. "She bought you the three days. I only had to buy the last hour."

The student, arriving to confirm the evacuation's final numbers, found every soul from the lowest district accounted for, and understood, watching the ruined causeway, that the reading and the rescue had never really been two separate efforts -- only two halves of the same three-day argument for taking the sky seriously before it proved itself right.
