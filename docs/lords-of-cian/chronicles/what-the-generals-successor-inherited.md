# What the General's Successor Inherited

*Locked canon, Batch 197, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-734`). The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XXXIX, wave 13 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The old general had died quietly of natural causes the previous winter, his career's second act as a
respected academy lecturer outlasting by several years the disgrace that had first defined it, and his
successor — a younger officer who had studied under him during the doctrine's own institutionalized
curriculum — arrived at the border engagement carrying, deliberately, a copy of the original report
that had once ended a career and eventually redefined one.

"He talked about you more than he talked about the tactics," the successor said, meeting Kanja under
the same kind of informal parley the old general had once risked. "Not with hatred. I want to be clear
about that, since I imagine you have reason to expect it. With something closer to respect he never
quite managed to explain fully, even to his own students."

"What did he tell you, in the end?"

"That the doctrine isn't a trick to be countered. It's an honest account of how things actually hold
together, applied by someone patient enough to keep listening long after everyone else assumes
they've heard enough. He said the mistake his whole institution kept making was treating that as a
flaw in the doctrine rather than a flaw in how carefully they were willing to look at their own work."

Kanja considered the younger officer for a long moment. "And what do you intend to do with that,
now that it's yours to carry instead of his?"

"Honestly — I don't know yet. I know I don't intend to spend a career pretending the report was wrong
just because it's inconvenient to have been right the first time. He taught me that much, if nothing
else." The successor held out the report, not as a challenge, simply as an acknowledgment. "He wanted
you to know it reached someone who'd actually keep reading it, once he couldn't defend it himself
anymore."

"Tell him — " Kanja paused, recalibrating the thought. "Tell whoever remembers him that it did. That's
the only message that matters here."
