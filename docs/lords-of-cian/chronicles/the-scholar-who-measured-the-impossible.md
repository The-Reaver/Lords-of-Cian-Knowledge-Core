# The Scholar Who Measured the Impossible

*Locked canon, Batch 124, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-421`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle VI, closing the
second wave. Rebellion era, from the perspective of a Trust-employed materials scholar studying the
resonance phenomenon academically. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The materials scholar assigned to formally study the Iron Bastard phenomenon had spent eleven years
publishing careful, unglamorous papers on structural fatigue in ordinary Trust equipment, and
approached the assignment the way she approached everything — as a problem with a physical
explanation, however inconvenient that explanation might prove to be for the officers hoping she'd
find a clean fix.

"Command wants me to tell them it's a weapon they can shield against," she told her own research
assistant, several months into a study that had produced an uncomfortable, unwelcome quantity of
consistent data. "I can't tell them that, because it isn't a weapon in the sense they mean. It's a
correct description of a real physical property every engineered structure already has, that nobody
ever bothered to weaponize before because nobody else has ever had the specific combination of
sensitivity and control this man apparently does."

"Is there a defense, then? Something command can actually use?"

"The only real defense is structural redundancy so extreme it stops being cost-effective to build —
enough independent load paths that no single resonance failure cascades into total structural loss.
We could theoretically build it. It would cost roughly four times what any current Trust vehicle
costs, weigh twice as much, and still be vulnerable in principle, just slower to fail." She set down
the latest data set, eleven months of careful, honest measurement that told a story nobody above her
wanted published as written. "The politically acceptable answer is that this is some kind of
forbidden or supernatural weapon. The actual answer is that it's applied physics, unusually well
understood by one specific person, and the only real defense is admitting that and engineering
around it honestly instead of hoping for a countermeasure that doesn't require changing how we build
things."

Her final report, submitted with full data and full honesty despite pressure from three separate
commanding officers to soften its conclusions, was quietly shelved rather than acted on — the
politically expedient explanation being cheaper, in the short term, than the genuinely expensive
structural changes her research actually recommended.

"They'll keep losing Crawlers to it," she told her assistant, once the report's fate was clear. "Not
because the answer wasn't available. Because the answer was inconvenient, and inconvenient answers
have a much harder time reaching the people who could actually act on them than comfortable ones do.
That's not a flaw in the physics. That's just a flaw in every institution I've ever studied a problem
for."
