# What the Smallest Boat Carried

*Locked canon, Batch 258, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1210`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
LXXII, wave 24, closing the wave. An orphaned stowaway becomes the seed of an informal, fleet-wide
fostering practice. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The stowaway turned up in the smallest boat the fleet had ever taken as a prize — a single-hand skiff
seized off a slaver's tender, empty of cargo, empty of crew, or so the boarding party thought until a
six-year-old girl climbed out from under a false floorboard, silent, filthy, and clearly used to
staying that way.

Nobody could account for her. The slaver crew, once questioned, claimed no knowledge of a child
aboard at all, which told Garren Hask everything he needed to know about how she'd been meant to be
sold — hidden, unlisted, off any manifest that might have made her someone's responsibility. Pell
Ostra found her first, curled in the skiff's bilge, and didn't try to pull her out so much as sit down
nearby and wait until the girl decided the space between them was safe enough to close on her own.

It took three days before she spoke at all, and when she did it was to Efa Gol, of all the crew, in a
single flat sentence: she didn't have anywhere to go. Efa Gol brought it to Kanja plainly, the way she
brought every hard problem the fleet's doctrine hadn't specifically anticipated. "We've freed hundreds
of captives and put them ashore somewhere safer than where we found them. This one doesn't have a
somewhere."

There was no grand decision made, no ceremony, nothing Garren Hask could log as a single clean event
the way he logged a battle or a rescue. The girl simply stayed — first on *The Audit*, under Pell
Ostra's informal watch, then moved between all three ships as the crew's own rotation allowed, fed and
taught letters by whoever had the time, claimed by no single person and unofficially looked after by
everyone.

"We're not equipped to raise a child," Danne Sok pointed out, not unkindly, months in, watching her
trace a chart line under his patient correction.

"No," Kanja agreed. "But we're equipped to make sure she's never hidden under a floorboard again. The
rest we'll work out as it comes."

Garren Hask, still uncertain exactly what category of ledger entry a found child belonged under,
settled on a new one entirely — not cargo, not captive, not crew. Just a name, and the date she
stopped needing to hide.
