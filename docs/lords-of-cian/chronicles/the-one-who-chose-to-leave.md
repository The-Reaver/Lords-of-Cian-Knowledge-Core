# The One Who Chose to Leave

*Locked canon, Batch 281, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1475`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle C, wave 34, first
entry. Age 198, V3 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Rowan Vail had run decoy lines under Efa Gol for thirty-one years, capably enough that no one, least
of all Kanja, had ever once questioned putting a raid's most exposed diversion in her hands. She asked
to speak with him alone, after a rescue that had gone as cleanly as any in memory, and said what she'd
apparently been carrying for longer than she let on: she wanted to be reassigned, permanently, away
from anything that still required frightening people to save them.

She wasn't leaving the crew, and she made that plain first, before he could mistake the request for
one thing when she meant another. What she could no longer square, after three decades of it, was the
part of the work that depended on the reputation's fear landing exactly as hard as it was built to
land — the demonic-eyed glow, the smoke, the silhouette engineered across two centuries to be as
frightening as it was effective. She'd seen what that fear did to guards who surrendered before a
shot was fired, and she'd started to see, more slowly, what it cost to be the hand that kept building
it bigger. She wanted the settlement work instead — placement, resettlement, the unglamorous ground
work of getting freed people somewhere safe to actually live, work that asked nothing of anyone's
fear at all.

He didn't argue that the fear was worth less than she'd come to think, and he didn't defend it either.
What he told her, instead, was that she wasn't wrong to want something built without it, and that the
crew needed that work done as much as it needed hers had been. Efa Gol, told afterward, said only that
she'd wondered for years when someone would finally say the quiet part of the doctrine's cost out
loud, and that Rowan Vail saying it without bitterness, and without asking anyone else to agree with
her, was its own kind of proof the crew was still a place people could leave honestly rather than
just wear out inside of.

She transferred within the month. Garren Hask's ledger noted the change without comment, the way it
noted every reassignment — except that this was the first line in decades marking someone stepping
away from the reputation itself on principle, rather than age, injury, or death.
