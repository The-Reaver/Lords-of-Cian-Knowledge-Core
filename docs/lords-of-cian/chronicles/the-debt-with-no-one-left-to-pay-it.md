# The Debt With No One Left to Pay It

*Locked canon, Batch 214, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-926`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle LI, wave 17. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The old man waited until the line had thinned to almost nothing before he sat down across from Kanja,
the way people did when they'd already decided the answer wouldn't be worth the wait but had come
anyway.

"I worked the Halloway smelting concern for thirty-one years," he said. "Underpaid the whole time, by
my own count and my father's before me. I can tell you the figure near exact."

Ezio had the district's surviving records spread across the table within the hour, and the trouble
declared itself almost as fast as the old man's account had. The Halloway concern had dissolved
nineteen years earlier — sold off in pieces, its debts settled or abandoned in a bankruptcy proceeding
so old the paper had gone soft at the folds. The owner who'd signed every wage ledger the old man
remembered had died eleven years before that. No heir had ever been traced. No successor firm held
so much as a legal thread back to the name.

"There's no one," Ezio said, turning the last surviving page over to be sure. "Not an owner, not an
estate, not a debtor of any kind left standing to collect from. Whoever owed him this died owing it,
and it died with them, legally speaking."

Kanja didn't tell the old man that first. He let him finish the account in full — every season, every
short payment, the exact wording of the foreman who'd explained each shortfall away at the time — and
wrote it down as carefully as he'd have written testimony from a debt he could collect on by evening.

"I know what you're going to say," the old man said, once he'd finished. "I've had thirty years to
work out there's nobody left holding the other end of this."

"There isn't. I won't tell you there's a path to being paid, because there isn't one, and you'd know
if I said otherwise."

"Then why write it at all."

"Because it happened, and it was wrong when it happened, and the fact that everyone who did it to you
is dead doesn't make either of those things stop being true. The book doesn't only exist to collect
debts. Sometimes it exists so that thirty-one years doesn't just disappear because no one's left to
answer for it."

The old man sat with that for a while, watching Ezio finish the entry — full figure, full account,
marked plainly at the top in a hand the crew had started using only for cases like this: *Recorded.
Unrecoverable. True.*

"That's not nothing," he said finally, standing to go.

"It's not everything, either. I won't pretend it is."

*Continuity notes (not narrative): the first entry with total, permanent non-recoverability — not
because of jurisdictional dispute ("The Ledger That Couldn't Cross the Border," an undisputed debt
unenforced because no authority will claim it) and not because a search ends without result ("The
Debt That Was Never About Money," a fraud proven but the boy never found) — here the debt itself is
undisputed and the method works perfectly, but every party who could possibly answer for it has been
dead or dissolved for decades. Introduces the "Recorded. Unrecoverable. True." marking as a new
standing practice for this category of case, distinct from every prior resolved, unresolved, or
partially-resolved entry. No new named characters; the old man is unnamed and one-scene. Closes the
Industrial Myth's seventeenth three-Chronicle wave (with "The Ledger That Named the Wrong Man" and
"What Ezio Refused to Enter").*
