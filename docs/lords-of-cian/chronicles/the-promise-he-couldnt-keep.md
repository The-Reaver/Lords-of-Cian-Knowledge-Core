# The Promise He Couldn't Keep

*Locked canon, Batch 196, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-706`). Bane Alias Chronicle XLI, wave 14 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

He told the settlement at Karrow's Bend that he would return before the season's first frost with
enough of the column to hold the pass against the Directorate reprisal everyone knew was coming for
having sheltered rebellion wounded. It was not an idle promise. It was the specific kind of assurance
the "Already-Finished Negotiation" reputation had trained people to trust completely — that if Bane
said a thing would happen, it had, in some sense, already happened, and the waiting was only for the
world to catch up to it.

He did not return before the frost. A crisis three provinces distant — a liberation operation that
could not wait and could not be delegated in time — held the column past the date he'd given, and by
the time word reached him that Karrow's Bend had been unable to wait any longer for a promise that
wasn't arriving, the reprisal had already come and gone.

He reached the settlement eleven days late to a scene that told him plainly what eleven days had
cost: homes burned, four dead, a community that had trusted a specific date enough to stop preparing
contingencies of their own because the date had come from him.

"You said before the frost." The settlement's spokesperson didn't shout it. The quiet was worse. "We
believed you. Some of us stopped digging our own shelter positions because you said before the frost
and that's never once been wrong before."

"I know what I said. I know what it cost you to believe it." Bane didn't offer the crisis three
provinces distant as an excuse, though it was the truth of what had held him. "I'm not going to tell
you the reason was good enough to make this acceptable, because there isn't a reason that makes four
people acceptable. I told you something would happen, and it didn't, and I let you build your safety
around a promise instead of around your own preparation. That's mine. I won't make that mistake with
anyone else's safety again."

He rebuilt what could be rebuilt, stayed considerably longer than the original promise had asked of
him, and changed, permanently and without exception, the way he made assurances to anyone afterward
— never again a specific date, never again a certainty the reputation would let people stop
preparing around.

"The name's supposed to mean the thing already happened," the spokesperson said, much later, some of
the anger settled into something more like grief. "It doesn't always. I think I needed to hear you
say that plainly, more than I needed you to apologize for it."

"Then I'll say it plainly, every time, from now on. It doesn't always. Build your own shelter
anyway."
