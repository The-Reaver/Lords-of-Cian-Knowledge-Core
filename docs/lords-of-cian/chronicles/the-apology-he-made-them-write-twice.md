# The Apology He Made Them Write Twice

*Locked canon, Batch 256, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1153`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle LXIX, wave 23, closing the wave. The first entry where the ledger's remedy is neither wages nor a contract release but a publicly read apology -- and the first draft is rejected as self-serving before a second, honest one is accepted.*

---

The workers hadn't asked for money. What they wanted, stated plainly by the eldest of them on the first morning, was for the administrator to stand in front of the whole yard and say, in his own words, what he'd actually done — a public accounting of two years spent publicly humiliating slow workers by name, docking pay as punishment dressed up as productivity policy, and blaming a supply shortage he'd caused himself on the workforce's own laziness. The wage shortfall from the false docking was real and would be repaid regardless. But the thing the yard actually wanted, Ezio understood inside an hour, wasn't on any ledger line he knew how to write.

The administrator agreed almost too readily, which should have been the first warning. His written apology, read aloud to Ezio before it ever reached the yard, apologized for "any misunderstanding about performance expectations" and thanked the workers for their "patience during a difficult season," language built to sound contrite while assigning fault to nobody and describing nothing that had actually happened.

Ezio handed it back. "You didn't write an apology. You wrote a notice."

"It says I'm sorry."

"It doesn't say for what." Ezio laid the actual finding beside the draft, line by line — the docking dates, the public humiliations by name, the shortage he'd caused and blamed on them — and told him plainly that the yard had heard vague administrator language before and would hear the difference instantly. Kanja didn't add pressure to Ezio's correction. He didn't need to. What he did was make clear the crew would witness whichever version got read, honest or not, and record which one it had been.

The second draft named the docking by date. It named the shortage as his own ordering error, not the workforce's fault. It named, by description if not by name, the workers he'd singled out publicly, and apologized to each category of harm specifically rather than to the idea of harm in general. Reading it aloud took him longer than the coin repayment ever would have, and he stumbled twice, visibly uncomfortable in a way the smooth first draft had never made him.

The yard didn't cheer. Nobody expected it to. What the eldest worker said afterward, walking Ezio to the gate, was that the first apology would have changed nothing — they'd have gone back to work under a man who'd said sorry without saying for what, and within a season it would have curdled into resentment on both sides. The second one, clumsy and specific and plainly hard for him to say, was the first time in two years any of them had heard him describe what he'd done in words that matched what they'd actually lived through.
