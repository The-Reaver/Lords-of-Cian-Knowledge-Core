# What Ghost Harbor Remembered This Time

*Locked canon, Batch 263, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1354`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LXXXI, wave 27, closing the wave. Ghost Harbor, fully evacuated and largely intact, formally thanks the school; the fourth-generation apprentice takes a real field role during the crisis for the first time, paying off his low-stakes debut from wave 24.*

---

The storm itself, when it finally arrived, matched the student's reading within the hour -- the causeway's loss the only structural damage of consequence, every soul from the city accounted for, and a harbor that would need months to rebuild a crossing rather than years to rebuild a district full of homes.

The boy had been given a real task for the first time, not a supervised low-stakes reading but a genuine piece of the evacuation itself -- running confirmed-clear reports between the lowest district's block wardens and the student's own command post, hours of exhausting, unglamorous work that put him inside a crisis with real lives on the line instead of a fishing run with none.

"You did the job," the student told him afterward, "and you didn't once try to make it look like more than running messages. That matters more than you think."

"I wanted to help read the storm," he admitted. "Not just carry reports about it."

"You will, someday, on a call this size. Today wasn't about whether you were ready to read a storm like this. It was about whether you could be trusted with something that mattered while somebody else did." She thought of her own first real crisis, three days away from the retired successor at the time, forced into her first fully independent call. "Everyone's first real one looks different. Yours looked like this. That's not smaller. It's just first."

Ghost Harbor's magistrate held a formal thanks at the harbor's rebuilt square two weeks later, the causeway still a gap in the skyline, the city otherwise whole. She didn't name Kanja in the address, didn't name the student either, credited instead "the reading and the readers, whoever carried them" -- language the retired successor, listening from the crowd, recognized as the same anonymous institutional credit the Trust's own regulation had given the school two years earlier.

"They keep doing that," the student said afterward, half amused. "Crediting the work and never the names."

"Good," the retired successor said. "That was always the design, whether anyone meant it that way at the start or not." She looked toward the ruined causeway, already scaffolded for rebuilding. "This city remembers fire and siege. Now it remembers a warning it trusted, too, and a bridge that held long enough. That's a different kind of memory to carry alongside the old one."

Kanja, unnamed in the address as he'd been in every crisis before it, thought the evacuation had proven something about the doctrine that no single fleet rescue ever quite had: that it could be trusted with an entire city's worth of ordinary lives, not just the sailors and captains who'd always been its first and most natural audience.
