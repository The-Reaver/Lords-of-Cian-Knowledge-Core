# The Debt That Was Never About Money

*Locked canon, Batch 198, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-769`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XLIV, wave 15 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The woman waited until the line had thinned before she approached the table, and when Ezio asked the
familiar four words, she answered with a question of her own instead of a figure.

"Can your book find a person?"

Ezio set his pen down. "That's not what it's built for. I don't want to promise you something it
can't do."

"I know what it's built for. I've watched it for two days." She said it steadily, though the steadi-
ness cost her something visible to hold. "My son was taken against an indenture debt I never signed
for, three years ago, by a labor broker who claimed my late husband owed him for tools he never
bought. I couldn't prove the debt was false. I still can't. But your book found things nobody thought
could be found — records everyone said were lost, testimony nobody expected to be believed. I'm not
asking you to write down what I'm owed. I'm asking if the same patience that builds those ledgers
could be pointed at finding where he was sent."

Kanja didn't answer with a promise, because the method had never claimed to be a search, and he
wasn't willing to let her hope run further than what he could actually deliver. But he didn't turn
her away either. He asked Ezio to pull the same threads they used on every fraudulent debt — the
broker's own transaction records, cross-checked against tool deliveries that never happened, indenture
transfers logged at ports and labor exchanges up and down the coast — and pointed the whole patient,
unglamorous machinery of the ledger method at a person instead of a figure for the first time.

It took the better part of three weeks, run alongside the district's ordinary wage campaign rather
than instead of it, and it did not end the way either of them would have chosen to write it if the
choice had been theirs. The trail found the broker's fraud clearly enough — the tool debt was
fabricated exactly as she'd said — but the indenture transfer itself led to a labor exchange two
territories distant whose own records simply stopped, with no further trace of where the boy had been
sent from there.

"I'm sorry," Kanja told her, when the search reached its honest end. "I can prove the debt was a
lie. I can't tell you where he is."

"You proved the lie." She said it without the anger he might have braced for, closer to something
worn and grateful both. "Nobody else even tried that much. I've carried three years of being told
there was nothing to look into. Now I know, at least, that the reason I couldn't find him wasn't for
lack of anyone trying."

The broker's fraud went into the district ledger as its own entry, documented and unpayable in the
usual sense, exactly like every other debt the campaign had ever recorded. The boy's location never
did.
