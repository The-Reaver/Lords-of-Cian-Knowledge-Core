# The Successor's First Command Alone

*Locked canon, Batch 259, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1240`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LXXV, wave 25,
closing the wave. Age 270, V4 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose.*

---

Five years earlier, he'd handed her an entire raid — command, the hostage standoff, all of it —
while staying aboard as a failsafe nobody had needed to use. This time there was no ship for him to
stay aboard. Efa Gol's successor ran the operation from a hundred miles away, by messenger and prior
planning alone, while Kanja worked a wholly separate liberation on the far side of the same
stretch of coast, the two missions timed to strike within a day of each other and neither one
staged with him anywhere near enough to matter if hers went wrong.

It didn't go wrong. Word reached him four days later, secondhand, the way most of the crew's own
operations reached each other across distance and time: a depot cleared, fifty-two freed, a
near-discovery handled by rerouting the whole approach rather than forcing through it, and a
decision — hers alone, made in the field with no one to check it against — to leave a smaller,
lower-value target unraided that season rather than risk the larger rescue for a marginal gain. It
was exactly the call the doctrine would have made. It was also, distinctly, not a call anyone had
told her to make; she'd made it the way the doctrine was supposed to work once it no longer needed
its founder standing nearby to keep working.

When they finally crossed paths again, weeks later, he didn't ask her to walk him through the
decision. There was nothing in it that needed correcting. What he told her instead was smaller and,
in its way, harder to say plainly: that the raid five years back, the one with him watching from the
rigging, had been a test she hadn't known she was taking, and she'd passed it then. This one hadn't
been a test at all. It had just been the work, running the way it was supposed to run, with or
without him within reach of it.
