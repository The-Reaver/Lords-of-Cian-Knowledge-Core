# The White That Took the Map Away

*Locked canon, Batch 265, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1391`). Bane Alias Chronicle XCI, wave 31. New
environmental register -- a whiteout blizzard strips Sovereign Eyes' visual enhancement to nothing,
forcing a full-Trinity ambush defense to run entirely on sound, vibration, and Cadence Ruin.*

---

The storm hit two hours before the pass narrowed, and by the time the column reached the choke
point where the Directorate had been waiting for three days, there was no map left to read. Snow
came sideways hard enough to erase the column's own front rank from its rear, and the world had
gone the single flat color of nothing at all.

Sovereign Eyes had never failed him before. It failed him now, not through any defect in the gear
but through a plain, honest limit nobody had thought to test: there was nothing left in the white
for a predator's enhanced sight to find purchase on. Light-gathering meant nothing against a wall of
falling snow with no shape behind it. For the first time since the visor's earliest version, Bane
was, by every visual measure, as blind as anyone else on the mountain.

"Eyes are gone," he said to Corren Halst, flat, no alarm in it. "We fight this one on sound."

He'd never needed to say it as a plan before. It had always been true underneath everything else;
tonight it was the only thing left true.

Cadence Ruin found the ambush before a single blade was drawn — not sight of men in white cloaks
against white snow, which no eye alive could have picked out, but the wrongness in twelve sets of
footfalls holding still too long in a wind that should have scattered any sound evenly across the
whole slope. Stillness has its own rhythm, and rhythm was the one language the storm couldn't take
from him. He read the ambush's shape the way he'd have read a heartbeat: twelve positions, staggered
in two arcs, closing on a killing ground the column was already three strides from entering.

Mafesto redirected the first volley by feel as much as by anything else, each arrow's approach a
vibration through packed snow and frozen rock that Kinetic Transfer caught and threw wide before
Onyx's own senses had fully confirmed the shot was loosed. Obsidian Malice discharged twice into the
white without a visible target, aimed at sound alone — the crack of a bowstring, the specific weight
shift of a man bracing to charge — and both landed true. Not one strike in the entire engagement was
made against something Bane could actually see.

It ended the way it had to end in weather like that: not cleanly, not with a tally anyone could be
proud of, but with the ambush broken and scattered into a storm that would finish what the Trinity
hadn't, and the column moving again before the cold could claim anyone standing still too long to
celebrate it.

Danne Sok found him afterward, checking the edges of his own vision out of old habit before
remembering there was nothing left out there to check. "You fought that whole thing blind."

"I fought it without my eyes," Bane said. "That's not the same thing, and it's the part worth
remembering. The storm took one sense. It didn't take the rest, and it never gets to take all of
them at once." He looked out at white that still hadn't thinned. "Sovereign Eyes was never the
thing doing the fighting. It was just the easiest one to notice."
