# Lauris Chronicle XIV: What Velith Left in the Log

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1631`). Fourteenth entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, the seventh entry of Strand K, drafted as part of a 50-entry wave across eight
strand-agents under Abad's blanket authorization. Dramatizes directly, for the first time, Velith's
death in a Vask Threnarr defensive operation at approximately age 1,200 (`MCD-165`) and the writing
of the longest single archive entry Lauris has ever produced -- Lauris arriving three days after the
engagement, absent by training-calendar distance rather than choice, and choosing solitary,
accuracy-focused private record over the Sister-Hold's communal remembrance custom. No new named
characters. Full text below.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Vask Threnarr, age approximately 1,200: "Velith is dead. I was not there in time.
I record what I know, which is less than I want, and I will not pretend otherwise by filling the gaps
with what I wish were true."*

I have written, across these accounts, of a first page at eight and a first relocation at fourteen,
each time noting Velith's presence at the edge of the record -- the girl who read over Lauris's
shoulder, who asked the obvious question, who noted with more precision than any adult in the
Sister-Hold what a fourteen-year-old's calm concealed. I write now of the last entry in which Velith
appears at all, and I want to say plainly, before I set down what I know of it, that this is the
hardest of Lauris's Kares Prime pages I have yet transcribed -- not because it is long, though it is,
by a wide margin, the longest single entry she has ever written in an archive otherwise built almost
entirely from restraint.

Lauris was, at the time, resident at Vask Karth-Ven, some eleven hundred years into a training
regimen that by then ran largely without ceiling or ceremony, visiting Threnarr on the schedule the
two Vasks' training calendars allowed rather than at will. She was not present when the raiding
incursion reached Threnarr's outer defensive line -- I have found no record specifying who the
raiders were, only that Threnarr's own defensive complement, drawn in part from the cohort of
forty-seven children the Sister-Hold had by then raised into their second century of life, held the
line without her. Velith, among those defenders, did not survive the engagement.

Lauris learned of it three days later, by courier, at Karth-Ven, and departed for Threnarr within the
hour of receiving the message -- a detail I mention because it is, I believe, the first recorded
instance in her life of Lauris moving with visible urgency rather than her usual unhurried economy.
She has never, in my years transcribing her, described to me what those three days of not-knowing-yet
felt like, and I have not asked. Some absences in a record are themselves the record.

What she wrote on arriving is the entry I have called, elsewhere, the longest she has ever produced --
some four times the length of her account of the Vask Olmedrin defense, a battle in which she held a
line alone for eight hours against four hundred raiders. I will not reproduce it here in full; I do
not believe it is mine to reproduce in full, and I think Lauris herself, were she asked, would agree
that some pages are meant to be read once, by the person who kept them, and referenced rather than
repeated by everyone after. But I will set down what I believe is its true center, because I think it
explains more about her than any battle I have catalogued: the entry does not describe the
engagement. She was not present for it, and true to a discipline I have watched her keep for four
thousand years since, she refused to record as fact what she had not personally witnessed. Instead,
the entry catalogues Velith -- every exercise they had shared since age eight, every log Velith had
ever read over her shoulder, the exact wording of the question Velith had asked her on the day she
left for Karth-Ven, reconstructed from Lauris's own memory rather than any external record, because
Velith's own archive of those years had been lost with her.

The Sister-Hold's own condolence customs, by what I have gathered of Karesian practice, ran toward
communal remembrance rather than private grief. Lauris did not attend the communal remembrance held
at Threnarr for Velith and the other defenders lost in the same engagement. She wrote her own entry
instead, alone, in the room where she had once, as a child, been read over the shoulder by the girl
she was now writing about, and she returned to Karth-Ven three days after that, resuming her training
on the schedule Tiramen had set before the courier ever arrived.

I asked her once, carefully, whether she regretted not attending the remembrance. She told me, and I
believe this is the truest thing I have set down in this entire account, that she did not think
Velith's memory needed a room full of people to hold it. She thought it needed accuracy. And
accuracy, she told me, was the one gift she had always been certain she could give.
