# The Night the Tide Turned the Battle

*Locked canon, Batch 202, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-880`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XXXV, wave 12 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The coastal forge site sat on a tidal flat that flooded and drained twice daily on a schedule the
campaign's own crew had tracked for six days before the Trust detachment arrived to burn it — arriving,
by design, at low tide, when the flat's exposed mudbanks offered clear approach on foot and no water
to slow a retreat if the strike went badly.

Kanja let them commit fully before he moved.

He'd held the Trinity back through the detachment's entire approach, timing not their arrival but his
own response against the tide table the crew had spent six days confirming — engaging only once the
turning tide had begun its advance, slow at first, then faster, across the exposed flat the detachment
had chosen specifically to avoid.

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System converted the incoming water's own force into stored charge as it
had once converted floodwater and storm surge alike, using rising water as both cover and weapon.
Obsidian Malice discharged low across the flat, the shockwave traveling through the shallow incoming
tide to disrupt footing across the detachment's entire line at once. Onyx read the retreat the
detachment attempted through water rising faster than their own path back to dry ground could outpace
— Cadence Ruin closing avenues of escape the detachment hadn't accounted for when they picked their
approach.

The detachment broke and fled through knee-deep water, several boats grounded on the very mudbanks
they'd used for their approach an hour earlier, now submerged beneath a tide the forge crew had simply
waited out.

"You let them walk right up to us and didn't move," the site's watch-captain said afterward, still
working out the timing. "Six days of tracking the tide table, and you used every minute of it."

"They picked low tide because it looked safest to them. Didn't occur to them that safest and
temporary aren't the same thing." Kanja watched the water continue its advance, already reclaiming the
flat entirely. "The tide was always going to turn whether they planned around it or not. I just made
sure we were the ones using it when it did."
