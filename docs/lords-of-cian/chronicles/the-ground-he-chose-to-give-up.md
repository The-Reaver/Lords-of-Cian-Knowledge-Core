# The Ground He Chose to Give Up

*Locked canon, Batch 196, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-702`). Bane Alias Chronicle XXXVII, wave 13 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The Directorate committed three full companies to retaking the Orrin overlook, more than had ever
been thrown at a single position held by Bane's column, and for the first time since the Black
Trench the numbers alone made the honest answer plain before the engagement had properly begun:
holding the overlook past nightfall would cost more of the column than the overlook itself was
worth.

"We could hold another six hours," the column's second said, reading the same numbers Bane was
reading and arriving somewhere short of his conclusion. "Maybe longer, if we commit everything we
have left."

"We could. And in six hours we'd have nothing left to commit to the position after this one, which
matters more than this ridge does." Bane didn't dress the decision up as anything but what it was.
"This isn't a stand worth making. I know that's not the story people expect from this name. I'd
rather give them a column that's still alive to fight the next position than a ridge with my name
attached to a defeat I could see coming and chose to walk into anyway."

The withdrawal ran clean — a fighting retreat down the overlook's eastern face, covering fire timed
in relays, nothing dramatic enough to be remembered as anything but a column leaving ground it had
decided wasn't worth keeping. Three companies took the overlook by morning and found nothing waiting
for them but an empty position and the specific, hollow anticlimax of a victory that had cost them
nothing because there had been nothing left to take.

"They'll report it as a retreat," the second said, once the column had regrouped a safe distance
clear, the overlook already behind them. "The Directorate will call it your first loss."

"Let them call it whatever gets it off their own ledger fastest. I know what it actually was." Bane
watched the ridge recede behind the column, no visible regret in it. "A loss is when you didn't see
it coming and it costs you something you needed. This was seeing it coming and choosing what it
cost instead. Those aren't the same thing, even if they look identical from the outside."

The overlook changed hands twice more over the following year, contested by neither side with any
real conviction, exactly the kind of ground that had never been worth what either side kept
spending on it.
