# The Bridge He Refused to Blow

*Locked canon, Batch 230, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1026`). Bane Alias Chronicle LVIII, wave 20. Detailed
full-Trinity combat showcase under a precision constraint. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

Drennock Bridge was the only crossing wide enough to move nine hundred refugees before the season's
floods made the lower fords impassable, and it was also, for the moment, held by a Directorate
garrison dug into both banks with orders to burn it rather than surrender it. Bane's column needed it
standing. The garrison's whole doctrine assumed nobody would.

"Every plan we've run against a held bridge starts with taking it apart," Danne Sok said, studying
the enemy's own demolition charges laced through the support piers. "They've built their defense
assuming we'll do exactly that, or they'll do it themselves the second it looks lost."

"Then we don't take it apart. We take it." Bane was already reading the piers the way Onyx let him
read anything built to bear weight — where the stress ran, where a strike would matter and where it
would only look like it mattered. "Mafesto doesn't get to throw anyone through a support beam
tonight. Obsidian Malice doesn't get to open up anywhere near the charges. We fight this one like the
bridge is a wounded man we're trying to save, not a wall we're trying to break."

It cost him the fight's easiest answers all night. Onyx's Cadence Ruin, which could have folded the
near bank's line in under a minute at full intensity, had to be metered down to something that
dropped men without dropping the ground they stood on. Mafesto's kinetic transfer, tuned for maximum
force, spent the engagement redirecting momentum sideways along the bridge's own length instead of
through it, throwing soldiers off the span rather than into it. Obsidian Malice discharged twice,
both times aimed out and down into the water rather than into the piers a stray arc might have
reached. Twice a charge-runner made it within reach of the demolition lines before Bane closed the
distance himself, blade-first, faster than the Trinity's ranged answers could risk being.

By the time the garrison broke, past midnight, the bridge stood exactly as it had before the fighting
started — no new cracks in the piers, no charges triggered, the whole structure indifferent to what
had just happened across its own length. Efa Gol crossed it at dawn walking backward, counting
refugees as they passed, and didn't stop counting until the last one's feet left the planking on the
far side.

"You could have ended that fight twice as fast," Corren Halst said afterward, watching the crossing
hold under weight it had never been asked to bear before. "Nobody would have blamed you for the
bridge."

"I'd have blamed me." Bane didn't look away from the water. "A fast win that costs us the only
crossing for forty miles isn't a win. It's a loan against the next nine hundred people who need this
exact bridge and won't have us here to fight for it."
