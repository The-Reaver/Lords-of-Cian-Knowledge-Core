# The Conscripts Who Never Wanted the Fight

*Locked canon, Batch 185, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-541`). The Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias
Chronicle XIV. Rebellion era, age 21, Ghost Harbor. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone
material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The warship the fleet boarded expecting a standard Directorate crew turned out to be crewed almost
entirely by forced conscripts — coastal villagers pressed into service under threat to their own
families, given weapons they'd never trained with and orders they had every reason to resent. They
surrendered within minutes, several visibly relieved the fighting had ended before they'd had to
prove how little willingness stood behind their resistance.

"You could have taken this ship as a standard capture," the flagship's own quartermaster observed,
watching the conscripts' obvious relief. "Nobody would question the prize regardless of who was
crewing it."

"I could have. I don't think that's actually what happened here, and I'd rather account for it
honestly." Kanja moved among the surrendered conscripts himself, gathering their own accounts before
deciding anything about their fate. "These aren't Directorate loyalists who lost a fight. They're
villagers who were told their families would suffer if they didn't crew this ship. Treating that the
same as capturing a willing enemy would be its own kind of dishonesty."

He released them rather than hold them as prisoners of war — provisioned, given safe passage back
toward waters where they could return home without the warship that had marked them as combatants,
the vessel itself claimed as a genuine prize while the men who'd crewed it under threat walked free.

"Word of this will reach the Directorate," the quartermaster said. "They'll know you let an entire
crew go rather than hold them."

"Let them know it. I'd rather the story that spreads be the true one — that this fleet can tell the
difference between an enemy and a man forced into an enemy's uniform — than let ambiguity make us
crueler than we actually are." Kanja watched the released conscripts' own small boats pull away toward
home. "Every one of them will tell their own village what actually happened here. That account is
worth more to this fleet's reputation than a few dozen prisoners would ever be."

The released conscripts' own testimony, reaching their coastal villages within weeks, became one of
the quieter but most effective pieces of the fleet's growing reputation for restraint — proof, told by
men who'd had every reason to fear the ghost fleet and found mercy instead.
