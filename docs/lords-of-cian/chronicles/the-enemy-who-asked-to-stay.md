# The Enemy Who Asked to Stay

*Locked canon, Batch 191, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-559`). Captain Alias Chronicle XVII. Rebellion era. Not
a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters.*

---

A Directorate officer, captured after a prolonged engagement and given the standard choice of parole
or continued custody, asked instead for something the crew had never once been offered by a genuine
adversary: to join them outright, having watched enough of how the crew actually operated to decide
his own side no longer deserved his service.

The crew's own reaction split immediately between suspicion and cautious curiosity.

"He could be exactly what he says he is, or he could be exactly the kind of plant a smart enemy sends
in precisely because we'd want to believe the first version," the crew's own security lead said,
laying the risk out plainly. "I don't know how you tell the difference in the moment he's asking."

"You don't, not entirely. That's the honest answer." Kanja met the officer directly rather than decide
from a distance, weighing the man's own account against everything he could observe in it. "I'm not
going to pretend I can be certain of anyone's sincerity the first time they ask for trust. I can offer
a path that tests it honestly, the same way trust gets tested for anyone new to this crew — small
responsibility first, nothing that costs us if it turns out wrong, built up slowly rather than granted
all at once."

The officer accepted the terms without argument, spending months in exactly the kind of limited,
closely watched roles the crew extended to any genuinely unproven newcomer — no faster path, no
special consideration for having once worn the other uniform, earning trust the same slow way
everyone eventually had to.

"You could have simply refused him," the security lead observed, months later, watching the former
officer's trust grow the ordinary way trust always grew in this crew. "Fewer risks that way."

"Fewer risks, and also fewer chances for someone to actually change sides honestly, if that's really
what happened here." Kanja watched the former officer working alongside crew members who'd once been
his own targets. "I'd rather extend the same test to a former enemy that I'd extend to anyone else
asking for trust, and let the test itself decide, than assume the answer before I've actually let him
prove it either way."

The former officer's trust, built slowly and never rushed, eventually matched any crew member's own —
proof, in the end, that the test itself had worked exactly as intended, regardless of which answer it
might have produced.
