# What the Third Generation Refused

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Crow King Alias Chronicle XLVII, wave 16.
Rebellion era, sometime after the third generation of the lineage began teaching. Not a territory
Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters beyond the already-locked
apprentice, her student, and her student's student, all unnamed matching established pattern.*

---

The plan was simple enough on its own terms: a false signal placed to convince a garrison quartermaster
that a supply convoy had already been rerouted, drawing him away from his post long enough for the
real convoy to pass unchallenged. The apprentice's student had built it and brought it to her own
student — the third generation, only months into carrying real operational weight — to run alone as a
test of readiness.

The third generation refused to run it.

"He buried his son two weeks ago," she said, when asked why. "I asked around before I agreed to take
this. He's not negligent. He's grieving, and grief makes a man leave his post the way fear or greed
never could. I won't build a lie around that particular door just because it happens to be standing
open."

"It's not personal," the student said. "It's the door that's open. That's the entire craft — you find
the door that's open and you use it. You didn't ask this many questions the last three times."

"The last three times the door was open because someone was careless, or arrogant, or looking the
other way on purpose. This door is open because a man is in pain. I don't think those are the same
kind of opening, and I don't think the craft is supposed to stop asking which kind it's found just
because the difference is inconvenient."

The disagreement reached Kanja before either of them intended it to — the student bringing it to him
not to overrule her own student, she was careful to say, but because she genuinely didn't know which
of them was right, and hadn't for two full days of turning it over.

"When I built this," Kanja said, "I never drew that line as cleanly as she's drawing it now. I used
whatever opening presented itself and told myself the cause justified the means. I'm not certain I
was wrong, given what the cause was. I'm also not certain I was right."

"So which of us do you side with?"

"Neither, and both. I think the fact that she's willing to refuse an order from someone two steps
above her, on a question neither of you has ever had to answer together before, is exactly the kind
of thing I hoped would happen eventually. I didn't teach either of you a fixed rule about grief and
open doors. I taught you a method. She's the one actually deciding what the method is for." He let
that sit before continuing. "Find another door. There's always another door. That's the part of the
craft that doesn't change generation to generation — only which ones you're willing to walk through."

The convoy passed three days later, delayed but unchallenged, through a door the third generation
found herself: a rumor, true and harmless, about a shipment of replacement tools the quartermaster
had genuinely been waiting weeks to receive.
