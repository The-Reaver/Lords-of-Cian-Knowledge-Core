# The District Chain

*Locked canon, Batch 183, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-535`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XIV.
Rebellion era, age 19. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material — a detailed, sustained
month-long campaign per Abad's craft instruction, distinct from the single-district scope of every
prior Industrial Myth entry. Kept deliberately unarmed. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No
new named characters.*

---

Three connected mill districts, all owned by the same regional trading concern and all suffering
variations of the same wage suppression, had never once coordinated a joint response — each district's
own workers assuming their grievance was local, unaware the same owner was running an identical
suppression scheme across all three simultaneously.

Kanja spent a full month moving between them, building three separate tallies that turned out, once
compared, to be a single unified pattern.

The first district took a week to document fully. The second, once workers there learned the first
district's tally already existed, took less than four days — the method itself accelerating as trust
in it spread ahead of him. The third district's workers approached him directly before he'd even
finished the second, having heard the pattern was spreading and wanting their own account included
before the case was presented.

By the month's end, the three tallies combined into a single case no individual district's numbers
alone could have made: the same owner, the same suppression method, applied identically across three
supposedly unrelated districts — evidence of coordinated intent rather than three isolated disputes.

"You didn't just document three districts," the trading concern's own regional manager said, faced
with the combined case and unable to treat any one district's grievance as isolated any longer. "You
proved we were doing this on purpose, everywhere at once."

"I proved what the numbers actually showed, once they were finally allowed to sit next to each other."
Kanja reviewed the combined tally, a month of patient work distilled into a single undeniable case.
"Three districts assumed they were each dealing with their own bad luck. They weren't. They were
dealing with the same decision, made once, applied everywhere it could go unnoticed. The numbers
were always going to say that, the moment anyone bothered comparing them properly."

The settlement covered all three districts simultaneously, the suppression scheme ended everywhere at
once rather than piecemeal — the tally method's first horizontal, multi-district application, and its
largest single settlement to date.
