# What the Proof Couldn't Undo

*Locked canon, Batch 204, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-895`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XLVII, wave 16. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The weaving district's overseer put a woman out of her tenement two days after she testified, and he
did it in broad daylight, in full view of the tally table, as if daring the ledger to do anything
about it.

Her testimony had been clean — a wage debt so plainly documented against the overseer's own
production counts that Ezio had needed less than an hour to corroborate it, the kind of entry that
usually meant an administrator opened his books within the week rather than risk the district around
him seeing the numbers stand unanswered. This overseer didn't open his books. He waited two days,
found a tenancy clause unrelated to her wages, and used it to put her and her two children into the
street with an escort of hired men standing by to make sure no one interfered.

Kanja reached the tenement row after it was already done. There was no ledger entry that reversed an
eviction that had already happened, no figure Ezio could correct that would put a family back behind
a door that had already been barred against them.

"I told her testifying was the safe thing to do," Kanja said, to no one in particular, standing in
the street with her belongings already stacked on the cart a neighbor had lent her. "I was wrong
about that. Not about the debt. About what saying it out loud would cost her before the debt was
ever paid."

"You didn't put her out," Ezio said. "He did. On a clause that has nothing to do with anything we
documented."

"That's true and it doesn't fix anything tonight."

He didn't turn the tally table toward retaliation, and he didn't pretend the eviction hadn't
happened by folding it quietly into the wage ledger as one more entry among many. He entered it as
its own separate line — the debt, proven and unpaid; the retaliation, dated, witnessed, and named as
what it was, a second wrong layered on top of the first, filed where it would sit permanently beside
the overseer's own account. And he spent that night and the next arranging what the ledger alone
never could: a room in a neighboring tenement, work for the winter at a district three days off where
the overseer's reach didn't extend, coin enough to make the gap survivable.

"The proof was supposed to protect her," he told Ezio afterward. "It protected the debt. It didn't
protect the person who told the truth to get it recorded. Those aren't the same thing and I let
myself believe they were for longer than I should have."

"So what changes."

"Nobody testifies again without knowing exactly what the book can promise and what it can't. And we
don't leave a district until anyone who spoke has somewhere to land if speaking costs them what it
cost her."

---

*Continuity notes (not narrative): a genuine limit of the ledger method dramatized directly — full,
clean documentation proves a wage-debt wrong conclusively but does not prevent retaliation for
testifying, and cannot undo an eviction already carried out through an unrelated pretext. Distinct
from "The Widow Who Asked for Blood" (a request for violence refused in favor of documentation, with
documentation as an adequate answer) and from "The Ledger He Couldn't Finish" (an interrupted, not a
failed, campaign): here the paperwork succeeds completely on its own terms and still fails to protect
the person who supplied it. Establishes a new standing practice going forward — the campaign begins
arranging concrete relief (housing, distant work, coin) for anyone who testifies, not only recording
their debt — without claiming this closes the gap for good. No new named characters. Second entry in
the Industrial Myth's sixteenth wave.*
