# The Grudge That Outlived the War

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XLIII, wave 14.
Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The old man refused the crew's help for reasons that had nothing to do with the Trust at all — a
decades-old feud between his own family and a neighboring one, predating the rebellion by a full
generation, that had left him distrustful of any outside authority arriving with promises of aid,
Trust or otherwise. The rebuild he refused wasn't even his; it was his neighbor's roof, and he'd rather
watch it collapse than see it fixed by anyone he suspected of eventually taking his neighbor's side in
a dispute that had nothing to do with burnings or garrisons at all.

"You're not going to talk me out of forty years," he told Kanja, blocking the path to his neighbor's
half-collapsed roof with a stubbornness that had clearly outlasted worse arguments than this one. "I
don't care whose side you think you're not taking. Everyone ends up taking a side eventually."

Kanja didn't argue the old grudge's merits, recognizing early that nothing he said was going to resolve
four decades of history in a single conversation, and didn't try to.

"I'm not here to settle whatever's between you and your neighbor. That's not mine to fix, and I
wouldn't trust myself to fix it fairly even if I tried." He said it plainly, without pushing past the
old man's blocked path. "I'm here because a roof's about to come down on a family that didn't do
anything to deserve losing it, whatever you and your neighbor have going on underneath that. I'll fix
the roof. I'm not going to pretend I've fixed the rest of it too."

The old man held his ground three more days before finally, wordlessly, stepping aside — not
reconciled, not persuaded the feud was foolish, simply worn down by watching a roof get fixed by people
who plainly weren't taking either side of an argument that had nothing to do with them.

"You didn't make him like his neighbor again," the settlement's own elder observed afterward, watching
the old man retreat to his own house, still visibly unhappy about the whole affair.

"No. I didn't try to." Kanja checked the finished roof one last time before moving on. "Some grudges
aren't mine to fix, and I've learned not to promise more than I can actually deliver. I fixed a roof.
I didn't fix forty years. Those were never the same job."
