# The Road, Revisited

*Locked canon, Batch 306, 2026-09-23 (`MCD-1788`). Ozmund Verehimu Character Chronicle series, Chronicle LIX, Draconis strand wave 3, closing the third Draconis strand wave (Chronicles LI-LIX).*

---

The last of these stories, the General told me, wasn't a story he'd been told so much as one he'd simply been present for, and he closed the whole of what he ever told me about Draconis with it, more than once, always in the same unhurried way, as though he were walking the road itself again each time he told it.

It happened without either man planning it. A routine matter had called them both south, some ordinary business of grain and boundary stones that needed a senior officer's eye and, as it turned out, no danger at all, and the shortest road to it ran, by simple accident of geography, past the same stretch of collapsed grain-store the General had ridden through as a boy in a column of six, decades before, on the afternoon that had started every story I have ever told about the two of them.

The General told me he hadn't thought of the place directly in years — not because the memory had faded, but because it had settled, the way old memories do, into something he carried without needing to look at anymore. It was Draconis who slowed first, without any obvious reason, some hundred paces short of the ruined store itself, and the General told me he understood, watching him do it, that some part of the man's body had recognized the ground before his mind had caught up to naming it. Draconis said nothing for a long moment, only sat his horse looking at the collapsed timbers with an expression the General couldn't fully read, and then remarked, mild and almost offhand, that this particular stretch had always held a chill to it he'd never quite been able to account for, in all the times he'd ridden past it since.

He didn't say why. The General understood, hearing it, that Draconis genuinely didn't know why — that whatever the body remembers of a place it once bled at, it doesn't always hand the reason up to the mind that's carrying it, and that Draconis had simply learned, over the years, to trust the chill without needing to solve it. The General told me he sat his own horse beside him a long moment, looking at the same ruined store, carrying the whole of what that afternoon had actually meant in a silence Draconis had no way to share, and said only that he'd always found it a grim stretch of road himself, and left it at that.

They rode on together without further remark, the General told me, and finished the business that had brought them south, and rode home again by the same road a few days later, and the General said neither of them ever spoke of that particular stretch again, though he noticed, in the years after, that Draconis had a way of quietly setting the pace a touch faster whenever their business happened to carry them past it, without ever once naming the reason to himself or to anyone.

The General told me, closing this story for what he said would be the last time he meant to tell it to me, that he had come to think of that whole stretch of years — the road and the wine and the beam, the guardhouse and the coastal command, the letter from Duskmere and the lake and the border bolt, all of it — as one long conversation the two of them had been having without either ever once speaking its true subject aloud. He said he didn't know, even by then, whether that made it the truest friendship he'd ever kept, or the loneliest, and had decided somewhere along the way that he no longer needed the difference settled to know which one he'd have chosen again, every single time, given the choosing back.
