# What Went Without, So Others Could Have

There was no battle in the winter the fleet nearly starved. That was, Garren Hask noted more than once
in the season's ledger, precisely the problem — a fight could be won or lost in an afternoon; a supply
line cut by an early ice-lock in the northern straits simply ground on, week after week, with nothing
to strike at and nothing that surrendered.

Three of the fleet's usual resupply ports had gone quiet within a month of each other — one to a Trust
blockade, one to a harvest failure that left the port with nothing to spare even for coin, one to a
storm that had taken the granary roof along with half the season's stores. What remained had to stretch
across three ships and their crews for longer than anyone had planned for.

Garren Hask brought the numbers to Kanja plainly, the way he brought every number. "We can feed
everyone at three-quarter rations until the ice breaks, or feed two-thirds of the crew in full and the
rest on charity from whatever we can beg or trade along the way. There isn't a version where nobody
notices."

"Three-quarter rations for everyone," Kanja said, without pausing over it. "We don't ration people by
rank on this fleet. We never have."

It held for six weeks before it stopped being simple. A young hand fell sick — not gravely, but slower
to mend than she should have on a full stomach — and Pell Ostra's own medical stores ran thinner
alongside the food. Efa Gol proposed, carefully, that the ill and the very young eat first and full,
the rest stretch further still to cover it. Nobody argued. Everybody felt it by the fourth week after.

Dol Maren solved part of it the unglamorous way — trading skilled hull repair, port by port, for
provisions rather than coin the fleet barely had to spare, turning three weeks of a shipwright's labor
into two weeks' worth of food nobody had thought to offer for money. It wasn't enough on its own. Danne
Sok's charts found a fourth port, small and unlisted on any Trust map, willing to trade quietly and
without asking questions about black sails, closing the last of the gap before the ice finally broke.

Nobody died of it. Nobody was saved dramatically from it, either — no single crisis, no single rescue,
just five weeks of everyone eating a little less than they wanted and nobody saying much about it
until it was over.

"That's not a story anyone will ever tell about us," the young hand said afterward, recovered, watching
the first clear supply run in weeks come in.

"No," Kanja agreed. "The ones people tell are the ones with a fight in them. This is the part that keeps
the fleet alive long enough to have the fights people remember, and nobody ever writes a ballad about a
ship that didn't starve." He didn't say it like a complaint. He said it like someone who'd long since
made peace with which parts of the work got remembered and which didn't.
