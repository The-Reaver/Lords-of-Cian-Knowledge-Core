# The Grief He Kept Until Morning

*Locked canon, Batch 193, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-609`). Captain Alias Chronicle XXXIV, wave 12 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

He held it together through the debrief, through the ledger tally, through every crew member who
needed him steady enough to lean on that night — and it wasn't until the deck had gone quiet, past
the hour anyone reasonably expected to still be awake, that Efa Gol found him alone at the rail,
staring at nothing in particular, holding a grief he'd deliberately kept off his own face for hours.

She didn't say anything at first. She'd learned, over years commanding the crew's diversion forces
herself, exactly when silence was the kindest thing to offer someone.

"You don't have to be steady for me," she said eventually, when it seemed like he might stay quiet
all night otherwise. "I already saw you hold it for everyone who needed it today. You're allowed to
put it down now."

"I keep thinking there was a version of today where I called it four minutes earlier and nobody died
at all." His voice, when it finally came, carried none of the composure it had held for the crew all
evening. "I've run it a dozen times since the debrief ended. Four minutes. That's all it would have
taken."

"Maybe. Or maybe four minutes earlier changes something else that gets someone else killed instead,
and you'd be standing here running that version instead, wondering the same thing about a different
name." She didn't offer it as comfort exactly, just as truth. "You can't actually know, and knowing
that doesn't make tonight hurt less. I'm not here to fix it. I'm here so you're not doing it alone."

He let her stay, the two of them at the rail long past the hour either of them should have been
awake, saying little else that needed saying. It wasn't the first time the crew had seen him
composed through a hard day and assumed that was the whole of it. It was one of the few times anyone
saw what came after, once the composure was no longer required of him.

"Thank you," he said finally, as the sky started to lighten. "For not needing me to be anything
tonight."

"That's the whole deal, far as I've ever understood it. You've never once needed us to be anything
we weren't. Figured it was time somebody returned the favor."
