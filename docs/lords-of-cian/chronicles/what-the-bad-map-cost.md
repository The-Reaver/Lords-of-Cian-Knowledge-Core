# What the Bad Map Cost

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Bane Alias Chronicle XVII, second entry in the sixth
wave. Rebellion era, age 19, months after the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The map came from a source that had never been wrong before — a Trust quartermaster's clerk who'd
sold three accurate troop dispositions in as many months — and it put a single reserve company
holding a supply depot at the Verrin crossing, lightly garrisoned, ripe for the kind of quiet
liberation that had become Bane's ordinary work. He moved on it with a column of forty, timed to
arrive an hour before dawn.

The reserve company was not a company. It was two, dug in and waiting, reinforced in the six days
since the clerk's information had changed hands and never updated.

Bane felt the wrongness of it before he saw it — the ground too quiet, the approach too clean, the
absence of the ordinary carelessness a lightly held position always left lying around — and called
the column back a full quarter mile before the ambush he'd walked them toward could close. It cost
him the depot. It cost him three days of the column's confidence in the intelligence that had sent
them there. It nearly cost him more than that.

"We could have kept going," the column's second said afterward, once they were clear. "You read it
in time. We got out clean."

"We got out clean because I read a mistake I made, not because the mistake wasn't mine." Bane didn't
dress it up as anything other than what it was. "I trusted a source because it had been right three
times. Three times isn't a pattern, it's a coincidence that hasn't broken yet. I sent forty people
toward a depot I hadn't verified myself, on information that was six days old before we ever moved
on it. That's not the enemy outthinking me. That's me not doing the part of the work that isn't
glamorous enough to get told as a story afterward."

He had the clerk's information cross-checked against two independent sources for every operation
after that, without exception, even when it cost days the column didn't always feel it had to spare.
The Verrin crossing depot was liberated four months later, correctly this time, garrison strength
confirmed by three separate accounts before a single boot moved toward it.

"You don't talk about Verrin much," the second said, much later, when the correction had become
routine enough to stop being remarked on.

"No. It's not a story people want carved into a gatepost." Bane checked the latest cross-reference
against the map in front of him, the same slow discipline it had cost him nearly a column to learn.
"It's the reason the other stories keep being true."
