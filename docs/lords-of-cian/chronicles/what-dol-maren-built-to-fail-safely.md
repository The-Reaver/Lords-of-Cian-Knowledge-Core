# What Dol Maren Built to Fail Safely

*Locked canon, Batch 255, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1123`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle LXVI, wave 22,
closing the wave. Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. Dol Maren's first dedicated entry in this alias's
own run.*

---

Dol Maren had built seventeen plank-bridges across the dredge canals in the weeks after the flood
that founded the alias, and he had never once, in all that time, told anyone how he actually rated
them.

The district assumed a bridge either held or it didn't. Maren rated each one against a number no one
else asked for: the heaviest load he expected it to be asked to carry, doubled, and then a further
margin on top of that for whatever he hadn't thought of. Bridge Eleven — a wide plank span over the
canal's narrowest crossing, load-rated for six laden dredge-carts abreast — had been standing for
eleven weeks without complaint when a Compliance requisition wagon, hauling confiscated tally
records out of a district Kanja's crew had just finished correcting, tried to cross it fully loaded
and considerably heavier than any wagon Maren had ever been told to plan for.

The bridge gave. Not all at once, and not where the wagon's own weight sat — three support beams on
the downstream side, the ones Maren had built to be the first thing to fail if the deck's own margin
was ever exceeded, let go in sequence, and the deck tilted the wagon and its two drivers cleanly into
the canal rather than letting the whole span come down on the workers crossing behind it.

"You built it to fail," Kanja said afterward, watching Maren's crew fish the sodden requisition
crates out of the shallows.

"I built it to fail somewhere I chose instead of somewhere it chose." Maren didn't look up from the
downstream beam he was already examining, running a thumb along the clean break where it had let go
exactly as designed. "A bridge that never fails is a bridge nobody's tested past its real limit. A
bridge that only fails where you put the failure is a bridge you can trust with people standing on
it." He nodded at the two drenched drivers, both furious and both unhurt, dragging themselves out of
the canal under their own power. "Nobody drowned. Nobody's under three tons of planking. That's not
luck. That's the margin doing exactly what I built it to do."

Kanja crouched beside the broken beam himself, turning it over. The fracture line ran clean and
almost deliberate, nothing like the ragged failure of wood simply overwhelmed. "How many more of
these could take the same load and hold?"

"None of them, if a wagon that heavy tries them. That's the point. I don't build for the load I hope
never comes. I build for the load I can actually plan around, and I make sure the part that gives
when something heavier arrives is the part that gives safely." Maren finally looked up. "You want a
bridge that never fails, you don't build a bridge. You build a wall, and then nobody crosses at all."

The requisition wagon's contents were returned to the district it had been taken from within the
week — soaked, but legible. Bridge Eleven was rebuilt with the same three sacrificial beams, in the
same place, by the same design.

*Continuity notes (not narrative): Dol Maren's first dedicated solo entry in the Trench Monarch's own
Chronicles -- he has extensive established material in the much later Sovereign Ghost of the Great
Sea alias run (`CC-120`, `CC-121`, and numerous naval-era Chronicles), but no prior entry centered on
his pre-Black-Trench bridge-building work beyond the bare fact of the seventeen plank-bridges
(`CC-121`). Establishes his engineering philosophy -- designing structures to fail predictably and
safely rather than never at all -- as the direct origin of the load-assessment competence he later
carries into shipwright work. A genuine structural-failure entry, distinct from `MCD-900`'s storm-
caused wall collapse (natural disaster, no design choice involved) and from every other engineering-
adjacent entry this run. No new named characters -- the requisition wagon's drivers are unnamed.
Closes the Trench Monarch's twenty-second wave (with "What the Charter Actually Said," `MCD-1121`,
and "What Whisper of Shadows Was For," `MCD-1122`).*
