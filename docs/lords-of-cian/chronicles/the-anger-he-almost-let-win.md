# The Anger He Almost Let Win

*Locked canon, Batch 206, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-901`). The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XLVII, wave 16. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The man they found tied to a dredge-cart axle at Fenrow Landing had cooperated with the tally
crew three weeks earlier — nothing more than confirming a wage figure to one of Kanja's clerks,
the same small act of honesty a hundred other workers had performed without consequence. Someone
had decided to make an example of him anyway. He was alive when Kanja arrived, barely, and the men
who had done it were still on site, unhurried, confident enough in whatever protection they thought
they had that they hadn't bothered to leave.

Every other confrontation on this canal had been fought with patience Kanja had never once seemed to
run short of — the restraint at the Coldrace road, the mercy he'd shown a defeated duelist, the calm
he brought to owners who lied to his face. None of it was present in him now. Corren Halst, who had
seen him angry before in the ordinary way anyone gets angry, had never seen anything like this.

Onyx came free of its sheath faster than Cadence Ruin's own reading should have allowed for, the
blade answering something that wasn't quite the calculated intention-sense it normally worked from.
The first man went down before he'd finished drawing his own weapon. The second didn't get the
chance to run. By the time Kanja reached the third — the one who'd given the order, by his own
men's frightened testimony — Soulbound Edge was humming with an intensity none of the crew had felt
from it before, something closer to the blade answering the wielder's fury than reading an opponent's
intent at all.

He had the man on the ground, blade at his throat, close enough to finishing it that Danne Sok
actually stepped forward to intervene before catching himself, uncertain whether stopping him would
be the right thing or an unforgivable one.

Nobody said anything for the length of several long breaths. Kanja was the one who broke it, not
because anyone stopped him, but because something in his own hand — not the blade, something older
than the blade — refused to close the last inch.

He stood up slowly, breathing hard, and looked at his own hand like it belonged to someone he didn't
fully recognize yet.

"I wasn't going to stop," he said, to no one in particular, or maybe to all of them. "I want that
said plainly, because I don't want to pretend otherwise later. I wasn't choosing restraint back
there. I was one breath from not choosing anything at all."

"You stopped," Corren Halst said carefully.

"This time." Kanja sheathed Onyx, hands still not entirely steady. "I've told myself the sword
answers what I decide, not what I feel. I believed that, up until about a minute ago. I don't know
anymore how much of what just happened was decision and how much was just — rage, finding a reason
to call itself justice." He looked back at the man on the ground, alive, and at the tally worker
they were already carrying toward help. "I don't regret that he's still breathing to be marched
somewhere and judged properly. I regret how close I came to not caring whether he was."

Garren Hask's ledger entry for Fenrow Landing recorded the injured worker's recovery, the three men's
formal handover to the district's own arbitration, and nothing at all about what had almost happened
in the minute before Kanja lowered the blade — a silence in the record that Halst, Sok, and Vos kept
between the three of them without ever discussing why.
