# Everything Said by Letter

closing the wave. Rebellion era (age 19-21), unarmed and non-combat throughout, matching the alias's
established ethos. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters.*

---

The administrator governing the milling combine two districts over refused, politely and repeatedly,
to grant an in-person meeting, citing a schedule that never seemed to open regardless of how many
weeks Ezio's letters allowed for it. So the entire exchange happened on paper, carried back and forth
by runner, over the better part of a month.

*Your letter of the ninth claims the combine's records show no outstanding wage discrepancy,*
Ezio's third letter read. *Your letter of the second, however, states the combine's records were
"lost in the spring flooding" and could not be produced. I would ask which of these two claims your
office intends to stand behind, since they cannot both be true.*

The administrator's reply took eleven days and conceded nothing directly, offering instead a longer
explanation of flooding damage, partial record recovery, and the general difficulty of historical
reconstruction — three full pages that Ezio read twice and answered with four lines.

*I accept that some records were lost. I have enclosed the testimony of forty-one workers, taken
independently, whose accounts of their own hours and pay agree with each other to within a coin in
thirty-eight cases out of forty-one. Records do not need to survive a flood if the people who lived
them did. I would ask what standard of evidence your office proposes to use instead, now that the
combine's own has been removed from consideration by your own letter.*

There was no reply to that one for nineteen days. When it came, it was shorter than any before it,
and did not attempt the earlier evasions at all — a grudging acknowledgment that the worker testimony,
independently corroborated at that consistency, would be treated as the operative record in the
combine's own settlement going forward, with a request, almost plaintive in its brevity, that Ezio's
office refrain from further correspondence on the matter.

Kanja never set foot in the milling district himself. Neither did Ezio. The entire campaign ran
through paper alone, letter answering letter, until the administrator's own words, laid one against
another, did the work that neither presence nor pressure had been needed to do.
