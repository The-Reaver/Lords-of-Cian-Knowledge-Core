# The Witness Stand Under the City

*Locked canon, Batch 279, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1457`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle C, wave 34, opening the wave -- the one-hundredth Blue-Collar Titan Chronicle overall. The alias's first formal legal-testimony register, distinct from every prior informal-accountability or custody register.*

---

The tribunal clerk read his name into the record as simply "the tunnel foreman" and Kanja let it stand, taking the witness stand in a case that had nothing to do with the war he'd fought and everything to do with the trade he'd carried out of it.

"You examined the tenement collapse at the eastern terrace," the magistrate said. "In your professional judgment, was the failure consistent with the contractor's claimed cause?"

"No," Kanja said. "The contractor claims ground settlement. Ground settlement doesn't leave a clean shear line across six support timbers cut to the same length by the same tool. That's not the ground failing. That's timber sized to pass an inspection and nothing more, cut short to save on stock and never meant to carry the load the plans specified."

The contractor's counsel pressed him twice, first on his qualifications, then on his motive, clearly hoping to surface either a reputation to discredit or a grudge to expose. Kanja gave him neither. He answered every question about his own training plainly and without embellishment -- self-taught, then guild-tested, then guild-certified on the strength of a warped axle housing years before this room existed -- and when asked directly whether he held any personal animus toward the contractor, answered just as plainly that he'd never met the man before that morning and had no stake in the case beyond the plain evidence in the timber itself.

Maret Vos took the stand after him, testifying to the same fraud from the materials side -- a procurement ledger that recorded full-length timber purchased and short-length timber delivered, the difference pocketed twice over. Between the two of them, the tribunal had no need of theater. Just a shear line and a ledger, laid out plainly enough that the contractor's own counsel stopped pressing before the second round of questions finished.

"You could have said more," Maret Vos observed afterward, watching the tribunal clerk file the verdict. "Named what it cost the families who trusted that wall."

"I could have. It would have been true, and it would have made a better story for whoever writes this down later." Kanja watched the contractor being led out through a side door, saying nothing to anyone. "But the tribunal didn't need a story. It needed to know whether the timber lied, and the timber already told them everything true that mattered. Anything I added past that would have been me, not the evidence."

"That's a strange kind of restraint for a man half this district still calls a titan."

"The titan doesn't need to convict anyone. The evidence does that on its own, if you let it speak instead of talking over it." Kanja collected his level from where he'd set it beside the stand -- brought along out of habit, never once needed inside the tribunal hall -- and looked back at the empty witness stand. "I've spent this whole trade learning that the truth usually doesn't need my help sounding true. It just needs someone willing to say it plainly and then get out of its way."
