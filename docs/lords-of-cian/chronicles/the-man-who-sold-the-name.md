# The Man Who Sold the Name

*Locked canon, Batch 264, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1366`). Captain Alias Chronicle LXVI, wave 22, closing the wave. An impostor sells "Captain's protection" as a scam; the crew handles reputation misuse warmly rather than violently.*

---

The rumor reached Efa Gol first, the way most rumors did: a man two ports up the coast had been collecting protection fees from frightened merchants, promising them the crew of the Second Chance would come running if trouble found their warehouses, and pocketing the coin himself. He never claimed to be Kanja. He was smarter than that. He simply let people assume, and answered to "Captain" when they did.

Corren Halst wanted to sail down and end it in an afternoon. Kanja wanted to see the man first.

He was younger than expected, and more frightened than expected, once he understood who'd actually walked into the room. "I never hurt anyone," he said immediately, hands up, backing into a barrel. "I never even said I was you. I just — people wanted to believe someone was watching out for them, and I let them believe it, and I took a little coin for letting them."

"You took their fear and sold it back to them," Kanja said. "That's not nothing."

He didn't turn the man over to the Trust courts, and he didn't hurt him. Instead he did something that unsettled the man far more than either would have: he offered him a real job, unloading cargo on the actual Second Chance for real wages, on the condition that every coin he'd collected under a false claim got returned personally, door to door, with an apology attached. It took him three months. Efa Gol tracked every repayment in the ledger under a line she labeled, without irony, "debt owed to fear, repaid in shoe leather."

The man stayed on afterward — not out of gratitude exactly, Callum Breck said, watching him haul crates with the rest of the deckhands, but because it turned out to be easier to actually be part of something than to keep pretending from a safe distance.

"The name was never mine to protect that jealously," Kanja told Halst that evening. "What I care about is whether people who trust it get let down. Fixing that mattered more than punishing him for trying."
