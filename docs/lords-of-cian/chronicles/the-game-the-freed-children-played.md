# The Game the Freed Children Played

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Scourge Alias Chronicle, wave 8. Long Mask era,
age 165, V3 gear (Golden Terror era). Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose. No new named characters.*

---

The settlement had been founded by Salt Keep survivors two decades past and had grown into
something close to a proper town, and the first thing he noticed passing through its market square,
unannounced and unrecognized, was a cluster of children playing a game that used his own name as its
rules.

One child wore a borrowed dark cloak and stood in the center of a chalk circle drawn on the packed
dirt, arms spread wide, while the others darted in and out of the circle's edge pretending to free
invisible captives from equally invisible chains, shrieking with delight every time the cloaked child
lunged and "caught" one of them, at which point — by rules he watched play out three full rounds
before understanding them — the caught child simply joined the circle's defenders instead of being
removed from the game.

"You're doing it wrong," he said finally, unable to help himself, crouching to their level at the
circle's edge. "The ones inside are supposed to be free. Not caught again."

The girl currently playing the cloaked role looked at him with the particular scorn children reserve
for adults who clearly haven't been paying attention. "That's how it works, though. Everybody he
frees joins him after. That's why nobody who's already free gets scared of the circle anymore — they
just help him catch the next one."

He didn't correct her. There wasn't, he realized, watching three more rounds play out exactly the
same way, anything to correct — some drifted, distorted, entirely unofficial version of a truth he
recognized without having taught it to anyone in this square: freedom, in this game as much as in
the real accounting he kept in his own head, was never really the end of an obligation. It just
changed which side of the circle you stood on.

"Who's he supposed to be, then?" he asked. "The one in the cloak."

"Everybody knows. The one from the story." She said it the way children say things too obvious to
need saying, already turning back to the game, already forgetting the stranger who'd asked. "The one
who doesn't stop."

He watched two more rounds before moving on through the square, unrecognized and unremarked, carrying
away nothing but the shape of a game that had gotten the mechanism more right than any adult account
he'd heard of himself in years — not a man who saved people and moved on, but one whose circle simply
kept getting larger, one freed hand at a time, for as long as anyone kept playing.
