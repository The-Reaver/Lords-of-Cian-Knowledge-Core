# The Argument Halst Won

*Locked canon, Batch 215, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-941`). Bane Alias Chronicle LVII, wave 19. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Bane wanted to move on Threll's Watch that night, while the garrison there was still reorganizing
after a supply shortfall and before word of the column's approach could reach them properly. Corren
Halst thought it was the worst plan he'd heard proposed in months, and said so, in front of enough
of the column that it stopped being a private disagreement the moment the words left his mouth.

"You're reading the shortfall as weakness," Halst said. "I'm reading it as a garrison that knows
exactly how thin it's stretched and is watching harder for exactly this because of it. A hungry
garrison isn't a careless one. Sometimes it's the opposite."

"We won't get a window like this again for a month."

"Then we wait a month. I'd rather wait a month than walk forty people into a garrison that's
expecting to be hit precisely because it's weak, and has nothing left to lose by making it costly."

It went on longer than either man expected it to, in full view of a column that had never seen the
two of them disagree this openly, and neither backed down for the sake of appearances. Bane laid
out the intelligence Vos had gathered on the shortfall's timing. Halst laid out three separate
incidents, going back to before the Black Trench, where a cornered force had fought harder than
its numbers suggested it should have. Neither man raised his voice past the point of argument into
something uglier, and neither pulled rank to end it — there was no rank to pull, not in the way
either of them understood the column's own shape.

Bane called it, eventually, by doing something the column had not seen him do before: he asked for
a show of hands among the dozen senior enough to have an informed opinion on it, made clear he'd
abide by what came back, and let the tally stand against his own read of the intelligence. Eight
sided with Halst. The column held its position and waited.

The window came again in eleven days, not a month, and the garrison — reinforced twice over in the
interval on intelligence they hadn't had the first time — would have cost the column dearly had
they moved when Bane first wanted to.

"You were right," Bane told Halst afterward, without performing the admission, just stating it.

"I've been wrong plenty of other times you didn't ask the column to vote on it." Halst meant it as
fact, not modesty. "Ask again next time. Don't wait until you're already leaning toward losing the
argument to remember you can."
