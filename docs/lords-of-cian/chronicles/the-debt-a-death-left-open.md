# The Debt a Death Left Open

*Locked canon, Batch 267, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1398`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XCII, wave 31, second entry. The method's first workplace-death liability case, extending MCD-1172's injury framework into a fatality no settlement can undo.*

---

There was no injured man to interview this time, no scarred hand to point to across a table. There was a widow, a dented safety chain the foundry had been told twice to replace, and a furnace crew who'd watched the chain give way under a load it had no business carrying anymore.

Ezio built the case the same way he'd built the forge-door injury case two years earlier — the written warnings, the comparable foundries that had made the same repair without incident, the honest documentation of what the hazard had cost — except this time the number at the end of it couldn't buy back what it was meant to compensate for, and both of them knew it before Ezio wrote the first line.

"An injury, I can put a figure against — reduced earning capacity, years of work at a lesser trade. A man's whole life isn't a figure I know how to write."

"Then don't write it as one. Write what's true: the chain should have been replaced, it wasn't, and a man is dead who would be alive if it had been. The settlement doesn't pretend to be equal to that. It's what the foundry owes his family going forward, nothing more, and the finding should say plainly that no number closes the actual debt."

The owner, unlike the forge-door case, didn't fight it. He'd known the chain was failing — his own foreman's second warning sat in the file, dated, unactioned — and he paid the widow a standing wage-equivalent for as long as she needed it, plus the boy's schooling until he came of age, without Ezio having to press for either. It was the case's own quiet weight, not any leverage the crew brought to it, that moved him.

The widow asked, before they left, whether the finding would say her husband's name. Ezio told her it would, in full, on every copy posted in every district the record reached — the first time the method had ever been asked, rather than simply choosing, to make sure a name outlived the number attached to it.

"We keep finding new things the ledger has to hold," Ezio said afterward, "that arithmetic was never built to carry."

"It carries what we ask it to carry. We just have to be honest about which part of the finding is the number and which part is the debt the number can't actually pay."
