# The Man Above the Man Who Owed Her

*Locked canon, Batch 214, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-927`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle LII, wave 18. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The dockhand's foreman opened his books without a fight, which should have ended it, and instead
told Kanja almost nothing.

"Every figure's honest," the foreman said, spreading the district's wage ledger flat on the table.
"I pay what I'm told to pay. I don't set the number. Take it up with the concern that holds my
contract, if you can find who that is — I've stopped trying."

It took Ezio the better part of a week to find who that was, because the foreman answered to a
labor broker, and the labor broker answered to a shipping concern that leased the dock rights, and
the shipping concern itself paid a fixed sum upward to a financier who had never once set foot in
the district and whose name appeared on no public ledger anywhere in it. Four layers stood between
the woman who'd first sat down at the tally table and the actual hand controlling what she was paid,
and every layer below the top could show, honestly, that it was passing along exactly what it had
been given to pass along.

"Nobody in this chain is lying," Ezio said, laying the four linked accounts side by side. "Nobody's
even hiding anything, technically. They're just answering questions about their own piece and letting
you assume that's the whole answer."

"So we go up."

"We go up four times."

It took another eleven days, tracing shipping manifests and lease agreements and a financier's own
correspondence obtained through a broker who'd soured on him years before, before the chain reached
its actual top — a man who had never met a single dockhand under his money, had never visited the
docks, and had set the base rate low enough three years earlier that every honest layer beneath him
compounded the shortfall without any one of them ever choosing to steal a coin.

Kanja didn't present it to the financier as an accusation when he finally reached him. He laid out
the four linked ledgers in order, foreman to broker to concern to source, and let the man see his own
signature at the top of a chain he'd clearly never imagined anyone would trace all the way back to
him.

"Every one of these men below you did exactly what you told them to, honestly," Kanja said. "That's
what makes this yours. Not theirs."

The financier settled within the week — not because he was threatened, but because, shown the whole
chain plainly for the first time, he found he had no honest argument that the debt belonged to anyone
but himself.

*Continuity notes (not narrative): the first vertical debt-tracing entry — following money upward
through a chain of honest, non-fabricating intermediaries to the actual controlling party, rather than
combining multiple lateral districts into one case. Distinct from "The District Chain" (Kanja
combining three peer districts' own tallies into one horizontal case against a shared owner, all
three districts equally implicated) and from every fabrication/counter-tally entry (no one in this
chain lies or hides records — the difficulty is structural distance, not dishonesty). Establishes
that responsibility in a layered chain settles at the top even when every intermediate layer is
individually blameless. No new named characters; the foreman, broker, concern, and financier are all
unnamed and off-page or one-scene. First entry in the Industrial Myth's eighteenth wave.*
