# The Grandchild Who Wanted the Old Stories

*Locked canon, Batch 193, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-597`). Captain Alias Chronicle XXII, wave 8 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Garren Hask was well past his hundredth year and still kept his ledgers, though his hands shook
enough now that a younger relative did most of the actual writing for him. That relative's own
daughter — Hask's great-grandniece, eight years old and utterly unimpressed by most things — had
one request whenever the strange, quiet man who visited sometimes came by the house: the ship
stories, told properly, not the shortened versions her family gave her.

"Tell her about The Audit," Hask said, settling into his chair. "The real version, not the one
where I make myself sound clever."

"You were clever," Kanja said. "You named two flagships better than the Directorate ever named
anything, and you kept better books than half the Trust's own treasury."

"That's not what she wants to hear about." Hask's eyes, still sharp despite everything else that
had slowed, found his great-grandniece's face. "She wants to know if the stories are true. Whether
there really was a man who came for people the Directorate had written off as gone, and whether he
actually cared about all of it, or whether that part gets added afterward to make war sound nicer
than it was."

The girl looked at Kanja directly, the way children did before they learned not to. "Well? Did you
care, or is that just what they tell people?"

"I cared," he said. "I still do. Ask your great-uncle what I called him, the very first week."

"He called me by my actual name," Hask said, "when most everyone else on that dock called me
nothing at all, or worse. That's the whole story, really. Everything after it was just what grew out
of that one small thing, over and over, for eighty years."

The girl considered this with the seriousness only children bring to matters adults have stopped
examining. "So the name wasn't because you were scary. It was because you were nice."

"Something like that," Kanja said. "Though I'd say fair, more than nice. Nice runs out. Fair
doesn't, if you keep choosing it."

She seemed satisfied with that, in the specific way of someone filing away a fact to repeat later
as if she'd always known it. Hask, watching her, looked pleased in a way that had nothing to do with
ledgers at all.
