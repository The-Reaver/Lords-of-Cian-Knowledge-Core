# What Came Down From the Ridge

*Locked canon, Batch 262, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1323`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LXXVII, wave
26. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241), a remote highland forge site. A
detailed, battle-intense Trinity combat showcase against a natural predator threat rather than a
Directorate force. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Nobody had told the campaign about the pack, because the settlement it threatened hadn't known to
mention it — a highland predator species, rare enough that most tours never crossed its range at
all, driven down out of thinning ridge country by a poor season's hunting into a forge terrace it
had no reason to recognize as dangerous ground. It came at night, the way it always came, six
animals moving with a coordination that owed nothing to any Directorate tactic and everything to
however many generations of hunting together in open country had taught them.

"This isn't a fight I know," Kanja admitted to the senior smith's successor, watching the first
pale shape cross the terrace's outer torchlight. "Every enemy I've fought here wanted something I
could reason with, eventually. This wants the sheep pen and doesn't care what stands in front of
it."

He met the pack without the Trinity's usual vocabulary of restraint calibrated to a human
opponent — Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System grounding a lunging animal's full weight through his
own frame and redirecting it wide rather than absorbing an impact meant to kill, a technique built
for a very different kind of force entirely. Onyx of Oblivion's Cadence Ruin read six separate,
non-human rhythms at once, tracking a coordinated approach that moved nothing like a trained
soldier's footfall, while Whisper of Shadows put him between the pack and the pen faster than any
of the six animals could adjust their own converging lines.

He didn't kill them, in the end, though every instinct in the fight argued it would have been the
faster resolution. Obsidian Malice's discharge, used at its lowest, most controlled intensity, was
enough to turn the pack back toward the ridge without ending a single one of them — a pressure wave
more than a strike, the animals reading it correctly as a threat too costly to press further rather
than a kill worth dying for.

"You could have just let them take a sheep or two," the successor said afterward, watching the
pack's retreat vanish back up into the dark ridgeline. "Would have cost less than a whole night's
fight."

"They weren't hunting because they're cruel," Kanja said. "They were hunting because the season up
there failed them. Killing them doesn't fix a bad season. It just moves the same hunger onto
whatever pack comes down next winter instead." He watched the empty ridge a moment longer.
"There's nothing to rebuild after this one. Just a fence that needs to be higher before next
season does the same thing again."

*Continuity notes (not narrative): the Lord of Embers' first natural-predator combat entry — a
genuine wildlife threat with no Directorate or human antagonist involved at all, distinct from
every prior environmental entry (a wildfire with no enemy, MCD-923; a storm-surge flood exploited
by an opportunistic raid, MCD-862; a sea storm plus naval strike, MCD-1314). Establishes the
Trinity's combat vocabulary adapting to a non-human, non-reasoning threat, and extends the alias's
established preference for de-escalation over lethality (MCD-553, MCD-869, the-storm-that-nearly-
took-the-anvil) to an ecological register — sparing the pack rather than a person. No new named
characters; the senior smith's successor appears consistently with her established role.*
