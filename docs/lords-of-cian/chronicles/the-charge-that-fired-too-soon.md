# The Charge That Fired Too Soon

*Locked canon, Batch 254, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1097`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXVII, wave 23. First
genuine Obsidian Malice equipment failure for this alias -- an electrical storm triggers a partial
premature discharge mid-approach.*

---

The storm had been building since midafternoon, close and heavy, the kind that made the hair on a
man's arms stand up an hour before the first strike. Bane knew storms like it from the Black Trench
and had planned the approach on the assumption it would only slow them down.

He hadn't planned for what it would do to Obsidian Malice.

Two years of dormant charge sat coiled in it at any given time, a slow accumulation entirely separate
from the three-to-five-second recharge it drew on in active combat — and no one, including Bane, had
ever tested what a sky already thick with its own competing charge would do to that reservoir at
close range. Forty yards out from the garrison's outer wall, still a full ten minutes from the
position they'd planned to strike from, the air itself seemed to reach into the weapon and pull.

The discharge that came out wasn't the controlled, aimed release he'd used a hundred times. It went
early, went wide, and lit the whole approach corridor in a flash the garrison's watch couldn't have
missed if they'd been asleep.

"That wasn't you," Danne Sok said, already moving, already reading the alarm bells starting up along
the wall.

"No." Bane didn't waste time confirming it twice. "Surprise is gone. Move like it already was."

He turned the ruin of the plan into the plan itself before the garrison's own confusion had settled —
came in fast and loud rather than slow and quiet, let them think the flash had been a deliberate
opening move rather than a misfire, and took the gate in the gap between their alarm and their actual
readiness. The garrison fell in six minutes, faster than the quiet approach would likely have managed
on its own.

It didn't make the failure disappear. Back at camp, with the storm finally breaking into real rain,
Corren Halst asked him plainly what had happened, because the crew had never seen the weapon do
anything without his intending it.

"It's not infallible," Bane said. "I've treated it like it was because it's never failed me before
tonight. That was luck, not design. We plan differently around storms from now on — assume the charge
answers to the sky sometimes as much as it answers to me."
