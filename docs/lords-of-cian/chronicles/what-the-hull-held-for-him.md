# What the Hull Held For Him

*Locked canon, Batch 261, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1306`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LXXXVII, wave 29, closing the wave. Safe arrival, and Dol Maren's reflection on trusting craft and doctrine together. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

Landfall came two days after the storm broke, the repaired keel intact, the calculation and the doctrine both having held exactly as far as either could be reasonably asked to hold. Dol Maren inspected the hull personally before anyone else was permitted aboard the dock, running his own hands over the same joinery Kanja had spent a full night's continuous attention keeping honest account of.

"Numbers still match what I built," he said, more to the wood than to Kanja. "That's not nothing, after a night like that."

"It held because you made the right call at the right hour, not because the joinery was perfect," Kanja said. "I gave you an honest number every time you asked. What you did with each one was entirely yours."

"I know that. I'm not thanking you for the decision. I'm telling you the calculation and the fact agreed with each other, all the way through, and that's the only outcome I've ever actually trusted." Dol Maren straightened from his inspection, something in his posture settling the way it hadn't quite settled since before the storm. "Most men on this crew would call last night close to a miracle. I don't believe in those. I believe the keel held because the joinery was sound, the read was honest, and the correction came in time. Three true things, not one lucky one."

"Would you sail with me reading it again?"

"I'd sail with fewer reservations than I had leaving the dock the first time." It was, from Dol Maren, close to the highest praise Kanja had heard him offer anyone. "I've spent a career trusting only what's been verified. You gave me a night of continuous verification I didn't know the doctrine could even offer, and it held up against a storm that would have sunk half the fleet's other repairs without ever telling anyone in time that they were failing. That's not faith. That's just a better instrument than the ones I usually have."

Garren Hask logged the voyage in the crew's own ledger without embellishing it — a keel repair, a storm, a course correction, a safe landfall — but added, uncharacteristically, one line beyond the bare figures: *Dol Maren asked. That alone is worth noting.*

"He doesn't ask for much," Kanja said, when Hask showed him the entry later. "I think that's exactly why it mattered that he asked for this."

"It's why I wrote it down," Hask said. "A man who trusts nothing but verified calculation just told the rest of the crew, without saying it outright, that your doctrine counts as one. That's a harder endorsement to earn from him than a speech would have been from anyone else."
