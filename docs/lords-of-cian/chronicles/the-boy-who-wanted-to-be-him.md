# The Boy Who Wanted to Be Him

*Locked canon, Batch 181, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-528`). Bane Alias Chronicle XIII, first entry in the
fifth wave. Rebellion era, age 19, shortly after the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. New
standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

A young recruit, barely old enough to have joined the crew at all, had absorbed every version of the
Bane stories circulating through the ranks and built from them a private idea of what the reputation
actually demanded — not the mercy, not the restraint, only the terror, stripped of everything that
had ever made it honest. He put that idea into practice the first time he faced a surrendering
Directorate scout alone, blade already raised to finish what the man's own raised hands had already
ended.

Kanja caught his wrist before the blow landed.

"He surrendered," Kanja said, quiet enough that only the boy heard it, the scout still frozen mid-
flinch. "You don't kill a man who's already stopped fighting. Not for this crew. Not under this name."

"I thought that's what Bane does. I thought that's why they're all so afraid." The boy's voice
cracked, more confusion than defiance in it. "I wanted to be what the stories say you are."

"The stories aren't the thing. They're what's left over after the thing's already happened, told by
people who weren't in the room to see why it happened the way it did." Kanja released the boy's wrist,
the scout already being led away unharmed by another crew member. "I've spent a year building a
reputation specifically so fewer people would have to die, not more. If you use my name as
permission to do what I've spent that year refusing to do, you haven't become anything I've built.
You've become the thing I've been trying to stop."

The boy said nothing for a long moment, the adrenaline of the near-killing draining into something
closer to shame. "I don't know what I'm supposed to take from this instead."

"Take this: the fear only ever worked because it was never actually used carelessly. The moment
someone starts swinging a blade because they think the name gives them permission to, the fear stops
meaning what it's supposed to mean, and it starts meaning something considerably more dangerous for
everyone standing near it — including you." Kanja watched the scout disappear into custody, unharmed.
"I'd rather you learn that from a corrected mistake than from actually killing someone who'd already
surrendered. Don't make me choose the harder lesson next time."

The boy carried the correction with him for the remainder of the war, referenced quietly by other
recruits who heard the story secondhand — not as another tale of Bane's terror, but as the one story
that explained what the terror was actually supposed to protect.
