# Lauris Chronicle XVIII: What Ilvane Left Behind

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1635`). Eighteenth entry in Lauris Letitia's own Chronicle
series, the fifth entry of Strand K (Kares Prime / deep past). Dramatizes the second of the three
inter-Vask security operations from the Long Operational Period (`MCD-1555`; the first is Chronicle
II, `MCD-1562`) -- a resource-scarcity conflict between Karesians resolved without lethal force. This
entry introduces one new named location, Vask Ilvane, a small, terminally failing Vask whose
remaining ~40 residents Threnarr and Aldreth (both already-locked locations, established as
neighboring Vasks in Chronicle II) dispute over absorbing -- collision-checked clean against the full
live ledger (zero prior hits for "Ilvane"). No new named characters; the dispute itself stays
between unnamed representatives of Threnarr and Aldreth, matching Chronicle II's own convention.
Full text below.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Kares Prime, age ~2,550: "Ilvane could not sustain itself past this winter. Both
Threnarr and Aldreth wished to inherit what remained of it. Neither wished to be the one who let it
fail first. I did not resolve which of them was right. I resolved that the forty women inside it
would still be alive when the argument ended."*

I have come to think of this entry, among all the ones she has shown me from the Long Operational
Period, as the one that most plainly reveals what the decline actually looked like from inside it --
not war, not catastrophe, but a slow arithmetic problem dressed, every few centuries, in the clothes
of a dispute between neighbors who had once had no reason to dispute anything.

Vask Ilvane, by the time this account begins, held some forty residents, down from a population her
archive estimates once numbered in the low thousands. Its central chamber's ventilation system had
failed twice in the preceding decade and could not, by the assessment of its own remaining
Iron-Speaker, be repaired a third time with the personnel Ilvane had left to repair it. Winter was
some four months distant. Every party involved understood, without anyone having to say it plainly,
that Ilvane would not survive the season as an independent settlement.

*

What Lauris records is not, strictly, a rescue. It is a dispute over who would be permitted to
conduct one. Vask Threnarr, her own birth-hold, held a historical claim on Ilvane's remaining archive
-- three of Ilvane's founding families had originally been Threnarr's own, some nine thousand years
prior, a lineage tie that under older cooperative-era tradition would have given Threnarr first right
to absorb a failing sister-settlement. Vask Aldreth held the more practical claim: its own galleries
sat nearer Ilvane's location by some six days' travel, and its Iron-Speakers had already begun,
quietly and without consulting Threnarr, preparing space to receive Ilvane's forty residents before
the season turned. Neither Vask, she notes, was acting from anything she would call bad faith. Both
were acting from the same underlying terror: that if the other absorbed Ilvane first, the historical
memory of what Ilvane had been -- its own small archive, its own small lineage, the last forty women
who remembered it as a living place rather than a name in someone else's record -- would be folded
into a rival Hold's account rather than preserved as anything resembling itself.

She arrived, by her own account, not because either side had summoned her, but because Ilvane's own
failing Iron-Speaker had, and had asked for something neither Threnarr nor Aldreth had yet offered:
someone to simply keep the forty residents alive and undivided while the two larger Holds settled
their claim.

*

I have read this entry more than most, because it is one of the few in which she describes
functioning less as a combatant and more as what I can only call a custodian. She spent, by her
account, some eleven days at Ilvane before either delegation arrived to formally press its case --
repairing what she could of the ventilation system herself, though her own training gave her no
particular advantage at the task beyond patience and raw physical capacity; cataloguing, at the
failing Iron-Speaker's request, the settlement's remaining archive in case it could not otherwise be
preserved intact; and making plain, to both Threnarr and Aldreth's advance representatives when they
did arrive, that she would not permit either Vask to relocate a single Ilvane resident until the
whole population had agreed, collectively, on which absorption they preferred.

The residents chose Aldreth, in the end -- not from any preference for its claim over Threnarr's
historical one, but from the plainer calculation that six days' travel in failing winter conditions
was a risk Ilvane's oldest residents could not safely take twice, should Threnarr's more distant
galleries prove less accommodating than promised. Threnarr, to its credit as she records it, did not
contest the choice once it was made. Its Iron-Speakers instead negotiated, directly with Aldreth's,
to receive Ilvane's archive on loan for transcription before the originals were folded permanently
into Aldreth's own collection -- a compromise Lauris notes she did not propose and had, by that
point, already stepped back from the negotiation entirely to let both Holds reach themselves.

Her closing line, four words longer than most of her Kares Prime entries end: *Ilvane no longer
exists as a Vask. Forty of its residents do, in Aldreth's galleries, with their own archive intact
and on loan to the Hold that might otherwise have let them scatter unremembered. I record this as a
successful outcome, though I am aware that a civilization counting successful outcomes in these
terms has already conceded more than it has preserved.*

I have never found a way to disagree with that last sentence, and I do not believe she wrote it
expecting anyone to.
