# The Last Name Anyone Used for Him There

*Locked canon, Batch 257, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1201`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XC, wave 30, closing the wave. A final Killane-specific oral-history reflection -- Danne Sok, decades on, defines what the alias actually meant to the people who lived it, closing the thirtieth wave.*

---

Danne Sok was old now, one of the last of the original crew still telling the story to anyone who asked, and she'd noticed over the years that the version people wanted to hear kept drifting further from the version she'd actually lived through -- more Trinity, more legend, less mud and timber and forty-hour stretches without sleep.

"Everybody wants the Blue-Collar Titan to be a story about power," she told the young apprentice sitting across from her, one of the guild's newest, too young to have known the siege as anything but a chapter in his own training. "It wasn't, mostly. Power was in it. It wasn't the point of it."

"Then what was the point?"

She thought about it longer than the question probably warranted, turning through thirty Chronicles' worth of memory -- floods and fires and vapor pockets, a boy redirected from vengeance into a trade, a widow who never once said thank you, a council that told him no and meant it, a well built for no reason but that a street corner deserved one. None of the singular moments, she decided, actually answered it on their own.

"The point was that he never once let us believe the tunnels only held together because he was strong enough to hold them." She said it slowly, working out the shape of something she'd known for years but never had to say aloud before. "Everybody else who came through here with power like his made people feel small next to it. He made people feel like the work itself mattered more than whoever happened to be doing it that day. That's a harder thing to be than a hero. Took me most of my life to understand why it mattered more."

"Is that why they don't call him that anymore? The Blue-Collar Titan?"

"They don't need to call him anything." Sok looked out at the guild hall around them, the council chamber down the street, the well still running clean at the plaza's center. "The tunnels are still standing. The guild still tests people the way he was tested. Nobody down here needed the name to survive without him. That was always what the name was actually for."

The apprentice sat with that a long while before he spoke again, the question every generation eventually asked once the legend had settled far enough into history to need explaining at all.

"Did you ever tell him that? What it actually meant?"

"Once," Sok said, smiling at a memory decades old now. "He said it was the best thing anyone ever told him. Then he went back to checking a support beam, same as always, like being told that didn't change a single thing about the work left to do."
