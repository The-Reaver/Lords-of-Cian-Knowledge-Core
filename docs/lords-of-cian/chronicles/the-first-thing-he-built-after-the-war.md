# The First Thing He Built After the War

*Locked canon, Batch 257, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1199`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle LXXXVIII, wave 30, opening the wave. A peacetime coda -- the first project Kanja undertakes after the Sewer War of Killane's ceasefire, contrasted against thirty waves of crisis-driven work.*

---

Nobody asked him to build it. That was the first difference he noticed, standing at the edge of a small plaza three days after the ceasefire, with no crisis pulling him toward it and no schedule dictating how fast it needed to go up. The war that had defined every hour of this alias's existence was over. The tunnels were certified sound. There was, for the first time in longer than he could easily remember, nothing underground that needed him.

He built a well instead. Not a monument -- he'd already made his position on those clear -- just an ordinary working well at the plaza's center, drawing on a clean aquifer his own tunnel surveys had mapped years ago and never had occasion to use, sited where four streets met so no single district could claim it as only theirs.

"You could take a week off," Garren Hask said, watching him lay the first course of stone with no more urgency than any of the peacetime housing work had carried. "Nobody would think less of you for resting."

"I know." Kanja set the stone, checked it, moved to the next. "I wanted to build something first. Something that wasn't in response to anything trying to kill somebody."

It was slower work than anything the siege had demanded, without the compressed urgency that had shaped thirty waves of crises answered in hours or days. He took two full weeks over it, more care in the joinery than strict function required, because for the first time the only deadline was his own patience with the work itself.

Children found the finished well before most of the adults did, testing the pump handle with the particular delight of something new that hadn't existed in living memory at that corner. None of them knew or particularly cared who'd built it. That, more than anything about the well itself, was the part Kanja found he'd needed.

"First thing since the war that wasn't built because someone might die without it," he told Hask, watching a small crowd gather around clean water running freely for the first time in years. "Just because a street corner deserved something good, with no other reason attached."

"How does it feel?" Hask asked.

"Quiet," Kanja said. "I could get used to quiet."
