# The Word Callum Breck Chose

*Locked canon, Batch 116, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-397`). Captain Alias Chronicle III, closing the wave.
Rebellion era, following Callum Breck's already-locked silence arc (`CC-119`) -- his four months of
near-total silence after Nev Torr's death at the Black Trench, broken first only to confirm Iron
Shallows' zero-casualty headcount ("Two hundred"). This Chronicle picks up after that first
confirmed utterance, not restaging it. Not a territory Chronicle. No new named characters beyond
the already-locked Callum Breck.*

---

It took another six weeks, after the two words that broke four months of silence, before Callum
Breck said anything that wasn't strictly functional — a bearing, a headcount, a supply figure, the
purely navigational speech he'd rebuilt his voice out of one necessary fact at a time. When he
finally did, it was not Kanja's name, and it was not the alias he himself had chalked onto a captured
officer's forehead a lifetime earlier at the Dredge-Line Ambush.

"Captain," he said, quietly, handing over a report on the coming week's supply routes, the word
arriving in the middle of an otherwise entirely functional sentence as though it had simply always
belonged there.

Kanja looked up, careful not to make more of it than the moment could bear. "You've never called me
that before."

"I know." Breck didn't elaborate immediately, the old caution still there even six weeks past his
first real words. "I gave you the other name. Trench Monarch. I didn't think about it, I just wrote
it down because I was angry and thinking about Sera, and it stuck to you before either of us had a
say in it. This one I thought about. For a while now, actually."

"Why this one?"

"Because Nev died under my watch, and you never once let anyone say it was my fault, not even when
I was too far gone to argue with them if they had." Breck's voice stayed level, the discipline of six
months of careful, rebuilt speech holding through a sentence that would once have broken it entirely.
"A monarch doesn't answer for what happens to the people under him. He just rules over it. You
answered for Nev. You answer for all of us, every time, whether or not it's convenient to. That's not
a monarch. That's just — a captain. The word's smaller than the other one. It fits better anyway."

He didn't wait for a response, returning instead to the supply figures as though the moment had
already closed itself, the way most of Breck's rebuilt sentences did. But the word stayed, spoken now
by the same man who had given him his very first title, and within the season it had spread through
the whole crew the way Hask's version of the same word had already begun spreading through the fleet
— two separate people, arriving independently at the same conclusion, for reasons that turned out,
when compared later, to be nearly identical.
