# The Ones Who Stayed Behind to Lie

*Locked canon, Batch 254, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1116`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXXXVI, wave 29. Five
volunteers stay behind during a forced retreat to fake continued resistance, buying the column time --
Bane wants to stay with them and is talked out of it, testing his restraint doctrine from the
opposite direction.*

---

The retreat had to happen faster than the column could physically move while still looking, from a
pursuing Directorate company's perspective, like it hadn't started yet. Five volunteers offered
themselves for the gap between those two facts before Bane had finished laying out the problem out
loud — all of them recently freed captives, none of them founding crew, people who'd been with the
column barely two months and had decided, apparently without needing to discuss it among themselves
first, that this was theirs to give.

Bane said no. Said he'd stay instead, that the column could move without him for a night, that this
wasn't a sacrifice anyone but him should be making.

They didn't let him.

"You're the reason half this column believes it's going to survive the winter," one of them told him,
plainly, the way people who've already decided something tend to speak. "If you stay here and we're
wrong about how this goes, that belief goes with you. We're not worth that trade. You know we're not,
and pretending otherwise doesn't make it true."

"That's not how I measure it."

"It's how the column measures it. It's how it has to. You walking at the head of that line means more
to more people right now than five more swords in a rear guard that's already lost either way. Let us
give this. You don't get to take every hard thing for yourself just because you're able to."

He didn't have an answer for that that held up against the arithmetic they'd already done without
him. He left, because they asked him to, and it cost him more to walk away from that fire than
anything Obsidian Malice had ever discharged.

The fires and the noise they kept up bought the column the six hours it needed to put real distance
between itself and the pursuit. Two of the five made it out afterward, moving fast and alone once the
deception had done its work. Three didn't.

Corren Halst found him standing apart from the column that night, not sleeping.

"They chose it," Halst said. "Not you."

"I know. That's the part I'm still working out how to carry — that the hardest thing I did tonight
was letting people who trusted me decide something for themselves that I would have taken from them
if they'd let me."
