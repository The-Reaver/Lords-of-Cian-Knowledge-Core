# The Voice Among Their Own

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Crow King Alias Chronicle XXII, wave 8, first
entry. Rebellion era, roughly two years after the Night of the Crow King (`MCD-236`, age 23). Not a
territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Three operations in a row had gone wrong in ways that pointed to nothing so simple as bad luck — a
rendezvous point compromised an hour before the crew arrived, a supply cache found empty when it
should have been full, a patrol schedule that seemed to already know which nights Kanja's people
would be moving. The pattern was clear enough that the crew's own leadership stopped assuming
misfortune and started assuming a leak.

Kanja didn't sweep the crew for suspects. He listened instead — the same discipline the Hymn-Engine
had always demanded of him, turned inward for the first time, sitting through routine planning
meetings for three straight days doing nothing but noting who spoke with genuine uncertainty about
operational details and who spoke with a fluency that didn't match how recently they'd actually
been briefed.

"You're not looking for a mistake," the crew's own security officer observed, watching him listen
rather than interrogate. "You're listening for the tell the same way you'd listen for a bad note in
the Braid."

"A lie sounds different from the truth, even when someone's gotten good at telling it. It's harder
to catch in one sentence. It's much easier to catch across three days of small ones, if you're
patient enough to actually listen instead of just watching for someone acting nervous." Kanja kept
his own suspicion off his face throughout, giving nothing away. "Most people who go looking for an
informant assume they'll look guilty. They usually don't. They usually just sound very slightly too
smooth."

By the third day, one voice had stood out clearly enough to act on — not through any confession, but
through the same trained-ear discipline that had once caught a triple-redundant sensor grid's own
blind spot, now turned toward a person instead of a garrison. The informant was quietly removed from
operational knowledge before any confrontation, the leak sealed without a single accusation ever
being spoken aloud.

"You never told him you knew," the security officer said afterward, still faintly amazed by the
restraint of it.

"He'll work it out eventually, once nothing he reports lands anywhere useful anymore. That's enough.
I didn't need a confession. I needed the leak to stop, and listening is the same craft whether I'm
using it to build a lie or catch one hiding among people I trust."
