# The Captain Who Surrendered Nothing

*Locked canon, Batch 200, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-816`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle XXXI, wave 11 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Every slaver captain he'd faced across nearly fifty years had, at the moment defeat became certain,
made the same choice a dozen different ways — surrender the ship, surrender the cargo, surrender
whatever remained of their pride in exchange for whatever remained of their life. The captain holding
the burning transport's helm that night made a different choice entirely.

"I'm not giving you the satisfaction," the man said, blood running freely from a wound the Ironhand
Gauntlets had put there minutes before, his crew already disarmed and his hold already breached. "You
don't get to add my name to whatever list you keep of the ones who bent. I built this trade with my
own hands. I'll end it the same way."

He held a burning spar against the deck's own oil-slicked timber, close enough to a hold that still
held forty unrescued captives that the threat needed no further explanation.

"Killing yourself doesn't undo anything you've done," he said, not moving closer, reading the man's
resolve as genuine rather than bluff. "It just adds one more thing to the account nobody gets to
settle now."

"Better than living to watch you parade me through whatever port takes pleasure in it."

"Nobody's parading you anywhere. I don't work that way, and you'd know that if the stories you've
clearly been telling yourself about me were worth believing." He weighed the moment carefully — forty
lives in the hold, one man determined to take himself and possibly the deck with him, and no version
of the Ironfall Boots' speed that solved a choice this personal by force. "But I'm not going to beg
you to live either. That's yours to decide, not mine to talk you out of."

The captain held the spar a long moment, visibly surprised by the absence of argument, and in the end
set it down — not because anything had been won from him, but because, as he said afterward, flatly,
being denied even the satisfaction of being begged had taken something out of the gesture he hadn't
expected to lose.

"You don't get my surrender," the man said, disarmed but unbroken, as the hold opened behind him.
"Just my staying alive. Don't mistake the two."

"I never have."

Forty captives walked free that night, and the captain lived to face whatever judgment his own trade's
few remaining allies still owed him — a distinction, the crew's own records noted afterward, between
defeat and surrender that this one man alone, among every slaver the Scourge had ever faced, had
insisted on keeping intact.
