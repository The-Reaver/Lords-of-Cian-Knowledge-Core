# Everyone Who Ever Learned to Listen

*Locked canon, Batch 261, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1309`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XC, wave 30, closing the wave. A capstone reunion of the doctrine's teaching lineage and recurring figures, with the new unresolved countermeasure left open. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

Garren Hask called it a gathering rather than a council, and by the time everyone who could reasonably be asked to come had arrived, it looked like neither — just a crowded dockside room holding more of the doctrine's own history than Kanja had ever seen assembled in one place at once.

The first student, teaching full time now, hands steady enough in conversation even if a live discharge had passed to other hands. The second student, a generation of her own teaching behind her, the third-generation apprentice beside her, old enough now to sit at the table rather than stand at its edge asking questions no one else had thought to ask. The Trust scholar, "Tension Reading" now a real institution carrying his name on its founding charter rather than Kanja's. Dol Maren, present because Garren Hask had specifically asked him to be, the two of them trading dry, economical assessments of the evening's turnout the way they traded numbers in the ledger. Even the Trust diagnostician came, uninvited but not turned away, standing near the door the way a man stands who hasn't yet decided whether he belongs in a room.

"Thirty waves," the second student said, raising a cup toward no one and everyone at once. "However many that comes to in actual years, I've genuinely lost count."

"Ninety Chronicles," Garren Hask said, precise as always. "I keep the number because someone should."

Kanja let the room talk around him for a while before anyone turned the conversation his way directly, content to watch a doctrine that had started as one man's hand against one tower's iron fill a room with people who'd each carried a piece of it somewhere he'd never have reached alone — the first student's decade of restraint, the second's proof it survived a generation without him, the third-generation apprentice's insistence that judgment was always his and never the method's, the scholar's patience that outlasted an institution's own refusal to look, Dol Maren's calculation finally willing to call something a fact instead of a hope, even the diagnostician's stubborn, ongoing study of a man he still hadn't decided whether to call an enemy.

"There's still the Crawler from the depot," the second student said eventually, quieter, the one thread in the room nobody had wanted to raise first. "The one that read true and still didn't fall."

"There is," Kanja said. "I don't have an answer for it tonight, and I'm not going to pretend the room owes me one just because it's a good night to ask for it." He looked around at everyone the doctrine had touched, gathered now in one room by nothing more than Garren Hask deciding it was worth the trouble of asking them all. "Thirty waves in, and the thing I'm proudest of was never the discharges. It's this — a room I didn't build alone, holding a doctrine that's stopped needing me standing in it to keep being true. Whatever the depot's answer turns out to be, it'll get found the same way everything else here did. Slowly, honestly, by more hands than just mine."
