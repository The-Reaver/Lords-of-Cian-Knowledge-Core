# The Rival Who Wouldn't Defect

*Locked canon, Batch 261, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1288`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LXIX, wave 23, closing the wave. The Trust diagnostician declines to defect, establishing an ongoing respectful rivalry rather than a resolution. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

Kanja expected the offer to land the way it had with the engineer who'd once crossed over with a folded set of schematics — an invitation to keep building, just not for the Trust anymore. He made it anyway, plainly, the day after the bridge.

The Trust diagnostician turned it down without much deliberation.

"He came to you because he couldn't stomach what his corps was doing to the men testing their own designs," the man said. "I understand that reasoning completely. It isn't mine. I'm not disillusioned with the Trust. I disagree with you about what the doctrine is *for*, and defecting wouldn't resolve that disagreement — it would just mean I stopped being honest about which side I'm actually on while I keep having it."

"You tried to kill me with a false read on a bridge that didn't need to fall."

"I tried to find out whether you could be beaten by someone who understood you completely. I still don't fully know the answer — you won that engagement by refusing to play by the rules I'd built, which tells me something real about you, but it doesn't tell me the doctrine itself has no weakness a patient enough opponent could eventually find." He held Kanja's gaze without hostility in it, something closer to the same scholarly hunger from the bridge. "I'd rather keep looking, from where I actually stand, than pretend I've changed my mind about something I haven't."

"So we do this again."

"Probably. Differently, next time — I don't imagine the same trick works twice on someone who's already seen it." The man inclined his head, something almost like respect in the gesture. "I want you to understand this isn't hatred. I've spent four years coming to understand your own method more completely than almost anyone alive who isn't you. That kind of study doesn't leave room for hating the subject of it. It leaves room for wanting, very badly, to know exactly how far it goes."

Kanja let him walk back across the same bridge they'd spent eleven minutes lying to each other over, unescorted, uncaptured — a decision that would cost nothing to reverse and everything to have made wrongly, and one he made anyway.

"You could have held him," the second student said, watching the man's figure shrink toward the Trust lines. "Traded him, or worse."

"I could have. I don't think it would have taught either of us anything the bridge didn't already." Kanja watched until the figure was gone entirely. "The engineer left because he couldn't live with what his side was doing to its own people. This one isn't leaving because he doesn't think I've beaten him yet, not really — he thinks I've beaten the trap he built. Those aren't the same thing, and until he decides they are, I don't think there's anywhere for him to go but back to studying me a little more carefully than the last time."
