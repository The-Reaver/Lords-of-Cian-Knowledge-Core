# The Man Who Padded His Own Page

*Locked canon, Batch 198, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-747`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XXII, wave 8 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Ezio found the discrepancy the way he found most of them, by reading a figure twice and trusting the
second read more than the first: a foundry hand's claimed six years of unpaid overtime, checked
against the foundry's own shift logs, came out closer to four.

He didn't raise it in the yard, in front of the line still waiting to give testimony. He waited until
evening, found the man alone by the cooling sheds, and set both figures down side by side without
accusation in his voice.

"Your number's high," Ezio said. "By about two years, against the foundry's own logs."

The man didn't deny it right away, which told Ezio most of what he needed to know before the man said
anything else. "Everyone's rounding up somewhere. I figured mine could stand a little more than the
others'."

"Some people are rounding down, out of fear they're not owed as much as they think. You rounded up.
Why?"

The man was quiet long enough that Ezio nearly walked away rather than push further, and then it came
out at once, the way things did when someone had been holding them too long alone. His daughter had
taken a fever that hadn't broken in a week, the healer's price climbing with every day it didn't, and
he'd looked at the tally sheet with a number already forming in his head before he'd finished doing
the honest arithmetic.

"I'm going to correct it," Ezio said. "The book has to be right, or none of the other four hundred
entries in it mean anything either — the whole strength of this ledger is that nobody can point to
one padded number and call the rest of it a lie the same way." He watched that land, watched the man
brace for exposure in front of the district. "But the correction happens quietly, between us, not
read aloud in the yard tomorrow. And what you need for your daughter isn't something this ledger was
ever going to solve for you regardless of what number went in it. I'll see what the crew can spare
tonight. That's a different kind of help than a wage figure, and it doesn't need to be true to be
given."

The entry went in at the corrected figure the next morning, unremarked, indistinguishable from any
other line on the page. The healer's fee was covered from the crew's own thin stores before the week
was out, in a transaction that never touched the ledger at all.
