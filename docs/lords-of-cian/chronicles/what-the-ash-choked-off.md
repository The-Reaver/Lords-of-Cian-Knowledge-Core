# What the Ash Choked Off

*Locked canon, Batch 285, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1506`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XCV, wave 32. A coastal volcanic ash-fall -- a hazard this alias's ninety-plus prior entries have never used -- fouls the Ironhand Gauntlets' plating mid-engagement, the sub-series' first genuine equipment failure for this alias, forcing the Forge-Coat and Ironfall Boots to carry a fight without them. Corrected Batch 321, 2026-10-02: an earlier draft staged this as a full-Trinity showcase (Mafesto, Obsidian Malice, Onyx of Oblivion), despite this Chronicle's placement well within the Long Mask era of just over 283 years, after Kanja's already-locked age-30 surrender of the Trinity to its sealed vault (`MCD-246`); replaced with his correct Long-Mask-era kit (`ARS-344` through `ARS-356`, plus the Rexmar Machete), and the equipment-failure beat transferred from Obsidian Malice's discharge housing to the Ironhand Gauntlets' plates.*

---

The mountain had been venting fine grey ash over the Lawless Reaches' coastal shelf for three days before the wind turned and put the whole plume out over open water, and it was into that falling ash, not a storm, that a Directorate patrol boat led its own opportunistic strike against a small allied resupply convoy anchored blind in it.

The ash did what fog and moonless night had done before it in this alias's run -- stripped sight from everyone on the water in equal measure -- and for the first several minutes it seemed like one more variation on a problem Kanja's gear had already solved more than once. The Smoke System carried him across the anchored convoy's deck rails the same way it had carried him through smoke and darkness a dozen times, reading the fall of ash-muffled footsteps rather than any shape the eye could catch.

The Ironhand Gauntlets were the piece that failed. Kanja felt it first as a half-second's hesitation where a strike should have been instant -- ash, drawn into the plating on an inhale of the gauntlets' own working joints, packing fine and grey against the mechanism the way it had never been designed to shed. The second attempt didn't close cleanly at all, the grip guttering short a body's length from where it needed to land.

He didn't fight the housing. He set them, dead weight now, against his hips, and let the Forge-Coat and Ironfall Boots do what his full kit together had never strictly required either of them to prove they could do alone. The Forge-Coat and Ironfall Boots took a boarding axe's full swing and put it back through the man who'd thrown it, then a second, then a third, redirected shock compounding across the deck rail in a rhythm Kanja had never needed to rely on so completely before. The Rexmar Machete and his own instinctive Rexmar-Mar tactical sense carried him below and around rather than through, denying the patrol boat's crew a single clean look at where he actually was, buying the seconds the Forge-Coat needed to close the gap on the last three men standing.

It ended with the patrol boat's crew disarmed and the convoy's own cargo intact, ash still sifting down over all of it in a fine, silencing grey that muffled even the sound of the fighting's end. Kanja worked the Ironhand Gauntlets open on the convoy's deck afterward, packed to the seam with the same fine grey powder, and said nothing for a while, turning them over in his hands the way a man turns over a tool that has simply, honestly failed him for the first time.

"It'll clean," he said finally, to no one in particular. "Just not before the next storm asks it to work again."
