# What the Numbers Owed Him

*Locked canon, Batch 243, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1065`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle LXI, wave 21. The first entry where the fully cross-referenced ledger finds a claimant owes money back rather than being owed it, testing whether the method's impartiality holds when the finding cuts against a worker instead of an administrator. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The man's name was already three lines down the tally sheet before anyone looked closely at the numbers behind it. He'd come to the recording tent certain of three years' unpaid wage shortfall, certain enough that he'd been telling the story to anyone who'd listen for a week before the crew ever arrived. Ezio took his testimony the same way he took everyone's: patiently, in full, cross-referenced against the mill's own production logs and against six other workers on the same floor.

The cross-referencing didn't confirm the shortfall. It found something else. Eighteen months earlier, during a fever that had kept the man from the floor for eleven weeks, the mill's administrator — a man nobody on the crew had any reason to think well of, whose other conduct toward his workforce Ezio's own book already documented as exploitative in a dozen separate ways — had quietly continued paying him half wages out of his own pocket, off any company ledger, to keep the family from starving. It wasn't charity the man had ever been told to expect or had any right to demand. It was also, once the true production numbers were run against his actual hours worked across three years, more than enough to erase the shortfall entirely and leave a small balance owed the other way.

Ezio showed Kanja the figures twice before either of them said anything. The administrator's other conduct was still what it was — nothing in this finding excused the wage floor he ran everywhere else, and the crew's book on him elsewhere stayed exactly as damning as it had been that morning. But this one column, on this one man, ran the wrong direction, and the ledger had never once adjusted a number to keep a story comfortable.

"You're not owed anything here," Kanja told him. "You owe a small amount back, and it's written down as such."

The man's face went through disbelief, then something closer to anger, before it settled somewhere neither of them had a name for. He hadn't lied on purpose — he'd genuinely believed the story he'd been telling, the way a wound believes it's still bleeding after it's closed. What broke the anger, eventually, wasn't Kanja's explanation. It was watching Ezio write the correction into the same book, in the same hand, with the same care, right next to forty entries that ran hard against employers on that same floor. No softer ink for a man on the other side of a finding. No harder ink either.

He paid it back over two months, in small amounts, and told people afterward — more than once, more than the crew ever asked him to — that the reason he trusted what the ledger said about the district's worst offenders was that he'd watched it say something he didn't want to hear about himself first, and it hadn't flinched either time.

*Continuity notes (not narrative): a genuinely new impartiality register never shown across the prior twenty waves -- every previous finding, adversarial or reconciled, has run against an employer, administrator, or institution (MCD-747's "never-adjust-the-numbers" discipline has always been demonstrated in that direction). This is the first entry where the fully cross-referenced arithmetic finds a worker-claimant owes a small balance back, and the method applies the same unflinching rigor to that finding as to any case against a hostile administrator -- cementing the crew's credibility as genuinely neutral rather than one-sided, a distinct thematic beat from MCD-1033's preemptive-compliance entry (which tested administrator motive, not directionality of the debt itself). No new named characters; the worker and administrator are unnamed and one-scene. Strictly unarmed and non-combat throughout.*
