# The Mountain That Ate His Voice

*Locked canon, Batch 260, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1259`). Crow King Alias Chronicle LXVII, wave 23,
opening it. A high-altitude pass strips vocal projection of its power, the first purely
environmental (not injury-based) limit on the Hymn-Engine's spoken half. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

Nobody had told the third generation what thin air did to a voice, and by the time she understood
it, they were already two days into the pass and it was too late to plan around.

The trick they needed was small -- turn a border patrol's attention away from a narrow saddle trail
for the length of one crossing -- and it should have taken four singers and twenty minutes. It took
four singers gasping through half-phrases that came out thin and cracked, carrying maybe a third as
far as they should have, swallowed by air too poor in itself to hold a sustained note the way the
lowland marsh always had. The patrol didn't fall for a lie that never reached them clearly enough to
be believed.

"It's not fear making them stop," Kanja said, watching the fourth singer double over. "It's the
mountain. We can't out-sing altitude."

They didn't try to. The fourth generation, along for this crossing, pulled out a set of signal flags
he'd built for exactly this kind of failure and never yet needed -- colored cloth against snow,
readable at a distance sound couldn't reach up here at all, a full deceptive pattern built entirely
out of visual misdirection instead. It took longer to teach the pattern by hand-signal than it would
have taken to sing it. It worked exactly as well.

"We should have built this before the mountain taught us we needed it," the third generation said
afterward, still hoarse two days later.

"We built it because a mountain taught us we needed it," Kanja said. "That's usually how the craft
grows. Nobody sits down and imagines every place it might fail. Something fails first, and then
somebody builds the piece that was missing."

The flag-pattern went into the same unwritten inventory as the tap-signal channel from the throat
wound years before -- another backup, built from a real limit rather than a hypothetical one, carried
forward the same way.
