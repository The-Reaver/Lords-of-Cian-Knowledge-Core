# The Column Built Without Metal

*Locked canon, Batch 124, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-420`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle V. Rebellion era,
a detailed Aegis-Talisman/Trinity showcase against a countermeasure engineered around a new
material principle. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The engineering corps' third attempt at an Iron Bastard countermeasure abandoned metal fittings
almost entirely — a column built from reinforced hardwood, ceramic composite plating, and rope-lashed
joints instead of the Dead Drakma and standard alloys every prior variant had relied on, engineered
specifically on the theory that a resonance harmonic needed something metallic to answer.

It nearly worked. Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System found the wooden plating far harder to read than
any metal hull — no clean resonant signature to catalogue, no obvious structural weakness the Aegis-
Talisman's frequency-inversion lens could immediately exploit — and for the engagement's first three
minutes, the column advanced further against him than any prior variant had managed.

Kanja adjusted rather than retreated. The lens found what the wooden plating couldn't hide: not a
metallic resonance, but the rope lashings holding the entire column together, natural fiber under
tension answering its own harmonic once the frequency search widened past metal entirely. The
rope-work began fraying and loosening across every joint simultaneously, the column's own
non-metallic ingenuity becoming, ironically, a more uniform vulnerability than metal fittings had
ever been — every lashing sharing the same tension-response signature in a way that welded joints
never had.

Obsidian Malice discharged into the first structurally compromised section, the column's wooden
plating splitting along seams the loosened lashings could no longer hold together, and Onyx cleared
the boarding attempt that followed with Cadence Ruin finding the same coordinated-timing gap it
always eventually found, regardless of what the attackers were carrying.

"You built around the wrong assumption," Kanja told the engineering corps' field observer afterward,
watching the disabled column's crew withdraw. "You assumed the resonance needed metal. It needs
tension — anything holding a structure together under stress has a frequency it answers to,
whatever material's actually doing the holding. Wood over metal just meant I had to look somewhere
else for the same kind of weakness. It was always going to be there. Structures don't get to opt out
of physics by changing what they're made of."

The observer's report, filed that evening, would become one of the Directorate's most closely
studied documents on the Iron Bastard doctrine — the first to conclude, correctly, that no material
substitution alone could ever fully close the vulnerability, because the vulnerability was never
about the material in the first place.
