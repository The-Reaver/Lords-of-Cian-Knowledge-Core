# The Numbers That Made Him Famous and Feared

*Locked canon, Batch 198, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-760`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XXXV, wave 12 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Word of the worst-off-first method had traveled ahead of the campaign by the time it reached the
milling combine's newest district, and Ezio noticed, on the first morning, that the workers waiting
at the drying sheds looked rehearsed in a way no genuinely worst-treated worker ever had before.

"Something's wrong with this line," he told Kanja quietly, after the fourth testimony came back
suspiciously round in its figures, suspiciously fluent in the language of grievance, each account
building sympathetically on the last with a consistency real suffering rarely produced on demand.

They cross-checked the sheds against the combine's own production logs the way they'd checked every
district before, and found the discrepancy this time running the opposite direction from every prior
one — not workers under-claiming out of fear, but a handful of the drying-shed hands claiming
hardship well past what the logs, or their own visibly better condition compared to workers
elsewhere in the yard, supported.

"They're not lying about being poorly treated," Ezio said, once the pattern was clear. "They're
lying about being the worst-treated, because the administration figured out your own method well
enough to stage the sympathetic entries first and hope we'd stop looking once we'd filled the
'worst-off' portion of the tally with numbers that read convincingly enough."

"So the fame did what fear used to do," Kanja said. "It taught them what I was looking for instead of
teaching them to hide it."

The fix wasn't a new confrontation — it was a quieter adjustment to the method itself. Ezio began
cross-referencing every early, emotionally fluent testimony against the district's least-visible
workers before finalizing any figures, deliberately seeking out the ones who hadn't been positioned
at the front of any line, staged or otherwise, and weighting the tally toward whichever account held
up hardest under the production logs rather than whichever account arrived first and most
persuasively.

It cost them two additional days in that district, verifying what would once have been accepted on
the first afternoon. The finished ledger, once complete, still opened with genuine hardship rather
than staged sympathy — but the method itself had changed, permanently, from that district onward, a
quiet evolution neither of them advertised to anyone outside the crew.

"We're going to have to keep adjusting," Ezio said, once the tally was filed. "Every district that
hears how we work gets a little better at working around it."

"Then we keep adjusting." Kanja closed the ledger. "The day the method stops changing is the day it
stops being harder to fool than whoever's trying to fool it."
