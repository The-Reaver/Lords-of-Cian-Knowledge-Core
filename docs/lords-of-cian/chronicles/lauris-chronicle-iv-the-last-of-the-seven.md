# Lauris Chronicle IV: The Last of the Seven

*Locked canon, Batch 293, 2026-09-17 (`MCD-1564`). Fourth entry in Lauris Letitia's own Chronicle
series, the first of the "Strand L" (the Ledger / present-day operational debts) thread. Advances
one of her standing debts -- the third of Operation 38's withheld facilities (`MCD-191`: "the third
remains an open Lords of Cian objective as of the present-day timeline") -- without resolving it,
matching the project's established deepen-don't-resolve convention for reserved threads. Deliberately
avoids the K-Theta cave-system reveal, which `MCD-193` reserves for a future book (Kanja and the
wider crew don't know of it yet). The facility itself stays uncoded, matching `MCD-191`'s own
phrasing, rather than inventing an unnecessary new proper noun. No new named characters. Full text
below. Abad's approval: "lock."*

---

*Archive fragment, present-day: "Two of the seven cleared. Five years since the last. The debt does
not age well left alone. I went to look at what is owed before it can be discharged."*

Ezio does not often ask Lauris where she intends to spend a week. He asked this time, because the
answer touched a thread that predates the Lords of Cian itself: the third of Operation 38's
withheld facilities, the one she chose, during her final Directorate weeks, to keep from her own
report rather than hand to processing she no longer trusted. Two of the original three have since
been cleared. The third has sat untouched for years — not forgotten, never forgotten, but genuinely
unlocatable with the precision an operation would require, until an Aerelin-network courier
surfaced a fragment of a supply manifest that narrowed the search to a specific coastal ridge.

She went alone, which I have learned by now not to argue against. I record this account from her
own report to Ezio rather than from having accompanied her, and I want to be honest that this is a
poorer document for it — there is a texture to what she chooses to omit in a spoken report that her
written archive entries preserve more faithfully, and I have had to reconstruct some of what
follows from both sources together.

The ridge held what the manifest fragment promised: a facility built into the rock face itself, its
entrance disguised as a collapsed mining adit, consistent in construction with the Ionic
Rite-derived architecture she has now catalogued at a dozen sites across two worlds. She did not
attempt entry. This is, I think, the detail that matters most about this account, more than anything
she found: she has cleared facilities alone that killed entire Directorate teams, and she chose,
here, not to try.

Her reason, recorded plainly: the entrance's defensive architecture read as considerably more
sophisticated than either of the two facilities already cleared from the same original seven —
layered rather than singular, the kind of design that suggested whoever built it had learned
something from watching its siblings fall. She does not know what is behind the door. She knows
enough to know that finding out alone would be a wager rather than an operation, and she has told
me, in words I believe her, that the debt has waited five years and can wait for a properly
resourced clearance rather than a solo one that might close the account by getting her killed and
leaving the subjects inside it worse off than before.

She marked the location. She recorded its defensive signature in enough detail that whoever
eventually clears it — herself, in time, or Kanja's Trinity, or some combination the Lords of Cian
has not yet assembled — will not be walking in blind the way six Directorate teams once did at the
Vask of the Hollow. She reported to Ezio not that the debt was closer to discharged, but that it
was, for the first time in years, precisely *located* — which she considers, and I have come to
agree, a genuine kind of progress, even if it looks from the outside like a week spent finding a
door and then not opening it.

Her closing note to her own archive, distinct from her report to Ezio: *The last of the seven is
real. It is guarded better than its siblings were. I did not open it. I am aware this is not the
version of this account I would have written twenty years ago. I record the difference without
judging which version was wiser.*

I have chosen to include that line because I think it says something true about where she is now
that no combat account could. She is not less capable than she was at Operation 17, alone against
twenty-three engineered subjects in a facility a mile beneath the sea. She is, I think, finally
willing to let a debt stay open a little longer in exchange for discharging it correctly rather than
quickly — and for a woman whose entire self-account has always been arithmetic, that is not a small
change.
