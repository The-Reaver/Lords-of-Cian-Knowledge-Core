# The Ruling That Changed Nothing

*Locked canon, Batch 259, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1250`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LXXXV, wave 29, first
entry. Age 200, V3 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The admiralty tribunal that had failed to agree on what the Scourge legally was reconvened, over a
century later, under three entirely different justices who had inherited the case file the way a
family inherits an unresolved dispute over land nobody living can quite remember the original
argument about. This time, remarkably, they reached a ruling. The Scourge was to be classified,
going forward, as an "unaffiliated maritime irregular of indeterminate jurisdiction, subject to
regional discretion" — a sentence that had taken four sessions and three redrafts to produce and
that settled, in practice, exactly nothing.

No admiralty court anywhere in the Trust's holdings was bound to enforce a classification with no
enforcement mechanism attached. No prize-law dispute had been resolved by it, because "regional
discretion" simply handed the same unanswered question back down to whichever port happened to be
asking it that week. The ruling was filed, published in the Trust's own legal record as required,
and cited exactly twice in the following decade — both times by advocates arguing opposite
conclusions from the identical sentence.

Kanja heard about it from Garren Hask, who'd tracked the case's slow progress through the legal
record purely out of the same completionist instinct that kept his ledger honest, and who reported
the ruling with something close to dry amusement. A hundred and two years, three sets of justices,
and the entire legal apparatus of the Sovereign Trust had produced, in the end, a sentence that
changed nothing about what he did the next morning or the morning after that.

"So it was for nothing," Kanja said.

"No," Hask said. "It proved something. Just not the thing they were trying to prove." What it
proved, in the end, was the same thing the first tribunal's deadlock had already suggested a century
earlier: that an institution built to classify things could spend a century trying and still fail to
build a box that fit a man they'd never once managed to actually catch.
