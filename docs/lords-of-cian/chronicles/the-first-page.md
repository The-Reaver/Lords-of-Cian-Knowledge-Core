# The First Page

*Locked canon, Batch 260, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1256`). Crow King Alias Chronicle LXIV, wave 22, opening
it. The lineage writes the discipline's underlying principles down as text for the first time. Not a
territory Chronicle.*

---

It was the third generation's idea, and she brought it to Kanja before she brought it to anyone else.

"Not tactics," she said, before he could ask. "Nobody's writing down how the Braid works, or what a
sensor grid looks like from underneath. That dies with whoever's holding it when it needs to die.
I mean the other part. The part underneath the part."

She had a single page, already half-filled in a careful, unhurried hand. Kanja read it standing,
the way he read most things that mattered. It said almost nothing about deception at all. *Listen
before you build anything. The plan is not the point; the people inside it are. Restraint is not
the absence of cleverness -- it is what cleverness is for.* A dozen lines like that, nothing a
Directorate cryptographer could use against a checkpoint, nothing that would tell an enemy where to
stand or when to move.

"You could teach a hundred people to sing the Braid and still lose the craft," she said, "if none of
them understood why we don't use it against a grieving man. That's the part I'm afraid of losing.
Not the trick. The reason we stop ourselves."

He asked her who else had seen it. She said no one -- not the fourth generation yet, not even a copy
kept anywhere but the one page in her own hand. "I wanted you to see it before it was more than one
person's memory of what you taught me."

He read it through twice more before he handed it back. "It's right," he said. "Every line of it.
Keep it close. Don't let it become a rule people follow because it's written down instead of because
they understand it -- the day it's the second thing, it's already worth less than the page it's
written on."

She folded it once, the same way she'd have folded a message meant to survive a search, and kept it
against her chest rather than in any pack. "I know exactly where I'm keeping it," she said. "Where
nobody thinks to look for the part that actually matters."
