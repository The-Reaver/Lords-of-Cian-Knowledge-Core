# The Last Names Before the Silence

*Locked canon, Batch 259, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1252`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LXXXVII, wave 29,
closing the wave. Age 308, V4 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose.*

---

Garren Hask no longer went out on raids at all by then, hadn't in years, and the ledger itself had
long since become the whole of what he still did for the crew — kept with the same exacting care it
had always carried, though his hands were slower now turning its pages and his eyes needed a lens
Pell Ostra had ground for him from spare Voidstone offcuts to read his own decades-old handwriting
clearly.

They went through the most recent stretch of it together on a quiet evening neither of them called
anything in particular, though both understood without saying so that evenings like it had grown
rarer than they used to be. Not a formal review. Just two men, one of them elderly in the plain way
of a very long life well-lived, going page by page through names neither had thought about in years
— the freed, the lost, the withdrawn, the held for account, the found and known imperfectly, every
register this ledger had ever needed across two hundred and seventy-eight years of keeping it.

"You could stop," Kanja said at one point, meaning it kindly. "Let someone else carry it from here."

"I could," Hask agreed, not disagreeing with the suggestion so much as setting it gently aside.
"I'm not finished with it yet." He turned another page. Somewhere in the ledger's oldest section,
faded now past easy reading, was a tally from the Black Trench that had started all of this — ninety-
three of a hundred and twenty survived, the first number he'd ever counted for a war he hadn't yet
understood he'd be counting for the rest of his life.

They didn't finish the ledger that night. There was too much of it left to finish in one sitting,
and neither man was in any particular hurry to reach the last page while there was still more to add
before it. Kanja left him to it eventually, the way he always did, the lamp still burning long after
the door closed behind him.
