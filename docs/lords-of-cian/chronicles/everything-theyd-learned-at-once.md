# Everything They'd Learned at Once

*Locked canon, Batch 262, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1335`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LXXXIX, wave
30. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241), near the tour's later stretch. A
detailed, battle-intense Trinity combat showcase against a Directorate assault combining multiple
previously-countered tactics at once. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose.*

---

It was, the senior smith's successor said afterward, the first time the Directorate had ever
actually done its own homework properly — not one tactic borrowed from a single failed prior
attempt, but four at once, stacked deliberately to deny the campaign the chance to answer them one
at a time the way it always had before. A sabotaged support structure timed to draw the Trinity
underground, the way the mine collapse once had (MCD-1083). A second team staged for the moment
attention split, the way the ambush at the surface once had. A counterfeit supply signal meant to
misdirect reinforcements, learned from the fabricated burning reports of an earlier season
(MCD-970). And beneath all of it, dense civilian foot traffic deliberately routed through the site,
betting that the crowd itself would slow any decisive response the way the pilgrimage once had
(MCD-1332).

"They've been watching every fight we've ever won," Kanja said, feeling the whole shape of the
trap close around him at once. "And they built this one specifically so that none of the answers
that worked before would work alone this time."

None of them worked alone. That was the fight's real difficulty, and its real solution — Mafesto's
Kinetic Transfer System grounding a collapsing support the instant it gave, the exact reflex the
mine collapse had trained; Onyx of Oblivion's Cadence Ruin reading the crowd's rhythm for the
hidden second team the exact way the pilgrimage crowd once demanded, finding the wrongness inside
the noise before it could spring; Obsidian Malice's discharge cutting precise, narrow containment
lanes through civilian density rather than open pulses, protecting bystanders exactly the way the
shrine procession had taught the Trinity to; and Garren Hask's own now-practiced eye, walking the
supply manifests in real time, catching the counterfeit signal before reinforcements could be
misdirected the way the fabricated reports once nearly managed.

It took every lesson the campaign had ever earned, applied at once rather than in sequence, and it
held — the collapse contained, the second team exposed before it could move, the crowd unharmed, the
real reinforcements arriving exactly where they were actually needed. The Directorate's own after-
action assessment, recovered afterward, called the operation a failure of coordination. It hadn't
failed for lack of coordination. It had failed because the thing it was coordinated against had
already learned, the hard way, every single piece of what it was built from.

"They gave us one fight," the successor said, surveying the site once it was over. "We answered it
with a dozen."

"That's the only version of this method that was ever going to survive this long," Kanja said.
"Not winning once. Remembering everything that ever tried to beat it."
