# The One Who Didn't Need Him Anymore

*Locked canon, Batch 215, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-940`). Bane Alias Chronicle LVI, wave 19. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The relief operation at Threndale had been running for two months before anyone in the column
realized who was running it. A converted granary had become a working clinic and distribution
point serving eleven hundred displaced people, organized well enough that Pell Ostra, passing
through to resupply, assumed it had grown up under some established local authority long before
the rebellion touched the region. It hadn't. It had grown up under a woman named for a settlement
that no longer existed, who Bane recognized the moment he saw her only because of the particular
way she still favored her left leg.

Two years earlier, in the first months after the Black Trench, she'd been one of a hundred and
forty people pulled out of a Directorate labor camp near Kessic — one name among many in Hask's
ledger from an operation Bane barely remembered the specifics of anymore, folded into dozens of
others that had run the same shape. She remembered every detail of it. She did not, watching him
now from across a supply line she'd organized herself, seem to need him to know that.

"You don't have to thank me," he said, when she finally approached him. "I can see what you built
here. It doesn't need anything from me it hasn't already got."

"I wasn't going to thank you." She said it without unkindness. "I was going to tell you I stopped
needing anyone to free me twice. The first time was yours. Everything since has been mine, and I'd
rather you see it that way than as something owed back to you."

He stayed two days, not as Bane but as an extra pair of hands moving grain sacks and mending a
collapsed section of fencing, and left the same way he'd arrived — without ceremony, without the
clinic's operation changing anything about how it ran once he was gone, which was, he told Halst
on the road out, exactly the outcome that mattered most to him about the whole visit.

"Two years of freeing people," he said, "and most of what I actually want to see is this — someone
who doesn't need me showing up again to keep doing it."
