# The Last Reading She Insisted On

*Locked canon, Batch 263, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1355`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LXXXII, wave 28, first entry in the wave. The retired successor, now very elderly and feeling the cold settle permanently into her hands, insists on making one more call herself -- a deliberate parallel to Sephtis's own late-life entry, distinct in its cause and its bearing.*

---

It wasn't a fever, the way it had been for Sephtis. It was slower than that, and the retired successor described it plainly, the way she described everything now: a cold that had moved into her hands over the past two winters and simply never fully left again, stiffening her grip on a chart the way age stiffened everything eventually, if a person lived long enough to let it.

"I can still read the sky," she told the student, who had come to check on her the way she did most mornings now. "I'm just slower getting it onto paper than I used to be. I want to make one more call myself, start to finish, before that stops being true."

The student didn't argue it, though she offered, gently, to write while the successor dictated. The successor refused. "Sephtis dictated his," she said. "I watched him do it, in full view of the fleet, and I understood then it was the bravest thing I ever saw him do -- not hiding what the years had taken. I don't want the same generosity extended to me from you. I want to prove my own hands can still finish what they start, one more time, even slow."

It took her the better part of an hour to chart what would have taken her twenty minutes decades earlier, her hand tracing the pressure lines with visible effort, the cold in her joints making each stroke deliberate where it had once been quick. The call itself, when she finally set it down, was correct down to the hour -- a coastal squall the fourth-generation apprentice confirmed independently before it arrived, matching her number precisely.

"You didn't need to prove anything," the student said, watching her set the instrument down at last. "We already knew you could still read."

"I didn't do it for you," the successor said. "I did it for the part of me that still needed to know I could finish something with my own two hands before I let anyone else finish things for me instead." She looked at the chart a long moment, the ink of her own increasingly unsteady hand next to decades of steadier entries in the same ledger. "Sephtis taught me that difference matters. Being helped and being replaced aren't the same thing, and a person ought to get to choose which one happens to them, for as long as choosing is still possible."

Kanja, present as he had been for so much of this lineage's history, understood he was watching the same courage Sephtis had shown from a fever-shaken hand, offered now from hands merely old, the two entries decades apart and built from entirely different causes, arriving at the same honest place.
