# What His Own Senses Couldn't Tell Him

*Locked canon, Batch 263, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1358`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LXXXV, wave 29, first entry in the wave. Kanja's own heightened Rex/Mar senses give him a false read on an approaching threat, establishing on the page for the first time that his biology is not itself a form of weather-sense.*

---

He felt it before anyone reached for an instrument -- a pressure change registering somewhere in the dense, tuned awareness that had always let him sense structural strain, mass shifting, danger approaching at a scale ordinary senses missed. It read, to him, like weather. Big, gathering, close.

It wasn't weather. By the time the student's own reading came back clear -- pressure steady, sky honest, nothing building for two days in any direction -- Kanja had already moved half a watch to a defensive posture against a storm that didn't exist, based on a certainty his own body had handed him with total confidence.

"You were wrong," the student said, not as an accusation, genuinely curious, because in all the years she'd known him, she'd never once seen his instincts betray him this plainly.

"I felt something," he said, working through it aloud rather than defending it. "Density, mass, pressure -- I've always read those the same way, across everything. A storm has weight to it, moving weight, and I've felt that weight coming before your own instruments confirmed it, more than once." He looked out at the honest, empty sky. "This time I felt weight, and there wasn't a storm behind it at all."

It took the fourth-generation apprentice, working through the ledger's own historical entries with more patience than either of the others had time for in the moment, to find the actual cause: a Titan-class vessel, entirely unrelated to any weather system, had passed far out at sea that same morning, its own mass registering on Kanja's density-tuned senses as something approaching with weather's own characteristic weight and direction, simply because mass read as mass to him regardless of what was carrying it.

"That's the answer," the student said, once the boy laid it out. "You didn't misread a storm. You correctly read mass moving at scale. You just assumed it had to be weather, because weather's the only mass-at-scale threat this alias usually asks you to notice."

Kanja considered that a long moment, something clarifying in it that went past this single incident. "I've spent this whole alias thinking of myself as the protection around your reading," he said. "Not a second reading of my own. This is the first time my own senses actually tried to hand you an answer, and it was the wrong one."

"Good," the student said, and meant it plainly. "Now we both know exactly where your gift ends and mine begins. That's not a small thing to have clean, after this many years of assuming it."
