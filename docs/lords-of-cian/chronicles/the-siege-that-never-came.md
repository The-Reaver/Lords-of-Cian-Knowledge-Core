# The Siege That Never Came

*Locked canon, Batch 223, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1011`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle LV, wave 19. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Every indicator said the assault was coming that night — troop movements doubled at the nearest
garrison gate, supply carts rerouted toward the district's northern edge, the particular quiet that
Kanja had learned, over five weeks, to read as the quiet before rather than the quiet of nothing
happening at all.

He spent the full day preparing for it anyway, the same way he'd have prepared for a certainty.

Obsidian Malice was charged and ready by midafternoon. Every collapsible chokepoint in the northern
approach was rigged and re-checked twice. The crew rotated into defensive positions two hours before
dusk, tense in the particular way that came from knowing exactly what was about to happen rather than
merely fearing it might.

Nothing happened.

Midnight passed with the garrison gate quiet again, the doubled troops apparently reassigned to
something that had nothing to do with the tunnel network at all. The supply carts that had seemed to
signal an advance turned out, by morning, to have been routed toward an entirely different district's
entirely unrelated resupply. Every read Kanja had made, every indicator he'd trusted enough to spend a
full day and a full crew's readiness on, had pointed at an attack that was never coming.

"Five weeks of reading this ground right, and I read it wrong once, on the one night it actually
mattered whether I got it right," he said to the crew chief near dawn, watching the tension drain out
of positions that had held all night for nothing.

"You didn't read it wrong. You read real movement, real rerouting, real quiet. It just wasn't aimed at
us." The crew chief didn't sound troubled by the wasted night the way Kanja clearly was. "That's not a
failure of the read. That's just what happens some nights, in a war where the other side moves for
reasons that have nothing to do with you."

"I know that. Knowing it doesn't make the last twelve hours feel like anything other than twelve hours
this crew won't get back — twelve hours of sleep lost, positions held, charges set and checked twice,
against nothing." Kanja walked the northern chokepoints himself, disarming the charges he'd rigged the
day before, the same careful attention he'd have given them if the attack had actually come. "I think
that's the part of this work nobody warns you about. It's not always the fight. Sometimes it's just
standing ready for one that never shows up, and finding out afterward that being wrong about danger
costs almost as much as being right about it would have."

The crew stood down fully by midmorning, no less prepared for the next real indicator than they'd been
for this one — the wasted night neither dismissed as meaningless nor treated as a mistake worth
correcting, simply absorbed the way any siege eventually absorbed its own long stretches of nothing.

"Same readiness tomorrow, if the signs say the same thing again," Kanja told the crew chief, checking
the last disarmed charge. "I'd rather spend twelve hours ready for a fight that doesn't come than one
hour unready for the one that does. That's not a trade I get to make cheaper just because it stings
more some nights than others."
