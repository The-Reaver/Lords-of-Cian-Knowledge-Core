# The Table Across from the Owners

*Locked canon, Batch 182, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-531`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XIII, first entry
in the fifth wave. Rebellion era, age 18, before the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. New
standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Every prior negotiation had run through intermediaries, foremen, or the crowd itself — this was the
first time the site owners consented to sit across from Kanja directly, in a formal room, with
nothing between them but a table and the tally sheets both sides already knew were accurate.

"You've made this considerably harder to ignore than a shouting crowd would have," the senior owner
said, opening with something between resentment and grudging respect. "I confess I expected someone
less patient than the reports describe."

"I've never found impatience won anything the numbers couldn't win on their own, given enough time to
be heard properly." Kanja laid out the tally sheets without ceremony, each figure already independently
verified, nothing left for the room to dispute on technical grounds. "I'm not here to threaten you. I'm
here because the numbers say this line is owed something specific, and I'd rather settle that plainly,
across this table, than let it become something louder than it needs to be."

"And if I simply refuse? You have no leverage in this room beyond the numbers themselves."

"The numbers are considerable leverage, when they're this hard to dispute honestly. But you're right
that I have nothing beyond them, in this room. I've never wanted anything more than that. A settlement
built on what's actually owed doesn't need anything beyond the numbers to hold." Kanja met the owner's
skepticism without flinching from it. "If you refuse, the line keeps working under the same conditions,
and I keep documenting exactly what that costs the men doing it. Eventually the numbers become
impossible to ignore in a much larger room than this one. I'd rather settle it here, quietly, before
that happens."

The owner studied him a long moment, weighing the actual absence of threat against the plain
inevitability of the argument. "You're offering me the chance to settle this before it becomes public
enough to cost me more than the settlement itself."

"That's exactly what I'm offering. I'd rather this resolve fairly than dramatically. The drama only
happens if fairness isn't available first."

The settlement was reached within the hour, the terms matching the tally sheets almost exactly — the
first time an owner had negotiated directly with the reputation rather than around it, and the
clearest evidence yet that the reputation's real power had always been the numbers underneath it.
