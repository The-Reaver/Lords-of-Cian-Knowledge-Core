# The Crossing With No Water

*Locked canon, Batch 281, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1473`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle XCVIII, wave 33. Age
155, V3 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The route ran three days across a salt flat with nothing standing on it taller than a man's knee and
nothing under it but cracked white ground that gave off heat like a second sun by midday — a corridor
the crew had never worked before, chosen by the traffickers running it specifically because no fleet,
however legendary at sea, had any obvious reason to follow a chain of captives this far inland. There
was no ship to disable, no harbor to hold, no night to hide the approach in; the flat was too flat for
that, visible for miles in every direction under a sky that offered no cover and a sun that offered no
mercy.

The Forge-Coat's thermal-regulating lining, built across two centuries mostly to manage cold water and
colder nights, worked harder in the other direction than its designers had ever tested it for,
venting heat through the same channels meant to hold it in. It bought him hours he didn't otherwise
have. It didn't buy the captives any — forty-one of them, chained in an open cart with no shade and a
water ration cut deliberately thin to keep them too weak to run, three days into a crossing that had
already cost two lives before he ever reached the caravan.

He couldn't fight the sun. What he could do was time the approach for the one hour before dawn when
the flat's heat finally broke, moving in fast and low while the guards were still sluggish from a
night spent as miserable as their captives, the Ironhand Gauntlets' knuckle plates ending the
confrontation in under two minutes against men too heat-sick to properly resist. The real work came
after: rationing what water the crew had carried in across thirty-nine surviving people who needed it
faster than caution allowed, walking the caravan's own emptied water casks back to a spring two hours
out rather than trusting the captives to the return crossing dry.

There was no legend in it, no smoke, no glowing eyes doing any of the frightening for him — nothing
about a salt flat responded to fear the way a slaving captain's crew sometimes did. Garren Hask's
count that season read thirty-nine freed, two lost before the rescue ever reached them, and a route
now known well enough that the crew began carrying water inland on principle, whether or not that
season's crossing needed it.
