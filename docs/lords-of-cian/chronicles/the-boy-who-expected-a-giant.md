# The Boy Who Expected a Giant

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XXXV, wave 12.
Rebellion era, age 18, before the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The boy was maybe nine, small even for that, and he had positioned himself at the edge of the Marrow
Landing crowd for the better part of two hours, waiting to see the man the printed sheet had called a
king who did not lie.

When Kanja finally crossed the square — tired, mud still on his boots from a morning spent at the
cistern site three districts over, in the middle of an unremarkable conversation with Garren Hask
about a supply discrepancy — the boy's face fell in a way that was visible from several yards off.

"That's him?" the boy said, to no one in particular, loud enough to carry.

Kanja heard it, and stopped rather than let it pass. "Depends who you were expecting."

"Someone bigger. The sheet made it sound like—" the boy struggled for the word "—like you'd be
taller. Like there'd be something about you that looked like the story."

"I get that a lot, lately." Kanja crouched down to something closer to the boy's own height, which
seemed to help less than he'd hoped. "What did you think I'd look like?"

"I don't know. Like you'd already won something, just standing there. You just look like you've been
working all day."

"I have been working all day. That's most of what the story leaves out, I think — that it's mostly
just work, done carefully, over and over, until it adds up to something a sheet can print." Kanja
didn't try to talk the boy out of his disappointment, which surprised Hask, watching from a few feet
off. "I'm not going to pretend to be bigger than I am. I don't think that would actually help you,
even if I could manage it for an afternoon."

"Then what's the point of the story, if it's not even true?"

"It's true. It's just true about smaller things than the story makes it sound like — an honest
number, checked twice, read out loud where everyone can hear it. That's not as exciting as a giant.
It's more useful than one, though, if you're the person who needed the number to be right." Kanja
straightened up again, mud and all. "I'd rather you walk away disappointed that I'm ordinary than
walk away believing something about me that isn't true. The ordinary part is the part that actually
works."

The boy didn't look fully convinced, and said so, but he stayed near the edge of the crowd a while
longer, watching the ordinary work continue — the ledger consulted, the supply discrepancy resolved
in a few unremarkable minutes — and left, eventually, having traded one kind of story for a quieter,
harder-to-name one that he would, though neither of them could know it yet, remember considerably
longer than the giant would have lasted.
