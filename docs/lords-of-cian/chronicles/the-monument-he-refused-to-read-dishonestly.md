# The Monument He Refused to Read Dishonestly

*Locked canon, Batch 184, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-538`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XIV.
Rebellion era, age 20, the Sewer War of Killane. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material.
Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

A monument raised to celebrate the Directorate's founding stood at the district's center, hated by
every worker who passed it daily — and structurally sound, Kanja found the moment he laid a hand
against it, no genuine flaw for any legitimate safety concern to seize on. His own crew wanted it
condemned anyway, torn down as unsafe rather than left standing as a daily insult.

He refused.

"You've read a hundred structures this war and never once hesitated to call one unsafe when it was,"
the crew's own organizer said, frustrated by the refusal. "This one's propaganda in stone. Nobody
would blame you for finding a crack that isn't quite there."

"I'd blame myself. That's the part that actually matters here." Kanja kept his hand against the
monument's own foundation, the reading unmistakable and unwelcome in equal measure. "Everything I've
built with this alias rests on people trusting that when I say a structure is unsafe, it actually is.
The moment I lie about that once, for a cause I believe in, I've told every worker who's ever trusted
my reading that the honesty was conditional all along. I won't spend that trust on a monument, however
much I'd like it gone."

"So it just stands. A tribute to everything we're fighting against, and we tear down nothing because
you won't lie about a crack."

"It stands because I won't lie about a crack. That's different from doing nothing." Kanja considered
the monument a long moment, weighing the crew's genuine frustration against the principle he wouldn't
bend. "If you want it gone, take it down honestly — organize the district, make the political case
plainly, tear it down as what it actually is: a monument nobody wants standing, removed because the
people living under its shadow decided they were done tolerating it. That's a real victory. A
fabricated safety condemnation is a lie wearing my reputation's clothes, and I won't let this
reputation be spent that way, not even here."

The district organized instead, the monument's removal voted through openly within the month, torn
down as exactly what it was rather than what Kanja could have pretended it to be — a slower path, and,
by his own accounting, a more honest one.
