# What He Read With His Eyes Shut

*Locked canon, Batch 257, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1182`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle LXXI, wave 24. Kanja's own temporary blindness during a rescue forces him to rely entirely on the tactile method the old Killane digger taught him -- a payoff showing that lesson saving him rather than others.*

---

The dust cloud caught him full in the face before he'd braced for it, a secondary settling from a collapse he'd already stabilized, and it left both eyes streaming and useless long enough to matter. Behind him, four workers were still pinned under a partial cave-in he hadn't finished reading before the grit hit.

"I can get you clear," Corren Halst started, already moving to take over.

"No." Kanja held up a hand, blinking against nothing but white sting. "Don't move me. I need the tunnel exactly where it is."

It was the old digger's lesson come due at last -- decades of tactile reading he'd learned as a supplement, a deeper layer beneath what his eyes could already tell him, and had never once needed as a total replacement until this exact moment. He put both palms flat against the rubble and stopped trying to see it. The shape of a collapse told itself through vibration and grain if you let it, the way a stone face told a mason which way it wanted to be cut, and he'd spent enough hours learning that language secondhand that it didn't abandon him now that his eyes had.

He worked blind for six minutes that felt considerably longer, reading pressure through his palms, tracking the pinned workers by voice and by the specific give of timber under his own weight, directing Obsidian Malice's strikes by feel and memorized angle rather than sight. Mafesto's kinetic transfer, which had never depended on vision in the first place, filled in what his hands alone couldn't -- but it was his own trained hands doing the actual reading, not the relic doing it for him.

All four came free before his vision cleared enough to confirm it visually, which it did just as Halst was reaching to guide his hands back onto solid ground he'd already found without help.

"You did that whole thing without looking," Halst said, still not quite believing it.

"I didn't need to look." Kanja blinked hard, the world swimming back into focus around four workers already standing. "I needed to feel it right, and somebody taught me how a long time before I ever needed to."
