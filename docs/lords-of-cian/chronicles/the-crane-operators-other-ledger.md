# The Crane Operator's Other Ledger

*Locked canon, Batch 198, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-751`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XXVI, wave 9 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Dol Maren had come to the shipyard district to look at a hoist crew had complained was groaning wrong
under load, not to help with Kanja's wage ledger at all, but he found himself keeping a second book
alongside his within the first afternoon, because the two problems kept turning out to be the same
one.

"That derrick's been running past its rated weight for two years," he told Kanja, showing him the
worn cable housing he'd climbed up to inspect himself. "Whoever's signing off on the maintenance
schedule has been skipping the semi-annual inspection to keep the yard's output numbers up. I've
written down every near-failure I could find — frayed lines, a brake drum worn thin enough to see
light through, a counterweight bolt pattern that's been re-torqued so many times the threads are
half gone."

"That's not a wage entry."

"No. But look at who's working the derrick shifts your ledger says are going unpaid overtime." He
turned his book to face Kanja's, the two sets of names running down facing pages. "Same crew, almost
exactly. The yard's cutting corners on maintenance to avoid paying for a second shift's proper
inspection window, and squeezing the existing crew's hours to cover the gap instead of hiring more
hands. Your ledger shows the wage theft. Mine shows why it's happening. Neither book makes sense of
the whole thing alone."

Kanja read both pages side by side the way he'd once read a moneylender's book against his own, and
saw what Maren meant plainly enough that he didn't need it explained twice. The district
administrators, presented with the wage ledger alone, might have argued the numbers down to
something they could absorb without real change. Presented with both together — the unpaid hours and
the structural hazard those hours were funding the neglect of — there was no version of the response
that let them fix one without the other.

"They're going to try to treat these as separate complaints," Kanja said. "Wages to one office, safety
to another, each one slower than the last."

"Then we submit them stitched together, page for page, so no clerk can file half of it and lose the
rest." Dol Maren was already threading the two ledgers into a single binding as he said it. "A hoist
doesn't fail because of one bad bolt. It fails because every bad bolt got explained away by
something else that looked, on its own page, like somebody else's problem."

The combined account moved through the district administration faster than either book alone would
have — not because the wages mattered more paired with the hazard, but because neither could be
quietly set aside once the other made plain what the corner-cutting had actually been paying for.
