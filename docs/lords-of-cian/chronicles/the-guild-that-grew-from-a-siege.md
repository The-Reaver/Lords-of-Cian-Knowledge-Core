# The Guild That Grew From a Siege

*Locked canon, Batch 195, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-662`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XXVII, wave 9 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

By the fifth week of the siege, enough tunnel workers, conscripted engineers, and volunteer diggers
had passed through Kanja's crews that several of them, on their own initiative, proposed something
none of them had been asked to build: a standing association of Killane's underground tradesmen,
independent of any single crew, meant to outlast the war itself.

"We want to formalize what's already happening," the senior organizer told him, a former
repair-crew foreman who'd joined six weeks earlier still doubting the whole effort. "Shared
knowledge of the tunnel network. A standard for what's safe to dig and what isn't, taught the way
you've been teaching it. Mutual aid when a crew loses someone. We don't want it to end when the
soldiers leave."

"That's not something I should be building," Kanja said. "It should belong to the people who'll
still be here long after I'm gone from this district entirely."

"You started it. Every lesson we've adopted, every safety standard the crews now share across
sections that used to work in total isolation from each other — that traces back to the same
teaching you gave for free because you thought it mattered more than the digging itself."

"Then let it be yours from the first day, not mine." Kanja set down the diagram he'd been using for
the standard training and pushed it across the table. "Name it something that isn't tied to me at
all. Build the standard so it survives whether I'm remembered rightly, wrongly, or not at all. A
guild that only holds together because of one man's reputation isn't a guild. It's a crowd waiting
to scatter the day that man's gone."

The association took shape within the month, its founding charter carrying no mention of the alias
that had inadvertently seeded it, its safety standard credited instead to "lessons learned during
the siege" — an anonymity Kanja insisted on and the founders, after some argument, eventually
honored, understanding it as its own kind of teaching.

"You could have let them name it after you," the crew chief said once the charter was signed. "It
would have cost you nothing."

"It would have cost them everything, eventually. A name outlives a man's reputation exactly as long
as the man's reputation holds up, and no reputation holds up forever." Kanja watched the founders
carry the signed charter off toward the surface, toward a peacetime none of them had fully believed
in six weeks earlier. "I'd rather they build something that doesn't need me to keep standing."
