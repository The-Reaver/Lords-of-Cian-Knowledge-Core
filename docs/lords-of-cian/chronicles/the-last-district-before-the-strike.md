# The Last District Before the Strike

and non-combat throughout. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new

---

The smelting district's neighboring foundry town was the last stop before the Furnace District
itself, and Kanja spent most of it sitting at the back of his own ledger tent instead of at the
table.

Two workers ran the questioning that week — a loading-yard hand and a kiln-tender, neither one part
of the crew, both trained by Ezio over the preceding month in the same patient method: the same four
words, the same worst-off-first order, the same cross-check against the district's own production
figures before an entry was called finished.

"You're not going to ask anything yourself?" the kiln-tender asked him, the first morning, visibly
unsettled by how little Kanja intended to do.

"Not unless you ask me to. You know this district. You know who's been quiet the longest and who's
been waiting years to be asked. I don't know any of that half as well as you do, and the method
doesn't need me standing at the table to work — it needs someone willing to write the truth down and
correct it upward when it's given too small out of fear." He nodded toward Ezio, seated beside the
two of them rather than across from them. "He'll catch what you miss for the first day or two. After
that, you won't need him either."

They were unsteady the first morning, deferring to Ezio on entries they could have judged themselves,
second-guessing figures that turned out, on cross-check, to have been right the first time. By the
third day the deferring had mostly stopped. By the fifth, the completed ledger — four hundred and
sixty names, cross-checked twice, structurally undeniable — was finished without Kanja having asked a
single worker a single question the entire week.

"They're going to say the Industrial Myth did this," the loading-yard hand said, looking at the
finished tally with something between pride and disbelief. "You didn't do any of it."

"They can say whatever they want. The name was never the part that mattered." Kanja looked at the
ledger, then at the two of them, then past them toward the smelting district's furnace glow on the
horizon, days away now and closer every hour. "The part that matters is that this district has two
people in it now who know how to do this without anyone's name attached to it at all. That's the only
version of this that was ever going to outlast me."

He left for the Furnace District the next morning. The two trained workers stayed behind to finish
delivering the settlement, and the ledger tent, when the district next needed it, opened without him.
