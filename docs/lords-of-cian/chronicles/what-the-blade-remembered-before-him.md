# What the Blade Remembered Before Him

*Locked canon, Batch 255, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1127`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle LXX, wave 24.
Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. A rare private exchange between Kanja and Onyx of Oblivion, no
combat, clarifying what "Soulbound" actually means. Onyx's lines corrected Batch 359, 2026-10-03, to the Voice Bible's utilitarian register (VB-063); no facts changed. Abad's approval: "go."*

---

Most nights the blade gave nothing that couldn't be read in how it moved. Onyx of Oblivion didn't
speak the way a person spoke, not even to Kanja; what passed between them came only through the
grip, bone-conducted, meaning arriving whole rather than assembled letter by letter. On the night in
question, cleaning the blade by lamplight after a day with no fight in it at all, his left hand
closed on the grip, Kanja asked it a question he'd never quite worked up to before.

*Who held you before me.*

What came back wasn't an answer so much as a fragment — an impression of hands, plural, none of them
his own, stretching back further than he had any way to measure. Not a name. Not a face. Just the
weight of having been chosen, and having chosen back, more times than he could count, by people whose
stories the blade carried the shape of without carrying the details.

"I thought Soulbound Edge meant you were mine," he said aloud, half to himself. "Like Mafesto is."

The correction that reached him through the grip was flat and exact. Mafesto had been forged and
bonded to him specifically, built for his hand and no other's. Onyx had existed for centuries before
that, had been chosen and had chosen in turn, again and again, by wielders whose names had long since
worn away from whatever memory the blade still carried of them. The bond wasn't ownership. It was
something closer to a standing invitation, renewed every time a new hand proved worth extending it
to — and withdrawn, the blade added in one cold fragment more, from at least one hand in its long
memory that hadn't.

"What happened to the one you left?"

The impression that answered came slower than any before it, the grip gone still and heavy under
his palm — not a refusal, and not the full account either; the blade gave the verdict and withheld
the rest. What came through was spare: a wielder who had, at some point centuries gone, asked the blade to
be a weapon for something the blade itself would not agree to be a weapon for. The bond had ended
there, on the blade's own terms, not the wielder's. Soulbound Edge, he understood then, wasn't only
the fraction-of-a-second instinct that had turned an honor blade's angle aside in a mutual killing
exchange months before. It was the whole relationship that made that instinct possible — a bond that
could be given, and had been given to him specifically, and could, in principle, be taken back.

"I don't plan on giving you a reason to."

The blade's response to that was a single settling of weight in the grip, wordless and brief, the
steel holding steady under his palm until he set it down for the night. He slept better than he had in weeks, for
reasons he didn't fully examine until morning — the particular relief, he decided, of understanding
for the first time that the sword at his hip wasn't a tool he'd been lucky enough to find, but a
choice something ancient kept making, on purpose, every single day he carried it.
