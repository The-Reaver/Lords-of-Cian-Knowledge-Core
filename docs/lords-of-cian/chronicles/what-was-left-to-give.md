# What Was Left to Give

*Locked canon, Batch 259, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1242`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LXXVII, wave 26. Age
130, V3 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Two years of failed rains had done what the slaving depot's own guards never managed: broken the
inland settlement long before any rescue reached it. Fifty-two captives freed from a grain-hoarding
operation that had been exploiting the drought as much as trading in people, and every one of them
walked out into a town with almost nothing left to receive them — granaries empty, wells low, the
settlement's own able-bodied residents already stretched past what they had to give a family, let
alone fifty-two strangers with nothing but the chains just cut from their wrists.

There was no fight left worth having by the time the depot fell. The actual work started after: the
captured operation's own hoarded grain stores, discovered intact behind a locked storeroom the crew
had assumed held weapons, turned out to be the drought's missing harvest, quietly stockpiled for
resale at famine prices rather than distributed. Redirecting it took three days of unglamorous
logistics — counting, rationing, arguing with a settlement council too proud and too desperate at
once to easily accept help from people they didn't know and had no reason yet to trust.

Nobody's gear solved any of it. The Forge-Coat didn't carry grain. The Sovereign Eyes couldn't read
a harvest that didn't exist. What moved sacks from a stolen storeroom to fifty-two families and a
town's own empty tables was simply hands, days, and the same patience the doctrine asked of every
surrender extended instead to a problem that had no one left to surrender to.

Garren Hask's ledger logged the rescue and, in a separate column he'd started keeping only in recent
years, logged the grain too — sacks distributed, families fed, a number that had nothing to do with
captives freed and everything to do with what freedom was actually worth to people who'd have
starved regardless of whether they'd ever been chained at all.
