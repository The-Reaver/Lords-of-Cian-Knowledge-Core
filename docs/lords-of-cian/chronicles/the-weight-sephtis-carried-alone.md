# The Weight Sephtis Carried Alone

*Locked canon, Batch 126, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-427`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle VI, closing the
second wave. Rebellion era, from Sephtis's own perspective -- already a locked named character. Not
a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new
named characters beyond the already-locked Sephtis.*

---

Sephtis had never told anyone, not even Kanja directly, exactly how many hours he spent before each
storm-timed engagement running the same calculation over and over, checking it against itself,
distrusting his own first answer until a second and third pass confirmed it — because the fleet's
confidence in his predictions had grown, across several successful engagements, into something that
felt less like trust and more like a weight he alone was expected to carry without ever showing the
strain of it.

"You make it look effortless," a younger crew member told him, the night before a new engagement,
watching him work through the calculations with what looked, from the outside, like calm
certainty. "Everyone just assumes you know."

"Everyone assumes that because I've made sure they never see the version of me that doesn't know
yet — the four hours before I'm confident, where I'm genuinely afraid I've gotten it wrong and
several hundred people are going to pay for my mistake." Sephtis didn't look up from his work. "The
miscalculation weeks ago wasn't the first time I've been wrong. It was the first time I've been
wrong in front of everyone, where the cost was visible enough that hiding the fear afterward wasn't
possible anymore."

"Does it help, that Kanja didn't blame you for it?"

"It helps that he built the whole plan afterward around not needing me to be perfect, instead of
quietly finding someone else to do this who might be. That's rarer than people realize, in this
position." Sephtis finally set his instruments down, the night's calculation as settled as it was
going to get before dawn. "I used to think the job was predicting the weather. It's not, not really.
The job is being willing to be wrong in front of people whose lives depend on you being right, and
trusting that the people relying on you will still trust you the next time, even knowing you can be
wrong. That's a harder thing to carry than the actual mathematics ever was."

He returned to his instruments before first light, running the calculation a final time the way he
always did, the fear he'd just described to a young crew member folded back, invisible again,
beneath the same calm certainty everyone else would see by the time the fleet needed to trust it.
