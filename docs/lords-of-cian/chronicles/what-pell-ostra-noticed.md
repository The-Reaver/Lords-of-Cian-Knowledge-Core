# What Pell Ostra Noticed

*Locked canon, Batch 183, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-536`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XV, closing
the fifth wave. Rebellion era, age 19. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated
in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters beyond the already-locked Pell Ostra.*

---

Pell Ostra had spent most of the war's demolitions work at a careful distance from Kanja's own
deliberately unarmed public persona, her own explosives and precision entirely unsuited to an alias
built around never carrying a weapon at all. She'd never once been asked to protect him during an
Industrial Myth operation, and had assumed, until the dye-works dispute, that she never would be.

"You're the last person I expected to be assigned to me today," Kanja observed, finding her posted at
the district's edge during the tally work, her usual demolitions kit conspicuously absent.

"I asked to be here, actually. Not assigned." She kept her attention on the district's perimeter,
watching for exactly the kind of threat the unarmed persona couldn't watch for itself. "I've spent
years protecting operations where you're the most dangerous thing in the room. I got curious what it
looked like protecting one where you're deliberately not."

"What does it look like?"

"Stranger than I expected. Usually my job is making sure nothing gets close enough to matter, because
you'll handle whatever does. Here, my job is making sure nothing gets close at all, because you've
taken the option to handle it off the table on purpose." She considered the distinction carefully,
turning it over as though examining it for the first time. "I think I understand the method better
for watching it up close. The unarmed part isn't a gap in your defenses. It's the actual point. My
job's just making sure the point doesn't get interrupted by someone who doesn't respect it."

"Does that change how you protect it?"

"Some. I've spent the day thinking less about intercepting threats and more about making sure nobody
ever gets close enough to test whether you'd actually stay unarmed under real pressure. I'd rather
protect the principle from ever being tested that hard than find out what happens if it is." Pell
Ostra's attention didn't waver from her watch. "I don't know that I've protected anything quite like
this before. I think I understand now why you wanted people watching who actually respected what
they were protecting, not just the man standing in the middle of it."

The tally work finished the day without incident, Pell Ostra's watch unbroken and, by her own account
afterward, considerably more demanding than she'd expected protecting an unarmed man to be.
