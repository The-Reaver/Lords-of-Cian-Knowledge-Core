# What Dol Maren Passed Down

*Locked canon, Batch 234, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1039`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
LIX, wave 20. Dol Maren's first succession/mentorship entry for this alias. Not a territory
Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The dockyard boy had no business being aboard *The Receipt* at all — a stowaway who'd hidden among
salvaged timber rather than run the smuggling errand his uncle had sent him on, discovered only when
he corrected, unprompted and from the wrong side of a supply crate, a plank-stress calculation Dol
Maren had already flagged for a second look. Twelve years old, filthy, certain he was about to be put
ashore at the next port and probably beaten besides.

He wasn't put ashore. Dol Maren looked at the correction, looked at the boy, and said only, "Do that
again," and the boy did, three more times on three more sections of hull, each time right.

What followed wasn't a single lesson but a season of them — the flexible-hull principle that had
outlasted a hurricane years before (already established, this alias's own history), broken down past
the finished technique into the raw feel of it: where wood wanted to give before it wanted to break,
how a hull under sustained stress spoke differently than one under a single hard impact, why a repair
rushed to look finished always failed sooner than one left visibly unfinished but sound. Dol Maren
taught the way he'd learned, hands on the wood more than words in the air, correcting by example and
rarely by lecture.

The real test came unannounced, the way the real ones always did. A squall caught the fleet two days
from the nearest safe anchorage, nothing as severe as the old hurricane but bad enough that a wrong
read on a stressed forward section could open real water. Dol Maren stood back from it — deliberately,
arms folded — and let the boy call the repair order alone: which section to reinforce first, which
could hold another hour untouched, which line to slack before it tore free of its own housing. Every
call landed right.

"You could've told me you already knew the answer," the boy said afterward, hands still shaking
slightly with the adrenaline of having been trusted with it.

"I did know it," Dol Maren said. "That's not the same as you not needing to find it yourself. A hull
doesn't care who's standing on it when it fails. It only matters that whoever's standing there knows
what to do."

Garren Hask, cataloguing the season's log later, noted only that the fleet now had a second pair of
hands who could read a hull the way Dol Maren did — no fanfare, a line entry beside the rest, the kind
of thing the ledger existed to make sure never went unrecorded even when nobody thought to make a
legend of it.
