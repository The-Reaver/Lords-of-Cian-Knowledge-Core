# The Convoy Caught Between Two Flags

---

The two fleets exchanging fire off a neutral trade route belonged to neither the Trust nor the
Directorate — a border dispute between two minor coastal powers that had nothing to do with Kanja's
war and everything to do with a merchant convoy that had been caught crossing exactly the wrong stretch
of water at exactly the wrong hour.

"Not our fight," Dol Maren said, watching the exchange through a glass from a safe distance. "Eleven
merchant ships trapped in the middle of somebody else's war. Nine of them civilian passenger vessels.
None of it's ours to be in."

"It is the moment civilians are dying in the crossfire of a fight they didn't choose." Kanja was
already moving toward the gear racks. "We're not picking a side. We're getting the convoy out from
between the two that already picked each other."

The extraction ran directly through the engagement's own crossfire, *The Audit* threading between two
fleets too committed to their own fight to immediately register a third party moving through the
middle of it. Mafesto absorbed two stray long-range strikes meant for neither combatant, converting
them into stored charge rather than damage. Onyx's Cadence Ruin disrupted the rhythm of a boarding
attempt one fleet had launched against a merchant vessel mistaken for an enemy transport, buying the
seconds needed to get her passengers clear. Obsidian Malice discharged once, disabling — not sinking —
a warship whose own captain had refused three separate warnings to clear a lane for the fleeing
merchants.

By the time the last merchant hull reached open water, both warring fleets had noticed the third party
moving unbothered through their own engagement, and both, for a strange, suspended moment, held fire
entirely rather than risk drawing the ghost fleet's full attention onto themselves instead of each
other.

"We just walked eleven ships out of the middle of somebody else's war," Dol Maren said, watching the
two fleets resume their own fight the moment the convoy cleared the danger, "and neither side fired on
us once we started."

"Because neither side actually wants this fight to become bigger than it already is. Everyone in this
war, every war, understands the same math — there's what you're willing to risk for your own cause, and
there's what you're willing to risk provoking a fight you didn't come looking for." Kanja watched the
merchant convoy scatter safely toward their own destinations. "We weren't a threat to either of them.
We were just a reminder that some costs aren't worth the fight, even to people already mid-fight."
