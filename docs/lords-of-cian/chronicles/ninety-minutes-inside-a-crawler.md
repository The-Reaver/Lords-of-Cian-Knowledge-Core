# Ninety Minutes Inside a Crawler

*Locked canon, Batch 113, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-388`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle III, closing the
wave. Rebellion era, during the original Iron Bastard's Stand (`MCD-238`, age 25), told from inside
one of the twelve Trust Crawlers. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The driver of the ninth Crawler in the column spent the last hour of the Iron Bastard's Stand
convinced, with total sincerity, that the vehicle around him was trying to kill its own crew.

It began as a rattle in the floor plating, easy to dismiss as rough ground. Within twenty minutes it
had become a vibration he could feel in his teeth, every rivet and weld point in the hull humming at
a pitch that seemed to climb no matter how the vehicle's own systems tried to compensate. By the
fortieth minute, small components had begun working themselves loose entirely — a hatch bolt spinning
free and rattling across the deck plating, a coolant line fitting weeping fluid from a seam that had
been solid that morning — and the crew had stopped speculating about what was happening and started,
quietly, bracing for whichever piece would fail next.

"It's not an attack," the gunner said, over the vibration, hands pressed flat against a control panel
that had started buzzing under his palms like something alive. "There's no impact. Nothing's hitting
us. It's like the whole vehicle just — remembered it was made of parts, and decided it didn't want to
be one thing anymore."

The driver didn't answer, because he didn't have an answer that wasn't worse than silence. He had
enlisted to drive an armored vehicle he'd been told, repeatedly, in training, was among the most
survivable pieces of equipment in the entire Trust arsenal — and here it was, ninety minutes into an
engagement against a single unarmed-looking man standing in open salt-pan, coming apart around him
one rivet at a time with nothing visibly touching it at all.

By the time the column's commander finally ordered the withdrawal, the ninth Crawler could no longer
maintain formation speed, its rear axle mount shaken loose enough to grind audibly with every
rotation, and the driver understood, guiding it back toward the rear lines at a crawl that made a
mockery of the vehicle's own name, that no report he filed afterward would ever adequately explain
what it had actually felt like — sitting inside four tons of Trust engineering, watching it slowly,
methodically, and entirely without visible cause, decide to stop agreeing with its own construction.

"We're not going back out in these," the gunner said, once they'd reached the rear lines and climbed
out onto solid ground that, for the first time in ninety minutes, felt genuinely solid. "Not against
him. Not in anything with a rivet in it."
