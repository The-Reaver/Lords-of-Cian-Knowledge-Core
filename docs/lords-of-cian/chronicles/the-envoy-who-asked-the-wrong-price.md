# The Envoy Who Asked the Wrong Price

*Locked canon, Batch 192, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-573`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XXVIII, wave 10 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The envoy arrived under a foreign nation's flag, one of the few outside powers with standing enough
to request an audience without it reading as provocation, and made his offer plainly: a substantial
sum, paid in Drakma-backed currency no rebellion could easily refuse, in exchange for exclusive rights
to the storm-forecasting method — not shared, not taught to Sephtis's own network, sold outright and
withheld from every other party on the water.

"You'd be the only nation with it," the envoy said. "Every fleet that sails against yours would be
sailing blind while you saw the weather eleven days out. That's not a small advantage. That's the
whole ocean, permanently, in your favor."

"It's not mine to sell that way," Kanja said. "It's Sephtis's method. And even if it were mine, I
wouldn't sell it exclusive to anyone. The day one nation owns the only working forecast on this coast
is the day every storm becomes a weapon only the rich get to use."

The envoy adjusted his offer twice, each time larger, before shifting register entirely — a quieter
conversation, held apart from the formal audience, about what might happen to a forecaster's network
if the offer were refused a third time. Not a threat delivered directly. An implication, carefully
worded enough to be deniable, that a method this valuable might attract interest from parties less
patient about negotiating for it.

Kanja had the embedded agent the envoy's own delegation carried — a specialist whose actual purpose
the implication was meant to conceal — identified within the week.

"Tell your government," Kanja told the envoy, at the second and final audience, "that the answer is
still no, and that whoever they sent to steal it instead of buy it is welcome to stay in custody until
your ship is ready to leave without them."
