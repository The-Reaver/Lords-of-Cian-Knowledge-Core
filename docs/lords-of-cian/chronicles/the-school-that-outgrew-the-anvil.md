# The School That Outgrew the Anvil

*Locked canon, Batch 262, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1321`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LXXV, wave
25, closing the wave. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241). An
institutional-legacy entry founding a formal smithing academy, distinct from every prior individual
apprenticeship entry. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

It had started, the senior smith's successor pointed out, as a fairly obvious problem of scale:
eighteen months and thirty-odd settlements meant the campaign could teach exactly as many
apprentices as could physically stand on the Anvil's own deck at once, and no more, and every
settlement the tour moved past without stopping was a settlement whose gifted hands never got
found at all.

"We can't be everywhere," she said, laying the idea out on the terrace table the way she'd once
laid out failed counterfeit dies. "But a school doesn't have to move. A school can sit still in
one place and pull students toward it instead of us pulling ourselves toward every student."

It wasn't Kanja's idea, and he made a point of saying so to everyone who assumed it was — a proper
building, permanent forges, a standing faculty drawn from journeymen the Standard had already
certified across a dozen settlements, teaching the open method to anyone who could make the
journey rather than waiting for the campaign to come to them. He gave the land. He gave the first
season's worth of Dead Drakma stock to get its forges lit. He deliberately did not put his name, or
any alias's name, anywhere on the founding charter.

"People will call it yours anyway," the successor warned him, watching the first cohort of thirty
students — some sent by settlements the campaign had visited, several who'd simply heard the
Standard's name and walked in off the road the way the boy with the ballad once had — line up for
their first lesson.

"Let them call it whatever they want," Kanja said. "As long as what they're actually learning
inside it is the thing that matters and not the name on the door."

The school outlived the tour by design, a fixed point the moving campaign could no longer be once
the eighteen months finally ran their course — thirty students becoming three hundred within a few
years, then thousands within a generation, the Open-Forge Standard's own certification eventually
run entirely out of a building Kanja had visited exactly once, at its founding, and never needed to
visit again for it to keep doing what it was built to do.
