# The Ledger That Named the Wrong Man

*Locked canon, Batch 214, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-924`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XLIX, wave 17. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The testimony came in clean, specific, and entirely false.

A dye-vat hand named the district's junior tally-clerk — not the overseer, not the owner, a
nineteen-year-old clerk two rungs above her own station — as the one who'd falsified her hours for
three straight months, skimming the difference for himself. She had dates. She had a figure. She
delivered it with the same flat, unhesitating certainty every honest testifier had delivered theirs
with for two years running.

Ezio cross-checked it against the production counts before he wrote a single line into the permanent
ledger, the way he cross-checked everything. The dates didn't hold. The clerk had been recorded at a
different district entirely for six of the eleven weeks in question, his hand in someone else's tally
book, verified by two other crews' own records.

"She's not confused," Ezio said quietly, once he was sure. "Confused people get details wrong.
She's got every detail right except the ones that would let anyone check her."

Kanja sat with that for a long moment before he called her back to the table.

"The clerk you named was working a different district for half the weeks you've dated. We can
prove where he was. I need you to tell me the truth about why you named him."

She didn't crumble the way a caught liar in a story would have. She told him plainly — the clerk had
turned her down when she'd asked him to falsify a favor for her sister, and she'd decided, watching
two years of the tally method dismantle men far more powerful than a clerk, that it could dismantle
him too if she aimed it right.

"I'm not writing it," Kanja told her. "Not the false claim, and not what you tried to do with it,
either. That goes nowhere. You walk out of here the same as anyone whose testimony never made the
book."

"He'll never know I tried."

"He won't hear it from me. That's not protection for you. That's because the book only exists to
find what's true, and if I let it be used to bury someone who did nothing, it stops being able to do
that for anyone, including you, the next time you actually need it."

She left without her claim entered and without an apology extracted from her either — Kanja didn't
ask for one. Ezio watched her go.

"She'll tell people we didn't believe her."

"Some of them will believe her over us. That's the cost of refusing this the same way it's the cost
of anything else we refuse to do with this book. We're not building something that's fast. We're
building something that's still true in twenty years."

*Continuity notes (not narrative): a genuinely new register — the ledger method weaponized by an
aggrieved testifier against an innocent third party, and the discipline of refusing a false claim
even at real relational cost to the crew's own standing with an otherwise-sympathetic worker.
Distinct from every prior fabrication entry: "The Numbers He Couldn't Prove" (an administrator
fabricates a counter-tally to muddy a true claim) and "The Numbers That Made Him Famous and Feared"
(administrators stage fake worst-off testimony to game the method) are both adversarial fabrication
from the employer side; this is the first entry where the threat to the method's integrity comes from
a genuine, otherwise-credible worker testifier herself, aimed at an innocent bystander rather than an
exploitative employer. Establishes explicitly that the ledger's core function is protecting what's
true, including protecting the innocent from it, not merely advocacy for whoever testifies. No new
named characters — the clerk and the testifier are both unnamed and one-scene. First entry in the
Industrial Myth's seventeenth wave.*
