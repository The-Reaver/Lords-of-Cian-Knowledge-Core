# The Wall He Got Wrong

*Locked canon, Batch 195, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-665`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XXX, wave 10 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Kanja had signed off on the western retaining wall himself, a quick assessment made at the end of a
long night with too many other sections still to check, and he had gotten it wrong. Not
catastrophically — nobody died — but wrong enough that the wall buckled under a load it should have
held, injuring two workers and setting the section's timeline back a full week of careful repair.

He didn't wait for someone else to raise it.

"I want the whole crew to hear this from me before it reaches them secondhand," he said, gathering
the section's workers the morning after. "I assessed that wall in under ten minutes, at the end of a
sixteen-hour stretch, and I told you it would hold a load it couldn't actually carry. Two people are
hurt because I was tired and confident instead of tired and careful, and confidence isn't a
substitute for the second look I should have taken."

"Nobody expects you to catch everything," one of the injured workers said, arm bound in a sling,
his tone carrying no real anger in it. "You've been right about worse walls than that one."

"Being right about worse walls doesn't earn me the right to be careless about this one. That's
backwards thinking, and it's exactly the kind of thinking that gets someone hurt worse than a
sprained arm next time." Kanja didn't soften the accounting. "I'm not telling you this to be
forgiven faster. I'm telling you because the fastest way to make sure it happens again is to let
everyone assume I don't make this mistake."

He reassessed the wall properly over the following two days, catching a second flaw the first rushed
pass had missed entirely, and rebuilt the section correctly before allowing anyone back into it —
work he insisted on doing personally, refusing every offer to hand the correction to someone else.

"You could have let this one go quietly," the crew chief said, watching him finish the rebuild.
"Most people would have."

"Most people aren't the one who told two workers a wall was safe when it wasn't." Kanja tested the
rebuilt section's bracing one final time, satisfied only once he'd checked it twice more than the
job strictly required. "I've told this crew for weeks that honest failure is worth more than a
perfect record nobody actually believes. I don't get to mean that only when the failure's small
enough to be comfortable admitting."
