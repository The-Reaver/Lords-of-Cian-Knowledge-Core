# The Guild That Made Him One of Their Own

*Locked canon, Batch 184, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-539`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XV, closing
the fifth wave. Rebellion era, age 20, the Sewer War of Killane. Not a territory Chronicle. New
standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The tradesmen's guild that had once tested him with a skeptical guild master, years of honest work
and honest failures since, voted to extend honorary membership — a formal recognition no reputation
alone could have earned, granted only through the accumulated judgment of working tradesmen who'd
watched him fail as often as succeed and trust him more, not less, for the failures he'd owned
plainly.

"We don't extend this lightly," the guild's own senior member said, the vote's result read aloud
before the assembled trade hall. "Most who ask for it never get it. We watched a structure come down
on your watch that no skill could have saved, and you told us plainly rather than let us believe
otherwise. We watched you admit a rushed reheat instead of claiming flawless work. Those failures,
told honestly, are why this vote passed as clean as it did. We don't trust perfect work. We trust
honest hands."

"I don't know that I've earned a place among tradesmen who've spent decades at this the way I've spent
one war at it." Kanja accepted the recognition without false modesty, genuine weight in it. "I've
learned more from this guild's own honesty about failure than from anything I brought to it myself."

"That's rather the point of the craft, if you ask most of us. Nobody who's any good at this work
avoided every failure. The good ones are the ones who tell the truth about the failures they did
have." The senior member extended the guild's own formal mark, offered rather than imposed. "You've
told that truth every time it mattered, even when it cost you. That's worth more to this hall than any
perfect record could be."

Kanja carried the guild's mark quietly from that day forward, a recognition earned not by the
reputation's legend but by the accumulated honesty behind every entry in it — the structural failure
he couldn't prevent, the misdiagnosis that hurt two men, the rushed reheat admitted rather than
hidden — each one, in the guild's own accounting, part of what had actually earned him the honor
rather than diminished it.
