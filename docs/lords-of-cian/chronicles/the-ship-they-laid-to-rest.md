# The Ship They Laid to Rest

*Locked canon, Batch 193, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-599`). Captain Alias Chronicle XXIV, wave 8 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The Audit had outlived every reasonable estimate of a hull's working life, and by the time Dol Maren
finally delivered the verdict none of the crew wanted to hear, everyone had already half-known it
was coming: the old flagship's frame had reached the point where continued patching wasn't repair
anymore, just delay.

"She can sail another season if we push her," Dol Maren said. "But she won't survive another storm
the way she used to, and I won't put a crew's lives on a hull I can't stand behind."

"Then she doesn't sail another season," Kanja said, without hesitation, though the decision clearly
cost him something to say plainly.

They didn't scrap her. They brought her into a quiet inlet, stripped everything worth keeping —
Garren Hask personally removing every ledger page ever written aboard her, going back to the day
Hask had named her — and gave her a proper decommissioning rather than a scrapping, the crew's own
version of a funeral for something that had carried them through more of the war than any single
person aboard.

Garren Hask read from the oldest ledger, the entries from the very first weeks, before the name
Captain had even settled into common use. Efa Gol told the story of Iron Shallows one more time,
the version everyone already knew by heart and asked to hear anyway. Callum Breck, who almost never
spoke in front of groups even decades removed from his own long silence, said a handful of words
about the deck he'd stood on the night he found his voice again, and how the ship herself had never
once asked him to explain the silence before it broke.

"She's just wood and iron," one of the newer hands said quietly, not unkindly, watching the
ceremony from the rail.

"She's the first place any of us learned we mattered enough to be come back for," Efa Gol said.
"Wood and iron doesn't do that on its own. We did it, on her deck, because he built it there. But
she held it. That's worth marking properly."

Kanja said the final word, simple and unadorned the way he kept most things that mattered most. "The
Audit," he said, the same name she'd carried since Hask first gave it to her. "For everything this
ship recovered that nobody thought was coming back. That was her purpose, start to finish, and she
earned every year she had it."
