# The Fleet Built to Ignore the Sky

*Locked canon, Batch 192, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-567`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XXII, wave 8 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The countermeasure took the Trust's shipwrights the better part of a year to build: a squadron of
twelve vessels specifically reinforced against the doctrine's usual advantages — deep-keeled hulls
weighted to resist heavy seas rather than ride them, storm-shuttered gun decks that could fight
through driving rain without fouling, crews drilled exclusively in low-visibility engagement so that
darkness and downpour no longer bought Kanja's boarding parties the confusion they depended on.

The squadron's commander called it, without much modesty, the fleet that had finally made the weather
irrelevant.

For the first engagement, he was largely right. Sephtis's storm arrived exactly as predicted, and the
squadron sailed through it without the disorder that had broken every fleet before it — gun decks
dry, formation intact, visibility drills holding the line of battle together through rain that would
once have scattered it. Two boarding attempts were repelled outright, the first time in the doctrine's
use that a storm-timed approach had failed to produce the confusion it was built to exploit.

"They've solved the problem," Kanja said afterward, studying the squadron's disciplined withdrawal
from a chart table still damp from the fight. "Not the storm. Us. They stopped being afraid of the
weather, and a crew that isn't afraid of the storm fights the same in it as out of it."

"That's not nothing to solve," Sephtis said. "That took them a year and twelve ships."

"No. It's not nothing." Kanja set down the chart. "It just means the next fight isn't about the storm
anymore. It's about what a crew stops watching for once it's convinced the storm can't hurt it."
