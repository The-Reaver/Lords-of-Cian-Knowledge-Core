# The Sky They Ordered Clear

*Locked canon, Batch 285, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1512`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle CI, wave 34. The doctrine's first purely joyful, zero-peril use: guaranteeing a clear sky for the fourth-generation apprentice's own coming-of-age day, with no threat, no combat, and nothing at stake but a festival.*

---

There was no engagement to time, no convoy to protect, no rival tradition to reconcile with. There was only a date on the calendar the whole school had somehow, without anyone officially deciding it, started treating as a small holiday: the day the fourth-generation apprentice reached her majority, two years and some months after she had already been trusted with a full fleet's fate on her own unconfirmed word at `MCD-1419`.

She had never once asked the school to guarantee her own weather. It was the student's idea, offered half as a joke and taken up in full seriousness by everyone else -- if the doctrine could hold a causeway's surge to the hour for a city's evacuation, it could certainly hold one afternoon clear for a girl who'd spent most of her adolescence holding storms back from everyone else.

The reading itself took the apprentice less than an hour, an easy call on a settled high-pressure ridge that needed none of the school's harder tools to confirm. The work that took longer was everything the festival demanded around it: tables carried down to the harbor flats, the rival fleet's dual-tradition sailor arriving with a dish from her own fleet's tradition that nobody at the school had tasted before, the northern pilot sending down a keg by messenger with no note beyond the pilot's own name, three generations' worth of readers and students and captains who'd sailed on the school's calls for years all crowding one stretch of harbor with nothing to forecast and nowhere they needed to be.

Kanja came without Onyx of Oblivion at his hip at all, the first time in this alias's run he had appeared anywhere near the school unarmed, and spent most of the afternoon doing nothing more consequential than carrying tables and losing, twice, at a dice game the student's crew had been running since before he'd first met any of them. Efa Gol found him there near evening, watching the apprentice laugh at something the rival sailor had said, and asked him, not entirely joking, whether he'd ever once used this alias for something that wasn't a storm, a siege, or a rescue.

"Once," he said. "Today." He didn't elaborate, and didn't need to; the sky held clear straight through to the evening the reading had promised, and for one whole afternoon on this coast, nothing at all needed reading.
