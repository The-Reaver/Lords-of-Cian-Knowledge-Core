# What She Taught Him Back

*Locked canon, Batch 201, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-851`). The Crow King Alias Chronicle XXXVI, wave 12 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The apprentice had spent the weeks after Kessin Ridge working with a small group of newer recruits
who hadn't been part of the assault, singers whose own confidence had taken a quiet hit watching a
battle won by Trinity strength rather than the craft they'd spent months training in — and she came
to Kanja with an idea he hadn't considered in years of building the method around deception alone.

"They think the craft only matters when it's hiding something or moving someone," she said. "I don't
think that's true. I think it can hold people up, too, not just fool an enemy."

She demonstrated rather than explained it further — the same breath control, the same sustained,
consistent tone the Braid demanded, but shaped now into something meant to be heard by their own
side rather than concealed from an enemy at all: a low, steady song sung openly to exhausted crew
members during a difficult march, carrying nothing false in it whatsoever, only encouragement built
on the identical technical discipline that had once fooled a triple-redundant sensor grid.

"I've never used it this way," Kanja admitted, watching a line of tired fighters visibly steady
under nothing but a true, well-sung reminder that they weren't marching alone. "I built the whole
craft around what could be hidden or thrown. It never occurred to me the same control could just be
offered honestly instead."

"You taught me the voice was the smallest part of the deception. I don't think you ever said it was
only useful for deception at all. That was my own assumption, not something you actually told me."
She let the song fade naturally rather than force its end. "I think I finally taught you something
back, for once, instead of the other direction."

"You did. And I intend to use it." Kanja meant it plainly, no reluctance in the admission at all.
"I've spent years thinking of the Hymn-Engine as a weapon that happens to sound like music. I hadn't
considered, until just now, that it might also simply be music that happens to double as a weapon
when the moment calls for one. That's not a small correction to make this late in learning
something."
