# What Efa Gol Saw From the Start

*Locked canon, Batch 191, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-560`). Captain Alias Chronicle XVIII, closing the fifth
wave — and closing the fifth wave for all eleven aliases. Rebellion era. Not a territory Chronicle.
New standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters beyond the
already-locked Efa Gol.*

---

Efa Gol had been present, in one capacity or another, since Warehouse Twelve — long enough to have
watched every name the crew ever settled on, from the earliest whispered "Trench Monarch" through
Bane, the Scourge, and every alias between, arriving finally at the one the crew itself had chosen
rather than had assigned to them by fear or classification.

A newer hand, curious about the accumulated years, finally asked her which name had actually meant
the most, watching her think it over with the particular weight of someone who'd genuinely earned the
right to an answer.

"I watched a boy become a dozen different names over the years," she said, choosing her words with
care. "Some of them terrified people. Some of them saved people. All of them were true, in their own
way, about something real he actually was in that moment. But Captain's different from the rest,
and I don't think it's just because it's the one his own people chose rather than the Directorate."

"What makes it different?"

"The others were mostly about what he did to the people outside this crew — the fear he built, the
legends that grew up around him. Captain's the only one that's actually about what he is to us,
specifically, inside it. I've watched him earn every version of that word, one decision at a time,
since before there was a single name worth using at all." Efa Gol looked out over the crew going about
its ordinary business, the weight of years settling into something closer to quiet satisfaction than
nostalgia. "I don't know which name history ends up remembering best. I know which one actually meant
the most to the people who lived every day of it alongside him. That's this one. It always has been,
since before it had a word to go with it."

The newer hand said nothing further, letting the account settle the way every account from the
earliest crew eventually did — a private history, offered plainly, closing out a run of stories that
had, across every alias the man had ever carried, always come back to the same accumulated truth: a
name earned, one decision at a time, by someone who never stopped choosing to earn it.
