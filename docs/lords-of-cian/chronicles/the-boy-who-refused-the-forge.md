# The Boy Who Refused the Forge

*Locked canon, Batch 202, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-884`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XXXIX, wave 13 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

He had a genuine gift for it — the crew's own senior smith said so within an hour of watching him work
a scrap of iron he'd picked up entirely on his own, untaught, with an instinct for the metal most
apprentices took months to develop. He was also, by his own flat refusal, not interested in becoming a
smith at all.

"Everyone keeps telling me I should want this," he said, setting the scrap down without finishing it,
watching the crew's floor with something closer to obligation than desire. "I don't hate it. I just
don't want it to be the thing I do."

"Then it doesn't have to be." Kanja didn't press him back toward the forge, didn't treat the talent as
a debt the boy owed anyone. "What do you actually want?"

The boy hadn't expected the question asked so plainly, and took a moment answering it. "I want to be a
healer. There's an old woman two districts over who sets bones and treats fever, and everyone acts like
that's a smaller thing than what you do here. I don't think it is. I think it's just quieter."

"It's not smaller. I've watched this campaign patch up as many broken bodies as broken buildings, and
I'd take a good healer over an extra smith most weeks without hesitating." Kanja meant it plainly,
without performance. "I'm not going to talk you out of a gift for iron you didn't ask for, and I'm not
going to talk you into a life built around it either. You get to choose which gift you actually want
to spend your years on."

The old woman two districts over took him on within the month, at the campaign's own recommendation
rather than despite it, and the crew's senior smith, watching him go, allowed herself one quiet
observation the boy never heard.

"Best hands I've seen on this floor in years, and we're letting him walk away from it."

"We're not letting him do anything. It was never ours to keep." Kanja watched the boy's departure with
no regret in it at all. "This campaign was never about making everyone a smith. It was about making
sure everyone got to choose what they actually wanted to build. Sometimes that's iron. Sometimes it's
somebody's mended arm instead."
