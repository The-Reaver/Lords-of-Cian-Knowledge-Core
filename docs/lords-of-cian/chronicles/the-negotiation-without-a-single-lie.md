# The Negotiation Without a Single Lie

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Crow King Alias Chronicle XXIX, wave 10, second
entry. Rebellion era, roughly three years after the Night of the Crow King (`MCD-236`, age 23). Not
a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The regional supply overseer had agreed to a parley only because he assumed, correctly, that the man
across the table from him had built an entire reputation on deception, and had therefore prepared
for exactly that — every offer weighed for a hidden trap, every claim treated as a probable lie until
independently verified, a negotiator who had made himself, by design, nearly impossible to deceive.

Kanja didn't try.

He laid out, in full and verifiable detail, precisely what his people knew about the overseer's own
supply shortfalls, precisely which routes were already compromised regardless of anything discussed
at this table, and precisely what a continued blockade would cost the overseer's own reporting
figures within the month — every claim checkable, nothing softened for effect, nothing held back as
leverage for a later reveal.

"You're not trying to trick me," the overseer said, several times, as though still waiting for the
trap he'd braced for to finally surface.

"There isn't one to find. You've spent your whole career learning to catch me at exactly this table.
Building another lie complicated enough to slip past that preparation would take longer than I have,
and you'd probably catch it anyway, given how carefully you've studied the craft. So I'm not
offering you a lie to catch. I'm offering you the truth, laid out plainly enough that checking it
costs you almost nothing, and asking you to weigh what happens if you don't take the terms while
you still can."

The overseer verified every claim independently before agreeing to anything, exactly as his own
preparation demanded — and found, at the end of it, nothing to catch at all, the terms exactly as
represented, the leverage exactly as real as stated.

"You beat my entire preparation," he admitted afterward, "by not needing any of the tricks I spent
years learning to see through."

"You prepared for a liar. I decided not to be one, this time, because you'd built yourself into
someone a lie couldn't move anymore. That's not a failure of the craft. Knowing when the craft
doesn't apply is the same discipline as knowing when it does."
