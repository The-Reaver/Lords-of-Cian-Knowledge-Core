# The First District to Ask for Him by Name

ethos. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Every district before this one had been chosen by Kanja and Ezio's own reading of where the worst
conditions sat, walked into unbidden, the ledger opened on the crew's own initiative. The runner who
found them outside the milling combine's territory carried something new: a formal request, written
by a district council no one in the crew had ever met, asking the Industrial Myth to come.

"They've heard what happens where you go," the runner said. "They want it to happen where they are."

Kanja read the letter twice before answering, and Ezio watched him read it the way he'd watch him
read a discrepancy in a ledger — carefully, for something that didn't sit right.

"That's not the same as being asked to help," Kanja said finally. "That's being asked to arrive. If I
go because I was invited, and the tally comes back smaller than they expected, or slower, or harder
to enforce than the story about me suggested it would be, I've made myself a promise I can't
guarantee, in a district that's already decided what my arrival means before I've asked a single
question."

"So we don't go."

"We go. But we go the same way we'd go anywhere — worst-off first, no promises made in advance, no
number decided before it's earned." He folded the letter and set it aside rather than carrying it as
proof of anything. "I'll tell the council that myself, before we open a single page."

The council, expecting the reputation that had preceded him, received instead a plain refusal to
promise outcomes and a request to see their own production records before anything else was
discussed — not the confident deliverer of justice their letter had imagined summoning, but a man
methodically declining to be anything other than exactly what the method had always been.

"You're smaller than the story," one councilman said, somewhere between disappointment and relief.

"The story was never accurate. It just traveled faster than the truth does." Kanja opened the ledger
to its first blank page. "What I can promise is that whatever's written in here will be true, and
that it'll take exactly as long as it takes. If that's still what you asked for, we can begin."

It was. The tally that followed ran no faster and no slower than any other district's, the invitation
itself changing nothing about the method once the work actually began — but it marked, for the crew,
the first clear sign that the reputation had outpaced them into places they hadn't yet walked, a fact
Kanja carried more warily than proudly from that district onward.
