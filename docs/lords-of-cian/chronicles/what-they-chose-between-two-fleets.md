# What They Chose Between Two Fleets

*Locked canon, Batch 192, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-581`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XXXVI, wave 12 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Sephtis called the second sky-reader to the same table where the two forecasts had first been laid
side by side, and asked her, plainly, whether she thought the triage decision had been made correctly.

"That's not really a forecasting question either," she said, echoing what her teacher had told Kanja
days earlier.

"No. But you're going to be asked to make calls like it someday without either of us in the room to
share the weight of it. I want to know how you'd think it through, not whether you'd agree with how he
did."

She considered it for a long moment. "I'd want a rule for it. Something better than 'whichever fight I
can be most present for.' That's honest, but it's not a method. It's just his own limits deciding for
him."

"Build the rule, then," Sephtis said. "Not to replace his judgment — he'll always have the final say
when it's his call to make — but so that when it's yours, you're not standing there with nothing but
instinct and two forecasts that both look equally urgent."

She spent the following weeks drafting exactly that: a framework weighing a fleet's own capacity to
absorb an unaided storm against the tactical stakes riding on the engagement, meant to make future
triage decisions defensible on more than presence alone. It never fully replaced judgment — Sephtis
made sure she understood that from the start — but it gave the next commander forced to choose between
two storms and one Trinity something more than a coin's weight to lean on.

"It won't make the choice painless," she told Sephtis, handing over the draft. "I don't think anything
would. But at least the next person won't have to invent the reasoning from nothing, the way he did."
