# What Pell Ostra Said About the Quiet Ones

*Locked canon, Batch 195, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-671`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XXXVI, wave 12 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Pell Ostra had wired more charges under this alias than any other stretch of the war, and when a
new demolitions apprentice, freshly assigned to the tunnel crews, asked her which of Kanja's
personas she thought suited him best, she didn't answer right away.

"Everyone talks about the Scourge, or Bane," the apprentice said, checking a fuse length against
her own instruction. "This one doesn't come up as much outside Killane."

"That's because this one doesn't need to be talked about to work." Pell Ostra tested the charge's
seating with the same patient precision she brought to everything, unhurried. "Bane walks into a
room and the room already knows what it's looking at before he says a word. This one walks into a
room and half the time nobody realizes what they're looking at until the work's already done and
he's moved on to the next section."

"Is that better or worse?"

"Neither. Different. Bane's a weapon that announces itself. This one's a weapon that could set a
brace, read a fault line, and hold a war down with a war club, and somehow the brace is what people
remember longest." Pell Ostra checked the timer on the finished charge, satisfied. "I think that's
the part that actually tells you something true about him, underneath every alias he's ever worn.
He's never needed the reputation to precede him. He's needed the work to hold. Everything else is
just what people decided to call the fact that it always did."

"You sound like you respect this one more than the others."

"I respect all of them for different reasons. This one, though—" she considered the charge one more
time before stepping back, satisfied it would do exactly what it was built to do, no more, no less
— "this one's the one that reminds me why I ever trusted a war effort built by a man who cared as
much about a tunnel's shoring as he did about the fight happening in it. Everything else he's
become since started somewhere. I think a good part of it started right here, underground, setting
timber nobody was ever going to write a song about."
