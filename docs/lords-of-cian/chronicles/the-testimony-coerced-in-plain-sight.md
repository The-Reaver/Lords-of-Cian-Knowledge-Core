# The Testimony Coerced in Plain Sight

*Locked canon, Batch 278, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1448`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle C, wave 34, first entry. A new coercion register, distinct from the earlier coerced-collaborator case (MCD-1157): here the false testimony is staged openly, in front of Kanja himself, betting his unarmed method has no way to challenge it in the moment.*

---

The administrator lined his workers up in the yard himself, in full view of the crew's arriving tent, and stood close enough behind each one to be heard clearly when it was their turn to speak. Every account came out the same: fair wages, fair hours, nothing to record. Ezio had never seen testimony arrive so uniform, or so obviously rehearsed in the flatness of every voice giving it.

"He's not hiding it," Ezio said quietly, watching the sixth worker in a row recite the same three sentences. "He wants us to see him standing there. He knows we won't grab him by the collar and make anyone say different."

"He's right that we won't." Kanja didn't confront the administrator, didn't call out the coercion in front of the yard where it would only cost the workers more once the crew moved on. He thanked each one for their testimony exactly as he thanked every other, gave no sign anything was amiss, and asked only for one more thing before the crew departed: a private follow-up visit to each worker's own home, alone, over the following week, framed to the administrator as standard record-keeping rather than doubt.

The private accounts didn't match the yard's uniform testimony at all. Wages fell short by a third across the board; the fair-hours claim collapsed the moment no one was standing close enough to be overheard correcting it.

"Every one of them told the truth eventually," Ezio said, laying the two sets of testimony side by side once the week was out. "Just not in front of him."

"That's not a flaw in what they told us in the yard. It's proof of exactly how much that man was willing to risk to keep it quiet." Kanja built the finding from the private testimony alone, careful to note in the record itself why the public accounts had been set aside rather than treated as a contradiction — coercion documented as its own fact, not smoothed over as an inconsistency the ledger couldn't explain. The administrator, confronted with a finding built entirely from testimony he'd never known was taken, had no rehearsed answer left standing.

"He thought standing there was enough to control what got written down," Kanja said. "It controlled what got said. Never what was true."
