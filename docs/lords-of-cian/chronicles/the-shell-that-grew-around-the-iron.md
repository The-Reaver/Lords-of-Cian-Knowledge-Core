# The Shell That Grew Around the Iron

*Locked canon, Batch 261, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1284`); corrected Batch 321, 2026-10-02 (the Trinity
gear reworded throughout to the Long-Mask-era kit, since wave 22 falls years past the Trinity's
age-30 surrender, `MCD-246`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LXV, wave 22. A detailed combat showcase
against a bio-armored Crawler variant built to exploit the doctrine's new living-structure
distinction. Not a territory Annals entry.*

---

The Directorate's engineering corps had learned the wrong lesson from the fig-root crossing — word of it reached them within weeks, garbled by three retellings, and what they built from it wasn't caution. It was a weapon. Six Crawlers rolled into the depot approach encased in a grown shell of estuary coral, seeded onto their armor plating months earlier and cultivated to maturity specifically for this engagement: living material fused to dead machinery, engineered to read as ambiguous under exactly the diagnostic question Kanja had only just learned to ask.

He put his palm to the lead Crawler's shell from forty yards, closing the distance at a dead run while he read it, and the signature came back exactly as intended — a living rhythm layered directly over a mechanical one, indistinguishable at any range that wasn't skin contact. Strike it as though it were alive and the coral would simply regrow around a wound, the Crawler untouched beneath. Strike it as though it were dead and risk nothing except discovering, mid-read, that some genuinely living thing had been bonded to the plating badly enough that killing the coral killed something it shouldn't have.

Doubled verification bought him the half-second the standing protocol always cost — and the second pass found what the first had missed: the coral's growth pattern was uniform, seeded and cultivated on a schedule, with none of the fig-root's self-directed, organic branching. A grown thing answering to a planting order wasn't the same as a thing that had chosen its own shape. Kanja drove the Ironhand Gauntlets not at the shell's tension but at the resin bonding layer beneath it, a narrow band between coral and iron that carried no living signature at all — the shell cracked free whole from three Crawlers in a single strike, exposing bare plating to a second strike a breath later.

The Forge-Coat and Ironfall Boots grounded the recoil of six mounted crossbows opening up from the depot wall the instant the shells came off, redirecting the impact through his own frame into the ground rather than absorbing it passively, buying the half-second he needed. The Rexmar Machete cleared the boarding crew off the fourth Crawler before its own crew realized the shell had failed to hold; the Smoke System closed the last twenty feet to the fifth unseen in the coral dust the cracked shells threw into the air; the same blade opened a seam in the sixth Crawler's own hatch before its driver finished reversing.

Five of six Crawlers disabled. The sixth reversed clear, its shell intact — and Kanja let it go rather than chase a target he hadn't yet learned to read with full confidence at range.

"They grew something alive to hide something dead inside it," the second student said afterward, turning a cracked shell fragment over in her hands, coral still faintly warm. "That's not a countermeasure. That's an insult to what you found on that bridge."

"It's both," Kanja said. "They heard 'the doctrine can't tell living from dead' and built a weapon out of the gap instead of asking why the gap mattered to me in the first place. I don't think they understood what they were copying. I think they just understood that it worked."
