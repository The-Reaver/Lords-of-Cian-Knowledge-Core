# The New Hand's First Night

*Locked canon, Batch 138, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-462`). Captain Alias Chronicle VIII. Rebellion era. Not
a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters.*

---

The newest recruit had joined the crew late enough in the war that every name people used for Kanja
around her arrived pre-loaded with years of history she hadn't lived through — Trench Monarch spoken
with old reverence by the longest-serving hands, Captain spoken by nearly everyone else with something
warmer and more ordinary, and she'd spent her first several days too uncertain of the difference to
ask which one she was actually supposed to use.

"You can just ask," Callum Breck told her, catching her hesitation on her third night aboard. "Most
new hands wonder the same thing and don't ask fast enough."

"I didn't want to get it wrong in front of him."

"There isn't a wrong answer, exactly. But if you want the honest read on it — Trench Monarch's a name
from before most of us knew him. It means something to the people who carried it with him from the
start. Captain's the name the rest of us gave him because it's what he actually is to us, day to day.
Neither one's owed more respect than the other. They're just different doors into the same man,
depending on how long you've known him." Breck considered it further. "Use Captain. It's not lesser.
It's just newer, same as you are."

She used it for the first time the following morning, tentative, testing the word's weight before she
fully trusted saying it. Kanja answered to it without comment, the same easy acknowledgment he gave
anyone using it, no correction offered because none was needed — the newest hand aboard had arrived
at the same name the whole crew had settled on years before her, without anyone having to explain why
it fit.

"Feels strange, calling him that so soon," she admitted to Breck afterward. "Like I hadn't earned the
familiarity yet."

"You don't earn the name by knowing him longer. You earn it by being part of the crew he's actually
building. You're part of it now. The name comes with that, not with time served." Breck's own voice
carried the particular warmth of someone still measuring his words carefully, four months of silence
still not entirely behind him even years later. "Welcome aboard, properly this time."
