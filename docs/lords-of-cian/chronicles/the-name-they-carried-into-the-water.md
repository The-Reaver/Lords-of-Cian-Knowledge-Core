# The Name They Carried Into the Water

closing the wave and the alias's pre-Black-Trench material. Rebellion era, age 18, the night before
the Battle of the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No
new named characters beyond the already-locked Efa Gol and Pell Ostra.*

---

Efa Gol and Pell Ostra had never had much reason to talk to each other at length before that night —
one worked crowds and attention, the other worked fire and structure, and the crew's early days
hadn't often put the two of them alone together with nothing urgent to do. The night before the
march to the Black Trench, waiting on packed gear neither of them could improve by checking it a
third time, they found themselves sitting the same watch by accident.

"Feels strange, doesn't it," Efa Gol said eventually. "Tomorrow's going to be different from
anything we've done so far. I don't think either of us really knows how different yet."

"I know some of it. Ostra's already spent from the accelerant work at Coldrace, and whatever's
coming is going to ask for more of that than any dredge site ever did." Pell Ostra didn't look up
from the charge she was still checking out of habit rather than necessity. "I keep thinking about
the name. The Trench Monarch. Feels like it belonged to something smaller than whatever we're
walking into tomorrow."

"Maybe it did. A name for dredge sites and tally sheets, and tomorrow's a battle." Efa Gol considered
that a while. "I don't think it disappears, though. I think it's more like — it's the foundation
something bigger gets built on top of. Everything he's proven on this canal, the patience, the
numbers, the restraint — that doesn't stop being true just because tomorrow needs something the name
never had to be before."

"You think it's still going to be him, after."

"I think it has to be. Whatever he becomes tomorrow, it's going to be built out of everything that
name already earned — the trust, the crew that followed a boy digging his own crown one shovelful at
a time, us, sitting here instead of anywhere else. None of that gets left behind just because the
fight gets bigger." Efa Gol looked toward the dark water beyond the camp, the same canal that had
carried every dredge site's manifest, every tally correction, every printed sheet the crier had ever
set to type. "I think we're carrying the name into the water tomorrow, one way or another. Whatever
comes out the other side is going to have all of this underneath it, whether anyone remembers to say
so or not."

Neither of them slept much that night. In the morning, the march to the Black Trench began, and the
Trench Monarch — the reputation earned one dredge site, one honest correction, one restrained mercy
at a time — walked into a battle that would, by its end, hand Kanja a war considerably larger than
any canal district had ever required, carrying, underneath whatever new name the fighting would
eventually earn him, everything the first one had built.
