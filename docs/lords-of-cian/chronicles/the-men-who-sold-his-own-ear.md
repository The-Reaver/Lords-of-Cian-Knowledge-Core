# The Men Who Sold His Own Ear

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XLVIII, wave 16, closing the wave. Rebellion era. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Word reached Kanja through a demolitions contact he trusted before it reached him any other way: a
band of hired freelancers, no Trust commission and no Directorate charter behind them, had begun
selling a crude imitation of the resonance doctrine to whichever side of a local dispute paid first —
collapsing a rival merchant's storehouse one week, a debtor's roof the next, always framed to
witnesses as "the same trick the Iron Bastard uses," a name lent without permission to any coin that
would have it.

He found their latest job before it finished — a granary wall half-brought-down over an unpaid debt
between two families who had, until that week, been neighbors rather than enemies.

"You're not reading anything," he said, watching one of them work a crude hand-drill and a length of
borrowed chain against the wall with none of the diagnostic patience the technique actually required.
"You're just breaking things loudly enough that people assume understanding where there's only noise."

"Works just the same," the leader said, unbothered. "Folk pay for the name. Don't much matter if the
method underneath is real."

It was the first time the doctrine had ever been stolen rather than learned, defected with, or
institutionally suppressed — not an engineer studying it honestly, not a student tempted by personal
grievance, but men who had never touched the actual listening at all, trading only on a reputation
built by years of work they had no part of.

Kanja didn't discharge anything. He laid a hand on the half-broken wall instead, read its actual
remaining tension in seconds, and stopped its collapse rather than finishing it — the granary's grain
store, and both families' winter, still intact behind a wall that would hold one more season with
honest repair.

"You've been selling my name to settle debts between people who used to share meals," he said. "The
technique you're faking isn't even the worst of it. It's whose disputes you're deciding, for coin,
with no more understanding of the stakes than of the wall."

The leader had no answer that held up under the granary owners' own furious stares, and left the
district well ahead of anyone organizing to stop him by force. Kanja stayed long enough to see the
wall properly reinforced by hands who actually lived there.

"I've had the doctrine turned against me, misread, half-taught, even feared for reasons that weren't
mine to control," he told the granary's owner before leaving. "I hadn't yet had it sold. I don't think
I care for that version of losing control over it any better than the others."
