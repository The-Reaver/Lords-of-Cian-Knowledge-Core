# The Ones Who Sold Him Out

*Locked canon, Batch 281, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1469`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle XCIV, wave 32, first
entry. Age 205, V4 gear. Not a territory Annals entry. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The rendezvous point had worked nine times running: a cove behind a freed fishing settlement whose
people had spent three years quietly feeding the crew's decoy runs directions, tides, and warning
when strangers asked too many questions about the coast. The tenth time, the strangers didn't have to
ask. Someone from inside the settlement had already told them everything — the cove, the schedule, the
signal — for a sum large enough that Garren Hask's ledger would later record it in full, without
softening the number into something easier to read.

The ambush was waiting in the rocks above the cove when Efa Gol's successor brought the skiff in, and
it would have worked if the informant had known one more thing: that the schedule he'd sold had
already changed two nights earlier, quietly, the way it always did after a rendezvous ran long enough
to become predictable. The strangers were set for a landing that wasn't coming. Kanja came in from the
water side instead, a half-hour behind the skiff and out of the line the ambush had been built to
cover.

The Forge-Coat's plating turned the first crossbow bolt at the shoulder seam before its owner had
finished loosing a second; the Ironhand Gauntlets closed the gap in four strides and put both archers
down before either reloaded. The same grounding weave caught a third man's own committed
lunge and returned it against the rock he'd been braced on, breaking nothing that needed breaking
twice. It was over in under a minute, the ambush undone as cleanly as it had been assembled, and none
of the four men in the rocks ever got a clear look at what had unmade their plan.

The informant himself wasn't among them — he'd taken his payment and left the coast entirely,
Efa Gol's people confirmed within the week, gone inland to someplace his neighbors would never find
him. Kanja didn't send anyone after him. What stayed with him instead, on the crossing back, was the
plainer arithmetic of it: three years of trust, built person by person, had come down in the end to
one man doing sums about what his silence was worth against what his information would buy — and the
doctrine that had always assumed the reputation's protection ran both ways had just been shown, for
the first time this plainly, that it only ever ran on the honesty of the people standing inside it.

Garren Hask logged the cove's name struck through, the rendezvous retired, and beside it a second
line Kanja asked him to add and then almost asked him to take back out: *not everyone we free stays
someone we can trust.* Hask left it in.
