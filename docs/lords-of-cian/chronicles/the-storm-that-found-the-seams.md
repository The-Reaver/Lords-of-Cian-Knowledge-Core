# The Storm That Found the Seams

*Locked canon, Batch 257, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1193`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle LXXXII, wave 28, opening the wave. A surface-storm-driven infiltration crisis -- weather, not sabotage, floods the tunnels from above, requiring surface/underground coordination under live conditions.*

---

No one had breached anything. That was the difference between this flood and every one before it -- the storm surface crews had been tracking for two days simply outlasted every drainage channel built to handle ordinary rainfall, and by the second night water was finding its way down through seams in the old surface culverts that had never once failed under normal weather.

Dol Maren caught it first from above, watching runoff patterns that had stopped matching anything the original drainage design accounted for. He got word below before the first trickle reached the working galleries, which bought Kanja the only real advantage he had against a threat with no schedule and no intention behind it at all.

"It's not coming from one point," Kanja said, reading the seep patterns along three separate walls. "It's finding every weakness the design never had to account for, all at once, because nobody built for a storm this size."

There was no gate to breach and no single source to seal. Mafesto's kinetic transfer mapped the pressure differential across the whole affected section in real time, distinguishing genuine structural risk from seepage that would hold on its own, while Maren coordinated surface-side redirection of the worst of the runoff before it ever reached the culverts feeding underground at all -- the two of them working from opposite ends of the same problem with no way to confirm each other's progress except by watching the water levels respond.

Obsidian Malice cut two emergency drainage channels through solid rock in places the original design had never needed them, buying capacity the storm alone had made necessary. It took the whole of the second night and into the next morning before the levels stabilized, surface and underground work finally converging on the same answer at almost the same hour.

Maren found him at the surface culvert afterward, both of them soaked through and neither particularly rested. "Weather doesn't care about your tunnel-reading. Doesn't care about anything, really."

"No," Kanja agreed, watching the runoff finally settle into channels built to actually hold it. "That's what made it honest work. Nothing to outthink. Just something to outlast, together, until it passed."
