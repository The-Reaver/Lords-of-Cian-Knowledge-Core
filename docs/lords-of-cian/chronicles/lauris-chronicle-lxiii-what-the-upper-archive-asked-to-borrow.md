# Lauris Chronicle LXIII: What the Upper Archive Asked to Borrow

*Locked canon, Batch 300, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1680`). Sixty-third entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, the sixteenth entry of Strand K, drafted as part of a second 50-entry wave.
Dramatizes an informal, pre-Directorate instance of Lauris's "careful witness" discipline (`MCD-161`)
being called on in a civic, non-combat capacity at age ~750 -- an unofficial precursor to, and
deliberately distinct from, the three inter-Vask disputes formally counted among her twenty-three
Long Operational Period deployments (`MCD-1555`, age 1,841-3,400). Extends Veska's established role
as "keeper of the cooperative-era upper-tier archives" (`MCD-167`). No new named characters. Full
text below.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Vask Karth-Ven, age approximately seven hundred fifty: "Veska asked me to read a
boundary record, not to fight over it. I told her I did not know the difference yet. She told me I
would, by the end of the week."*

I have set down, across several of these accounts, that Lauris's earliest inheritance from the
Sister-Hold that raised her was not density but the practice of careful witness -- watching,
recording, and withholding judgment until the data is complete. I have shown that inheritance at
work in a training bout she watched at eight and in a stance she helped rebuild at four hundred. I
set it down here in a register I have not yet given it: called upon not by her own instructors, but
by the wider archival institution Veska Karth-Ven had spent her long life serving, for a purpose that
had nothing to do with combat at all.

The dispute itself was small, by the standard of anything else in this account, though I do not
think it felt small to the two Holds involved. A boundary marker between Vask Karth-Ven's own outer
grazing terrace and a neighboring settlement's claimed water channel had, over several thousand
years of gradual archival transcription and re-transcription, accumulated two subtly different
readings of where the original marker had actually stood -- a discrepancy of perhaps forty paces,
meaningless in any year with adequate rainfall, and a source of quiet, accumulating friction in the
years without it. Veska, who held the Vask's own copy of the record in trust, had exhausted every
comparison her own archival training suggested and found the two versions equally internally
consistent. She came to Lauris not as a combatant, but as the one person in the Vask whose account of
a document neither side had already made up its mind about might be trusted by both.

*

I want to be careful, setting this down, not to overstate what this was. It was not a security
operation of the kind she would later conduct by the dozen for the Sealbound Directorate, and it was
not one of the inter-Vask disputes her own later ledger would count among the twenty-three
deployments of the Long Operational Period -- those, by her own record, would not begin for another
eleven hundred years, and I do not believe it is my place to blur the line between what she was asked
in this quieter, earlier moment and what she would later be asked as the civilization's default
answer to conflicts it could not otherwise resolve. What this was, plainly, was an archivist asking a
younger woman she trusted to read carefully, and a younger woman discovering, for what her own record
suggests was the first time, that the discipline built into her at eight had a use entirely separate
from anything Veska or Tiramen or Voreth had ever trained into her on the floor below.

Lauris spent four days with the original transcriptions, cross-referencing survey markers against
three independent geological records the two disputing Holds had each, in their own accumulated
confidence, stopped consulting. She found the error -- not in either Hold's own reading of the
marker, but in a copying mistake made some six thousand years earlier, when a transcriber working
from a worn original had recorded a numeral imprecisely, and every subsequent copy had faithfully
preserved the imprecision rather than the original fact. Her own closing note to Veska, brief and
characteristically without embellishment: *Neither Hold was lying. The record was. I have corrected
the record. I do not know what either Hold will decide to do with a boundary that no longer favors
either of their existing arguments.*

*

Veska's own reflection on the outcome, recorded some weeks later once both Holds had, by her account,
accepted the correction with considerably more grace than she had privately expected, is the one I
have chosen to close this entry with: *I asked her to read a boundary record because I trusted her
more than I trusted either Hold's own certainty, including my own. I did not expect her to enjoy it.
She tells me she did. I believe her, and I think this is the first time I have understood that what
we have built on the floor downstairs is only half of what this girl is for.*

I asked Lauris once, long after, whether she remembered the dispute at all. She told me she
remembered the copying error more clearly than either Hold's names, and that she had decided, reading
it, that she preferred correcting a record no one had meant to falsify to correcting a person who
had. It is, I think, a small distinction. I have come to believe it is also, in her, an unusually
important one.
