# The Chain That Freed Itself

*Locked canon, Batch 200, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-817`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle XXXII, wave 11 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The holding deck's chains had worn thin enough at one weld point that a dozen captives had already
worked themselves loose by the time the smoke rolled across the transport's upper decks, and rather
than wait huddled for rescue to reach them, they came up through the hold's access ladder swinging
their own broken links as weapons before he'd finished clearing the first line of guards.

"Behind me," he called, adjusting on instinct to a fight that had just become something he hadn't
planned for — not a rescue of passive captives, but a joint action alongside people who'd decided,
correctly, that freedom wasn't something to simply wait politely for.

A woman at the front of the group didn't fall in behind him. She swung her length of chain into a
guard's exposed flank instead, timed to land the instant the Ironhand Gauntlets forced the man's guard
open, the two strikes landing close enough together that neither of them, afterward, could say for
certain which one had actually put the guard down.

"We've been planning this for two weeks," she said, breathing hard, already moving to the next
engagement rather than waiting for him to catch up to the fact she wasn't going to stay behind him.
"Since we heard the rumors you'd been working this strait. We weren't going to just sit in that hold
hoping the story was true."

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System, tuned to absorb a boarding party's blows, found itself instead
storing charge from the captives' own improvised strikes as readily as from the guards' answering
ones — a battle fought, for the first time in years, with more hands actively swinging than his own
gear alone accounted for.

The transport's captain surrendered to a deck he no longer controlled in any direction, disarmed by a
combined line he couldn't tell apart — legend and liberated captives moving as one force rather than
rescuer and rescued.

"You could have gotten yourselves killed," he said afterward, checking the woman's chain-scarred
hands for injury she waved off without much patience for the concern.

"We could have gotten killed sitting still too. At least this way it was our choice." She met his
look steadily, unapologetic. "You free the lock. We decide what we do once it's open. That's not you
doing us a favor. That's the whole point of the lock coming off at all."

He didn't argue the point, because there wasn't one worth making against it, and the sixty-three
freed that night walked off the transport as a group that had, in the truest sense the word allowed,
freed a good portion of itself.
