# What the Hardened Crews Stopped Fearing

*Locked canon, Batch 192, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-568`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XXIII, wave 8 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The squadron's second engagement, three weeks later, was where the confidence its crews had earned
against the first storm became the actual opening.

A crew drilled not to fear the weather stopped watching it as a threat at all — stopped posting the
extra lookouts, stopped rotating exhausted watch-standers early, stopped treating a driving rain as
anything but routine weather to be endured rather than a condition an enemy might be actively using.
Kanja's approach exploited exactly that shift: not the storm's confusion, which the squadron had
solved, but the certainty the solving had bought them.

Mafesto crossed open water to the lead ship's stern in conditions the crew had stopped bothering to
watch closely, the Kinetic Transfer System banking the boarding leap's full force before a single
lookout registered the approach. Obsidian Malice discharged into the ship's own storm shutters — the
exact reinforcement built to keep the gun deck dry and fighting — jamming three of them shut in their
frames and turning the squadron's own countermeasure into a trap sealing its gunners in place. Onyx
moved through a deck that had stopped expecting anyone to move through it at all, Veil Piercer finding
gaps in a defense built for weather rather than for an actual boarding party standing in front of it.

"You solved the storm," Kanja told the flagship's disarmed captain, once the fight was done. "You
never solved the part where solving it made your people stop looking."

"That's not a fair fight," the captain said. "You used our own confidence against us."

"I used exactly what you built," Kanja said. "A crew that isn't afraid of anything eventually stops
checking whether it should be. That's not a weather problem. That's every fleet that's ever won a war
against fear and then forgotten fear was never the only danger."
