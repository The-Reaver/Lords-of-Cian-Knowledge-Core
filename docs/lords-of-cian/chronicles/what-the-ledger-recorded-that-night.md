# What the Ledger Recorded That Night

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XXX, wave 10.
Rebellion era, age 18, before the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters beyond the already-locked Garren Hask.*

---

Garren Hask had two entries to make the same week, and he made them both the same way he made every
entry — plainly, completely, with nothing softened and nothing left out, because that was the only
kind of book he knew how to keep.

*Deepcut, lost. Two companies, open ground, no terrain advantage available. Ordered retreat.
Correct call — site rebuildable, crew and records preserved. Not a victory. Recorded as a loss.*

*Ferrymouth, lost the same week Marrow Landing was saved. Family recovered unharmed at Marrow
Landing; Ferrymouth's dredge line and five-district documents burned, undefended, same night. Both
outcomes from a single choice made under a time no one could beat. Recorded as both — a save and a
loss, not weighed against each other, not resolved into a single verdict.*

Kanja found him at it that evening, the ledger open to both entries at once, and read them without
comment for longer than the entries themselves took to read.

"You could write it differently," he said finally. "Deepcut as a tactical withdrawal instead of a
loss. Ferrymouth as an acceptable cost instead of a loss. Plenty of people would."

"I know how plenty of people would write it. That's exactly why I don't." Hask didn't look up from
the page, still correcting a figure in the margin with the same steady hand he'd used the night he
first cross-checked a rounded tally against his own. "The day I start writing losses as something
softer than what they were, the books stop being worth anything. Not to you. To whoever reads them
after both of us are gone and needs to know what actually happened, not what we wished had happened."

"Even when the truth makes the choice look worse than it was?"

"Especially then. A choice that looks good written honestly doesn't need softening. A choice that
only looks good once it's been softened wasn't as good as the softening makes it seem, and somebody
ought to be able to tell the difference, someday, by reading these pages instead of asking either of
us to remember it fairly." Hask finally looked up. "You made the right call at Marrow Landing. I
believe that. I also believe Ferrymouth burned because of it, and both of those things belong in the
same book, in the same hand, without one being allowed to erase the other."

Kanja read the entries once more, start to finish, and didn't ask him to change a word.

"That's why I gave you the ledgers in the first place," he said. "Not because you'd make me look
good. Because you wouldn't, if I didn't deserve it. I'd rather carry an honest account of a bad week
than a flattering one."
