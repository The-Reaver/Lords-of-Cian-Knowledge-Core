# The Reef That Grew Back Wrong

*Locked canon, Batch 281, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1476`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle CI, wave 34. Age 175,
V3 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The reef off the Salt Keep's old anchorage had been healthy water once, before decades of slaving
traffic, wreck debris, and the crew's own repeated engagements in its shallows had done what no single
battle ever could on its own: ground the coral down season by season until the fish the freed
settlement depended on for half its food had started, quietly and then not quietly at all, to stop
coming back.

No one had raided anything here in eleven years. The settlement built on the Salt Keep's own
liberation was thriving by every other measure — self-governing, well-traded, exactly the kind of
place the crew's work was meant to produce. It was the reef itself, not any remaining enemy, that had
become the problem, and it had become one slowly enough that Kanja hadn't noticed it until a local
fisherman said it to him plainly, not recognizing who he was speaking to: the water they'd been given
back wasn't the water it used to be.

There was nothing in the Forge-Coat's design, nothing in Mafesto's kinetic systems or Obsidian
Malice's discharge, built to grow coral back. What there was, instead, was time and hands, and Kanja
spent eleven days of it working alongside the fisherman's own people — clearing wreck iron by hand
from shallows too delicate for anything heavier, laying transplant frames a marine-minded elder from a
neighboring settlement had spent a decade perfecting on her own reef, hauling stone for a breakwater
meant to keep future storms from finishing what years of hard use had started.

It wasn't glamorous work, and nothing about it asked for a persona at all; he did it as a hired hand
with steady arms and no name worth mentioning, and the settlement never connected the quiet stranger
helping haul frames to the legend that had freed their parents' generation. Garren Hask's ledger, when
the eleven days were done, logged no rescue and no number of captives at all — only a note that the
cost of a rescue doesn't end when the fighting does, and that some of what centuries of doing this work
had taken couldn't be paid back with a raid, only with the much slower coin of showing up afterward and
staying long enough to fix what the fighting itself had broken.
