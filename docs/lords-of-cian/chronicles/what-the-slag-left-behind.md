# What the Slag Left Behind

*Locked canon, Batch 238, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1050`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LVIII, wave 20.
Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose.*

---

The complaint didn't arrive as a raid or a fire. It arrived as a farmer, three days' walk from the
site, leading a wagon of dead irrigation water down to the settlement's forge terrace and asking,
flatly, who was going to answer for it.

Six weeks of heavy salvage-smelting — the densest run of the tour so far, rebuilding a district's
entire tool-stock from scrapped Trust ironwork in under a month — had sent slag runoff seeping
through the terrace's drainage cuts and into a channel that fed farmland the campaign had never
touched, belonged to no settlement on the crew's own ledger, and owed nothing to anyone standing on
it.

"We didn't burn your fields," Kanja said, walking the length of the dead channel himself, the water
running the wrong color even where it caught the light. "But I don't need to have lit the fire to
own what came out of mine."

"That's not how any of this usually works, is it." The farmer wasn't angry so much as tired, past
the point of expecting an answer. "The Lord of Embers rebuilds what burns. Nobody burned this. It
just happened, the way things happen when enough of you are working this hard for this long in one
place. Who rebuilds a field that nothing ever attacked?"

There was no enemy to answer for it, no siege to break, no reheat to slow down and do more carefully
— every register the floor had ever tested itself against assumed a punishment with a shape and a
source. This had neither. It was simply the size of the work itself, eighteen months and thirty-one
settlements deep, finally costing something no rebuild speed could reach back and prevent.

He halted the site's smelting entirely for four days — the first voluntary stoppage of the whole
tour — and put apprentices who'd spent six weeks learning to build up to work tearing down instead:
cutting a proper diversion trench above the drainage cuts, packing a lime-treatment basin to catch
what the old channel had already carried, work with no tool-stock at the end of it and nothing to
show a watching enemy or a Directorate report.

The season's ruined crop itself he couldn't touch. No basin unmade the harvest already lost, no
diversion trench reached backward into ground already poisoned before anyone knew to look for it.
He had the campaign's own stores cover it instead — grain and coin, not iron — and said so plainly
when the farmer came to collect it.

"This doesn't rebuild anything. It just pays for what I can't." Kanja counted out the last of it
himself rather than hand it off. "Everything this floor's ever been good for assumes there's
something on the other end to rebuild toward. There isn't, here. There's just a field that was fine
before I arrived and isn't now, and no version of 'metabolizes punishment' that reaches back far
enough to un-cause that."

The farmer took the payment without much warmth in it — it wasn't owed as a favor, and neither of
them pretended it was — but came back to the diversion trench a season later anyway, to see if it
held.

It held.
