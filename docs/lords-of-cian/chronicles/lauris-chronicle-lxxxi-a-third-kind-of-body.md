# Lauris Chronicle LXXXI: A Third Kind of Body

*Locked canon, Batch 300, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1698`). Eighty-first entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, a Strand D entry giving full-scene treatment to Operation 24, the fluctuating-
density entity designated Subject AE-001 (previously only summarized at `MCD-182`), continuing the
Specialist sub-period. Dramatizes the encounter `MCD-182` already locks as Lauris's first direct
evidence that the engineering tradition's source materials were not limited to Karesian biology --
that other, unidentified species were being processed by the same apparatus at facilities she had not
yet located. No new named characters; Subject AE-001 already locked, stays a designation rather than
a name, matching `MCD-182`'s own phrasing. Zero collisions.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Specialist designation LL-001-S-024: "I have spent twenty-three operations
assuming the tradition's raw material was Karesian, because Karesian was the only body I recognized
in what it built. Subject AE-001 was neither Karesian nor Cian-born. I have been assuming too narrow
a crime for far too long."*

Operation 24 came to her as a containment-and-neutralize contract of a shape her instrumentation had
never quite catalogued before: a hostile entity haunting the flooded lower districts of a river-delta
settlement, its density signature reading, on the Directorate's own remote instrumentation, as
unstable to the point of near uselessness — spiking and collapsing across a range that made
conventional threat-assessment protocols simply refuse to resolve a number. Two Directorate response
teams had already been driven back by it, neither managing so much as a clear visual identification
before withdrawing.

She engaged it herself on the third night of her own reconnaissance, in the flooded lower level of an
abandoned granary the entity had apparently claimed as a den. I have asked her to describe the fight
itself more than once, and she gives, each time, roughly the same account: not difficult in the way a
capable opponent is difficult, but disorienting in a register she had rarely encountered before or
since. The entity's density did not merely rise and fall the way any combatant's output naturally
does under strain. It fluctuated on a rhythm she could not predict, spiking to a crushing mass one
moment and collapsing to something scarcely denser than water the next, as though its own body could
not settle on what it was supposed to weigh.

She ended the engagement with the Spine of Dagon, timing the killing strike to a collapse-phase
rather than a spike, a judgment call she has told me she made on instinct rather than calculation and
has never fully been able to reconstruct the reasoning behind since.

*

What occupied the greater portion of her archive entry, and what she has told me she considers the
operation's true significance, came afterward, in the hours she spent examining the body before the
flood tide reclaimed the lower granary entirely. The remains fluctuated even in death for some minutes
— residual instability, she concluded, rather than any lingering vitality — before settling into a
final biological structure she was able to examine properly. And that structure matched neither of the
two templates her career had, by then, trained her to expect.

It was not Karesian. The density-bearing tissue architecture, the load-distribution lattice she had
learned to recognize in every engineered Karesian subject from the Twin Anomaly forward, was simply
absent. Nor was it Cian-born human substrate carrying an induced or grafted anomaly, the pattern she
had catalogued at Brokenwall and would catalogue again at Velaris. It was, by every marker her own
considerable expertise could apply, something else — a biological architecture she did not recognize,
built around principles that achieved density instability rather than density mastery, as though
whoever had engineered it had been experimenting with a different source material entirely and had
not yet, or perhaps never intended to, solve for the stability her own body and Karesian engineered
subjects alike both possessed.

She wrote, in the entry she has since read to me in full, that the discovery unsettled her more than
almost anything Operation 23's instrumentation had — because instrumentation, however troubling, could
still be explained as evidence of a single crime reaching backward into a single people's history. A
third kind of body, engineered from source material that was neither hers nor Karesian, implied
something considerably larger: that the apparatus beneath the Directorate was not conducting one
long crime against one people's biology. It was conducting research, broadly and without evident
preference for source, wherever a usable substrate could be found — and if it had found one substrate
she did not recognize, there was no honest reason to assume it had found only one.

"I have been treating this as an account with a single debtor," she wrote. "I no longer believe that.
I do not yet know how many debtors there truly are, or how many kinds of body have already been spent
paying whatever this apparatus considers its own accounting owed."

She filed the contract as closed, the entity's threat neutralized precisely as required, and did not
report the biological anomaly in any form the Directorate's own review would flag as noteworthy. Her
private notation of the discrepancy — the third kind of body, unidentified, unexplained, unresolved —
joined the widening private ledger that had, by then, grown considerably beyond what any single
operation's report could account for.

Her closing note for Operation 24: *I do not know what this creature was, or where it was taken from,
or whether anyone mourned it before I did. I know only that the crime I am investigating is larger
than the people it is being committed against. I have been asking the wrong-sized question since
Operation 3. I intend to stop.*
