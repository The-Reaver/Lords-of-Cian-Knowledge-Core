# The Pass That Wouldn't Close

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XXVI, wave 8.
Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign. A detailed showcase of the Trinity defending a
mountain supply pass. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters.*

---

The narrow mountain pass was the only overland route between the campaign's coastal forge sites and
the inland settlements waiting on its tools — a single switchbacked trail, cliff on one side, drop on
the other, wide enough for a loaded mule train and nothing wider. A Trust detachment had fortified the
high ground above it weeks earlier, waiting for exactly the kind of supply convoy the pass could not
be avoided by.

Kanja climbed the cliff face alone ahead of the convoy, Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System converting
every handhold's strain into stored charge rather than fatigue, and reached the fortified overlook
before the detachment's own watch had finished counting the convoy's approach below.

Obsidian Malice discharged directly into the overlook's own rockwork, the shockwave collapsing a
section of prepared ambush position before a single arrow could be loosed downward. Onyx cleared the
remaining defenders along the ridge in a sequence too fast for any signal to reach the detachment's
reserve force stationed at the pass's far mouth — Cadence Ruin unmaking their formation before they
understood the overlook itself, their entire advantage, was already gone.

The reserve force, arriving at the pass's far end expecting an ambush already sprung in their favor,
found instead a cleared ridge, a waiting convoy, and Kanja standing alone at the trail's narrowest
point, blocking the only way through.

They did not press the engagement.

"You closed a pass by yourself before the convoy ever reached the dangerous part," the convoy's own
lead driver said afterward, still working out the sequence of what had happened above them. "Didn't
even hear the fighting from down here."

"That was the idea. A pass this narrow, the fight was never going to be won down where you were
standing." Kanja looked back up at the ridge, quiet now. "Best defense of a chokepoint is making sure
nobody ever gets to use it as one. Whoever holds the high ground first decides whether this pass is a
trap or just a road. I got there first."
