# Lauris Chronicle LXVII: What a Civilization Takes Its Time Deciding

*Locked canon, Batch 300, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1684`). Sixty-seventh entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, the tenth entry of Strand K. Set at age ~3,580-3,600, immediately following
Chronicle LXVI (`MCD-1683`). Dramatizes the opening of the Iron-Speaker deliberation already locked
at `MCD-174` (380 years, before agreeing to support her departure) -- the formal presentation of her
decision to the assembled Iron-Speakers and the earliest, most contested years of a deliberation the
chronicle does not compress into a single sitting, since a body already established elsewhere in
this ledger as resolving disputes across decades and centuries (`MCD-1633`, `MCD-1635`) would not
resolve a question this large quickly. Reuses Veska Karth-Ven. No new named characters beyond one
unnamed senior Iron-Speaker voice, matching the established convention for secondary figures in this
strand (Chronicles II, XVI, XVIII). No contradictions.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Vask Karth-Ven, age ~3,600: "I have now waited twenty years for an answer I did
not expect to need waiting for. I do not resent the waiting. I helped build the reasons it is
necessary."*

I want to begin this entry by correcting an impression I fear the last one may have left. Lauris
did not, having found her answer at Vask-of-Vasks, simply announce it and depart. What followed was
something I have come to think of as one of the more remarkable pieces of governance this whole
long chronicle records — not because it was dramatic, but because of how seriously an entire
civilization, reduced by then to fourteen thousand women across twelve Vasks, took the question of
letting one of them go.

She brought the request first to Veska, who had known her since age fourteen and remained, by that
point, the closest thing Lauris had to a formal sponsor within Karth-Ven's own governance. Veska's
own recorded response, which Lauris preserved in full rather than summarizing — a rarity, in an
archive that generally condenses even its own most consequential exchanges — was neither approval
nor refusal: *You are asking me to help you take the last documented Karesian this world will ever
produce and send her somewhere none of us can follow or verify. I will carry this to the
Iron-Speakers myself, because I think you deserve to have it carried by someone who has watched you
long enough to trust the asking. I cannot promise you what they will decide.*

*

The full Council of Iron-Speakers convened within the year — a rarer event, by the civilization's
own late-era standards, than its formal architecture suggests, since twelve Vasks reduced to a bare
operational minimum could not easily spare every senior voice from its own duties at once. I have
reconstructed the substance of that first hearing from three partial records: Veska's own account,
a fragment from the Vask-of-Vasks archival minute-keeper, and Lauris's own entry, which is, true to
her nature, the shortest and least emotionally legible of the three.

What emerges, cross-referenced, is a body genuinely divided, and divided for reasons I find I
respect rather than dismiss. One senior Iron-Speaker — unnamed in every surviving record, though
Veska's account suggests she spoke for a substantial faction — argued plainly that a civilization on
a terminal demographic curve, with Lauris as its sole documented deterrent against every incursion
its thinned trade-points could no longer repel alone, could not responsibly release its own last
line of defense to a world it had no way of verifying existed as described. This was not, I want to
be clear, an argument born of possessiveness. It was, by every account I have read, a genuine and
carefully reasoned fear — the fear of a people who had already lost almost everything they once
were, being asked to risk the one asset standing between what remained and its final ending.

Lauris's own recorded answer to this argument, delivered — her account says — without raising her
voice, is the line I have chosen to build this entry around: *I have served this civilization for
seventeen hundred and sixty years since I achieved karth-ven, and I do not think either of us
believes I am leaving because I have stopped caring what happens to Kares Prime. I am leaving
because I have exhausted what remains here for me to learn, and a civilization this small cannot
afford to keep a resource it can no longer teach anything new. I would rather be a testimony you
sent out than a weapon you kept sharpening against a whetstone that has nothing left to give it.*

*

The Council did not decide that day, nor in the years immediately following. I have found, scattered
through both Veska's continuing correspondence and Lauris's own archive, evidence of at least four
further formal hearings across the ensuing decades — proposals for a limited departure with a
mandated return, a counter-proposal that she train three further Karth-Sera successors before any
departure could be considered, a faction that wished to send an accompanying delegation she herself
quietly discouraged, believing correctly that no delegation could survive what she might encounter.
None of these proposals was accepted outright; none was rejected outright either. What I have come
to understand, reading this correspondence in full for the first time in my own years narrating her,
is that the Council was not stalling. It was doing, at civilizational scale and across centuries, the
same thing Lauris herself had done alone at Vask-of-Vasks: refusing to settle for an answer that did
not close every column it had opened.

Her own closing entry for this period, written some twenty years into the deliberation and clearly
not intended as a conclusion, only a marker: *They have not said yes. They have not said no. I find,
somewhat to my own surprise, that I am not impatient. I spent one hundred and eighty years deciding
to ask. I think it is only fair that they be given longer than that to decide how to answer.*
