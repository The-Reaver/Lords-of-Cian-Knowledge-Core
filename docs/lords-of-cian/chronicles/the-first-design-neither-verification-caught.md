# The First Design Neither Verification Caught

*Locked canon, Batch 261, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1308`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LXXXIX, wave 30. A new Crawler countermeasure partially defeats doubled verification and the standing-alone check, left deliberately unresolved. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The Crawler at the depot's edge read clean on the first pass, clean on the second, and clean on the third question Kanja had added to his own standing practice since Sennow Crossing — not only was the tension true, and not only was the target genuinely alone, but nothing about the structure carried the falsified-signature deception the wall had once taught him to watch for. Three checks, three honest answers, every one of them wrong.

He didn't learn that until the discharge, timed and placed exactly as every verified read demanded, produced almost nothing — a shudder through the Crawler's plating, a fraction of the disabling effect a clean hit should have carried, the vehicle rolling forward regardless as though the resonance had found something to answer to and then simply declined to fully transfer the blow.

"That's never happened," the second student said, watching from twenty yards back. "Not once, not even against the four-alloy variant."

"It's happening now." Kanja pulled back, recalculating with a speed the doctrine's whole run had trained into him and finding, for the first time, that the recalculation didn't produce an answer. The tension had been real. The target had been alone. The signature hadn't been falsified. Every safeguard he'd built across dozens of engagements and years of hard-won refinement had returned an honest, true, correctly verified reading — and the discharge still hadn't worked the way an honest, true, correctly verified reading always had before.

The Crawler withdrew rather than press an advantage its own crew likely didn't fully understand they'd been handed, and Kanja let it go, unwilling to spend a second discharge chasing a mechanism he couldn't yet name.

"Someone built this on purpose," he said, once the depot's approach had gone quiet again. "Not a countermeasure that hides the tension, or falsifies it, or denies the read a structure to answer with at all. Something that lets the read through — true, honest, unfalsified — and then defeats what the read is *for*. I don't know how. I don't know if it's the rival, or the engineer's old corps building past what he gave them, or someone else entirely who's spent years studying the same reports the scholar's academy has been publishing openly for anyone patient enough to read them."

"What do you do about it?"

"Tonight, nothing. I don't discharge against a mechanism I can't yet explain, and I'm not going to pretend three correct answers add up to understanding a fourth question I didn't know to ask." Kanja looked back once at the depot before turning from it. "Every refinement this doctrine's ever earned came from surviving something it hadn't yet accounted for. I'd rather carry this one unsolved for a while than force an answer onto it just because not knowing sits uneasily."
