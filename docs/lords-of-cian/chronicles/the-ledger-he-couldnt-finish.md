# The Ledger He Couldn't Finish

*Locked canon, Batch 198, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-761`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XXXVI, wave 12 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The runner arrived at the ledger tent in the middle of the tanning district's third day of testimony,
out of breath and carrying word that pulled Kanja away before the day's tally had reached even half
its expected names — an urgent matter elsewhere in the crew's wider campaign, pressing enough that he
left within the hour, taking nothing with him but the clothes he stood in.

"I don't know how long I'll be gone," he told Ezio, at the tent flap, the ledger still open on the
table behind him with two hundred names yet to be heard. "You finish it. You and Pell both know the
method as well as I do by now."

"Better, in places," Ezio said, without either pride or false modesty, simply stating what both of
them already knew. "Go. We'll have it done and filed by the time you're back, whenever that is."

It was the first time since the campaign had started that the Industrial Myth himself was absent for
the bulk of a district's resolution — no worst-off-first walk-through, no seated standoff, no
personal testimony taken by his own hand for the two hundred names still waiting. Ezio ran the
questioning. Pell Ostra, freed from guarding a man who wasn't there to guard, took over the crowd
management Efa Gol usually handled, keeping the line orderly through two more full days without
incident.

The district didn't seem to mind the absence the way Ezio had half-feared it might. A few workers
asked where he'd gone, were told honestly that the crew didn't know and wouldn't guess for them, and
gave their testimony anyway, apparently satisfied that the ledger itself, and the two people running
it, carried enough weight on their own.

"They didn't need him here," Pell said, once the tally closed, four hundred and eleven names strong,
cross-checked and complete, filed with the district administration under the same method as every
ledger before it. "I half expected the district to stall until he came back."

"That was always supposed to be the point," Ezio said, closing the binding on the finished book.
"If this only works because he's standing in the yard, it's not a method. It's just him. I'd rather
find out now, without him here to lean on, whether it's actually the second thing."

Kanja returned eleven days later to a completed, filed, undisputed ledger he'd had no hand in
finishing, and read it start to finish in silence before saying anything at all.

"You didn't need me for this one."

"No," Ezio agreed. "We didn't. I think that's the best thing either of us could tell you."
