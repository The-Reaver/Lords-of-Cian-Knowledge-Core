# The Soldier Who Stayed

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XXVII, closing
wave 8. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

He had fought against the campaign twice — once at a burning he still would not describe in detail,
once at a rebuild site he'd been sent to disrupt and instead spent three days quietly failing to bring
himself to sabotage. When his unit was recalled after the second engagement, he did not go with it. He
walked back to the settlement he'd been ordered to hurt and asked, plainly, if there was work for a man
who knew how to build as well as he knew how to break.

Kanja put him on the floor without ceremony, the same way he'd put every other new hand to work, and
did not raise the question of his past himself.

"Most people here know what I used to do," the former soldier said, weeks later, unprompted, working a
seam alongside a crew that had stopped watching him with suspicion sometime in the second week. "I
keep waiting for it to matter more than it seems to."

"It matters. I haven't forgotten it, and I don't expect anyone else here has either." Kanja checked the
seam's work without singling it out from the rest of the floor's output. "What it doesn't get to do is
decide what you're allowed to become now. I've watched this campaign rebuild things that got burned
worse than any grudge I could hold against one man who changed his mind halfway through a war."

"That's a more generous line than I was expecting."

"It's not generosity. It's the same principle I've built this whole campaign on, pointed at a person
instead of a district. Something gets broken, it doesn't have to stay broken. Doesn't matter if it's a
forge or a man's own judgment about which side he wants to be standing on." Kanja moved down the floor
to the next station. "You made your choice. I'm not interested in re-litigating it for you every
morning. I'm interested in what you build from here."

He stayed through the remainder of the campaign, and beyond it, into settlements that had no memory of
which uniform he'd once worn — known, eventually, for nothing more remarkable than being good with his
hands and slow to speak of what had come before.
