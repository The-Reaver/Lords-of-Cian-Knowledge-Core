# The Blade at the Captive's Throat

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Scourge Alias Chronicle, wave 8. Long Mask era,
age 138, V3 gear (Golden Terror era). Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose. No new named characters.*

---

The slaver captain had a boy of perhaps fourteen held against his chest with a blade at the throat
before the smoke had finished rolling across his own deck, and he said the only thing left worth
saying once every other exit had closed.

"Stand down, or I open his throat before you take a single step closer. I've heard what you do to
crews that fight you. I've never heard what you do to a man holding exactly this."

The Forge-Coat's grounding weave had already absorbed everything the deck's remaining defenders could
throw. Mafesto's stored charge sat ready, more than enough to end the standoff in a heartbeat, and
every ounce of it was useless the instant a single wrong heartbeat meant a dead child instead of a
disarmed captain.

He stopped where he stood, hands open, and did the only thing the moment actually called for, which
had nothing to do with any weapon built into the last hundred years of his gear. He waited.

"You're not going to do anything," the captain said, reading the stillness as hesitation.

"I'm doing exactly what the situation asked for. You're the one running out of moves." He kept his
voice level, unhurried, the Sovereign Eyes' amber gaze fixed not on the captain but on the boy's own
face, watching for the one thing he actually needed — the moment fear gave way to enough presence of
mind to help. "Your whole crew's down. Your ship isn't going anywhere. The only thing keeping you
alive right now is that I have better things to spend the next minute on than proving a point to you.
So we're going to stand here, exactly like this, until you get tired before I do."

Six minutes passed before the boy's own trembling steadied enough to do what fear alone hadn't let
him — a small, deliberate shift of his weight, forward and down, out from under the blade's angle by
a margin of inches that the captain, exhausted and increasingly desperate, was too slow to correct
for.

The Ironhand Gauntlets closed the distance in the same half-second the boy cleared it, and the blade
never finished its arc toward anything but empty air.

"You waited," the boy said afterward, still shaking, disbelieving. "Six minutes. You just — waited."

"Sometimes the fastest way through a fight is refusing to have one until the other person runs out of
reasons to keep having it." He checked the boy for injury with hands gentler than the gauntlets'
plating suggested they could manage. "You did the hard part. I just made sure it was safe when you
did."
