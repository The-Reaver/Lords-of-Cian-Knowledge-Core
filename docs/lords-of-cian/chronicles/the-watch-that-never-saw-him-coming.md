# The Watch That Never Saw Him Coming

*Locked canon, Batch 259, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1249`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LXXXIV, wave 28,
closing the wave. Age 260, V4 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose.*

---

The depot's watch rotation was, by every account gathered beforehand, genuinely competent — six
guards, staggered shifts, overlapping sightlines across every approach, a garrison that had held
against two previous rescue attempts by other, less careful operations. Nothing about this raid
involved fighting any of them. That was the point.

The Sovereign Eyes' V4 lenses carried a fully dark stealth mode alongside their active-threat and
surveillance settings, the amber glow that defined the legend everywhere else in the loadout dimmed
to nothing at all — no signature, no silhouette a trained eye could catch even watching for it,
just structural overlay data read in total darkness with no light emitted to give the reading away.
He used it for three hours straight, moving through the depot's every corridor, mapping locks,
counting captives, locating every guard's position and rotation timing without a single one of them
registering so much as a change in the air.

The captives were freed by hand, one at a time, chains worked loose in total silence, each one led
out through a route plotted from the same overlay before any guard had reason to notice a headcount
had changed. By the time the watch rotation's own morning shift change caught the discrepancy — six
fewer bodies than the ledger said should be chained below — there was no trail to follow and no
struggle to reconstruct. Nothing had happened, as far as any watchman could report. Forty-one people
had simply stopped being there.

Garren Hask's own ledger entry that night was shorter than most, because there was less to it than
most: no fight, no injury, no reputation invoked at all, just a number and a method that left the
depot's garrison still convinced, months later, that their watch rotation had never once been
breached.
