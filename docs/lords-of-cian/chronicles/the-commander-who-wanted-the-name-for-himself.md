# The Commander Who Wanted the Name for Himself

*Locked canon, Batch 196, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-698`). Bane Alias Chronicle XXXIII, wave 11 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The friction came from inside the rebellion this time, not from the Directorate — a company
commander who'd built a real, respectable record of his own against Trust garrisons in the eastern
foothills, and who had grown, over the better part of a year, quietly resentful of how completely
the Bane name had come to eclipse every other rebellion victory in the stories people told.

He said it plainly, to Bane's face, the first time their columns crossed paths after a battle his
own company had won largely without help.

"My company took the Threshway garrison on our own. Forty casualties, six weeks of planning, real
cost we paid ourselves. Within a month everyone from here to the coast was calling it one of your
victories, because your name's attached to the war now and mine isn't attached to anything. I'm not
ungrateful for what you've done. I'd like my own people to be remembered for what they actually did,
instead of folded into a legend that wasn't even there."

Bane didn't argue the complaint, didn't defend the legend's carelessness, didn't offer the easy
reassurance that would have cost him nothing and fixed nothing. "You're right. That's not something
I did on purpose, and it's not something I can fully undo once a story's already traveled the way
that one has. I can do something about the next one, if you'll let me."

He spent the following week doing something no account of the Bane name had ever recorded him doing
before: personally correcting the record on the Threshway garrison, town by town, crediting the
commander's company by name for a victory the growing myth had quietly stolen from them, refusing
every opportunity offered to let the credit slide back toward himself instead.

"You didn't have to do that," the commander said afterward, some of the resentment genuinely eased,
though not all of it — some things a single correction couldn't fully undo. "The name would have
kept the credit either way. Nobody would have questioned it."

"That's exactly the problem with it. Nobody questions it, so nobody has to earn correcting it, so it
just keeps happening until every victory anyone else fought for gets folded into mine whether I want
it or not." Bane's voice carried none of the weight he saved for garrisons, something closer to plain
tiredness instead. "I don't get to control what people believe about me. I can at least control
whether I let a lie stand once I know it's costing someone else what they actually earned."
