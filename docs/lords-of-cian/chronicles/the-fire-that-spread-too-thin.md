# The Fire That Spread Too Thin

*Locked canon, Batch 140, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-465`). Umoja Chronicle II. Homage-era Chicago,
Umoja/Kofi. Territory Chronicle -- Kofi as protagonist, Kanja an unnamed background guest with no
command, credit, or resolution authorship. New standalone material. No new named characters.*

---

A rival tenant organization on the district's far side, unconnected to any of Umoja's own established
networks, asked Kofi to help weld their own fractured leadership together the same way he'd welded
the tenders and haulers into one front during the Furnace District Strike years before. He agreed,
confident the same instinct that had worked then would work again.

It didn't, not fully.

"One Fire" reached the tenant organization's members the way it always did — a felt, immediate sense
of shared cause where distrust had stood before — but it didn't hold the way it held within Umoja's
own established relationships, thinning out within weeks as old grievances between the organization's
factions reasserted themselves once the immediate crisis that had brought Kofi in passed.

"I don't understand," Kofi admitted, watching the coalition he'd helped build fracture again barely a
month after it had seemed solid. "It worked the same way it always works. I felt it take, the same as
every other time."

"Maybe it took the way it always takes. Maybe that was never the problem." An older organizer from the
tenant group, watching the same fracture with more resignation than surprise, offered the observation
without unkindness. "You've spent years building trust with your own people before you ever needed
One Fire to hold anything together. These factions met you a month ago. The gift gave them a feeling
of shared cause. It didn't give them the years of history that actually makes a coalition outlast the
crisis that built it. Maybe it was never really the fire holding your own coalition together all this
time. Maybe it was the years underneath it."

Kofi sat with that longer than he wanted to admit finding necessary. "I've been crediting the gift for
something I actually built the slow way, without realizing it."

"Not entirely. The gift's real. I felt it too, the day you came." The organizer's tone stayed level,
neither dismissing the ability nor overselling it. "I just don't think it's a substitute for the years.
I think it's an accelerant. Fire needs something to burn. Here, there wasn't enough underneath it yet."

Kofi returned to the tenant district in the following months, not with the gift alone this time but
with the same patient relationship-building he'd never fully credited as the actual foundation of his
own home ground — work that took considerably longer, and held considerably better, once it finally
did. An unnamed man Kofi had crossed paths with once, years before, passed through the district
during this slower rebuilding and said nothing about it to anyone.
