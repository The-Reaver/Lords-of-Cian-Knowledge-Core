# The Strait That Froze Early

*Locked canon, Batch 246, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1074`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LXI, wave 21, first
entry. A detailed V4-gear combat showcase in the sub-series' first cold/ice environment. Not a
territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The northern strait wasn't supposed to freeze for another six weeks. The slaving convoy running it
had planned around that certainty, ice-free water being the only thing standing between their route
and the longer, better-watched southern passage. The strait froze anyway, eleven days early, and
caught their lead ship fast in pack ice a half-mile short of open water — with forty-one captives
chained below deck and a garrison of guards who now had nowhere to run that the ice wouldn't also
take from them.

He came across the floes on foot, alone, the rest of the crew holding position at the ice's edge
where the ship couldn't reach them. It was the kind of ground the Forge-Coat's designers had never
built for on purpose — no traction underfoot that behaved the way solid stone or packed earth did,
every footfall a small negotiation with a surface that wanted to become water again the instant it
was trusted.

The Ironhand Gauntlets solved half the problem without being asked to. Blood-heated since the V4
refit, they kept his fingers answering commands at a speed cold steel and colder air would otherwise
have stolen from him one degree at a time — the difference, on ice, between a grip that held a rail
through a fall and one that simply reported the fall was happening. He used that grip twice crossing
the last hundred yards, catching a shifting floe's edge both times before his weight could commit to
water instead of ice.

The Ironfall Boots' impact soles, built to destabilize a standing man, did something else entirely on
pack ice: the same stomp-transmitted tremor that dropped opponents on solid ground here cracked a
spiderweb fracture straight through a guard post's footing, dropping two guards through the ice
before either got a shot off — not the boots' intended use, and not one he'd planned for, only
noticed and taken in the second it was available to him. Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System did the
rest of the work at close range, redirecting a boarding axe's own swing into the man who'd thrown it,
careful, the way it always was, to spend exactly the force it had first absorbed and no more.

Obsidian Malice never left its housing. A discharge on ice, against a hull already stressed by
freezing water, risked opening the ship's hull to the sea faster than anyone below could be gotten
off it — a risk he'd weighed and set aside before he'd finished crossing the floes, not a decision
made under pressure but one carried onto the ice already made.

Below deck, the cold had done its own damage before he ever reached it. A child, maybe seven, had
stopped shivering entirely — the specific, quiet stillness that meant the body had given up trying to
warm itself. He broke the chain, wrapped her in the one dry length of sailcloth he had, and opened
two of the Mend-Line's six reservoirs against her core rather than a wound — off-label, the adhesive
compound never designed for warming rather than sealing, but the seal it formed held heat in as
efficiently as it held blood, and her shivering started again nine minutes later on the crossing back.

Efa Gol's decoy line wasn't run that night — nothing to draw attention away from on an ice field with
no one watching but the guards already dying on it — and Garren Hask's count, when it was finally
taken from a safe fire two hours later, read forty-one freed, one carried the last hundred yards
against a body that had nearly stopped fighting to stay warm on its own.
