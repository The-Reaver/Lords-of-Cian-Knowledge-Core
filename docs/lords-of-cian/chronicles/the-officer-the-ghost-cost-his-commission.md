# The Officer the Ghost Cost His Commission

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle XL,
first entry in the fourteenth wave. Rebellion era, age 22, roughly a year after Ghost Harbor
(`MCD-235`). Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material — a genuine collateral human cost
within the Trust's own ranks. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The man who came aboard under a truce flag wasn't a captain anymore, hadn't been for eight months, and
made that clear before he said anything else — a former Trust patrol officer, stripped of rank and
command for filing a sighting report his superiors had decided reflected worse on his judgment than on
the ghost fleet's actual behavior.

"I reported what I saw," he said, standing on *The Audit*'s deck with the specific bitterness of a man
who'd lost everything for telling the truth. "A vessel that vanished from open water with no wreckage,
no wake, nothing that fit any explanation a naval board would accept. They didn't call me a liar. They
called me unreliable. Same result, different word."

Kanja said nothing at first, recognizing in the man's account the exact engagement — the allowed
sighting, deliberately unresolved, that had opened the alias's very first Chronicle a year before.

"That was us," he said finally. "I let your patrol see it and vanish on purpose. I told you at the
time it would cost your side something. I didn't think through what it would cost you specifically."

"No. You didn't." The man's voice cracked, briefly, before he steadied it. "Fifteen years of service.
Gone, because the story I told was true and true wasn't the same as believable to men who'd rather
discipline an inconvenient witness than admit something outside their understanding was operating in
their own waters."

"What do you want from me?"

"I don't know yet. I didn't come here for revenge, or I'd have brought a weapon instead of a truce
flag." He looked out at the water, the same water that had cost him everything. "I think I came
because you're the only person alive who actually knows I wasn't lying. Everyone else who mattered
decided I was, and there's nothing I can do about that except know, myself, that I wasn't."

Kanja had no restitution to offer that would restore fifteen years of service, and didn't pretend
otherwise. "I can't give you your commission back. I can tell you, for whatever it's worth from the
person who cost it to you, that what you saw was real, and what it cost you was a genuine wrong done by
people who chose comfort over believing an honest report."

"That's not nothing." The man managed something that wasn't quite a smile. "It's not enough. But it's
not nothing."

He left without asking for anything further, and Kanja carried the encounter forward the same way he'd
carried the merchant captain's dead crew months before — a cost the reputation had extracted from
someone who'd never chosen to be part of the story, weighed and remembered rather than dismissed as
the acceptable price of a strategy that worked.
