# What the Hull Told Dol Maren

*Locked canon, Batch 121, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-411`). Sovereign Ghost Alias Chronicle V. Rebellion
era, a detailed naval technical showcase during a genuine storm engagement, from Dol Maren's
perspective -- already a locked named character (`CC-120`/`CC-121`). Not a territory Chronicle. New
standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters beyond the
already-locked Dol Maren.*

---

Dol Maren had told the fleet's captains, more than once and in his usual near-total verbal economy,
that *The Audit* could outrun a storm no ordinary hull should survive, and it took the Trust
blockade squadron's own pursuit into a genuine hurricane's outer bands to prove it in front of
witnesses who mattered.

"Report," Kanja said, over the howling wind, watching Maren's hands move across the hull-stress
readouts he'd personally rigged along the keel.

"Starboard planking's flexing three degrees past design tolerance. Not failing. Flexing on purpose
— I built the give into the joints myself, so the hull bends with the swell instead of fighting it."
Maren didn't look up from the readouts. "Pursuit ships are rigid-hulled. Built for calm-water speed,
not this. Every swell that's testing us is breaking something on at least one of theirs. Number,
material, conclusion: four ships pursuing, two Drakma-cracked hull seams confirmed by sound
already, zero on ours."

The blockade squadron broke off within the hour, two of its four ships listing visibly and taking on
water from stress fractures Maren had predicted before the storm's outer bands had even fully
closed, while *The Audit* rode the same swells that were dismantling her pursuers with barely a
groan from her deliberately flexible frame.

"You built a ship to survive weather nobody sane sails into on purpose," Kanja said, once the pursuit
had scattered and the worst of the storm's outer bands had passed. "That's not luck. That's years of
thinking about a problem most shipwrights never bother to solve, because most ships never need to
outrun anyone badly enough to risk it."

"I've had four years now to think about exactly what this fleet needs a hull to do that no design
manual accounts for." Maren finally looked up from the readouts, satisfied with numbers that held.
"Everybody else builds ships to survive the sea. I built one to survive being chased through it. Not
the same problem. Took me most of those four years to understand that before I could actually solve
it."

The storm passed within the day, and the blockade squadron's own after-action report, recovered
weeks later, would describe losing two ships to "an unnatural storm response consistent with rebel
naval technology" — never once suspecting that the only technology involved was a shipwright's
patient understanding of ordinary stress and flex, applied more carefully than anyone on the other
side had thought to try.
