# What the Successor Chose to Keep

*Locked canon, Batch 259, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1254`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LXXXIX, wave 30. Age
312, V4 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Efa Gol's successor had run decoy and diversion command alone for over forty years by then, long
enough that fewer and fewer of the crew still thought to call her anything other than what she'd
become in her own right. This operation was hers to plan from the ground up, the way most were now,
and she planned it differently than either Kanja or Efa Gol would have.

Where Efa Gol had always favored a single, well-placed decoy force drawing pursuit cleanly away, and
Kanja's own instinct ran toward direct confrontation dressed in enough theater to end the fight
before it properly started, she split the operation into three smaller, quieter diversions instead —
less dramatic, less suited to the legend's usual visible signature, but harder for a well-prepared
garrison to identify as a single coordinated threat. It worked because it didn't look like anything
the reputation had trained enemies to expect.

He watched the plan unfold from a position that had nothing to do with commanding it, the same way
he'd watched her first solo command from a distance seven years earlier. This wasn't a doctrine
handed down and executed faithfully. It was a doctrine she'd absorbed thoroughly enough to depart
from on her own judgment, keeping what still worked and discarding what no longer fit the garrisons
she was actually facing rather than the ones the reputation had originally been built against.

Afterward, he told her plainly that he'd have run it differently, and that hers had worked better.
She didn't need the compliment, though she didn't turn it away either. What she said instead stayed
with him longer than the praise had: that she hadn't kept his method because it was his. She'd kept
the parts that still solved the problem in front of her, and built something of her own for the
parts that didn't — which, she pointed out, was exactly what he'd once told her the whole doctrine
was for.
