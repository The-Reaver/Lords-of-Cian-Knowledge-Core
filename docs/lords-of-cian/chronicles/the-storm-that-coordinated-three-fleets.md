# The Storm That Coordinated Three Fleets

*Locked canon, Batch 160, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-504`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XI.
Rebellion era, age 29, the Gale Straits. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material — a
detailed, large-scale combat showcase per Abad's craft instruction, applying the storm-timing margin
learned in Chronicle X. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Three allied commands, spread across the Straits and unable to coordinate by any conventional signal
fast enough to matter, converged on a single Directorate stronghold using nothing but Sephtis's storm
prediction as their shared clock — each fleet ordered to strike at the precise hour the storm's
leading edge would reach their own separate position, arriving from three directions within minutes
of each other without a single message passed between commands on the day itself.

Kanja's own fleet held the wider safety margin this time, the lesson of the escort's loss carried
directly into the planning.

Mafesto's sensors read the stronghold's defensive layout from the sea, confirming the timing held
exactly as calculated — the northern allied fleet's opening strike drew the garrison's main defense
seaward just as the southern fleet's own arrival caught the stronghold's rear entirely undefended.
Kanja's fleet, arriving from the storm's own leading edge with the weather itself as cover, closed the
final gap, Obsidian Malice breaching the harbor's chain boom in a single discharge timed to the
precise moment all three fleets needed the passage open.

The stronghold fell in under two hours, its garrison overwhelmed by a coordinated assault none of its
own defensive doctrine had ever accounted for — three separate commands acting as one without a
shared signal beyond a shared sky.

"Three fleets, no communication between them beyond a prediction," the northern fleet's own commander
said afterward, genuinely impressed by a coordination his own doctrine would never have attempted.
"I've never fought alongside anything like that."

"Neither had I, before Sephtis proved it was possible. The storm doesn't care which fleet is
watching it. It arrives the same for everyone within its reach." Kanja surveyed the captured
stronghold, no losses on his own side this time, the wider margin holding through the engagement's
full duration. "That makes it the one signal three separate commands can all trust without a single
message passed between them."
