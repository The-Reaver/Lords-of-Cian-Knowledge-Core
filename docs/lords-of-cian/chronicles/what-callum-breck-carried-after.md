# What Callum Breck Carried After

*Locked canon, Batch 194, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-632`). The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XXVII, wave 9 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

By the time five districts had agreed to a shared tally standard and the water logs had spread to
every site on the canal, Callum Breck had mostly stopped thinking of the name as something he'd
given away one angry night with a piece of chalk. It had become simply a fact of the world, the way
the canal itself was a fact of the world, and he had assumed the strangeness of having caused it
would fade the way most strangeness eventually does.

It didn't fade. It changed shape.

"You've gone quiet again," Kanja said, finding him on the dock after the five-foreman meeting had
broken up, watching the last of the delegations disperse toward their own districts.

"I keep doing the arithmetic on it. Five districts, tonight, agreeing to something none of them would
have agreed to a year ago — because of a name I put on a man's forehead without thinking past my own
anger that night." Breck didn't look up from the water. "I didn't build any of what happened after.
You did that. But I keep finding pieces of it that trace back to three words of chalk, and I don't
know what to do with the size of that."

"You don't have to do anything with it. It happened. It's not asking you to manage it."

"That's not really true, though, is it. Every time the name gets bigger, I feel like I'm the one who
has to answer for whether it deserves to be." Breck finally looked over. "Halst tells the story like
it's a good one. Maybe it is. I just keep waiting for the day it stops being about the daughter I was
thinking of, and starts being about something too large for either of us to actually be
responsible for."

"I think about that too, more than I let on." Kanja sat down beside him, the dock's boards still
damp from the day's work. "But I don't think the size is the problem. The name growing bigger than
either of us intended isn't a debt either of us owes. It's just what happens when something true gets
repeated by enough people who needed it to be true. You didn't build the five-district agreement.
Neither did I, really — the foremen built it, because the name gave them a reason to trust each other
enough to try. That's not weight either of us has to carry alone. It's weight the work itself is
carrying, now, whether we watch it or not."

Breck considered that for a long moment, and didn't fully let go of the unease, not that night. But
something in the arithmetic shifted slightly — less a debt he owed the name, more a thing he'd helped
start and no longer had to personally hold up.

"I still didn't mean to make you a king," he said finally, echoing himself from weeks before.

"I know. I don't think either of us gets to decide anymore whether the crown fits. We just have to
keep making sure it's earned."
