# What Efa Gol Came to See

*Locked canon, Batch 273, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1417`). Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XCIII, wave 31,
closing the wave. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241). The alias's first
entry featuring a visiting fleet crew member from outside the campaign itself. Not a territory
Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Efa Gol came aboard The Anvil between operations, three days with nothing decoy-shaped or diversion-
shaped asked of her, and spent most of the first of them simply standing on the forge deck watching
apprentices work a shift change she hadn't been briefed on and didn't need to be. Nobody had told her
what to expect. She'd commanded crowds into believing a fight was somewhere it wasn't often enough to
recognize, within an hour, that this crowd wasn't performing anything at all.

"You run this the same way you run a diversion," she said to Kanja eventually, watching a boy no
older than fourteen correct an older apprentice's grip without either of them treating the correction
as anything but ordinary. "Nobody's pretending to know more than they do. Nobody's pretending not to
be scared of the fire, either. It just doesn't stop anybody's hands."

"That's most of it," Kanja said. "The rest is just showing up again the next day."

She hadn't come for a lesson in smithing, and didn't ask for one. What she'd actually come to see, she
admitted on the second evening, sitting apart from the forge floor's noise with a cup of something
Garren Hask had brewed too strong on purpose, was whether a thing built to outlast destruction looked
any different from the inside than it looked from a decoy line watching it survive from a distance.
"I lost my partner at the Black Trench," she said, plainly, the way she said everything. "Everyone
kept telling me the pair system held because I was strong enough to take it. I never believed that.
I think it held because it was built to hold, the same way this is."

Kanja didn't offer her anything softer than that. "The method doesn't need me standing in it to keep
working. I've seen that proven a dozen times over on this tour alone. Maybe that's the whole of what
you came to check."

"Maybe," Efa Gol said. "Or maybe I just wanted to watch something get rebuilt on purpose, for once,
instead of after the fact."

She left on the third morning the way she'd arrived, without ceremony, taking nothing back to her own
crew but the plain confirmation that the thing she'd guarded from a distance for two years actually
looked, up close, exactly like what its reputation claimed. Garren Hask logged the visit in three
words, the only entry of its kind in the whole campaign ledger: *she came. Understood.*
