# The Truce They Wouldn't Honor

*Locked canon, Batch 250, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1087`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LXII, wave 21. A detailed full-Trinity combat showcase defending the fragile mutual-berth truce between two rival weather traditions from a holdout faction that rejects it -- the first Storm That Walks entry where the Trinity protects an agreement between two fleets rather than one fleet, vessel, rescue, or institution. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The truce had held for two seasons: Kanja's fleet and the rival squadron giving each other's ships a wide
berth around dangerous weather, each trusting the other's reading enough to not need to see it proven.
Not every captain in the rival squadron had voted for that trust. Four ships hadn't, and on the night of a
joint calibration drill -- both fleets holding position in the same strait to compare readings against the
same incoming system, an act of good faith neither side had ever risked before -- those four broke
formation and came in dark, meaning to prove the truce had always been a trap by springing one.

Kanja was already moving before the first ship cleared its own line, because the successor's reading had
flagged the holdouts' engine-heat signature against the storm's own thermal gradient an hour earlier, and
because protecting this drill meant protecting both fleets equally or it meant nothing at all.

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System took the first holdout's ramming run head-on and returned it doubled
through the attacking hull's own keel, not toward the rival flagship it had been aimed at but straight down
into open water -- a deliberate redirection, costing Kanja more control than simply absorbing the blow
would have, because a wrecked holdout drifting into the rival squadron's own anchored ships would have
looked, to anyone watching from either fleet, exactly like the fight the holdouts wanted to start. Onyx's
Cadence Ruin read the storm's own gust pattern and timed three strikes into the second holdout's rigging
during the half-second lulls when neither fleet's watch-crews could mistake the sound for weather,
disabling it without a single crew member harmed. Whisper of Shadows carried Kanja across open water
between lightning strikes to board the third holdout directly, Soulbound Edge answering not with force but
with precision -- three cuts through control lines, no blood, the ship dead in the water and its crew left
very much alive to explain themselves to their own commander. Obsidian Malice took the fourth, a full
discharge into the sea ahead of its bow that didn't touch the hull at all, only threw up a wall of displaced
water no captain sane enough to still be arguing about truces would sail through.

Four ships disabled. No one from either fleet dead. The rival squadron's own commander reached the holdouts
before dawn and took them into her own custody, in full view of Kanja's fleet, a public answer to a public
provocation.

"You could have let us handle our own," she said to him afterward, not quite a complaint.

"I could have," Kanja said. "But then it would have looked like your fleet attacked mine, or mine attacked
yours, and the only people who'd have believed the truth after that would have been the four men who
already knew it." He nodded at the disabled ships. "This way, everyone in both fleets saw the same thing at
the same time."

She considered that, and didn't argue it.
