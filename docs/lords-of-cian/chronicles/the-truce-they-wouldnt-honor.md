# The Truce They Wouldn't Honor

*Locked canon, Batch 250, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1087`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LXII, wave 21. A detailed Long Mask-era gear combat showcase defending the fragile mutual-berth truce between two rival weather traditions from a holdout faction that rejects it -- the first Storm That Walks entry where Kanja protects an agreement between two fleets rather than one fleet, vessel, rescue, or institution. Not a territory Annals entry. Corrected Batch 321, 2026-10-02: an earlier draft staged this as a full-Trinity showcase, despite this Chronicle's placement well within the Long Mask era of just over 283 years, after Kanja's already-locked age-30 surrender of the Trinity to its sealed vault (`MCD-246`); replaced with his correct Long-Mask-era kit (`ARS-344` through `ARS-356`, plus the Rexmar Machete and his own instinctive Rexmar-Mar tactical sense). Also corrected a tech anachronism -- this world has no engines beyond the Hymn-Engine/Meridian Engine -- "engine-heat signature" reworded to "galley-fire signature."*

---

The truce had held for two seasons: Kanja's fleet and the rival squadron giving each other's ships a wide
berth around dangerous weather, each trusting the other's reading enough to not need to see it proven.
Not every captain in the rival squadron had voted for that trust. Four ships hadn't, and on the night of a
joint calibration drill -- both fleets holding position in the same strait to compare readings against the
same incoming system, an act of good faith neither side had ever risked before -- those four broke
formation and came in dark, meaning to prove the truce had always been a trap by springing one.

Kanja was already moving before the first ship cleared its own line, because the successor's reading had
flagged the holdouts' galley-fire signature against the storm's own thermal gradient an hour earlier, and
because protecting this drill meant protecting both fleets equally or it meant nothing at all.

The Forge-Coat and Ironfall Boots took the first holdout's ramming run head-on and ground it sideways,
redirecting the shock doubled through the attacking hull's own keel, not toward the rival flagship it
had been aimed at but straight down into open water -- a deliberate redirection, costing Kanja more
control than simply absorbing the blow would have, because a wrecked holdout drifting into the rival
squadron's own anchored ships would have looked, to anyone watching from either fleet, exactly like the
fight the holdouts wanted to start. His own instinctive Rexmar-Mar tactical sense read the storm's own
gust pattern and timed three strikes of the Rexmar Machete into the second holdout's rigging during the
half-second lulls when neither fleet's watch-crews could mistake the sound for weather, disabling it
without a single crew member harmed. The Smoke System carried Kanja across open water between
lightning strikes to board the third holdout directly, the Rexmar Machete answering not with force but
with precision -- three cuts through control lines, no blood, the ship dead in the water and its crew
left very much alive to explain themselves to their own commander. The Ironhand Gauntlets took the
fourth, one controlled blow into the sea ahead of its bow that didn't touch the hull at all, only threw
up a wall of displaced water no captain sane enough to still be arguing about truces would sail through.

Four ships disabled. No one from either fleet dead. The rival squadron's own commander reached the holdouts
before dawn and took them into her own custody, in full view of Kanja's fleet, a public answer to a public
provocation.

"You could have let us handle our own," she said to him afterward, not quite a complaint.

"I could have," Kanja said. "But then it would have looked like your fleet attacked mine, or mine attacked
yours, and the only people who'd have believed the truth after that would have been the four men who
already knew it." He nodded at the disabled ships. "This way, everyone in both fleets saw the same thing at
the same time."

She considered that, and didn't argue it.
