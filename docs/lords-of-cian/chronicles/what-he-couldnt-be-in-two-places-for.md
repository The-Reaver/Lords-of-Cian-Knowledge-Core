# What He Couldn't Be in Two Places For

*Locked canon, Batch 215, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-938`). Bane Alias Chronicle LIV, wave 18. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Both messages reached the column within the same hour, carried by riders who'd pushed their
animals the whole way and had nothing left to add beyond what was already written down. At
Corvain's Rest, upriver, someone had fouled the settlement's one well with something that turned
the water and had already taken two children sick. Half a day's ride the other direction, a column
of some sixty refugees moving toward the coast had been spotted by a Directorate patrol and would
be intercepted well before nightfall unless someone reached them first.

There was no version of the map that let him reach both.

Bane split the column in the time it took to read the second message twice. Maret Vos would take
thirty riders for the refugee column — fast, light, nothing that would slow them reaching the
interception point in time. He would take the rest toward Corvain's Rest, because a poisoned well
was a problem he understood how to solve with his own hands and Vos had led enough engagements now
to run an interception without him standing over Vos doing it.

"You could come with me instead," Vos said, meaning it as a real offer, not a challenge. "The well
doesn't need a legend behind it. Dirty water is dirty water no matter who's standing next to it."

"It needs someone who knows what he's looking at faster than I'll learn it on the ride." He was
already checking the satchel of what little the column carried for exactly this. "The refugees need
someone who won't hesitate before committing thirty riders against however many that patrol turns
out to be. That's you. I've watched you not hesitate."

He reached Corvain's Rest by midafternoon and traced the contamination to an upstream tannery pit
that had been quietly emptying into the water table for a season, unrelated to any war at all — an
ordinary, ugly accident rather than sabotage, fixed by the unglamorous work of diverting the pit and
digging a second well while the sick children were tended by whatever the settlement's own healer
knew. Vos reached the refugee column forty minutes before the patrol did, and the interception
never happened.

Both went right. Bane didn't let himself call it a victory the same size as the ones where
everything had depended on him alone.

"We could have lost either one," he told Vos that night, when the riders had rejoined. "We didn't,
and I'm not going to pretend that was certain going in. Next time the numbers might not favor us
twice in the same hour."

*Continuity notes (not narrative): first entry to place Bane in a genuine dual-crisis dilemma
forcing him to personally choose one site over the other and trust a second command entirely to
someone else at the same time — distinct from "The Column He Didn't Lead" (a single delegation test
with no competing crisis of his own) and from the wildfire-versus-ambush choice in "The Fire He
Chose Over the Ambush" (one crisis abandoned outright, not run in parallel by a trusted second).
Both outcomes succeed here, deliberately, with the entry's real weight carried by Bane's own
acknowledgment that the numbers might not always favor a split this cleanly. No new named
characters beyond the already-locked Maret Vos. Closing entry, eighteenth wave (with "The Crossing
They Almost Didn't Make" and "What Stays After the First One").*
