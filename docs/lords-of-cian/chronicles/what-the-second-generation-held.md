# What the Second Generation Held

*Locked canon, Batch 275, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1421`). Captain Alias Chronicle XCI, wave 31. A detailed full-Trinity combat showcase where the crew's own children take an active operational role for the first time, not just a voice at the dispute council or a name cut into the memorial wall.*

---

The grain depot at the north end of the settlement wasn't Pier Nine's business by any charter clause anyone could point to, but three families who'd once sheltered under the crew's own protection during the lean winter (`MCD-600`) had built their new lives around it, and word of an opportunist raiding crew working the rebuilt coast came to Efa Gol the same way most things came to Efa Gol — through a discrepancy in a manifest that shouldn't have existed. She brought it to Kanja without ceremony. He brought it to the crew the same way.

Mira came without being asked twice. Twenty now, steady in a way that had nothing left in it of the girl who'd once stood silent at a council deadlock, she'd spent the years since carving Joran's name into the seawall (`MCD-1372`) learning the depot district's own bones — which pilings had been driven true and which had been rushed, which roof trusses were load-bearing and which were decoration left over from a hastier age. Nobody had assigned her that knowledge. She'd simply gone looking for something useful to do with a grief that had never fully found its shape, and construction was where she'd landed.

The raiders came at first light, eleven of them, counting on the depot's isolation and the settlement's thin watch. Corren Halst read the approach from the water within the first minute and had the evacuation lanes called before the first boat touched the dock. Efa Gol cut the seaward retreat the way she always did. Pell Ostra's capstan-line net went up across the loading channel without a spark, the way it had a dozen times before.

It was Mira who caught what none of them had — a groan in the depot's main roof truss, half a second before it would have mattered, the same rushed joinery she'd catalogued weeks earlier under a district engineer's indifferent nose. She didn't have Trinity gear or Mafesto's own senses. She had a trained eye and the nerve to shout across a battlefield at the one person who could do something about it in time.

Kanja heard her. Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System caught the collapsing truss mid-fall and redirected the whole weight of it sideways into an empty loading bay instead of the sheltering families beneath it, the impact traveling out through the pilings Mira herself had already flagged as sound enough to take it. Obsidian Malice discharged once, low and controlled, scattering the boarding line without touching the building they'd all just fought to keep standing. Onyx read the raiders' footing on wet planking the way it always had, landing every strike to disarm rather than kill — eleven men taken alive in under nine minutes, the depot untouched but for the one beam that had needed to come down anyway.

Efa Gol found Mira afterward, hands still shaking, checking the truss work a second time out of pure habit rather than doubt. "First time one of you actually did something out there instead of just being safe because we did," Efa Gol said, not quite managing to keep the pride out of her voice.

Mira looked at the wall in the distance, the one with her father's name on it, and didn't answer right away. "Felt different," she finally said. "Being the one who saw it coming, instead of the one who has to live with what happens after."
