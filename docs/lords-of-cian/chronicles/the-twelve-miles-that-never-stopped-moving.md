# The Twelve Miles That Never Stopped Moving

*Locked canon, Batch 241, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1060`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXII, wave 21. A detailed
full-Trinity combat showcase built around continuous motion rather than a held position -- a running
defense of a refugee convoy across twelve miles of changing terrain, never once stopping to fight from
fixed ground. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The column couldn't stop. That was the entire problem the Directorate cavalry screen understood
before Bane did — six hundred refugees on foot, a wounded rearguard, and a pursuing force that only
needed to keep pace and wait for exhaustion to do what a direct assault couldn't. Standing and fighting
would save the convoy the pursuit. It would also cost them the two remaining days of daylight they
needed to reach the border crossing before its garrison changed hands. So Bane made the only call that
kept both: they would fight the whole twelve miles without ever slowing the column down.

It meant asking the Trinity to do something none of its three pieces had been built to do continuously
— fight on the move, at pace, across ground that changed under them three times before noon.

Through the first four miles of open grassland, Mafesto carried the weight. Every cavalry charge that
came in from the flanks met Bane mid-stride, the Kinetic Transfer System pulling their momentum
sideways and out through his own footing rather than absorbing it to a stop — a rider's full gallop
redirected into the ground at an angle that dropped the horse without killing it and sent the rider
tumbling clear of the column's own line of march. He never planted his feet to do it. The column never
slowed.

The grassland gave way to a stand of close pines where cavalry couldn't follow, and the Directorate
infantry that had been shadowing the column on foot closed the gap instead, trying to bottleneck the
refugees against the treeline. This was Onyx's ground: Cadence Ruin folded the lead infantry squad's
coordination apart before they reached striking distance, and Veil Piercer picked the two disguised
scouts out of a knot of what looked like fleeing civilians before either could signal the column's
exact position back to the main force. Neither ability stopped him walking. He read the trees and the
men in them at the same pace he was already moving through both.

The last three miles ran along an exposed causeway with no cover on either side, and the pursuing
commander finally committed everything he had, betting a single concentrated push would force the
column to stop and form a defensive square or be run down in the open. Obsidian Malice answered it —
two years of dormant charge released in one arcing discharge along the causeway's full length,
timed to the second the main assault line committed past the point of easy retreat. It didn't need
Bane to stop and aim it. He released it mid-stride, already three steps further down the causeway
before the charge finished landing.

They crossed into the border zone an hour before dusk, the column's pace never once dropping below a
walk in twelve miles, and the pursuing force scattered rather than regrouped for a final push it no
longer had the numbers or the daylight to make.

"You fought the whole way and never planted your feet once," Efa Gol said, counting the last of the
refugees across the line.

"Stopping to fight it properly would have won the battle and lost the crossing." Bane didn't slow down
even then, already moving to the head of the column. "This wasn't a fight. It was twelve miles we had
to survive without admitting that's what we were doing."
