# What Stood While It Was Falling

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XXVI, second
entry in the ninth wave. Rebellion era, age 20, the Sewer War of Killane. Not a territory Chronicle.
Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The ambush hit the same moment the overloaded gallery finally began to give — a coordinated strike
that hadn't planned the timing but benefited from it anyway, twenty soldiers pressing the crew from
both tunnel mouths while the ceiling itself groaned toward collapse over their heads.

Kanja fought and shored in the same motion.

Mafesto's plating absorbed the opening volley while his free hand drove a brace into the widening
crack overhead, the Kinetic Transfer System converting an incoming strike's force into exactly the
downward pressure the brace needed to seat properly — violence and engineering drawing from the
same well, neither slowing the other down. Obsidian Malice discharged into the tunnel's near mouth,
scattering the advance there, and in the five-second recharge window that followed, Onyx held the
line alone while Kanja's other hand set a second brace, checking its angle by feel in the middle of
a fight neither task had the luxury of pausing for.

"The roof or the soldiers," a crew member shouted, unsure which crisis he was meant to be helping
with.

"Both. Same problem." Kanja drove a third brace home between two blocked strikes, Cadence Ruin
handling what his hands couldn't. "The roof's not going to wait for the fight to end, and the fight
won't wait for the roof. Hold the line where I tell you, brace where I tell you, and trust that I
can read both at once."

The ceiling held on four hastily seated braces rather than the six a careful job would have used,
imperfect but sufficient, and the ambush broke within minutes against a defense that never gave the
soldiers an opening the collapsing gallery might otherwise have handed them for free.

"I've never seen anyone fight and build in the same breath like that," the crew chief said
afterward, checking the braces' work with visible disbelief that they'd held at all.

"It's not really two things. Reading a collapsing structure and reading an attacking line use the
same eye — where's the weight going, where's the pressure building, where does it give if I don't
act in the next second." Kanja tested the nearest brace with his own weight, satisfied. "I spent a
war learning to read one. It turns out the other was never as different as it looked."
