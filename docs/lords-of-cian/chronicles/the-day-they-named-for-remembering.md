# The Day They Named for Remembering

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Captain Alias Chronicle, wave 11. Rebellion era, the
first anniversary of the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose. No new named characters beyond already-locked crew.*

---

Nobody had planned it as a tradition. The first year after the Black Trench, someone — nobody could
later agree who first suggested it — proposed that the crew mark the date rather than let it pass
like any other day, and by the time Kanja heard the idea it had already taken enough shape that
refusing it would have meant refusing something the crew had decided mattered to them.

"We're not celebrating a battle," Callum Breck said, when asked what the day was actually for. He
still spoke carefully, years after his own recovered voice, choosing words the way a man chooses
footing on uncertain ground. "We're remembering the people who didn't leave it with us. Nev Torr.
Everyone else the Trench took. A day where we say their names out loud on purpose, instead of only
when something reminds us."

They kept it simple the first year: names read aloud by whoever had known each person best, food
shared on deck rather than belowdecks, no speeches beyond what people chose to say. Kanja read three
names himself — recruits from before the crew had earned any name at all, people who'd died before
"Captain" had ever been spoken over them.

"You don't have to read any," Corren Halst told him quietly, watching him prepare the short list.

"I want to. I was there for all three. If I don't say their names, who else remembers the specific
weight of it the way I do?"

By the fifth year, the observance had grown without anyone deciding it should — settlements the crew
had helped began sending their own remembrances, former crew who'd moved on returned for the day when
they could, and the reading of names took the better part of an hour rather than minutes. Garren Hask
began keeping a formal record of it in its own ledger, separate from operational logs, specifically
so the list would never be allowed to shrink through forgetting.

"It's becoming something bigger than we meant it to be," Efa Gol observed, watching the crowd on the
fifth anniversary.

"Maybe. Or maybe it was always going to be this size, once it started, because grief this real
doesn't stay small on its own. I'd rather it grow honestly than stay small out of habit."
