# What Efa Gol Asked Him to Listen To

*Locked canon, Batch 237, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1049`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LX, wave 20. A warm, wholly domestic closer: Efa Gol asks Kanja to read a rigger's pulley that belonged to her dead pair-partner, no enemy or operation involved. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

Efa Gol found him between engagements, off duty in every sense that mattered, and set a small rusted
pulley wheel on the table between them without explaining what it was first.

"Tam's," she said, once he'd looked at it long enough to be waiting on her. "Dock rigger's pulley. He
carried it strapped to his belt from before I knew him until the Black Trench. I've kept it since. It
still turns. I want to know if it's lying to me about that."

Kanja had read siege towers, blockade rigging, a mountain crossing's cable anchors. He had never once
been asked to read something this small for a reason that had nothing to do with whether it would hold
against an enemy.

"You could have any smith in the fleet look at a pulley," he said, not refusing, just naming the choice
she'd made.

"A smith would tell me if it still works. I'm not asking if it still works." She didn't elaborate
further, and he didn't make her.

He turned it over once, the metal worn smooth in places a rigger's hands would have gripped it
thousands of times, and put two fingers against the housing the way he'd put a whole palm against
towers and hulls and a foundry wall that had asked nothing of him at all. The resonance came back small
and slow, the honest signature of a well-made tool that had done exactly the work it was built for, for
exactly as long as it had a hand to do it in — no hidden crack, no failing seam, nothing dishonest in it
at all. Just years, settled into the metal the unhurried way years settle into anything that's earned
them.

"It's not lying to you," he said. "It's tired the way anything is tired after doing its job for as long
as this one has. But there's nothing failing in it. Whatever's holding, it'll keep holding a good while
yet."

Efa Gol picked it back up, turned it once in her own hand the way he had in his, and said nothing for a
moment long enough that he let it stand.

"He'd have laughed," she said eventually, "knowing the Iron Bastard put his whole doctrine to work on a
dock pulley. He always said I overbuilt everything I cared about." She pocketed it. "Thank you for not
asking why I needed to know."

"I've read a hundred things this month for a hundred reasons," Kanja said. "This was the first one that
was actually about somebody, and not about what somebody was trying to do to somebody else. I'm glad it
held."

She was already walking off before he finished the sentence, the pulley back where it had ridden for
years, no worse for having been listened to.
