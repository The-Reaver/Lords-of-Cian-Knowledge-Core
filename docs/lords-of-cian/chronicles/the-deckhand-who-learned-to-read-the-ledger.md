# The Deckhand Who Learned to Read the Ledger

*Locked canon, Batch 222, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1001`). Captain Alias Chronicle LIV, wave 18. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The deckhand was seventeen and had been aboard eight months before anyone realized she couldn't read
— she'd covered it the way plenty of dockworkers covered it, by memorizing the shapes of common
words, by asking a shipmate to "just read that one out, my eyes are tired," by volunteering for tasks
that never required it. It was Garren Hask who finally noticed, watching her hold a supply manifest
upside down for a full minute before quietly setting it aside, and it was Hask who brought it to
Kanja rather than embarrassing her by raising it himself.

"She's sharp," Hask said. "Sharper than half the hands who can read circles around her. Nobody ever
sat her down and taught her, and she's too proud to ask."

"Then somebody should ask her, not wait for her to admit it."

He did it himself, that evening, and didn't dress it up as anything but what it was. "I hear you're
better with rope and rigging than half this crew. I also hear nobody ever taught you your letters."

Her jaw set. "I manage."

"I'm not saying you don't. I'm saying you shouldn't have to spend energy managing something you don't
need to manage at all. I'll teach you, if you want it. Evenings, whenever we're not running. No one
else has to know until you want them to."

She agreed, mostly, she admitted later, because she was too tired of pretending to say no. They used
Hask's old ledgers for the lessons — worn pages of prize-tallies and supply counts going back years,
because Kanja reasoned a sailor ought to learn her letters on words that meant something to her life
rather than borrowed primers written for children in a Trust schoolhouse she'd never see the inside
of. Within a season she could read a manifest without turning it over twice. Within a year she was
correcting Hask's own arithmetic, on the rare occasion his tally ran short, and taking evident pride
in catching him at it.

"You didn't have to do this yourself," Hask told him once, watching the lesson from across the deck.
"I could have taught her. Or Halst. Half this crew can read."

"I know. But I asked her first, and she said yes to me before she'd have said yes to anyone else, and
I wasn't going to hand that off just because I had better things to do. There aren't many things I get
to give this crew that don't cost something. This one didn't cost anything but time, and I had it."

She never did tell the rest of the crew how it started, letting most of them assume she'd simply
always known. Only Hask, and Kanja, and eventually her own apprentice years later — taught the same
way, on the same worn ledgers — ever knew the truth of where it began.
