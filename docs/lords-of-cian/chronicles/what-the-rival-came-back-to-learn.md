# What the Rival Came Back to Learn

*Locked canon, Batch 192, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-566`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XXI, wave 7 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The former director of the Trust's weather service arrived at Sephtis's tent unannounced, without
rank, without the staff he'd once commanded, carrying nothing but two years of his own service's
recorded observations and a question he hadn't been able to put down since his resignation.

"I don't want the trick," he said, before Sephtis had finished setting down what he'd been doing.
"I know now it isn't one. I want to know why my instruments, my staff, my method — thirty years of
institutional weather science — couldn't do what you do with a folding table and a set of charts
half my service would call primitive."

"They're not primitive. They're just not looking at the same things yours are trained to look at."
Sephtis gestured him to sit. "Your service measures what the sky is doing right now, as precisely as
it can. I spend most of my time on what the sky was doing three days ago, over water your instruments
never reach, and what that tells me about what it's going to be doing here in eleven days. It's not
a better method. It's a different question."

"Teach me the question, then."

It took him four months to unlearn enough of his old training to properly hold the new question, and
longer than that to stop reflexively reaching for instrument readings before he'd finished reasoning
through what the pattern itself was telling him. Sephtis never once mentioned the wager, or the public
resignation, or the two years the man had spent insisting the method didn't exist. What he did do,
eventually, was hand him a forecast to make on his own, unsupervised, and let the man's own results
be the only correction offered.

"You were wrong to call me a liar," Sephtis said, the day the man's own first independent prediction
came in accurate to within the hour. "You weren't wrong to want proof before you believed something
that inconvenient. That's not the same failure. Most people never learn the difference."
