# The River Two Villages Wouldn't Share

*Locked canon, Batch 284, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1498`). Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XCVI, wave 32,
closing the wave. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241). Not a territory
Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The two settlements sat within sight of each other across a single shared river, and had not
traded a civil word across it in longer than either could agree on, over a diversion channel dug two
generations back that gave the upstream village first claim on the water and left the downstream one
with whatever ran past afterward. Both wanted the campaign's forge. Neither would let the crew build
it on the other's bank, and neither would let workers cross the river to reach it if it went up on
theirs.

Kanja had settled land-title disputes before by refusing to rule at all, handing the question back
to a district's own elders and standing aside from the outcome. This one, he judged within a day of
listening to both sides repeat the same two-generations-old grievance, wouldn't be settled that way
— the elders on each side were the grievance, inherited and re-argued so many times that neither
side remembered wanting anything but to be the one who didn't lose.

He didn't take a side, and he didn't defer to one either. He offered the one thing both villages
wanted more than the argument: a forge built on neither bank, floated on a moored platform at the
river's own center, drawing water and ore from both sides equally and reachable by a footbridge from
each — access conditioned, publicly and permanently, on the diversion channel being reopened to
split flow evenly between them. Take the forge, share the water. Keep the grievance, and the forge
went to a third settlement waiting two days downriver with no complaint about sharing anything.

The upstream village's headman objected first, loudest, and longest — until his own smiths pointed
out that half a reliable flow fed by a working forge outperformed the full flow he'd been defending
for a generation on principle alone. The channel reopened within the month, tended jointly for the
first time since it was dug, watched over from a platform that belonged, on paper and by design, to
neither side.

"You didn't fix what they've been angry about," Garren Hask observed afterward, walking the new
footbridge's rail. "You just made the anger cost more than giving it up did."

"That's most of what a fair trade ever does," Kanja said. "I didn't come here to tell two villages
to stop hating each other over a ditch. I came to build a forge. It just turned out the only place I
could build it was standing exactly between them."
