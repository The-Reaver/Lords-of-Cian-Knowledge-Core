# What the River Barge Couldn't Outrun

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LIII, wave 18. Rolling Foundry Campaign, age 27. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The season's ore ran downriver on flat-bottomed barges through a stretch of white water no larger
vessel could take, three days' haul that couldn't be routed any other way without adding two weeks
to the campaign's schedule. A Directorate strike team knew the stretch as well as the campaign did,
and had chosen the tightest bend in the rapids to stage their ambush, where the current itself would
do half their work for them.

Kanja rode the lead barge rather than the bank, Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System adjusting stride
by stride to a footing that pitched and dropped with every surge of white water under the hull —
absorbing the barge's own violent motion into stored charge the same way it absorbed a blow,
turning the river's chaos into fuel rather than fighting to stand still against it.

Obsidian Malice discharged downstream rather than across, a channeled strike that used the current's
own push to carry its force further into the strike team's staged position on the bend than any dry-
ground discharge could have thrown it. Onyx tracked the fight by the water's own rhythm as much as
by sight, Cadence Ruin finding the pattern in six attackers working from anchored positions the
rapids never let them abandon for better footing, the way the barge's own crew could.

The strike broke against a target that had turned the rapids into an ally rather than an obstacle
to survive, the barges running the bend's own dangerous water straight through the ambush and out
the far side without slowing, ore intact, not one barge holed below the waterline.

"You fought that whole engagement without ever finding solid ground," the barge's steersman said,
still gripping the tiller hard enough to whiten his knuckles, once the current eased past the bend.
"I've run this stretch forty times and I still don't trust my footing on it."

"Neither do I. Didn't need to, this once." Kanja watched the bend recede behind them, the strike
team's staged position already lost to the current's own pull. "They picked ground that fights
everyone equally. Difference was, I stopped trying to beat the water and just let it carry the work
instead. Rapids don't care whose side you're on. They'll move whatever you hand them, hard as you
hand it."
