# The Weight Maret Vos Chose to Carry

*Locked canon, Batch 241, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1061`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXIII, wave 21. Closes wave
21. A quieter, reflective closer giving Maret Vos his own dedicated perspective within Bane's own
run for the first time, payoff to wave 18's "What He Couldn't Be in Two Places For." Not a territory
Chronicle.*

---

It had been three weeks since Bane split the column and handed him the interception alone, and Maret
Vos still hadn't told anyone how close he'd come to saying no.

Not because he doubted he could run it. He'd run the numbers on the ambush the same way he'd once run
the load calculations on a half-collapsed crane before he trusted his weight to it, and the numbers had
held. What he hadn't said out loud, not even to Corren Halst or Danne Sok, was that for the better part
of the hour it took to close on the ambush site, he'd kept waiting for Bane to appear anyway — to have
misjudged the poisoned well as the smaller crisis and come to take the interception back — and the
waiting had cost him nothing, because Bane never came, and the interception ran exactly as planned.

He found Bane alone at the column's edge, checking hobbles on the pack animals, and said the thing
plainly rather than let it sit any longer.

"I kept waiting for you to show up and run it yourself."

"I know." Bane didn't look up from the hobble he was checking. "I could see it in how you set the
ambush — closer to the road than you needed to be, like you wanted room for someone else to arrive and
fix it if you got it wrong."

"You could have told me that before I left."

"Would it have changed anything?"

Maret Vos thought about it honestly, the way he thought about a beam before he trusted weight to it.
"No. I'd have run it the same way either way. I just would have known you already saw it."

"Then I told you what mattered and saved the rest." Bane finished with the hobbles and straightened.
"You didn't need me to fix your plan, Maret. You needed to find out you didn't need me to. Those
aren't the same thing, and only one of them was mine to give you."

It was the plainest thing anyone had said to him about that night, plainer than the interception's
own clean success, and it was the part he carried back to his own tent afterward — not the ambush
itself, which had gone exactly as planned, but the specific, unfamiliar weight of having run something
that mattered and found, three weeks later, that he'd trust himself to run the next one without asking
whether Bane was watching at all.
