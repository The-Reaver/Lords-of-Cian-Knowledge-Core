# The Bridge That Wasn't There

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Crow King Alias Chronicle XVI, wave 6, first

---

The Kelder Ford was the only crossing for two days' march in either direction, which was exactly
why the Directorate garrison assigned to hold it had stopped worrying about deception entirely.
Water carried sound differently than marsh grass or open scrub, its own engineers had assured the
post commander — no chant could hide in a current, no false signature could survive the constant,
genuine roar of moving water loud enough to drown out anything short of cannon fire.

They had built their confidence on the wrong half of the problem. The river didn't need to hide a
sound. It needed to carry one.

Kanja's people spent three nights learning exactly how the ford's own rapids folded and scattered
noise across the water's surface, then used that same folding to throw a chant from the western
bank so that it arrived at the garrison's listening post sounding, unmistakably, like it originated
from the eastern one — a full company massing on the wrong side of a river that offered no way to
verify the claim except by sending scouts across it themselves.

"Send the patrol east," the post commander ordered, watching his own sentries strain to place a
sound that seemed to come from everywhere and nowhere across the water at once. "If there's a
company massing over there, I want eyes on it before dawn."

The patrol crossed. The ford, for the first time in weeks, sat undefended on its western approach
for the four hours it took Kanja's actual column — the one the false signal had never claimed
existed at all — to cross clean, upstream of the ford itself, through water the garrison had judged
too shallow and too loud to matter.

"You didn't fool the water," the crew's own quartermaster observed afterward, studying the current
that had done exactly what its own engineers promised it would. "You just let it lie for you."

"It was never going to lie for me on its own. It just carries whatever's put into it honestly,
which is its own kind of trustworthy — until you understand exactly how it folds a sound before it
arrives. Then honesty becomes the whole trick." Kanja watched the last of the column clear the
water. "Every terrain has a version of this. The ford wasn't a wall. It was a messenger nobody
thought to question."
