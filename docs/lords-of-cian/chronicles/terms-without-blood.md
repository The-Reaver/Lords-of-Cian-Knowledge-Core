# Terms Without Blood

*Locked canon, Batch 199, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-777`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle XXII, wave 8 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The privateer coalition had held the western shoals for longer than the Trust had bothered to contest
them — a loose alliance of captains who answered to no crown and raided anyone unlucky enough to sail
those waters unescorted, ghost fleet included, at least according to the one previous encounter both
sides remembered differently.

Their delegation came aboard *The Audit* under a truce banner none of them fully trusted would hold,
led by a spokesman who kept one hand near his own blade the entire negotiation.

"Last time our ships crossed paths, you sank two of ours," he opened. "We're not here to pretend that
didn't happen."

"Your ships were raiding a relief convoy," Kanja said. "I'd sink them again under the same
circumstances. But that's not why you're here, or you'd have brought more than a truce flag."

The spokesman's jaw worked for a moment before he said what he'd actually come to say. "The Trust's
started paying bounties on any hull flying no flag they recognize. Doesn't matter if we're raiding
them or not — unaffiliated ships are targets now, ours included. We'd rather not be your enemy while
that's true."

What followed took the better part of an afternoon and produced nothing that resembled a treaty in the
formal Trust sense — no seals, no signatures, nothing either side could later point to as binding paper
— just a spoken understanding, witnessed by both crews standing on deck rather than hidden below in
private cabins: the coalition's ships would leave rebellion-aligned convoys unmolested, and in exchange
the ghost fleet would neither hunt them nor report their positions to anyone who might.

"No paper," the spokesman said, visibly uneasy with the absence of anything he could hold onto. "How
do either of us trust that?"

"Paper's easy to break when nobody's watching you break it. A spoken term, witnessed by two full
crews who'll remember exactly what was said — that's harder to walk back without every sailor on both
sides knowing you did it." Kanja extended a hand, empty, no weapon anywhere near it. "I'd rather have
your word, kept because you know we're watching, than your signature on a document neither of us
actually respects."

The spokesman took the hand. The coalition kept the term for the rest of the war, not because any
document compelled them to, but because two full crews had stood on that deck and watched the terms
get spoken aloud, and neither side wanted to be the one who'd been seen breaking a promise in front of
witnesses who'd remember.
