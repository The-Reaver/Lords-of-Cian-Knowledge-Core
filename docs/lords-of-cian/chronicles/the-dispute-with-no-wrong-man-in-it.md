# The Dispute With No Wrong Man In It

*Locked canon, Batch 255, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1124`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle LXVII, wave 23.
Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. The first labor dispute the tally method finds a true number for and
still cannot resolve, because no one involved has done anything wrong.*

---

The owner was a widow named nothing in Garren Hask's records but "the rope-walk on the third
canal," and she came to Warehouse Twelve before her workers ever did, which had not happened before.

"I know what you do here," she said. "Check the figures. Tell an owner what he actually owes and
make him pay it. Go ahead. Check mine."

Hask checked them for three days, cross-witnessed against her own workers' independent accounts, the
way the method demanded. The figures matched to the coin. Her twelve rope-walkers were owed, by the
district's own standard wage, considerably more than she had ever paid them — and she had never once
falsified a ledger, never skimmed a shortfall into her own pocket, never done anything the tally
method existed to catch. The rope-walk's income, after the cordage market's price had collapsed two
seasons running under a Trust tariff neither she nor her workers had any power over, simply did not
contain enough coin to pay the wage the work was genuinely worth. She had been paying what she had,
every week, honestly, and it had never once been enough.

"There's no theft here," Hask told Kanja privately, laying both sets of figures side by side. "There's
just not enough money. I don't have a method for that."

Kanja sat with the rope-walk's twelve workers and their honest owner in the same room, an
arrangement the tally table had never needed before, because there had never before been a dispute
where every party in it was telling the truth. He didn't invent a number. There wasn't one to invent
— no owner's hidden margin to expose, no theft to correct, no fraud to hold anyone accountable for.
What the tariff had taken, the district's own resources genuinely could not replace.

"I can't make this right," he told them plainly, the first time he had said those specific words at
that table. "I can find you the true number. I did. It just doesn't tell you where the coin to match
it is."

What came out of the room, three hours later, wasn't a settlement so much as an agreement to carry
the shortfall together — the widow committing every coin above bare survival cost to closing the gap
as the tariff eased, the workers agreeing to a documented, visible deficit rather than a silent
unpaid one, both sides signing a figure that everyone understood would take years, not weeks, to
close. Hask entered it in the ledger anyway, in its own column, marked not as debt owed by a person
but as a number the district itself still had to answer for.

"That's not a win," Kanja said, watching the twelve workers file out no richer than they'd walked in.

"It's not a loss either," Hask said. "First time I've ever had to write that down."

*Continuity notes (not narrative): the first labor dispute in the sub-series with no owner
wrongdoing to correct and no clean resolution available -- distinct from the domestic inheritance
dispute (`MCD-945`, no owner or tally figure involved at all) and from every dispute resolved through
exposing theft, fraud, or bribery. The tally method's honest arithmetic is shown finding a true
number and still being powerless to close the gap it reveals, extending the method's established
limits (already tested by collateral, unquantifiable loss at `MCD-1062`) into ordinary economic
scarcity rather than extraordinary circumstance. No new named characters -- the widow and her twelve
rope-walkers are unnamed. First entry in the Trench Monarch's twenty-third wave.*
