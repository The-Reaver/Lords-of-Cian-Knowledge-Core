# The Ledger That Wasn't His

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LI, wave 17, closing the wave. Rolling Foundry Campaign, age 27. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The moneylender operated three districts ahead of wherever the campaign was scheduled to arrive,
which was, Ezio Valcari pointed out, exactly the point. Families burned out or simply frightened
into moving early borrowed against next season's harvest to buy tools and grain before the crew
ever reached them, at rates that turned a bad season into a debt no ordinary season could climb
back out of.

"He's not doing anything the Trust would call illegal," Ezio said, laying the collected promissory
notes out across the counting table. "Every signature's real. Every rate's within what the local
custom allows, technically. He's just fast, and he's early, and he knows we're always a little
behind the fear that runs ahead of us."

"Then we don't chase him." Kanja studied the notes, dozens of names he didn't recognize attached to
debts that would outlast the campaign by years. "We make him irrelevant before he gets there next
time."

He had the crew forge a standing offer instead of a confrontation: any district the campaign was
scheduled to reach could draw tools, seed iron, and basic hardware on the campaign's own account,
repayable in kind — finished work, apprenticed labor, surplus ore, whatever a family actually had to
give rather than coin they didn't — settled whenever it settled, forgiven outright for anyone the
war had left with nothing at all. Riders carried the offer three districts ahead of the crew's own
arrival, the same ground the moneylender had been working alone.

The moneylender came to The Anvil himself once the pattern of empty districts made itself clear,
angrier than frightened. "You've cost me most of a season's business without laying a hand on me."

"I haven't laid a hand on you. I've just given people somewhere better to go." Kanja didn't rise
from the bench he was working at. "You were never the problem I set out to solve. You were just the
only offer standing in a gap I hadn't filled yet. Fill the gap, and there's nothing left for a rate
like yours to attach itself to."

"And what happens to my business when this campaign moves on in six months and takes its generous
offer with it?"

It was the one question Kanja didn't have a clean answer for, and he didn't pretend to. "Then you'll
still be here, and whatever debts remain will still be real, and I won't have fixed the whole of it
— only bought people a season's breathing room to build something sturdier than either of us while
they had it." He set his tools down. "That's most of what this floor's ever managed against anything.
A season's breathing room, spent as well as the people getting it know how to spend it. I'm not
apologizing for it."
