# The Ghost Fleet's Own Reckoning

*Locked canon, Batch 258, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1228`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
XC, wave 30, closing the wave. A reflective ensemble closer synthesizing waves 22-30 against Garren
Hask's true-record ledger. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Garren Hask opened the second volume of the ledger to its latest page one evening at anchor, all
three flagships and the fourth, still-unnamed hull gathered together in the same sheltered water the
fleet had used a dozen times before for exactly this purpose — not strategy, not repair, just the crew
being in one place at the same time, which had grown rarer as the fleet's own reach had grown wider.

"Ninety entries now, near enough, since the first time anyone wrote 'the Sovereign Ghost of the Great
Sea' as anything more than a rumor a frightened lookout swore she'd seen," Hask said, running a thumb
down a page thick with names. "More failures in this book than I think anyone outside this crew would
expect of a legend. A fever that killed three of our own. A man who sold us out because his family was
held hostage. A body the sea never gave back. Bounty-hunters and honest sailors and desperate
captains, and not one clean, uncomplicated story among the lot of them."

"Would you rather it read clean?" Kanja asked.

"No," Hask said, without hesitation. "Clean would be a lie, and this book was never built to tell one.
What it's built to hold is what actually happened — the mercy that worked and the mercy that cost us
something real, the reputation that saved a town and the reputation that got an innocent ship fired on
by mistake, the doctrine that held under everything from an ambush built to weaponize it to a betrayal
from inside our own crew. That's not a legend. That's just an honest account of what restraint
actually costs and what it actually buys, kept by people willing to look at both."

Efa Gol, listening from the rail, added the only thing she thought the ledger had never quite managed
to say outright. "It buys people time they wouldn't have had otherwise. That's all it's ever really
been. Not fear. Not a myth. Just more time, for more people, bought one choice at a time."

Dol Maren, Pell Ostra, Danne Sok, Callum Breck — the whole long-serving core of it, gathered on one
deck under an ordinary evening sky, no battle behind them and none yet ahead, listened to that and
found nothing in it to add or correct.

Kanja took the ledger from Hask's hands, read the newest entry without altering a word of it, and
passed it back. "Keep writing it exactly like this," he said. "The truth was always the only thing
worth the name."
