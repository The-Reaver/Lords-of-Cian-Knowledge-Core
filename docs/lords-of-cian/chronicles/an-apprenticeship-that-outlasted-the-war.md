# An Apprenticeship That Outlasted the War

*Locked canon, Batch 114, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-391`). Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle III, closing the
wave. Rebellion era, during the Rolling Foundry Campaign (`MCD-241`, age 27), from the perspective
of one of the 120,000 settlement residents reached across the eighteen-month tour. Not a territory
Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters.*

---

She was fourteen when The Anvil anchored off her settlement for six days, and the thing she
remembered most clearly, decades later, was not the militia training or the free repairs the crew
worked through without ever once mentioning payment. It was the afternoon Kanja spent teaching her,
personally, how to true a bent hinge without cracking the metal.

"Most people rush the reheat," he told her, watching her hold a scavenged door hinge to the forge's
edge with more patience than most fourteen-year-olds could manage on a hot afternoon. "You want it to
be done, so you pull it out too soon, and the metal doesn't forgive you for that. It just remembers
you rushed it, and it breaks wrong later, somewhere you won't be watching when it happens."

"Is that a lesson about hinges, or about something else?"

He laughed, surprised, and didn't answer directly. "Both, probably. Everything worth doing right
punishes you eventually for doing it fast instead. Doesn't matter if it's a hinge or a rebellion or a
person. The reheat's the reheat. You either give it the time it actually needs, or you find out later
what corners you cut."

Six days was not long enough to make her a smith, and he'd never pretended it would be. What it did,
she understood only much later, was give an entire settlement — one of a hundred and twenty thousand
people reached across eighteen months aboard a single converted ore-barge — a working knowledge of
their own tools that no Trust confiscation could fully undo again, and a connection, through crew
members she'd never see again, into a Ghost-Lattice network that would carry word of a raid, a
shortage, a birth, a death, faster than any official channel the district had ever been permitted to
use.

She became her settlement's own smith eventually, self-taught past the six days' foundation, training
apprentices of her own the same patient way she'd been trained — and when she was old, decades after
the war that had made "Lord of Embers" a name people still recognized, she told her own apprentices
the same thing about the reheat, without ever fully explaining, unless they asked directly, exactly
where she'd first heard it.

"He wasn't trying to make an army out of us," she'd say. "He was trying to make sure that whatever
came next, after the fighting was over, we'd still know how to build things ourselves instead of
waiting on someone else to do it for us. The fighting stopped eventually. The building never really
did. I think that might have been the actual point the whole time."
