# What Efa Gol Never Asked Twice

*Locked canon, Batch 259, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1246`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LXXXI, wave 27,
closing the wave. Age 290, V4 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose.*

---

Efa Gol had stepped back from decoy command forty years earlier, cleanly, before her instincts could
slow enough to cost anyone a life she'd have blamed herself for the rest of hers. She was old now in
the plain, ordinary way most of the crew eventually became old — not frail exactly, but settled,
content in the specific way of someone who had already done the work that mattered to her and knew
it.

He found her on a quiet stretch of coastline she'd chosen for exactly that quiet, and told her, in
roughly the same words Efa Gol herself had once used on him without knowing whether he'd ever answer
them: that a question had been sitting unresolved in the ledger for over a hundred years, asked once
by someone else and never actually answered. Whether he regretted it. The persona, the mask, the
two-hundred-and-sixty years he'd have spent by then as someone other than the name he was born
under.

She didn't push him toward an answer the way the original question had been asked. She'd learned,
across a long life spent reading men who reported to her, exactly when a question deserved patience
instead of pressure. So she waited, and what came back, eventually, was not the clean yes or no the
ledger's unresolved line was still technically owed.

"I don't know what I'd have been without it," he said. "I know what it's cost. I know what it's
bought. I don't know how to weigh those against each other, and I've stopped trying to, because the
weighing was never going to change what still needs doing before it's finished." She didn't ask a
follow-up. She'd asked enough questions, across enough decades, to know that this one — like the
question that started it, more than a hundred years earlier — wasn't going to close any more cleanly
this time than it had the first time it was raised, and closing it clean had never really been the
point of asking.
