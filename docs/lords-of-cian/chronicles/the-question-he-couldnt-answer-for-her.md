# The Question He Couldn't Answer for Her

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Captain Alias Chronicle, wave 12. Rebellion era. Not
a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters (the crew
member in crisis is deliberately left unnamed).*

---

She'd killed a man three days earlier, in the ordinary course of an engagement that had otherwise
gone well, and something about this particular one hadn't left her the way the others had. She
stopped eating with the rest of the crew. She stopped talking beyond what her duties required. By
the fourth day, Danne Sok brought it to Kanja directly, worried enough to break his own usual
reluctance to involve anyone else in someone's private trouble.

He found her belowdecks, sitting with gear she wasn't actually cleaning.

"I don't know how to ask what's wrong without it sounding like I'm asking you to justify it," he
said, sitting down near her rather than across from her. "So I won't ask that. I'll just sit here,
if that's useful, and you can tell me whatever you want to tell me or nothing at all."

It took a long while before she spoke. "I keep waiting to feel like the others felt normal again
after. Like Corren said it would. It's been three days and I still don't."

"I can't tell you when it'll feel normal again, or whether it will at all in the same way it does
for someone else. Everyone carries this differently, and I'd be lying to you if I pretended I had an
answer that fit everyone the same." He didn't offer false comfort, didn't rush her toward resolution.
"What I can tell you is that not feeling normal about taking a life isn't a flaw in you. If anything
I'd worry more about someone who felt nothing at all."

"Does it get easier for you? After all this time?"

He considered the question honestly rather than answering it quickly. "Some of it does. Some of it
never has, and I've stopped expecting it to. I don't think easier is actually the right thing to
hope for. I think staying someone who still feels the weight of it, even when it's necessary, might
matter more than the weight going away."

She didn't have an answer to that, and he didn't push for one. He stayed a while longer, saying
little else, until she was ready to rejoin the rest of the crew on her own terms rather than because
anyone had decided it was time.
