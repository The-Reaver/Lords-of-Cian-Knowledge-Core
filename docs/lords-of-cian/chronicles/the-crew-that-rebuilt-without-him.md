# The Crew That Rebuilt Without Him

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XIX, wave 6.
Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The storm held The Anvil eleven days short of the coast it was meant to reach, pinned in a sheltered
strait while the sea outside broke against headlands too dangerous for any barge to cross. Word
reached the crew on the ninth day, carried overland by a runner who had crossed three flooded rivers
to deliver it: the settlement two weeks further down the route had been burned, a punitive strike
answering for supplies the district had quietly diverted to the campaign months earlier.

There was no way to reach them before the storm broke. Kanja calculated the earliest possible arrival
at sixteen days, minimum, even pushing the crossing harder than any prior leg of the tour.

"Then we're sixteen days too slow," Garren Hask said, reading the runner's account a second time.
"They'll have nothing left standing by the time we get there."

Kanja read it a third time himself before answering. "Maybe. Or maybe they won't wait on us at all."

The runner, when pressed, had one more thing to add: the settlement had been one of the first stops
on the tour, eight months earlier — six days of training before The Anvil moved on. Basic joinery,
basic forge discipline, the same hinge lesson, the same reheat patience, taught to whoever showed up
wanting to learn it.

Sixteen days later, when the strait finally cleared and The Anvil made the crossing at its fastest
recorded pace, Kanja found not a settlement waiting on rescue but one already three-quarters
rebuilt — support beams standing, a working forge relit from salvaged stock, and residents who had
organized themselves into work parties the same week the ash was still warm, using nothing but what
six days, eight months prior, had given them.

"We didn't know if you were coming at all," the settlement's own lead builder told him, surveying the
framework rising around them. "Didn't feel right to just sit in the ash and wait to find out."

"You weren't supposed to wait." Kanja walked the rebuild's perimeter, checking joints that needed no
correction. "I never wanted this to be a thing only I could do. I wanted it to be a thing I could
teach once and never have to do again for the people who learned it."

"Well. You got your wish. Wasn't sure you'd be glad about it."

"I'm gladder about this than almost anything else the campaign's built." He meant it, watching a
construction he'd had no hand in rise exactly the way the method intended it to. "A rebuild I have to
be personally present for isn't a method. It's just me. This—" he nodded at the standing framework
"—this is the actual thing I was trying to build the whole time."
