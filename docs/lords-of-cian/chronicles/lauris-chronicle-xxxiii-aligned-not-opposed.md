# Lauris Chronicle XXXIII: Aligned, Not Opposed

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1650`). Thirty-third entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, continuing "Strand D" into the Disillusionment sub-period in earnest. Full-scene
treatment of Operation 31 (previously only summarized at `MCD-189`): the target designated Subject
IM-099, revealed to be Kareth-Vassen Aerelin, a Cian-diaspora Kareth War-Order operative running an
autonomous 60-year campaign against the engineering tradition's trafficking networks. Dramatizes the
mutual recognition, the staged termination that closed the Directorate's contract, and the formation
of the ongoing informal intelligence alliance `MCD-189` already locks -- Lauris's first deliberate act
of working against, rather than merely doubting, the institution she served. No new named characters;
Aerelin already locked. Zero collisions.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Specialist designation LL-001-S-031: "I was sent to kill her. I have instead
agreed to help her disappear, and to report her disappearance as a death. This is the first
contract in my career I have not, in the sense the Directorate would recognize, actually completed."*

Operation 31 arrived, on its face, as one more acquisition-or-termination contract against an
independent operator: Subject IM-099, a figure the Directorate's own intelligence file described as
running organized-crime operations across several Korren-adjacent trade routes, responsible for the
disruption of at least four Directorate-affiliated trafficking arrangements over the preceding two
years. Lauris's brief, unremarkable on its surface, instructed her to acquire the subject alive if
practical, terminate if not.

She located the subject within eleven days — her tracking methods by this point in her career had
sharpened considerably past what her earliest contracts had required of her — and observed for a
further four before making contact, a delay her archive records as deliberate rather than cautious.
Something in the subject's own operational pattern had struck her, across those four days of
observation, as familiar in a way the Directorate's file did not account for: the targets chosen were
not rival criminal enterprises, as the file characterized them, but specifically and exclusively
trafficking operations she recognized, from her own widening private catalogue, as fronts for the
engineering tradition's biological-material supply lines.

The confrontation itself, when it came, resolved in minutes rather than the extended engagement
either party's reputation might have suggested. Subject IM-099 did not fight to win. She fought, by
Lauris's own reading of the engagement's shape, to be taken seriously long enough to speak — and
when Lauris permitted the opening, the subject identified herself plainly: Kareth-Vassen Aerelin, a
Kareth War-Order operative some eighty thousand years into a life Lauris's own six-thousand-year span
could not yet fully comprehend, running an autonomous campaign against the same apparatus Lauris had
spent nine operations learning to fear the shape of, under cover the Directorate itself had
mistaken, for sixty years, for ordinary organized crime.

I have read Lauris's account of the conversation that followed more than once, because I believe it
is, among everything in this stretch of her archive, the closest she comes to describing relief. Two
operatives, each convinced for years that her own suspicion of the institution she nominally served
was a private and possibly unsound conviction, discovering in the space of a single exchange that the
suspicion was shared, corroborated, and considerably further advanced on the other side than either
had dared assume alone. "I have spent six operations believing I was the only one who had noticed,"
she wrote. "I was wrong. I do not know whether that makes the account lighter or heavier. I know only
that it no longer belongs to me alone."

What followed was practical rather than sentimental, in keeping with both women's evident
temperament: a staged termination, convincing enough to satisfy the Directorate's own verification
protocols, that closed Subject IM-099's file as Reclassified — Deceased while leaving Aerelin
entirely free to continue her sixty-year campaign under a cover the Directorate now considered
permanently resolved. The alliance the two women formed that day — informal, uncoordinated with any
larger authority, answerable to neither institution either of them had once served — was, in Lauris's
own later assessment to me, the first deliberate act of her career that could not be characterized,
by any honest reading, as merely withholding information. It was the first time she had actively
built something the apparatus beneath the Directorate was not supposed to know existed.

She filed her report to the Directorate accurately as to the contract's outcome and entirely falsely
as to the subject's actual fate — the first such deliberate falsification in an archive that had, up
to this operation, confined itself to omission rather than fabrication. I have asked her whether she
considers this the moment her Disillusionment stopped being a private conviction and became an act.
She has told me, more directly than she answers most such questions, that she does.

Her closing note for Operation 31: *Aligned, not opposed. I did not expect, when this contract
began, to end it with an ally rather than a body. I have decided I prefer this outcome, and I intend
to arrange more of them where the arithmetic allows it.*
