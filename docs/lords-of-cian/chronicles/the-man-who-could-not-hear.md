# The Man Who Could Not Hear

*Locked canon, Batch 201, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-843`). The Crow King Alias Chronicle XXVIII, wave 10 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The garrison commander at Verrow Hold had lost most of his hearing to an artillery accident years
before the Rebellion began, relying on a second officer's written reports and his own trained eye
for troop movement rather than anything spoken aloud — and every plan Kanja's people built assumed,
by long and reliable habit, an ear at the other end of whatever signal they threw.

The first attempt, a false thermal bloom seeded the usual way, meant nothing to a commander who
never personally listened to sensor readings at all, relying instead on a subordinate's written
summary that arrived, delayed and filtered, well after the signal's usefulness had already passed.

"He's not deaf to the trick," the apprentice observed, studying the failed attempt afterward. "He's
deaf to the medium entirely. Everything we do assumes someone's actually listening in real time.
He's built his whole command style around not needing to."

"Then the lie has to travel a way he actually receives it." Kanja spent two days studying exactly
how Verrow Hold's written reports moved — who drafted them, how quickly, what details a rushed
subordinate tended to compress or omit under pressure — before building, for the first time, a
deception meant to be read rather than heard: a captured courier's genuine handwriting, carefully
studied and reproduced, carrying a false troop count sized precisely to what a hurried report-writer
would plausibly compress from real information.

The forged report reached the commander through his own trusted channel, read by his own eyes in
his own silence, and moved his reserves exactly as intended — no chant, no resonance, no sound of any
kind involved anywhere in an operation built entirely around a craft everyone in the crew had always
assumed required a voice.

"You didn't sing a single note," the apprentice said afterward, turning the whole operation over in
her mind. "I didn't think the craft worked without one."

"The craft was never actually about the voice. It's about finding exactly how a person receives
what they trust, and shaping something true-looking to travel that same path. My voice is just the
tool I've used most often, because most people listen. He doesn't. So the tool had to change. The
discipline underneath it didn't."
