# The Well That Closed Over the Boy

*Locked canon, Batch 255, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1144`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle LXXXVII, wave 29,
closing the wave. Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. An intimate, small-scale rescue no blade can reach.*

---

The well on the ridge road had been sealed for years, its shaft narrow and its stonework crumbling,
which was exactly why nobody had thought to warn a seven-year-old chasing a dropped ball not to lean
too far over the collapsed cover that had once sealed it. The boy went down eleven feet before a
ledge of broken stone caught him, conscious, shouting, and utterly beyond reach of anything wider than
a grown man's shoulders.

Onyx could open a seam in a shield wall and pierce a concealment no blade should see through, and it
was useless here. Veil Piercer had once freed nine trapped miners by finding the space between a
collapse's intention and its material — but a mine collapse was rubble, something to be parted. A
sealed well's shaft was simply too narrow for anything but a body small enough to fit down it, and
the blade at Kanja's hip, however gifted, was not going to make his own shoulders any narrower.

Dol Maren reached the well within minutes of the shout going up, and spent the first several of them
not reaching for a rope at all, but studying the shaft's stonework with the same assessing calm he
brought to every load he'd ever rated. "The ledge holding him won't hold long. Stone's crumbling
under him same as it did above him. We've got minutes, not an hour."

There was no equipment on hand built for a rescue this narrow. What there was, was Kanja's own body,
which Maren judged — correctly, checking it the way he checked everything, against a real number
rather than a hope — was the closest thing to small enough and strong enough at once that the well
was going to allow. He went down headfirst on a line Maren played out by hand, no room to turn, no
room for Onyx at his hip to do anything but scrape uselessly against stone with every foot of descent,
finding the boy by touch in the dark before he found him by sight at all.

The stone ledge gave way within seconds of Kanja getting an arm around him, the boy's added weight
finally more than the crumbling shelf could bear — and Maren's line, already taut, was the only thing
that kept both of them from dropping the remaining distance into whatever waited below. He hauled them
both up hand over hand, no gift, no blade, nothing but a rope, his own judgment of the stone's
remaining strength, and the raw physical work of pulling two people up eleven feet of narrow shaft
before the well finished collapsing behind them.

The boy's mother reached the surface before they did, and Kanja, coughing well-dust and holding a
frightened, unhurt child, found he had nothing useful to say to her beyond the plain fact that her son
was alive. Maren, coiling the rope with the same careful precision he gave every finished piece of
work, said even less.
