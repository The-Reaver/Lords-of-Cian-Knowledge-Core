# The Nine Days Nobody Slept

*Locked canon, Batch 191, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-558`). Captain Alias Chronicle XVI, first entry in the
fifth wave. Rebellion era. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material — a detailed,
sustained multi-day endurance showcase per Abad's craft instruction. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The siege ran nine days, longer than any engagement the crew had endured under the Captain name, food
and rest both stretched thin enough that morale itself became the actual battlefield somewhere around
the sixth day — not a single dramatic crisis, but the slow, grinding erosion of a crew's will to keep
holding a position that showed no sign of resolving.

Kanja didn't rest more than anyone else. He made certain of that deliberately.

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System let him function on far less recovery than the ordinary crew around
him needed, and rather than use that advantage to work through the nights alone, he spent it moving
constantly among the crew instead — relieving exhausted watches personally, working alongside
whoever's shift ran longest, making certain nobody carried the siege's ninth day believing they carried
it more alone than he did.

"You could rest properly," the crew's own senior watch officer said on the seventh night, finding him
still moving among the exhausted crew rather than take the recovery his own biology could have used.
"Nobody would think less of you for actually sleeping through a full watch."

"I know nobody would. That's not why I'm not doing it." Kanja kept working, voice steady despite nine
days of the same grinding pressure everyone else was carrying. "I could survive this siege on
considerably less than the rest of you need. That's exactly why I don't intend to take more rest than
you're able to, not while you're all still out here holding the same line. The name doesn't mean
anything if it only shows up for the dramatic parts and disappears for the grinding ones."

The siege broke on the ninth day, relief finally arriving, the position held intact through nothing
more dramatic than nine days of a crew that never once believed itself abandoned to carry the burden
alone.

"Nine days, and you never took more than the rest of us," the watch officer said afterward, genuine
weight in the observation. "I don't know that most commanders would have made that choice."

"Most commanders aren't standing here able to survive on less than their crew needs and choosing not
to use that gap for their own comfort." Kanja watched the relief forces arrive, the siege's real cost
finally ending. "I'd rather earn every day of this the same way you all had to earn it. That's the
only version of the name I've ever wanted to carry."
