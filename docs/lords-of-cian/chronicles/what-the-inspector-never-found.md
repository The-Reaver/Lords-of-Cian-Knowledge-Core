# What the Inspector Never Found

*Locked canon, Batch 203, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-892`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XLVII, wave 16. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The Directorate's structural inspector arrived without warning, as the standing regulation required,
to certify that Killane's municipal repair crews weren't using wartime cover to reinforce tunnels for
purposes beyond ordinary maintenance — a legitimate process the resistance had known for weeks it
would eventually have to survive rather than avoid.

Kanja was on the work crew's own roster as a journeyman brace-setter, and there was no version of the
morning where he could disappear before the inspection reached his section.

"Your papers say six years' trade experience," the inspector said, walking the section Kanja had
reinforced three days earlier — reinforced for reasons that had nothing to do with municipal safety
and everything to do with holding a defensive line the inspector was never meant to notice. "Walk me
through this brace pattern. Municipal code calls for standard spacing here. This isn't standard."

"Standard spacing assumes standard load, and this section's carrying more traffic than the code ever
accounted for — cart trains coming through twice what they used to, since the flood rerouted two
other passages onto this one." Kanja answered the way six real years would have taught him to answer,
without hesitation, without the small telling pause of someone reciting a prepared lie. "I widened the
spacing on the compression side and tightened it on the tension side. Code's a floor, not a ceiling.
Any brace-setter worth his papers reads the actual load before he reads the regulation."

The inspector crouched, testing a joint with a tool Kanja recognized and had used himself a hundred
times, checking for the wrong sound in the wood — the sound of a structure hiding a purpose the paper
trail didn't account for. He found nothing but a joint that answered exactly as it should, load-bearing
and legitimate down to the grain.

"Your explanation's better than most journeymen give me." The inspector straightened, unconvinced of
anything except the craftsmanship in front of him. "Most men panic when I ask why they deviated from
code. You made deviating from code sound like the more careful choice."

"It was the more careful choice. I'd rather explain an honest deviation to an inspector than explain
a collapse to a family." Kanja held the man's gaze, offering nothing but the truth of the joint itself
— true in every particular the inspector could test, and silent on the one particular that mattered
most. "You're welcome to test every brace in this section. I built each one to answer the same
question you're asking me now, whether or not I expected anyone to ask it."

The inspector tested four more joints, found four more honest answers, and moved on to the next
section without a second glance back — the defensive reinforcement standing exactly where it needed
to stand, certified sound by the very authority it was built to withstand, because every word Kanja
had given him was true.

"That's the closest call this district's had all war," the crew chief said afterward, watching the
inspector's party clear the block. "You didn't lie to him once."

"I didn't need to. Everything I told him was true — I just didn't tell him what the truth was for."
Kanja checked the joint the inspector had tested last, resetting one peg the man's tool had loosened.
"That's the only kind of cover that actually holds under real scrutiny. Not a story. Just work good
enough that nobody asking the right questions ever needs to hear a wrong answer."
