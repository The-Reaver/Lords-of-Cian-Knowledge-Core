# The Hand That Chose to Leave

*Locked canon, Batch 217, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-955`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle LIII, wave 18. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

He had been aboard since before Ghost Harbor, one of the quiet, competent hands nobody wrote ballads
about, and when he came to Kanja's cabin to say he was leaving at the next port, he didn't frame it as
an argument the way the grieving crewman had, months before. He framed it as something already decided.

"I'm not angry," he said, before Kanja could ask. "I want to be clear about that, because I know how the
last one of these conversations went, and this isn't that. I've just watched us let go men who'd have
killed people I care about given the chance, three times now, and I've decided I can't keep being part
of the letting go. Not because I think you're wrong. Because I don't think I can hold the belief the
rest of you hold, and I'd rather leave honestly than stay and slowly turn into the crewman who couldn't
let it go without ever saying so out loud."

Kanja didn't argue him out of it. That was, more than anything else about the conversation, what struck
Garren Hask afterward, entering it into the ledger with a note that wasn't quite like any other entry
he'd made. "Most captains would've tried to talk him out of it. You didn't even start."

"He's not wrong about himself," Kanja said. "He knows what he can carry and what he can't, better than
I could tell him. Trying to argue him into staying would just mean asking him to lie to himself about
it a while longer, and I've watched what that does to people." He paused. "The doctrine doesn't need
everyone to believe in it. It needs the people who stay to actually mean it. That's the only kind of
crew worth having."

The parting itself was unremarkable in exactly the way that made it land — no confrontation, no
farewell speech, a quiet transfer of his gear to the launch at the next port, Corren Halst and Efa Gol
both seeing him off with an embrace rather than a debate, Dol Maren pressing a small carved token into
his hand that neither of them explained to anyone else afterward.

"Where will you go?" Danne Sok asked him, at the rail.

"Somewhere I can still fight for something, without having to let go of the people who'd have hurt the
ones I love. I don't know yet where that is." He looked back at the three flagships riding at anchor
one last time. "I'm not going to tell anyone what we are, if that's what you're asking. I just can't be
part of it anymore."

Garren Hask's ledger carried his name — Hask kept it, quietly, the same way he kept every departure and
every arrival — but it does not appear here, by the fleet's own long-standing habit of letting people
who choose to step out of the story keep that choice entire.

departure is peaceful, mutually respectful, and explicitly not framed as failure by either side,
establishing that the doctrine's maintenance (per wave 16's own precedent) sometimes costs the fleet a
person rather than a conflict. The departing hand is deliberately kept unnamed on the page per the
crew's own stated convention, avoiding a new named character. Second entry in the eighteenth wave.*
