# The Handshake That Wasn't Kept

*Locked canon, Batch 194, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-621`). The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XVI, wave 6 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The owner at the Silt Row dredge site had shaken Kanja's hand in front of two dozen witnesses,
agreed to the wage correction the tally sheets required, and asked for three days to arrange the
payment through his own counting-house. Kanja gave him the three days. The crew withdrew from the
site entirely, exactly as promised, trusting the handshake the way they'd trusted every handshake
since the method had started working.

On the fourth day, word came back that the owner had used the three days to quietly reroute Silt
Row's manifests through a different registry entirely, one that didn't recognize the wage
correction at all.

"He played you," Corren Halst said, when the report reached them. "Took the goodwill and used it to
disappear the paperwork before anyone could hold him to it."

"He did." Kanja didn't sound surprised, only tired in a specific, familiar way. "I knew that was
possible when I agreed to give him the room to arrange it honestly. I gave it to him anyway, because
the alternative was never giving anyone the room to be honest, and I'm not willing to run the whole
district on the assumption that everyone will eventually lie to me."

"So what now? Go back in and take what he owes?"

"No. Now we find out exactly which registry he moved the manifests to, and we get three independent
clerks from three separate counting-houses to look at both sets of numbers side by side, in public,
where he can't quietly correct the correction a second time." Kanja was already sketching the route
on a scrap of the old tally paper. "He didn't break the method. He broke his word. Those aren't the
same failure, and they don't get the same answer. The method survives a broken handshake. It just
takes longer, and it takes witnesses instead of trust."

It took eleven days. The three clerks confirmed the original figures without exception, the
rerouted registry was exposed as exactly what it was, and the owner paid twice what he would have
owed if he'd simply kept his word the first time — the cost of the delay added openly to the
settlement, at the clerks' own insistence, not Kanja's.

"You could have just gone back and made him pay that first day," Halst said afterward, watching the
final ledger close. "Would have been faster."

"Faster isn't the same as sound. If I answer a broken promise with force, every owner on this canal
learns that the choice was always going to end in force eventually, so why bother keeping the
promise at all." Kanja folded the closed ledger under one arm. "I'd rather they learn the opposite.
That breaking the handshake costs more than keeping it would have. That's a lesson that holds after
I'm not standing in the room to enforce it."
