# The Report No One Wanted Read

*Locked canon, Batch 195, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-663`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XXVIII, wave 10 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The foreman running the district's forced-labor repair crews had been ignoring structural defects
for months, pushing conscripted workers through sections everyone underground already knew were
unsafe, because the reported repair schedule looked better on paper than an honest one would have.

Kanja spent four nights compiling his own inspection.

He didn't confront the foreman directly. He wrote it up the way a genuine municipal defect report
would read — section by section, each fault documented with the same precision he'd have used on
any legitimate assessment, cross-referenced against the foreman's own falsified repair log so the
two documents, laid side by side, told an unmistakable story of neglect.

"You can't just hand a foreman a report and expect him to act on it," the crew chief said, reading
over the finished document. "He'll burn it before anyone official sees it."

"Then I don't hand it to him first." Kanja made three copies by hand, tedious work he insisted on
doing himself rather than risk a scribe's transcription error undermining the report's credibility.
"One goes to him, so he knows we know. One goes to the district's own labor overseer, who has every
reason to want an honest repair schedule now that she knows a dishonest one exists. One stays with
us, in case either of the first two disappears."

The foreman's response, when the labor overseer's copy surfaced independently of anything he
controlled, was not defiance — it was the particular quiet of a man who understood, immediately,
that the falsified log could no longer protect him from what the honest report already proved.

"I could have you removed from this district for this," he told Kanja, the threat carrying no real
weight behind it.

"You could. It wouldn't change what's already documented in two other copies, or the fact that the
overseer's already reading hers." Kanja didn't raise his voice. "I'm not trying to end your
position. I'm trying to end workers dying in sections you knew were unsafe and reported as fine
anyway. Fix the schedule honestly, and this report never has to matter again outside the three
copies that already exist."

The repair schedule was rewritten within the week, honestly this time, and the foreman — whether
from genuine correction or simple fear of a fourth copy someday surfacing — never falsified another
entry for the remainder of the siege.
