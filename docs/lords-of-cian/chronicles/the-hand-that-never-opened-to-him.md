# The Hand That Never Opened to Him

*Locked canon, Batch 193, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-595`). Captain Alias Chronicle XX, wave 7 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Three years aboard, and the deck hand who ran the aft winch still wouldn't meet Kanja's eyes for
longer than it took to answer a direct question. He worked hard, never shirked, never complained,
and never once softened toward the warmth that had settled over most of the rest of the crew like
weather.

Efa Gol noticed it before Kanja said anything about it. "He's not going to come around. I've watched
him three years. Some people don't."

"Everyone comes around eventually," Kanja said, though without much conviction behind it.

"No. Not everyone. Some people were burned badly enough, early enough, by someone who wore kindness
as a tool before they ever met you, and no amount of consistency fixes that — it just gets filed
under 'not yet proven wrong.' He's not being unfair to you. He's protecting the last thing he's got
left of himself."

Kanja didn't push it. He kept working alongside the man the same way he worked alongside everyone
else — no special effort, no extra warmth aimed at cracking something that didn't want to crack,
just steady, ordinary presence — and the distance never closed.

Years later, on the night before the man finally left the crew for a quieter trade inland, Kanja
found him packing.

"I know I never gave you what everyone else gave you," the man said, not quite an apology.

"You never owed me that. I've never once needed you to trust me the way the rest of them do. I
needed you to do good work and be treated fairly while you did it, and you did, and you were. That
was always the whole deal — not the only deal I offer, but a real one."

The man considered that for a long moment. "Most captains would've made it about themselves eventually.
Wanting the room, or the gratitude, or something."

"I know some who did. I try hard not to be one of them." Kanja held out a hand — not for anything,
just as the offer it had always been. The man shook it, the first time in three years he'd let the
contact last more than a second, and left the next morning without either of them treating it as a
victory.

as legitimate rather than as a problem to be solved. Extends Efa Gol's already-locked
long-tenured, perceptive characterization. No new named characters. Second entry in Captain's
seventh wave.*
