# The Squall That Broke the Line

*Locked canon, Batch 258, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1211`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
LXXIII, wave 25, first entry in the wave. A detailed full-Trinity combat showcase fought through an
active storm rather than around it. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose.*

---

The Trust flotilla commander had chosen the timing deliberately — a genuine squall rolling in fast
from the west, visibility dropping to nothing, seas heavy enough that any crew without the fleet's own
discipline would have been fighting the water before they ever fought a ship. He'd read, correctly,
that the ghost fleet's reputation for elusiveness relied on clear conditions to work its evasions in.
What he hadn't accounted for was a captain willing to fight straight through the storm rather than
around it.

Kanja went over *The Audit*'s rail into rain that stung like grapeshot, the Sovereign Eyes' overlay
the only thing rendering three enemy hulls clean through water thick enough to blind unaided sight,
each one closing on a different heading in the chaos the storm had handed the Trust commander as
cover. Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System read wave-impact and gunfire both through the same
shuddering deck, absorbing a broadside that would have staved in *The Audit*'s flank and redirecting
the force down through the hull's own flexible frame — Dol Maren's design proving its worth against
cannon fire for the first time, not just weather.

Obsidian Malice found its targets by sound alone where sight failed, two precision discharges
silencing a pair of deck guns before their crews could reload past the storm's own delay. Onyx of
Oblivion carried him hull to hull through water breaking over the rail with every step, Whisper of
Shadows keeping him clear of a boarding pike he never saw coming and Soulbound Edge deciding a close
exchange with the flotilla commander himself in the space between two swells, disarming rather than
killing, the blade reading intent to kill against intent to survive and choosing the ending that let
both keep breathing.

The storm broke the engagement before the fight itself did — visibility collapsing to nothing, both
sides forced to disengage or risk the sea finishing what neither fleet had managed to. Kanja hauled
the disarmed commander aboard rather than leave him in open water, and by the time the squall passed
the man had nothing left to fight with and nowhere left to retreat to.

"You used the storm," the commander said, soaked through, furious less at losing than at having his
own advantage turned inside out. "That wasn't supposed to be possible."

"Storms don't take sides," Kanja said. "You just have to be willing to stand in one."
