# The Pass That Wanted Him Dead First

*Locked canon, Batch 196, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-687`). Bane Alias Chronicle XXII, wave 8 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

No Directorate company held the Kessarine Pass that season. The pass held itself — a switchback
climb through altitude cold enough to crack unprepared skin, narrow enough in three places that a
single misstep meant a drop no one would survive, and it was the only route that could put Bane and
four others at the besieged relay post below the ridge before the post's water ran out entirely.

There was no enemy to read, no trap to see coming, no reputation to spend. Just the climb.

The storm caught them on the second day, high enough that the wind carried ice instead of rain, and
for six hours the five of them moved roped together along a shelf too narrow to argue about
alternatives on. Bane took the lead position not because his biology made the cold easier to bear —
it didn't, not meaningfully, not at that altitude — but because the lead position was the one most
likely to find the next handhold wrong first, and he'd rather be the one who found that out.

"You don't have to take point the whole way," one of the four said, near the worst of it, voice
half-lost to the wind. "You're not built any better for this than the rest of us are."

"I know. That's exactly why I'm not letting anyone else find that out the hard way." Bane tested the
next hold before trusting his full weight to it, the same as everyone behind him would have to. "The
armor doesn't help here. The strength doesn't help here. All I've got on this shelf that you don't
is that if I go, four people lose their lead. If any of you go, I lose one of four, and the post
below still doesn't get its water. That's not nobility. That's just where the numbers point."

They lost no one. They reached the relay post nine hours behind schedule, hands too numb to properly
feel the ropes they were still carrying, and found the post's small garrison rationing what little
water remained down to swallows, three days from empty.

"We thought help would come from the eastern road," the post's watch commander said, disbelieving
that anyone had come over the pass at all in that weather. "Nobody takes the Kessarine in a storm
season."

"Nobody who has a choice." Bane set down the supply the four of them had carried the whole climb.
"We didn't have one. The eastern road adds four days you didn't have."
