# The Story Corren Halst Still Told

*Locked canon, Batch 201, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-848`). The Crow King Alias Chronicle XXXIII, wave 11 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Corren Halst told the story of the four-person campfire more often, by his own admission, than any
account of the far more famous Wetlands operation that had actually earned the Crow King his name —
a preference that puzzled newer crew members who assumed the smaller story mattered less simply
because fewer people had heard of it.

"Everyone already knows about the Wetlands," he told one such recruit, young enough to have joined
well after the Crow King's reputation had calcified into legend. "Three hundred voices, the marsh, a
scarecrow left as a signature — it's practically a fixture by now, told the same way every time. Four
people badly arguing around a fire that a patrol walked past out of pity isn't a story anyone tells
for him. It's a story that tells you something true about him, which is a different thing entirely."

"What does it tell you?"

"That the thing everyone eventually calls genius started out as four exhausted people getting it
completely wrong, and instead of deciding the idea itself was bad, he spent the following months
figuring out exactly what had gone wrong with the execution." Corren Halst said it with the ease of
something turned over in his own mind many times before. "Most people, humiliated that badly, quietly
drop the idea and try something else. He went the other direction. I think that's the actual lesson
buried in the marsh grass and the crown of feathers, if anyone bothered to look past how impressive
the finished version turned out to be."

"Do you think he remembers it the same way you do?"

"I know he does. He's the one who told me, years after the fact, that he thinks about that fire more
than he thinks about the Wetlands most days — because the Wetlands already worked, and there's not
much left to learn from something that already worked. The fire is where the actual work happened.
Everything since has just been getting better at what that failure first pointed him toward."

The recruit asked, before the conversation ended, whether Corren Halst minded being remembered mostly
for laughing at a mistake rather than for anything that came after it.

"Not even slightly," he said. "Somebody has to remember the part before it got good. I don't mind
being the one who does."
