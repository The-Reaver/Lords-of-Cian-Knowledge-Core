# The Duel Off the Sovereign Coast

*Locked canon, Batch 199, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-799`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle XLIV, wave 15 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The challenge arrived through the same neutral intermediary who'd once carried a historian's request —
a Trust naval champion, undefeated in eleven ship-to-ship duels fought under the old formal rules
neither side had observed in years, proposing a single engagement: his flagship against *The Audit*,
no boarding, no gear, no Trinity, just two hulls and two crews deciding the matter the way captains had
settled disputes for generations before density and Talismans made such contests obsolete.

"It's a trap," Dol Maren said immediately. "Has to be."

"Maybe. Or maybe he genuinely wants to know if he's actually good, instead of just undefeated against
captains who never had our advantages to begin with." Kanja considered the terms for a long moment.
"No powers. No gear beyond what any hull carries. Just sailing and gunnery, my crew against his."

He accepted, and held to the terms exactly — Mafesto, Onyx, and Obsidian Malice all held back entirely,
the engagement fought as a pure contest between Dol Maren's two years of hull refinement and the Trust
champion's own genuinely earned eleven-duel record.

It ran close for the better part of two hours, both captains reading wind and water with real skill,
both crews executing maneuvers that would have impressed any naval academy neither side had actually
attended in years. The champion's ship was faster on a reach; *The Audit* turned tighter, Dol Maren's
years of hull-trim work paying out exactly where it mattered most, in the narrow margin of a duel with
no other advantage permitted to decide it.

The decisive moment came from patience rather than aggression — Kanja held a maneuver two full minutes
longer than instinct argued for, letting the champion commit fully to what looked like a winning angle
before the delayed turn opened a clean shot across his rigging that ended the engagement without a
single hull breached below the waterline.

The champion struck his colors himself, unprompted, before his own crew could argue the point. "Eleven
duels," he said, crossing to *The Audit*'s deck once the engagement formally ended, "and that's the
first time I've actually lost to skill instead of to something my opponent had that I didn't."

"You nearly had it. The reach advantage was real." Kanja extended a hand, meaning the gesture the same
way he'd meant it with the privateer coalition months before. "I don't think I've fought anyone who
sailed that well in a long time."

"Is that meant as comfort for losing?"

"It's meant as the truth. Losing well to a real skill is a different thing than losing to a trick.
You should know the difference, because it's the only one that actually tells you anything true about
yourself."
