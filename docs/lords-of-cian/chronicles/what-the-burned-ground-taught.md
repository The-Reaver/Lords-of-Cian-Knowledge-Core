# What the Burned Ground Taught

*Locked canon, Batch 236, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1044`). Crow King Alias Chronicle LVIII, wave 20. Direct
follow-through on wave 19's unrecovered regional breach ("What They Couldn't Take Back," `MCD-995`).
Not a territory Chronicle. Opens wave 20.*

---

They did not go back to reclaim the eastern grain-road country. Kanja was plain about that before
anyone set out: the burned regional variant stayed burned, the ground stayed ceded, and nothing about
this return was meant to undo what a season's worth of failures had cost. They went back to find out
whether the third generation's own fix actually held against the same kind of patience that had beaten
them there once already.

Her fix was not a cleverer signal. It was the opposite of clever, which was precisely her point.
"You said it yourself," she told Kanja, laying out six weeks of proposed operations against a map of
a different, uncompromised stretch of the same grain roads. "Not skill. Time. They didn't outthink us.
They just watched us long enough to learn what we always do. So we stop always doing anything." Where
the old doctrine ran a consistent method until a region's geography or garrison forced a change, her
revision deliberately broke its own patterns on a schedule no outside observer could predict — a clean
extraction one week, a loud, half-convincing near-failure the next, a full month of no activity at all
in between, some of it real operations and some of it manufactured noise with nothing behind it,
indistinguishable from outside the craft itself.

It cost more. Manufactured noise took as much coordination as a real operation, for no return but
denying an enemy a stable pattern to study. Kanja said as much, watching her account for the added
weeks of work. "You're asking the crew to spend real effort on operations that accomplish nothing on
their own."

"They accomplish the one thing the eastern grain roads proved we needed," she said. "They make sure no
outpost ever again gets a full season of us behaving consistently enough to learn from." She didn't
argue that it made the method safer. She argued that it made the method survivable at the scale it had
grown to, which was a harder and less comfortable claim, and she held to it.

They ran six weeks of it against a garrison with every reason to be watching closely — a company that
had heard, secondhand and exaggerated, what happened to the outpost two provinces over, and had spent
the intervening months building its own careful log of anything resembling the pattern. The log filled
with contradictions it couldn't reconcile: a signal that read as genuine panic one week and as
transparent theater the next, evacuations that happened and evacuations that were rehearsed and
abandoned, no version of events consistent enough to act on with confidence. By the sixth week the
company's own reports had stopped calling any of it the Crow King's work at all, filing it instead
under unexplained regional unrest.

It held. Once. Under conditions the third generation had chosen and controlled, against an enemy who
hadn't yet paid the eastern grain roads' price for patience.

"It'll hold until someone patient enough to watch us for longer than six weeks comes along," she told
Kanja afterward, without triumph. "I'm not claiming I fixed it. I'm claiming I gave us more time before
whatever beats this finds us again."

"That's all the last fix ever was too," Kanja said. "That's all any of them have been."

*Continuity notes (not narrative): the first entry across all twenty waves to return directly to the
consequences of a prior entry's genuine, unrecovered failure ("What They Couldn't Take Back," Chronicle
LVII, wave 19) and dramatize the crew's own doctrinal response to it in the field, rather than a
retrospective reflection on the loss (contrast Commandant Voris's kept-trophy and secondhand-lesson
entries, Chronicles III and XLII, both told from the *opponent's* side). Gives the third generation her
first authorship-of-doctrine beat distinct from her prior solo-command entry (Chronicle LVI, wave 19):
this time she designs a structural revision to the method itself — deliberately irregular, partly
manufactured operational noise, to deny any single observer a consistent pattern to study — and
explicitly does not claim it solves the underlying problem, only buys more time, honoring wave 19's own
refusal to resolve the method's limits into false confidence. No new named characters; the outpost,
company, and grain-road region are the same unnamed setting established in Chronicle LVII, deliberately
not given a proper name. Opens wave 20.*
