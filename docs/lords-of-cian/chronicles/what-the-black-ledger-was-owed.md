# What the Black Ledger Was Owed

*Locked canon, Batch 231, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1030`). The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle LIX, wave 20.
Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench -- Mafesto remains dormant and Obsidian Malice undeployed until the
Black Trench (`MCD-232`), so this is a solo-blade Onyx of Oblivion showcase exploring an unused
register of the Black Ledger. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The owner had run three months ahead of his own back pay and four districts ahead of anyone able to
collect it. Every time Kanja's crew got close enough to sit him down at a tally table, he was already
gone — a different warehouse, a different name on the lease, the same forty-one workers left holding
wages that existed only as a number Garren Hask kept carrying forward on the ledger, unpaid.

He finally ran out of districts at a dockside grain warehouse on the canal's eastern spur, and he'd
hired guards this time instead of running — six of them, spears and a net-caster, arranged the way
men arrange themselves when someone has finally told them what they're actually being paid to stop.

Kanja walked in without drawing first, because the tally was the point and blades were what happened
when the tally got ignored. It got ignored. The net-caster loosed early, a wide weighted throw meant
to end the fight before it started — Onyx read the cast a half-beat out from center and Kanja simply
wasn't where the net finished closing, already inside the gap it had opened in the guards' own
formation. Two spearmen came at the seam together, and Cadence Ruin caught the offset between them
the way it had caught worse men before: the blade didn't need to be faster than either spear, only
faster than the half-second of coordination binding them into one attack instead of two. Both went
down disarmed, not dead. A third came from an angle meant to be unseen, and Onyx turned Kanja into it
before the strike finished forming — the same unhiding it had always done, nothing new, just precise.
The last three guards looked at four fallen colleagues, still breathing, and set their weapons down
without being asked twice.

The owner hadn't moved. He didn't have the ledger to negotiate with anymore, and he knew it.

Kanja didn't kill him and didn't need to threaten him to get what forty-one workers were owed — he
needed the man's own signature on a repayment schedule, which took less time than the fight had. What
took longer, and what the district hadn't seen before, was what happened when the signature was done:
Onyx's flat pressed once against the man's collar, the same contact it had given a dead honorable
officer a year and a half earlier, except this man was very much alive to feel it land. The mark
wasn't a killing stroke closed into a record. It was a debt opened and left visibly, quietly unpaid —
not to any court, not to any owner's rival, just to whoever already knew what a thin black line meant
when it sat on a man's collar in daylight.

He wore it for five months. Word traveled the way word did along that stretch of canal, and doors
that used to open for his coin started closing on principle before he'd said a word. He came to
Warehouse Twelve himself in the end, unescorted, the full repayment in hand a season ahead of the
schedule he'd signed, and asked — quietly, without the bluster he'd walked in with the first time —
whether the mark could be closed.

Kanja checked the ledger against Hask's own figures before he answered. Every coin was there.

"It was never a punishment," he told him, pressing two fingers to the same collar a second time. The
mark lifted clean, the way it had never needed violence to be placed and needed none to be released.
"It only ever recorded what was true. You made it stop being true. That's the only thing that was
ever going to close it."
