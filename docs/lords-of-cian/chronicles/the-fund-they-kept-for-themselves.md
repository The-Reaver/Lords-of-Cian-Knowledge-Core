# The Fund They Kept for Themselves

*Locked canon, Batch 267, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1397`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XCI, wave 31, first entry. The method's first case turned inward on a workers' own institution rather than an employer.*

---

The complaint came the way every other one did — a folded scrap of paper passed hand to hand until it reached the tent — except this one named no owner, no overseer, no administrator at all. It named the burial-and-sickness fund the dredge workers had built themselves, three coppers a week out of every wage, and the man they'd elected two years running to keep it.

Ezio read it twice before he said anything. "We've audited a hundred men who wanted to take from workers. We've never audited the fund the workers built to protect themselves from men like that."

"Does that change how we read the numbers?"

"No. That's what worries me."

It shouldn't have taken long — a fund that size kept simple books, deposits against three categories of payout — but the treasurer, a well-liked man named for his steady hand with the ledger nobody else wanted to keep, had learned the method's own trick better than most administrators ever bothered to. He hadn't stolen outright. He'd padded his own family's sickness claims by degrees so small no single entry looked wrong, three years running, the same slow bleed Ezio had once found in a mill owner's books and called theft without hesitating. He didn't hesitate here either, once the cross-reference was run against the fund's actual disbursement records and the treasurer's own household expenses matched too cleanly to be coincidence.

The workers who'd elected him twice wanted it handled quietly — a private repayment, no public finding, the man's own shame enough. Kanja wouldn't let Ezio write it that way. "We built our name on saying the numbers don't care who's asking for mercy on the other side of them. If I let that stop the day it's one of ours being audited, everything before this was a performance, not a discipline."

The finding went up the same way every finding did — posted, dated, signed, the treasurer's own name on it beside the figure he owed back. He paid it in full within the month, kept working the dredge line under the new treasurer's watch, and never asked to be spared what he'd have demanded of anyone else in his place.

"It's harder when it's someone who built the thing with you," Ezio said, closing the case.

"It should be. That's not a reason to write it softer. It's the whole reason the number has to mean the same thing every single time it's written down."
