# Lauris Chronicle XXXVII: What a Revival Would Require

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1654`). Thirty-seventh entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, part of the wave-one push of Strand L. Advances the Drowning Vault's 120
(`MCD-183`), the largest and previously least-dramatized of her standing operational debts, without
touching the discharge Book 5 reserves for it (`MCD-216`: "the discharge of her standing operational
debts -- the Drowning Vault's 120 released"). Deepens rather than resolves -- a joint planning
session with Sephtis establishes that the population's 20-to-8,000-year generation-age spread
(`MCD-183`) makes a single unified revival protocol impossible, reframing the debt as considerably
more complex than a release event, without attempting or completing any part of that release. No new
named characters. Narrated by Fermand Aurelias.*

---

*Archive fragment, present-day: "One hundred and twenty. Ages twenty to eight thousand at
generation. I have been treating this as a single number for longer than I should have. It is not a
single number. It is one hundred and twenty separate problems wearing the same designation."*

I do not often find myself present for a working session between Lauris and Sephtis that produces no
resolution whatsoever, and I want to record this one carefully, because I think its lack of
resolution is precisely what makes it worth recording. They met, at Lauris's own request, in the
Karkosa's archive room -- the same room, I am told, where Sephtis first laid his own archive open to
her, fourteen hours across a single sitting, years before I knew either of them. This meeting ran a
single afternoon, and produced not an answer but a considerably better account of the question.

The subject was the Drowning Vault. Lauris recorded her personal commitment to release its secured
population, one hundred and twenty Karesian-derived subjects, years ago -- a commitment she has
carried, unfulfilled, since before I began transcribing her archive at all. I had understood the
debt, insofar as I understood it, as a matter of scale and access: a facility she cannot yet safely
breach and hold, a population too large to extract without the resources the Lords of Cian has not
yet assembled for the purpose. What this session established, to my considerable surprise, is that
scale and access are the smaller half of the problem.

Sephtis raised it first, working from the site inspection Lauris recorded at the time of her second
Drowning Vault engagement: the secured population's ages at generation span from twenty years to
eight thousand. "A subject generated eight thousand years ago and held in suspended maintenance since
has spent a span of time in that state that most Karesians never spend awake in one unbroken stretch,"
he said, or words close enough to it that I trust my transcription. "A subject generated twenty years
ago has spent a comparative eyeblink. You cannot revive both the same way and expect either result to
be survivable."

Lauris did not dispute this. I think, from her own account afterward, that she had already begun to
suspect it, and had simply never had the specific archival comparison in front of her that would
confirm it. What followed was not a plan -- neither of them pretended otherwise -- but an inventory
of the problem's actual shape. Suspended-maintenance physiology across so wide a generation-age range
would likely require staged revival rather than a single coordinated action: the youngest subjects
first, whose bodies and, if any remain, whose minds have the least accumulated suspension to
reconcile; the oldest last, and possibly individually, with medical and psychological support neither
Lauris nor Sephtis currently has assembled and neither is confident exists anywhere on Cian in a form
suited to the task. Some fraction of the population, Sephtis noted without either of them dwelling on
the possibility longer than the fact required, may not have minds to revive in any meaningful sense
at all, depending on what twelve thousand years of continuous generation and suspension actually did
to them -- a possibility Lauris's own site inspection had already flagged as open, and which this
session did nothing to close either way.

"I have been thinking of this as a door," Lauris said, near the end of the afternoon, in a line I
record because I believe it says something true about how she has carried this particular debt
compared to the others. "Something I open once, and everyone inside walks out. It is not a door. It
is a hundred and twenty doors, and I do not yet know how many of them open the same way."

Sephtis offered no timeline, and Lauris did not ask for one. What they agreed to, in the absence of
anything more concrete, was only this: Sephtis would begin, alongside his existing archival work,
cataloguing what fragments of Karesian medical and developmental practice either archive already
holds that might eventually inform a staged approach, and Lauris would continue what she has already
been doing for years -- visiting, maintaining, confirming the population's stability has not
deteriorated in the meantime. Neither of them described this as progress toward discharge. Both of
them, I think, understood it as something closer to finally measuring a debt they had previously only
carried by its weight rather than its shape.

Her closing note, entered alone after Sephtis had gone: *One hundred and twenty, still secured. I now
know more about how little I know than I did this morning. I am choosing to call that an improvement.*
