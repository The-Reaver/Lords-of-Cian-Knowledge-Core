# The Junction That Wouldn't Fall

*Locked canon, Batch 195, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-673`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XXXVIII, wave 13 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The central junction connecting four separate gallery sections was the single point every
extraction route in that quarter of the tunnel network depended on, and the Trust's command had
finally identified it as such — committing to a sustained assault meant not to break through in one
push, but to wear the junction's defenders down over days until exhaustion did what force alone
hadn't.

Kanja held it for four days straight.

Not continuously in open combat — the assault came in waves, hours apart, each one testing whether
the defenders' endurance had finally cracked — but Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System bore the
accumulated cost of dozens of smaller engagements the way it had once absorbed a single volley,
grounding fatigue itself as much as any individual strike, while Obsidian Malice's discharge cycle
became something closer to a rhythm than a weapon, five seconds of recharge stretched across hours
of intermittent pressure rather than a single battle's compressed urgency.

"They're not trying to break the junction anymore," the crew chief observed on the third day,
watching another wave recede without having gained an inch. "They're trying to break you.
Everything they're sending in is sized to cost you sleep, not ground."

"Then I sleep in shifts and hold it in shifts, same as everyone else down here has had to." Kanja
rotated a fresh set of defenders into the junction's forward positions, keeping himself at the
point most likely to be tested next rather than resting on a reputation that couldn't actually
substitute for genuine endurance. "A siege that's given up on breaking through fast is a siege
that's decided attrition's the only weapon left. Attrition works both directions. I'd rather out-last
them than out-fight them, if that's the fight they've chosen."

The assault broke on the fourth day, not from any single decisive strike but from an attacking
force that had simply run out of fresh waves to send, its own logistics unable to sustain a siege
of endurance against defenders who rotated shifts while the junction's central figure, by every
account, never once left his post for longer than the width of a single rest cycle.

"Four days," the crew chief said, once the retreat was confirmed. "I don't know how you held that
pace without breaking."

"I didn't hold it alone. I held the position they were testing hardest, and trusted the rotation
around me to hold the rest." Kanja finally allowed himself a full rest cycle, the junction secured.
"Endurance isn't one man refusing to tire. It's a crew that doesn't need one man to be the whole
answer for four straight days."
