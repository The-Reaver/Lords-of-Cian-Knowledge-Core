# Lauris Chronicle X: What the Log Was Not Meant to Show

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1627`). Tenth entry in Lauris Letitia's own Chronicle
series, the third entry of the Strand K (Kares Prime / deep-past) thread, drafted as part of a
50-entry wave across eight strand-agents under Abad's blanket authorization ("lock it, continue
uninterrupted, test and push to main"). Dramatizes for the first time her earliest childhood years
at Vask Threnarr under archivist Olda Threnarr-Iralek (`MCD-164`/`166`), set at age eight -- the
origin of her lifelong "careful witness" discipline (`MCD-161`) and her earliest recorded exchange
with cohort friend Velith (`MCD-165`/`1553`). No new named characters. Full text below.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Vask Threnarr, age eight: "Exercise: record the bout between Iron-Speaker Vann
and her second. I did not record who won. I recorded when the ground changed its mind."*

I have transcribed a great many of Lauris's Kares Prime archive entries by now, and the earliest of
them are, without exception, the ones I find myself returning to when I want to understand what the
woman in front of me actually is. This one is the earliest I have found with her own hand behind it
rather than Selene's or Olda's -- an exercise log from her eighth year, one page among what must once
have been hundreds, the rest lost or never preserved past the Vask's own archival culling.

The exercise itself was unremarkable by Threnarr standards. Olda Threnarr-Iralek, the Sister-Hold's
historical archivist and the woman charged with Lauris's education in lineage and observation both,
ran her cohort of forty-seven children through a standing assignment: watch a sanctioned training
bout between two adult combatants, and produce a written account of what occurred. Most children,
Olda's own surviving notes suggest, produced narrative. A won. B lost. A struck first. It is, I am
told, the natural shape a child's account of a fight takes, on any world.

Lauris's account did not take that shape. I have the page in front of me now, in my own careful
transcription rather than the original, which the Threnarr archive would not release even to her --
but the content is exact. She recorded no winner. She recorded, instead, four discrete moments in
the bout at which she judged the ground beneath one combatant's feet had shifted its relationship to
that combatant's weight, each moment timed against the bout's own progression, each accompanied by a
one-line note on what changed and why she believed it changed. The final line of the account reads:
*Iron-Speaker Vann's second lost when her weight stopped agreeing with the floor. This happened
once, quietly, forty counts before either of them noticed.*

Olda did not correct the assignment. She did not, by her own later account, know quite what to do
with it. She filed it, privately, alongside a note of her own that has survived in the same archive:
*The girl is not watching the fight. She is watching what the fight is built from. I do not yet know
if this is a gift or an inconvenience.* It went no further than that note for six more years. I want
to be precise about what I mean when I say the Sister-Hold did not flag Lauris's early anomalies --
this is what I mean. Not concealment. Simply the ordinary caution of people who had learned, across
thirty-one thousand years of watching their own children, that precocity and consequence are not the
same thing, and that naming a thing too early can shape it as surely as ignoring it can.

Velith sat beside her for that exercise, as she sat beside her for most of them. I have found only
one line of Velith's own in the record of that day, quoted in a cohort-instructor's aside rather than
preserved directly: she leaned over, read Lauris's page before it was submitted, and said, *You have
written down everything except who won. Everyone will ask you who won.* Lauris's recorded answer,
four words, already carrying the flat cataloguing register she would keep for the rest of her
recorded life: *No one asked me that.*

I do not think this exchange meant, to either child, anything beyond a single afternoon's small
disagreement about how an assignment ought to be done. I think it means something else entirely,
read from where I am sitting now, some four thousand years and one dead cohort-friend later. Velith
saw, at eight years old, what the rest of the civilization would take fourteen more years to measure
formally: that Lauris was already recording a different order of fact than the one everyone around
her assumed was being asked for. Not who wins. What the winning is made of.

I asked Lauris once, directly, whether she remembered writing that page. She said she did not
remember the bout itself, or even Vann's name with any confidence. She remembered, precisely, the
sentence about the floor, and she remembered Velith reading over her shoulder and asking the obvious
question a child asks. She did not tell me what she felt reading it back, all these centuries later,
and I did not ask a second time. Some entries, I have learned, are theirs to keep the shape of. Mine
is only to set down that the page exists, that it was the first, and that everyone who watched a
fight with Lauris Letitia in the room, for the whole of the four thousand years since, has eventually
stopped asking her who won.
