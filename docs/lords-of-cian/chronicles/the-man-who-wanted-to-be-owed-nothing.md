# The Man Who Wanted to Be Owed Nothing

*Locked canon, Batch 198, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-757`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XXXII, wave 11 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The senior joiner had worked the shipyard's timber shop longer than anyone else in it, and he was the
only man in the district who refused, flatly and without explanation, to give the ledger table a
single figure.

"I don't need your book," he said, when Ezio asked a second time, more gently, in case the first
refusal had been misheard. "I've fed myself and my trade for forty years without anyone's charity,
and I'm not starting now with a rebel's pity written next to my name where every apprentice in this
shop can read it."

"It's not pity," Ezio said. "It's a debt. Every other name in this book is the same kind of entry —
what this yard took that it never paid for."

"Every other name in that book is a man asking to be helped. I've never asked anyone for help in my
life and I'm not going to let a ledger decide for me that I should have." He said it without anger,
with the particular flat finality of a man who had built an entire sense of himself around never
being owed, and turned back to his bench before Ezio could answer.

Kanja, watching from across the yard, told Ezio to let it stand. The entry went in as a blank line —
the joiner's name, his trade, no figure beside it, marked only "declined by the worker's own choice."
No pressure followed. No one returned to ask him again.

Weeks later, once the settlement had come through and every other timber-shop hand had been
compensated in full, the joiner watched younger men he'd trained collect wages he'd earned in equal
measure and receive nothing himself, by his own stated will, and something in the arithmetic of it —
forty years against theirs, his own hands the steadiest in the shop, his name the only blank line in
a finished book — settled differently than he'd expected it to.

He came to Ezio alone, at the end of a shift, and didn't apologize for the earlier refusal because he
didn't consider it one.

"Put the number in," he said. "Not because I need it. Because I earned it the same as they did, and
a blank line next to the best hands in this shop reads like I did something wrong, when what I did
was decide something for myself. Let the decision be that I'm choosing it now, not that I couldn't
choose before."

Ezio wrote the figure in exactly as given, no correction made, no comment attached — the same entry
it would have been the first day, delayed by nothing but a man's own timing.
