# What the Blade Cut Free

*Locked canon, Batch 194, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-636`). The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XXXI, wave 11 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The support timbers at the Undertide access shaft gave without warning, three weeks after Deepcut,
in the middle of a shift change that put twice the usual number of dredgers underground at once.
Nine were trapped behind the collapse by the time the alarm reached the surface, and the shaft's own
foreman gave the trapped section, honestly, less than an hour before the flooding that followed every
canal-side collapse reached them.

Kanja went down without waiting for the engineers already being summoned from three districts over.

"There's no time to shore this properly," the foreman said, watching him study the collapse by
lamplight. "We'd need beams we don't have and hours we don't have more of."

"I'm not going to shore it. I'm going to cut through it." Kanja drew Onyx, and for the first time in
anyone's memory turned the blade not toward an enemy but toward a wall of collapsed timber and packed
earth, testing the gift he'd only ever used against intention and armor against something that had
no intention behind it at all. "Veil Piercer finds the seam in a thing's structure — the gap between
what's solid and what only looks solid. A shield wall has that gap because men hold their line
imperfectly. I don't know if collapsed earth has one. I'm about to find out."

It did, though not cleanly, and not without cost — the blade parting a narrow channel through the
debris that no ordinary digging could have opened in the time remaining, but the effort of finding a
seam in something with no intention to read against cost more than any duel had, sweat and
concentration replacing the swordcraft's usual certainty with something closer to blind, repeated
searching. Twice the channel nearly collapsed on itself before he found the next gap. By the time the
last section opened into the trapped pocket, water was already at the ankles of the nine men waiting
inside.

They came out one at a time, hauled through a channel barely wide enough for a single body, water
rising behind the last of them by the time the final dredger cleared the opening.

"That's not what the sword's for," the foreman said afterward, staring at the narrow, precise channel
still holding against the flood behind it.

"I don't know what the sword's for, exactly. I know what it's done today, which is more than I
expected when I drew it." Kanja was breathing harder than any duel had left him. "Onyx finds gaps.
I've only ever asked it to find them in men before. I don't know if I could do this again on
purpose, or if it worked because I was desperate enough not to overthink whether it should."

Nine men walked out of the Undertide shaft who would not have, and the story that spread afterward
carried none of the usual battle language — no duel, no defeat, just a narrow channel cut through
collapsed earth, and nine names that made it home for dinner.
