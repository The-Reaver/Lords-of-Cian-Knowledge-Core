# The Flood That Needed No Enemy

*Locked canon, Batch 221, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-993`). The Crow King Alias Chronicle LV, wave 19. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The dam above the settlement had been failing for years before it finally went, and when it went it
went at night, with no army involved anywhere in it — just a wall of water moving faster than anyone
on foot could outrun through streets none of them could see in the dark, and no Directorate patrol,
no garrison, no enemy of any kind to blame or deceive.

Kanja and the apprentice were two days' travel from the nearest operation when the water hit, close
enough only by chance to reach the settlement's edge within the hour. There was no plan to build, no
pattern to deceive anyone with, because there was no one to deceive. There was only several hundred
people scattered across streets that no longer matched what anyone remembered of them, and a rising
water line that gave them perhaps twenty minutes before the lower quarter went under entirely.

"This isn't what the craft is for," the apprentice said, already moving, already reading the
darkness for where a voice could still carry over the water's own roar.

"It's exactly what the craft is for," Kanja said. "We just never needed the lying part before."

What they ran was the coordination discipline stripped to its barest form — no falsehood anywhere in
it, no Braid, no false signal aimed at confusing anyone. Just the same trained, carrying voice built
to cut through chaos and confusion, calling true directions to true people: which street still held,
which alley had already gone under, where the high ground actually was rather than where panic told
people it should be. The apprentice took the lower quarter. Kanja took the streets nearer the break
itself, where the water was fastest and the screaming loudest.

They lost the night to it entirely, and lost twelve people to it despite everything, a fact neither
of them tried to soften afterward. But several hundred others reached high ground who would not have
found it fast enough on their own, guided by two voices that never once needed to tell anyone
anything but the truth.

"I don't think I understood, until tonight, how much of what we built was really just this," the
apprentice said, once the water had stopped rising and the settlement's survivors had begun, in the
grey light before dawn, to count who was missing. "The lying was never the point. The lying was just
the hardest version of getting people to move together when it mattered."

"The easiest version is this," Kanja said, looking out over water that had taken twelve lives and
spared several hundred more. "I wish we needed it more often."
