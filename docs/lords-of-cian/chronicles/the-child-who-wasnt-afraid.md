# The Child Who Wasn't Afraid

*Locked canon, Batch 151, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-478`). Bane Alias Chronicle XII, closing the fourth
wave. Rebellion era, age 19, shortly after the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. New
standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Every adult in the liberated settlement gave Bane the same wide, careful berth the reputation had
trained them to give him, even the ones grateful enough to say so aloud — except the youngest child
among the freed, too small to have absorbed the stories the way the grown captives had, who walked
straight up to him while the settlement's adults were still working out how close was safe to stand.

"You're not scary," she informed him, with the flat certainty small children bring to observations
adults have overcomplicated. "Everyone keeps saying you are. You don't look scary. You look tired."

Nobody moved to pull her back immediately, the moment too strange and too plainly harmless to
interrupt.

"I am tired," Bane said, crouching to something closer to her own height, careful in a way he wasn't
with anyone who understood what the caution was for. "Most days. I don't think that's something the
stories usually mention."

"Why do you let everyone be scared of you if you're not actually scary?"

"Because the being-scared part does something useful, most days. It ends fights before they start.
It keeps people like you safer than fighting would." He considered how to say the rest of it simply
enough for her to actually hold. "I don't love that it's the part people remember most. I'd rather be
remembered for the tired part, if I had the choice. But right now, this is what keeps working, so
this is what I do."

"That's a strange thing to be good at." She said it without judgment, just genuine curiosity, before
losing interest entirely and wandering off toward something that held her attention considerably
better than a tired stranger in armor.

"She's the first person in this settlement who's talked to me like I'm just a person," Bane told the
crew's forward scout afterward, something unguarded in it that hadn't been there in months. "I'd
almost forgotten what that felt like."

"Children haven't learned the story yet. That's the whole difference." The scout watched the girl
disappear into the settlement's ordinary evening business. "Might be worth remembering there's still
people out there who'll meet you as the tired part first, before the story ever gets to them."
