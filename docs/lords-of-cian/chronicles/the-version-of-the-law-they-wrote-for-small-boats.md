# The Version of the Law They Wrote for Small Boats

*Locked canon, Batch 263, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1339`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LXVI, wave 22, closing the wave. The storm-interval verification regulation is formally amended with a small-craft addendum, closing the equity gap surfaced at Chronicle LXIV.*

---

The addendum was four paragraphs, which the student thought was absurd given how long it had taken to write them, and which the retired successor thought was exactly right, because the first version had taken three pages and still missed something.

*Any vessel operating beyond the certified radius,* it read, *may request a standing seasonal reading rather than a per-voyage consultation, delivered by whatever means the reading station and the requesting harbor agree between them -- posted notice, relay skiff, or a trained local volunteer certified to relay but not to override.* No fifty-league requirement. No captain's license to put at risk. Just a name on a list and a promise that someone, somewhere, would think of them before the weather turned.

It had taken the student three trips past the old radius, one skiff owner's mended net, and a season of doing the work by hand before anyone at the Trust agreed the gap was real rather than an acceptable cost of scale. The liaison who'd once demanded her ledger on the dock was the one who finally carried the draft up the chain -- not because he owed her anything, he said, but because he'd read enough of her honest misses by then to trust that if she said something was missing, it probably was.

"You didn't write this because the law was wrong," the successor told her, the day the amendment was posted at every certified station on the coast. "You wrote it because you went and looked at what the law actually did to people who weren't in the room when it was made. That's rarer than being right the first time."

The skiff owner who'd mended nets and said nothing about being hungrier some weeks was on the list within the month, her harbor's name added by hand before the ink on the official copy had fully dried. She didn't say much when she heard. She went back to her nets, and the next uncertain week, a relay skiff reached her before she'd have had to guess.

Kanja, watching the amendment get posted, thought less about the words themselves than about how small the fix had turned out to be, once someone had actually gone and found the shape of the hole. "Four paragraphs," he said. "That's all it took."

"Four paragraphs and three trips in a borrowed boat," the student said. "The paragraphs were the easy part."
