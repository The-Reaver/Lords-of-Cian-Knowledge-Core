# The Building That Wouldn't Choose a Side

*Locked canon, Batch 143, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-468`). Jibaro Chronicle II. Homage-era Chicago,
Jibaro/Omoba. Territory Chronicle -- Omoba as protagonist, Kanja an unnamed background guest with no
command, credit, or resolution authorship. New standalone material. No new named characters.*

---

The neighborhood schoolhouse Omoba's people occupied past the one-day threshold wasn't like the
seminary or the empty lot before it — its own history was genuinely mixed, decades of real
after-school programs and community meals run out of its basement alongside decades of documented
neglect toward the same children the programs claimed to serve, a legacy that resisted the clean
either/or "The Occupation" had handled cleanly before.

The doors didn't refuse to yield the way the seminary's had.

"It's not working the way it worked last time," one of Omoba's own people reported, three days in,
genuine confusion in it. "The building's just... a building. Nothing's stopping anyone from walking
in or out."

Omoba spent that evening sitting with the schoolhouse's own history rather than pushing the
occupation further, reading through records of both the genuine good the building had done and the
genuine harm alongside it. "I don't think the gift is failing here. I think it's telling us something.
It only refuses force against institutions with nothing to be ashamed of. This one has plenty to be
ashamed of, mixed in with what it did right. Maybe it can't fully commit to protecting something that
isn't fully innocent."

"So what do we do? Walk away?"

"No. We do the part the gift can't do for us." Omoba gathered his people differently the following
morning — not an occupation any longer, but a negotiation, laying out both the school's real failures
and its real value directly to the district board, demanding specific, binding changes rather than
relying on an ability that had rightly declined to hand them an easy win against something genuinely
complicated. "The gift protects the clean cases. This was never going to be one of those. That doesn't
mean the fight isn't worth having. It just means we have to actually win this one the ordinary way."

The negotiation took months, harder and slower than any occupation, and produced a real, binding
commitment neither pure force nor the gift's protection could have manufactured on its own.

"I used to think the ability made the cause simple," Omoba reflected afterward. "I don't think that
anymore. I think it just tells you, honestly, which fights are simple and which ones were always
going to require the harder work regardless."

An unnamed man who had stood at the schoolhouse's edge throughout the negotiation, offering nothing
but his own quiet presence, left once the agreement was signed.
