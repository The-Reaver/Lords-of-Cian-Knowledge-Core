# The Gathering at the Ghost Fleet's Anchorage

*Locked canon, Batch 199, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-800`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle XLV, wave 15 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Three flagships rode at anchor together for the first time in the fleet's history — *The Audit*, *The
Receipt*, and the newly refitted *The Ledger* — in a hidden cove none of the crew had used twice in a
row all year, gathered for no operational reason beyond the fact that, for once, nothing urgent was
demanding any of them be anywhere else.

Garren Hask had his ledger out, as always, but for once it recorded nothing more consequential than a
running tally of who'd contributed what to the evening's shared meal, laid out across planks between
the three hulls.

"A year since Ghost Harbor," Dol Maren said, settling beside Efa Gol with a plate she hadn't had time to
eat from properly in weeks. "Three ships. A shipwright who's stopped counting how many hulls she's
rebuilt. A decoy operator with a notebook full of plans she never used."

"And a demolitions specialist who's cleared more harbors than she's blown up," Pell Ostra added,
settling in beside them, chalk stains still faint on her forearms from the afternoon's work.

Callum Breck said little, as he still did most evenings, but sat close enough to the fire to be counted
present without needing to be counted for conversation — a comfortable silence the whole crew had long
since learned to read as its own kind of participation.

"Where's Danne Sok?" Corren Halst asked, looking around the gathered fires.

"Charting," half a dozen voices answered at once, with the specific fondness of people who'd stopped
being surprised by it months ago.

Maret Vos arrived last, having spent the afternoon reading a second letter from the settlement he'd
visited months before — the school still thriving, the young woman who taught there sending news of a
new class of children who'd never know what a manifest written in chains had cost the people who'd
taught them to read.

Kanja moved between the fires without settling at any one of them for long, the closest thing the
evening had to a formal presence, listening more than speaking, the same quiet attention he'd given
every crisis all year now simply turned toward a night that wasn't one.

"A year of being a ghost," Garren Hask said eventually, closing his ledger on the meal's final tally
and looking out at the three ships riding easy at anchor. "Feels like it should have cost more than it
has."

"It's cost plenty," Kanja said, thinking of an officer stripped of his commission and a merchant crew
who'd never asked for any of it. "We just don't let the cost be the only thing we remember when we
count what the year actually gave us back."

The fires burned low, the three flagships sat quiet at anchor, and for one night the entire fleet —
scattered across a war that never fully paused for anyone — simply stayed still together, counted and
whole, exactly the way Efa Gol's ritual before every sailing had always hoped to find them.
