# Lauris Chronicle LXI: What Velith Carried Between Visits

*Locked canon, Batch 300, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1678`). Sixty-first entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, the fourteenth entry of Strand K, drafted as part of a second 50-entry wave.
Dramatizes a scheduled Threnarr visit at age ~280, deepening the friendship with Velith already
locked at `MCD-165`/`1553` and the correspondence pattern established at Chronicle XI (`MCD-1628`,
"sometimes, when the Vasks' training schedules allowed it, and less often than either of them would
have liked"). Shows Velith entering Threnarr's defensive complement -- the same body Chronicle XIV
(`MCD-1631`) already establishes was "drawn in part from the cohort of forty-seven children" and in
which Velith is later killed -- extending rather than contradicting that entry. Reuses Iron-Speaker
Vann (`MCD-1627`). No new named characters. Full text below.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Vask Threnarr, age approximately two hundred eighty: "Velith has begun standing
where the line will someday need to hold. She asked me to watch her stance and tell her honestly
whether it would fail. I told her honestly. It will not fail from what I can see of it today."*

I have written already of the exercise log Velith read over Lauris's shoulder at eight, and the
three-line entry she left behind when Lauris departed for Karth-Ven at fourteen, and I have promised
myself, setting down this account, that I would not let those two brief appearances stand as the
whole of what the friendship was before its ending. This entry is my attempt to keep that promise.
It covers a single visit, one of the "less often than either of them would have liked" the Sister-
Hold's own honesty had prepared them both for, some two hundred sixty-six years into a correspondence
that, by the volume surviving in both archives, never once went quiet for longer than a season.

Lauris returned to Threnarr on the training-calendar rotation Karth-Ven and Threnarr's Iron-Speakers
had settled between them years earlier -- a fixed handful of weeks, roughly once a decade, timed to
avoid disrupting either Vask's own instructional calendar. Velith, by this visit, was no longer the
girl who had asked an obvious question about a fight neither of them had understood the same way. She
had entered training with Threnarr's own defensive complement two years prior, a decision her surviving
letters describe with characteristic understatement: *Someone has to stand where the line will need to
hold, and I have decided it may as well be someone who already knows how to watch carefully before she
decides anything.*

*

I want to be honest about what I find difficult in transcribing this entry, which is that I already
know, from Chronicle XIV, how this particular thread of Velith's life ends. I have tried, in setting
this account down, to resist writing it as though the ending were already visible to either woman in
the room -- it was not, by any account either of them left, and I think it would be a disservice to
both of them to let hindsight pull warmth into foreboding where none was intended at the time. What I
have instead is a record of an ordinary visit between two women who had known each other since
before either could properly walk, one of them already the civilization's quiet answer to problems
it could not otherwise solve, the other choosing, in her own smaller and no less deliberate way, to
become part of the answer too.

Velith asked Lauris to watch a defensive stance she had been drilling under Iron-Speaker Vann's own
instruction, the same combatant whose training bout Lauris had once recorded at eight without naming
a winner. I have the exchange from both women's own writing, and it is, I think, the clearest evidence
I have yet found that the "careful witness" discipline Lauris inherited from her earliest years had,
by this point, become something Velith herself had learned to ask for directly, the way one asks a
trusted instrument for a reading rather than a friend for comfort. Lauris told her, plainly, what she
saw: the stance held its weight correctly, and would continue to hold it, provided Velith remembered
to redistribute onto her rear foot a half-second earlier than her instinct currently allowed. Velith's
own note on the correction, written that same evening: *She did not tell me it was good. She told me
exactly what was wrong with it and exactly when it would stop being wrong if I fixed it. I have decided
I prefer this to being told it was good.*

*

They spent the remainder of the visit as I have gathered they spent most of their visits by this
period -- reading each other's logs, Lauris's from Karth-Ven's training floor and Velith's from
Threnarr's own defensive drills, each correcting small inaccuracies in the other's account of events
neither had witnessed firsthand. I have found, among Velith's surviving pages, a line I have chosen to
close this account with, because I believe it says plainly what the correspondence had by then become
for both of them: *We do not write to each other because we miss each other, though we do. We write
because neither of us trusts an account of anything, even our own lives, that only one of us has
checked.*

Lauris departed for Karth-Ven ten days later, on the same schedule that would bring her back to
Threnarr, by the Sister-Hold's own honest estimate, less often than either woman would have liked, for
the better part of a thousand years yet to come.
