# The Hearing at Canal House

*Locked canon, Batch 194, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-645`). The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XL, wave 14 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The summons was unlike anything the canal district had sent him before — not a threat, not a raid
warning, but a formal notice from Canal House, the Sovereign Trust's own administrative seat for
district disputes, calling the Trench Monarch to appear before an inquest into "irregular economic
activity across five contiguous dredge districts."

"It's a trap," Danne Sok said immediately. "They can't beat you in the field, so they're trying to
get you into a room they control."

"Maybe. I'm going anyway." Kanja was already arranging the tally records he'd need to bring. "If I
refuse, every owner on the canal gets to say the numbers couldn't survive a real hearing — that the
whole standard only worked because nobody official ever examined it closely. I'd rather walk into
the room they control and prove the figures hold up there too."

He went unarmed, Onyx left behind by his own choice rather than any requirement, and sat through six
hours of formal questioning from three separate Trust adjudicators, each working from a different
angle — coercion, fraud, unlawful assembly — none of which found purchase against records that had
already survived four independent cross-checks before ever reaching Canal House at all.

"You're asking this tribunal to believe a boy your age built an economic standard spanning five
districts through honest documentation alone," the senior adjudicator said, late in the hearing,
something close to genuine disbelief in the question. "No coercion. No threat implied by your
reputation alone."

"I'm not asking you to believe anything. I'm asking you to check the numbers, the same way five
districts' worth of clerks already have." Kanja laid the full record out for the third time that day.
"If there's coercion in these figures, it'll show in a discrepancy somewhere between what's recorded
and what actually left each site. There isn't one. You're welcome to look as long as you need to."

They looked for three more hours. They found nothing to charge him with, and the inquest closed
without a ruling either way — not an acquittal, not a condemnation, a formal non-finding that left the
five-district standard standing exactly where it had stood before the summons arrived.

"That's not really a victory," Sok observed, as they left Canal House.

"No. It's better than a victory, in a way. A victory would mean I convinced them. A non-finding means
the numbers spoke for themselves, and there was nothing left for me to convince anyone of." Kanja
didn't look back at the building behind them. "I'd rather the record stand on its own than on
anyone's trust in me, including a tribunal's. That's the whole point of keeping it as carefully as we
have."
