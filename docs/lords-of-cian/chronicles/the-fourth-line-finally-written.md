# The Fourth Line, Finally Written

*Locked canon, Batch 264, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1388`). Captain Alias Chronicle LXXXVIII, wave 30. The charter's long-blank fourth clause is finally written, closing the succession thread opened across the run.*

---

Garren Hask called the council session himself, though everyone in the room understood by then that "council session" had come to mean something different than it once had — not an emergency, not a crisis, just the institution doing the slow, patient work it had been built to do. The charter's fourth line had sat blank for years, deliberately, through a health scare, a five-year promise, a rotating chair, and a conversation between Kanja and Efa Gol that none of them had been in the room for but all of them had felt the shape of afterward.

"I think we're finally ready to write it," Hask said, "not because we've solved the problem. Because we've lived long enough with the problem to know what the honest answer actually is, instead of the answer that would have felt safest five years ago."

What went onto the page wasn't a name. Corren Halst had been right about that from the start. It was a structure: the rotating chair, term-limited, open to any member who'd earned the crew's trust the ordinary slow way; the ledger's keeping passed hand to hand through a taught, deliberate lineage rather than inherited by blood or rank; the memorial wall standing as the crew's own memory in stone, outlasting any single person's recollection; and one line, the hardest of the four to agree on wording for, addressing Kanja himself directly — that his own presence, whenever it eventually ended, whether through his death or simply through time finally outpacing what this crew could keep pace with, would be treated exactly like any other departure the charter already protected: mourned, honored, and never once treated as the moment the whole institution stopped being able to stand.

"You're writing me out of being irreplaceable," Kanja said, reading it.

"We're writing you into being remembered instead," Hask said. "That's a better inheritance than irreplaceable ever was. Irreplaceable means it falls apart without you. This means it doesn't."

He signed it last, under his own name rather than any alias, the same act Garren Hask had performed years before when he first called him something other than what the Directorate had.
