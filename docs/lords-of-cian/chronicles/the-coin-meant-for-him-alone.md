# The Coin Meant for Him Alone

*Locked canon, Batch 256, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1152`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle LXVIII, wave 23. The method's first direct personal bribe offered to Kanja himself, distinct from every prior tactical or leverage dispute -- a genuinely new individual-corruption-temptation register.*

---

The administrator waited until Ezio had stepped out to cross-reference a shipment log before he made his offer, which told Kanja most of what he needed to know about the man before he'd said a word. It wasn't a bribe shaped like negotiation — no partial settlement, no lesser figure meant to end the case early. It was a plain, private sum, more than the entire district's back wages combined, offered for one thing only: that the crew move on without ever opening the ledger on this mill at all.

"No one else needs to know we spoke," the administrator said, sliding the figure across on a scrap he clearly intended to burn afterward. "You've moved past a dozen districts already. One more skipped changes nothing to anyone but you."

Kanja didn't raise his voice, didn't reach for the coin, didn't perform outrage for an audience that wasn't there to see it. "You're not offering me money to leave. You're offering me money to decide two hundred workers' wages are worth less than what you're holding out right now. That's the actual trade, whether you say it that way or not."

The administrator tried the figure again, higher, then tried a different angle — that the crew clearly worked for something, that everyone worked for something, that pretending otherwise was its own kind of dishonesty the ledger's own rigor should recognize. It was, Kanja thought afterward, the closest anyone had come to arguing the method's own discipline against itself: if the crew claimed to deal only in what could be proven, wasn't a private offer between two men, provable to no one, exactly the kind of thing that could simply not exist once refused.

"It'll exist," Kanja told him. "Not on any page. But it'll be true, whether anyone writes it down or not, that you offered and I said no. That's enough for me to know what you are, even if it never reaches your workers' ears."

He told Ezio when Ezio came back in, plainly, without theater — not to build a case out of it, since an unwitnessed offer proved nothing an honest ledger could use, but because the crew's own discipline about never adjusting a number to suit a story cut both ways. If Ezio was going to trust every figure Kanja ever handed him without checking it twice, Kanja owed him the same transparency about the one number that never made it onto any page at all. The mill's real ledger opened the next morning exactly as scheduled, the shortfall documented in full, the bribe never mentioned to the workers who benefited from the finding and never needed to know how close it had come to not happening.
