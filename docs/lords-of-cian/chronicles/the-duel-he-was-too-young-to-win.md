# The Duel He Was Too Young to Win

*Locked canon, Batch 194, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-628`). The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XXIII, wave 8 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The net-caster who came for him at the Undertide flats had fished the canal's shallows for longer
than Kanja had been alive, and he did not fight the way soldiers or duelists fought. He fought the
way a man fights something he intends to catch rather than kill — trident in one hand, weighted net
coiled in the other, moving with a patience that had nothing to do with swordcraft at all.

Onyx read intention. It did not read a net, thrown not to wound but to bind, and the first cast took
Kanja's blade arm before Cadence Ruin had found any rhythm in the throw worth collapsing — there was
no rhythm, only a fisherman's unhurried cast, thrown the same way it had been thrown at fish for
thirty years.

"You fight like the reports said," the net-caster said, circling now, trident low. "Doesn't help you
much when there's nothing to read a fight into. I'm not fighting you, boy. I'm catching you."

The second cast tangled Kanja's legs. He went down hard into the shallows, and for several seconds —
longer than any duel in the last year had lasted against him — he simply could not bring the sword to
bear at all, weighted cord biting into his sword arm, the trident's point resting, unhurried, an inch
from his throat.

It was not Onyx that got him free. It was youth, or something like it — the specific, ordinary
strength of an eighteen-year-old body that had spent three years hauling flooded dredge carts before
any of this started, twisting hard enough against the net's give to buy a half-second the fisherman
hadn't accounted for, close enough now to grapple rather than fence. He drove a shoulder into the
man's knee, took the trident's shaft in both hands before the point could finish its work, and won
the rest of it on the ground, in the mud, with no elegance in it at all.

"You didn't cut your way out of that," Corren Halst said afterward, helping him up, the net still
tangled around one boot.

"No. I wrestled my way out of it, because the sword had nothing to work with and my own arms were
the only thing left." Kanja worked the last of the cord loose, unhurried despite the mud still
drying on half his body. "He wasn't wrong, calling me a boy. A grown man doesn't get caught in a net
he should have seen coming. I got lucky that being young enough to still be strong for my size
mattered more today than being skilled enough to see the throw."

"That's not a story that's going to sound as good as the sword ones."

"Then it won't get told as often. That's fine. It's the truer one."
