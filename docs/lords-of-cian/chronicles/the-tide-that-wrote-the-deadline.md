# The Tide That Wrote the Deadline

*Locked canon, Batch 276, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1424`). Bane Alias Chronicle XCIV, wave 32. New
environmental register -- a tidal sea-cave extraction run entirely against a natural deadline, with
Obsidian Malice withheld the whole operation per the water-safety constraint already established in
"What the River Carried Between Them" (`MCD-1100`).*

---

The depot wasn't marked on any map Corren Halst had ever seen, which was the point of it. The
Directorate had cut a holding cell into a sea-cave two miles up the coast from the nearest garrison,
reachable only at low tide through a mouth that drowned itself twice a day on schedule. Six captured
couriers were inside, and the tide chart gave the column one window: in, out, gone, before the sea
came back to take the only way through.

"Three hours," Danne Sok said, reading the water line against the rock the way he read everything
else — carefully, and only once. "Less, if the moon's early."

Bane went in first. The passage narrowed fast, wet stone underfoot, the air thick with brine and old
rope. Mafesto's footing-based Kinetic Transfer System, built to redirect force through solid,
predictable ground, found the slick rock unreliable in a way dry earth never was — every step a
small negotiation with a surface that wanted to slide. He adjusted his stance lower, slower, trusting
weight over speed.

Onyx of Oblivion did the rest. Cadence Ruin read the cave's own resonance — the drip and pull of water
finding its level, the specific wrongness of six heartbeats held too still behind a sealed grate two
turns ahead, a guard's breathing catching on a rhythm that didn't match the room he was standing in.
No torchlight wasted, no wrong turn taken.

The one tool he didn't use at all was Obsidian Malice. Salt water carried a live discharge further
and less predictably than any battlefield he'd fought on, and a cave mouth two-thirds flooded already
was no place to test how far. He told Corren Halst as much before they went in, flat: "It stays dark
the whole way. If it comes to that fight, it's blades." It didn't come to that fight — three guards,
overwhelmed in the dark by Cadence Ruin's read of where they stood before they knew anyone had
entered, went down to plain steel and Mafesto's redirected momentum, nothing more exotic than that.

They found the couriers chained to a shared bar, weak but alive, and the water was already climbing
the passage floor by the time the last chain came free. The landward tunnel out was steeper, drier,
and longer than the way in — a route the depot's own builders had cut for supply carts and never
bothered to seal, because no one sane came at a sea-cave from the wrong tide to begin with. They came
up it at a dead run, Bane carrying the weakest courier over one shoulder, and broke into open air with
the sea already boiling white through the mouth they'd used to get in.

Danne Sok looked back at the water sealing itself behind them like a door closing on schedule.
"Cutting it close."

"The tide doesn't negotiate," Bane said. "Everything else in this world does, one way or another.
That's why I like fighting people instead of it."

but unreliably, a different limitation. First entry structured around a natural (tidal) deadline
rather than an enemy-imposed or exhaustion-driven one. No new named characters -- Corren Halst and
