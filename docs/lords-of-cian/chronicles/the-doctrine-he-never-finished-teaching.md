# The Doctrine He Never Finished Teaching

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XLIV. Rebellion era,
age 25, some time after "The Second Student" (wave eight, this same alias). Not a territory
Chronicle. New standalone material — a bittersweet reflective entry. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters beyond the already-locked student.*

---

The student found him during a rare stretch without an engagement pending, and asked a question he'd
apparently been carrying for some time without finding the right moment to raise it.

"You taught me the technique. You taught me the discipline behind it — verify twice, know the
difference between what I can bring down and what I should, all of it." He hesitated. "You never
taught me why you built it in the first place. Not the practical reason. The actual one."

Kanja considered the question longer than its simplicity suggested it deserved. "I'm not sure I have
taught anyone that, including myself, in words that hold up well once I try to say them out loud."

"Try anyway. I'd rather have an incomplete answer than none."

"I built it because I got tired of structures deciding things about people's lives without anyone
bothering to understand how those structures actually held together first — a wall, a gate, a
Crawler, doesn't matter which. Something holds a lot of power over what's possible for the people
around it, and almost nobody who wields that power has ever actually listened to what it's made of.
I decided I wanted to be someone who had." He paused. "That's not a complete answer. I don't think I
have a complete one. I've spent years building the technique and I'm not certain I've ever fully
articulated the reason underneath it, even to myself."

"Then I suppose I'll keep carrying an incomplete answer too."

"Maybe that's honest, in its own way. I don't think this doctrine was ever meant to arrive finished
— not the technique, and apparently not the reasoning behind it either. You'll keep filling in your
own version of the answer as you use it. I'm still filling in mine."

The student left with the same open question he'd arrived with, no more resolved than before, and
found, somewhat to his own surprise, that the honesty of an unfinished answer sat easier with him
than a tidy one would have.
