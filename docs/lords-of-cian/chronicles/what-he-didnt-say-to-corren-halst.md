# What He Didn't Say to Corren Halst

*Locked canon, Batch 194, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-639`). The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XXXIV, wave 12 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Corren Halst had learned, over the months since Deepcut, to recognize the particular quiet Kanja fell
into on nights after something had gone right in every visible way — a settlement reached, a site
held, a crowd that had believed every word of a speech that had, in fact, been entirely true. It was
not the quiet of exhaustion. It took him longer to place what it actually was.

"You've been like this since the market sheet went up," he said finally, the two of them alone on
watch, the rest of the crew asleep. "Everyone else thinks it's good news. A printed sheet, your name
carried further than a rumor ever traveled."

"It is good news. I know it's good news." Kanja didn't dispute the fact of it. "I just don't know
anymore whether I believe the thing that's being carried."

"What do you mean?"

"I mean the sheet says a king does not deny and does not exaggerate. That's a fine thing to read
about someone. I don't know if it's fully true about me, or if it's just true often enough that
nobody's caught the difference yet." He kept his eyes on the dark water rather than on Halst. "Every
correction I've ever made, I made because I found the error myself, before anyone else could. What
happens the day I don't find one? What happens the day the sheet says something about me that isn't
true, and I don't catch it in time to fix it?"

"Then you fix it when you catch it. Same as always."

"That's easy to say when the errors have all been small enough to fix quietly. I don't know that
they'll always be small." Kanja was quiet again for a moment. "I think what actually unsettles me
isn't the sheet. It's that I don't fully know, some nights, whether I believe my own reputation the
way everyone else has started to. I know the numbers are true. I don't always know if the story
built on top of the numbers is."

Halst didn't offer reassurance immediately, which was, Kanja would think later, the right instinct —
not agreement, not argument, just space to let the doubt finish saying what it needed to say.

"I don't think you have to believe the story," Halst said eventually. "I think you just have to keep
checking the numbers the way you always have. The story's other people's job to believe or not. Yours
is just to keep making sure it stays true enough that believing it isn't a mistake."

Kanja considered that, turning it over slowly. "That's a smaller job than the one the sheet describes."

"Maybe. I think it's the only one that was ever actually yours to do."

Nothing was resolved that night, not fully — the doubt didn't leave, only found somewhere honest to
sit instead of somewhere unspoken — but it was, Kanja would recognize later, the first time he'd said
any of it aloud to anyone at all.
