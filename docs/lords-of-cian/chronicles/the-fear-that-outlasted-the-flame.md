# The Fear That Outlasted the Flame

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XXVIII, wave 9.
Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The rumor had no single origin anyone could trace, only a shape that repeated itself, district to
district, ahead of the campaign's own arrival: that the Lord of Embers rebuilt quickly, yes, but only
the districts that impressed him; that the ones who couldn't organize fast enough, who were too poor
or too broken already to help themselves, got left to the ash they'd been given. No burning had ever
actually confirmed it. It didn't need to. By the time The Anvil reached the district where the rumor
had taken deepest root, half the settlement refused the crew's help outright, certain that accepting
it and somehow failing to measure up would be worse than never having accepted it at all.

"We heard what happens to the ones who can't keep up," the district's own eldest resident told him,
arms crossed, refusing even to let the crew survey the damage. "Figured better to manage what little's
left ourselves than to owe a debt we can't pay back fast enough."

Kanja had nothing to burn faster and nothing to rebuild stronger that could answer this. The ability
that had outlasted every punitive strike thrown at it across the entire campaign had no purchase at
all against a fear that had never needed a fire to begin with.

"There's no version of this where I judge a district for how fast it heals," he said, and understood,
saying it, that the words alone would not be enough — that the rumor had traveled further and dug in
deeper than any single conversation could undo in an afternoon. "But I can't prove that to you by
telling you. I can only prove it by staying, and rebuilding at whatever pace this district can actually
manage, and still being here at the end of it."

He stayed eleven days longer than the schedule allowed for, working at a pace the district itself set
rather than one the campaign's record demanded, asking nothing of anyone that hadn't already been
freely offered. By the time The Anvil finally moved on, the rebuild was slower than any other site on
the tour and, by every measure that mattered to the people who'd lived through it, complete.

"You could've moved on days ago," the elder admitted, watching the barge prepare to leave. "Would've
been the smarter use of your time."

"Maybe. But the fastest rebuild in the world doesn't undo a fear this district's been carrying for
months before I ever arrived." Kanja looked back at the district, ash long since cleared, nothing
about it hurried. "Some things don't metabolize the way a burning does. You can't out-rebuild a rumor.
You can only outlast it, honestly, for as long as it takes people to stop believing it."
