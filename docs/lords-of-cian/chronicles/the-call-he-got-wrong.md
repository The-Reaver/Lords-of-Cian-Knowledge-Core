# The Call He Got Wrong

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Captain Alias Chronicle, wave 7. Rebellion era. Not
a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters beyond
already-locked crew.*

---

The raid went wrong in the specific way Kanja had insisted it wouldn't — he'd overridden Corren
Halst's read of the tide timing, certain his own sense of the causeway's flooding pattern was more
reliable, and it wasn't. Six crew members ended up waist-deep in rising water two hundred yards from
the extraction point with the causeway they'd planned to use already submerged, and it was only
Obsidian Malice's discharge blasting a secondary path through a collapsed wall that got them out
without anyone drowning.

Nobody was badly hurt. That didn't change what it had cost, or whose read of the situation had been
right.

"I want it said plainly," Kanja told the crew that night, before anyone could soften it for him.
"Corren called the tide correctly. I overrode it because I trusted my own instinct over someone
who's read these waters longer than I've been alive in this body. That was a mistake, and it put
six of you in water that could have killed you."

"You got us out," someone offered.

"I got us out of a problem I caused. That's not the same as not causing it." He looked at Corren
Halst directly. "Next time you call a read against mine, and I disagree, I want you to say so twice
before I'm allowed to override you again. Once clearly isn't enough anymore. I've shown I can talk
myself past it."

Corren Halst, who had spent the whole debrief expecting to be the one apologizing for not pushing
harder in the moment, hadn't expected this instead. "You don't have to make it a rule over one bad
call."

"I do, actually. Because the alternative is that I quietly decide I was right this time and wrong
last time, and the crew never actually learns whether their captain's judgment can be corrected or
whether it just gets thanked for the recovery afterward." He didn't dress it up further. "I was
wrong. You were right. That has to cost me something, or it doesn't mean anything the next time it
happens."

The rule held. Three months later, Corren Halst called a route wrong against Kanja's own
preference, said so twice, and Kanja deferred without argument — and the crew noticed, the way
crews always notice which promises actually survive contact with pride.
