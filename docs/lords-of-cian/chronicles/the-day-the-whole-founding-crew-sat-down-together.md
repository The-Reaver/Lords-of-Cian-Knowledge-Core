# The Day the Whole Founding Crew Sat Down Together

*Locked canon, Batch 264, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1389`). Captain Alias Chronicle LXXXIX, wave 30. A warm, conflict-free founding-anniversary gathering, the sub-series' fullest celebratory entry.*

---

Nobody planned it as a formal occasion, which was, everyone agreed afterward, exactly why it worked. Efa Gol simply noted, checking a date against the ledger for an unrelated reason, that it had been exactly that many years since the morning after Iron Shallows, when Garren Hask had first called a young man fresh off a battle he barely believed he'd survived something other than the alias the Directorate had already begun writing into its files. She said it out loud at the evening meal, more observation than announcement, and somehow by the following week half the crew's history was sitting at the same long table without anyone having sent a single formal invitation.

Corren Halst came, between her rotating-chair duties, still carrying the same steady bearing that had once led a split engagement without needing him in the room. Callum Breck came with the trainee he'd taught the shore-watch craft to, proud in a way he'd never have shown before Ghost Harbor taught him a different kind of confidence. Garren Hask, slower now but stubbornly present, brought the notebook that had started this whole run of years, and the ledger's new keeper beside him, the same person who'd once sounded out letters under Kanja's patient finger. Efa Gol sat beside Mira, twenty now and fully, unhurriedly herself. Danne Sok's daughter came in off a real posting to tell stories that made the newer hands laugh. Maret Vos came too, quietly, the way he did most things, having chosen this a second time and never once regretted saying so out loud.

Pell Ostra told the story of the prank she'd spent a year planning, again, exaggerated further each time she told it. Sera came down from her hall for the evening, healer's satchel left behind for once, just a daughter among the people who'd raised her alongside her own father.

Kanja said little most of the night, content to let the table run itself the way the whole crew had learned to. When someone finally asked him to speak, he didn't reach for anything grand.

"I've had a lot of names," he said. "This is the only one any of you ever gave me for free."

No new named characters. Second entry, wave 30.*
