# What He Told the Ones Who Wanted to Follow Him

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle III, wave 14,
closing the wave. Rebellion era (age 19-21), unarmed and non-combat throughout, matching the alias's
established ethos. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters.*

---

Seven workers came to the ledger tent the morning the district's settlement closed, not to give
testimony — that was already done — but to ask, together, to be taken along when the crew moved on
to the next district.

"We've seen what this does," the one who spoke for the group said. "We want to be part of it. Not
watch it happen somewhere else next season. Be there when it does."

Kanja had turned down requests like it before, quietly, one worker at a time, but never all at once,
and he took the question seriously enough to sit with them properly rather than answer on his way
out the tent flap.

"I understand why you want that," he said. "I'm not going to take you with me. Not because you
wouldn't be useful — you would. Because this district needs seven people who know exactly how this
method works standing in it after I'm gone, not seven fewer people standing in it because they
decided the work only mattered wherever I happened to be walking next."

"But it works because you're there," one of the seven said. "We've all seen it. The name matters."

"The name gets doors open faster the first time. After that, it's the method doing the work, not me
— you watched the tanning district figure that out on its own with no one from my crew even asking a
question. If the only place this can happen is wherever I'm standing, I've built something that dies
the day I do, or the day I'm needed somewhere else and can't be in two places." He looked at each of
them in turn. "Your district's owners know your names now. They know what a completed ledger looks
like, what a fair settlement sounds like, what it costs them to be caught underpaying. That knowledge
doesn't travel with me. It stays here, in you, whether I'm in this district or three hundred miles
from it."

"So what do we do instead?"

"The same thing here that I do somewhere else. Ask the same four words. Start with the worst-off.
Write it down exact, correct it upward when someone's too afraid to ask for the whole truth. You
don't need my name attached to any of it to do it right — you need the discipline, and you've already
watched it closely enough to have most of that already."

They didn't all stay. Two left with the crew's blessing anyway, judged genuinely suited to travel and
teach the method elsewhere. The other five stayed in their own district, and within the year had run
two more ledgers of their own, unassisted, in neighboring towns Kanja's crew never visited at all.
