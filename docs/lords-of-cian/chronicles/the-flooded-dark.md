# The Flooded Dark

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XX, wave 7.
Rebellion era, age 18, before the Black Trench -- Mafesto remains dormant and Obsidian Malice
undeployed at this point in the timeline, so this Chronicle is a solo-blade entry testing Onyx of
Oblivion against an environmental extreme rather than an opposing swordsman. Not a territory
Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The ambush at the Underlow culvert was built around a single fact its planners understood better
than any prior opponent had: every torch the crew carried into the flooded access tunnel would
eventually go under water, and Onyx of Oblivion's gifts, whatever else they could do, could not put
light where there was none.

The first torch died forty paces in. The second went out at the first submerged bend, and after
that nobody tried to relight a third.

"We should pull back," Corren Halst said quietly, his voice flat and close in the total dark, water
at his knees and rising. "We can't fight what we can't see."

"We can't see with our eyes. That's not the same thing." Kanja's voice didn't carry the strain
Halst's did. "Whisper of Shadows was never really about light. It un-hides intention, not shapes. In
the dark, it's the only sense any of us have left that actually works."

The first attacker came from the left, and Kanja turned into the strike a half-second before it
would have been visible even in daylight — not because he'd heard the man, though he had, but
because something under the hearing had already flagged the shape of the intention closing on him.
Onyx met steel it couldn't see with a precision that shouldn't have been possible for a blade guided
by nothing but water-muffled sound and a gift built for shadow, not blindness.

It was not, by any measure, a clean fight. Twice the sword's edge found only water where an attacker
should have been, the gift's un-hiding no substitute for eyes when a man held perfectly still against
a current instead of moving toward his intention. A third exchange landed a shallow cut along Kanja's
forearm before Onyx corrected, Cadence Ruin catching a rhythm the darkness had almost let slip past
entirely.

They cleared the culvert in absolute darkness, six attackers down, one crew member gasping and
half-drowned from a current that had nearly done what no blade in the tunnel had managed, and came up
into torchlight looking, all of them, considerably less certain than the story that would eventually
be told about it.

"That was closer than any of the daylight fights," Halst said, wringing water from his sleeve.

"It was." Kanja didn't dress it up as anything else. "The sword reads intention. It doesn't read
water, and it doesn't read a man who's learned to hold still in the current instead of moving toward
me. Whoever built that ambush understood exactly what Onyx isn't, better than anyone's understood it
before. I'd like to know who taught them that, before they get the chance to teach anyone else."
