# Three Hundred Voices, One Lie

*Locked canon, Batch 112, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-384`). Crow King Alias Chronicle II. Rebellion era, a
new deployment of the Hymn-Engine (`MCD-236`) against a different, more sophisticated sensor
network than the one it was invented against. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material.
Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The Trust's answer to the Voskharen Wetlands humiliation had taken eight months to build: a
triple-redundant thermal grid layered across the Coalfell garrison's perimeter, engineered
specifically so that no single false reading, however convincing, could fool all three overlapping
systems at once. Its designers had studied the Hymn-Engine's ~19 Hz sub-bass chant exhaustively and
concluded, correctly, that a single synchronized frequency could no longer produce a uniform false
signature across three sensor arrays calibrated to disagree with each other by design.

They had not accounted for three hundred voices singing three different lies at once.

Kanja's people had spent six weeks rehearsing what the crew came to call the Braid — not one chant
but three, interleaved and layered across the full fighting company, each sub-group's frequency
tuned to falsify exactly one of the three redundant arrays while leaving the other two, from that
sub-group's own position, reading true. No single voice lied to all three systems. Every voice lied
convincingly to one, and the arrays, designed to distrust each other into revealing a single
coordinated deception, instead cross-confirmed three different fabricated patterns as three
independently corroborating truths.

"It shouldn't be possible," the garrison's own sensor technician would tell the inquiry board weeks
later, still visibly shaken. "The whole design assumed a liar only tells one lie. Nobody built for a
liar who tells three lies at once, each one true enough that the other two systems vouch for it."

The compound emptied across four hours under a triple-redundant grid that recorded, with total
internal consistency, three simultaneous and mutually corroborating pictures of nothing happening
at all. By the time the false thermal bloom finally faded from all three arrays at once — the Braid's
final, coordinated resolution, timed to the second across three hundred voices spread over a mile of
marsh — the compound's last fighter had already crossed clear, and the only evidence the Trust ever
recovered was a second scarecrow, crowned in black feathers, propped on the array control station's
own housing.

"You built the Hymn-Engine to fool one system," the crew's lead singer said afterward, hoarse from
six weeks of rehearsal and four hours of sustained performance, "and everyone since has been trying
to build a sensor grid that can't be fooled the same way twice. Nobody's stopped to ask what happens
when the lie doesn't repeat. It just gets more voices."
