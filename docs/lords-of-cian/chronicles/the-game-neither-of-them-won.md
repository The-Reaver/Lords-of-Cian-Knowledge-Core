# The Game Neither of Them Won

*Locked canon, Batch 201, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-853`). The Crow King Alias Chronicle XXXVIII, wave 13 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The Directorate tactician assigned to the Voss Corridor had studied every documented Crow King
engagement with a thoroughness that matched, and in places exceeded, the crew's own after-action
reviews — not the same officer who'd once attacked trust itself, nor the intelligence officer who'd
later understood the craft's mechanism directly, but a third mind entirely, one who had drawn a
different lesson from both of their failures: don't try to catch the lie, and don't try to poison
trust either. Simply refuse to commit resources to anything not independently confirmed by at least
two unrelated sources.

It was, on its own terms, a sound doctrine, and it held.

Every false signal Kanja's people seeded across six days of careful preparation met the same
response: acknowledged, logged, and never acted upon without a second, unrelated confirmation that
never arrived, because there was nothing real behind the first signal to generate one. The
tactician's caution cost him nothing tangible and denied Kanja's people every opening the deception
was meant to create.

But caution that thorough had its own cost, one the tactician hadn't fully weighed: real information,
arriving through a single legitimate source, met exactly the same skepticism as everything false had.
A genuine warning about troop movement two days later was logged and shelved alongside a week of
discarded false signals, treated with identical suspicion, and acted on too late to matter.

Neither side won the corridor. Kanja's deception failed outright, cleanly and completely; the
tactician's own doctrine, applied without exception, cost him a real advantage his caution had made
him incapable of recognizing when it actually arrived.

"He beat the craft," the crew's own planner said afterward, reviewing a week that had produced
nothing for either side. "That's not nothing."

"He didn't beat it. He made himself equally blind to the lie and the truth, and paid for both in
different currencies. I don't think either of us actually won anything at Voss Corridor. I think we
both discovered a real limit, his and mine, and neither of us got to walk away from it feeling
clever." Kanja didn't dress the outcome up as anything other than what it was. "Sometimes the honest
account of an engagement is that nobody comes out ahead. I'd rather write that down plainly than
pretend a draw was secretly a win."
