# The Gate the River Swallowed

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XLVI, wave 16. Rebellion era. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The Directorate had raised a sluice gate at the narrows to choke off river traffic feeding three
downstream settlements, and unlike every prior target the doctrine had ever been asked to read, this
one sat fully submerged — a full arm's length of moving current between Kanja and the iron itself.

He knelt at the bank first rather than wading in.

"Every gate I've ever read, I've read through air," he said, half to himself, half to Mafesto's
listening awareness. "Water doesn't carry the harmonic the same way. It doesn't lose it, either. It
just changes what it sounds like on the way through."

The first attempt taught him that directly. He reached the Aegis-Talisman's frequency toward the gate
from the bank and the return came back muddled, the current itself layering its own constant hiss
over whatever tension signature the ironwork actually carried, indistinguishable at that remove from
ordinary river noise.

"Reading it from dry ground isn't reading it at all," he admitted. "It's guessing with better
posture."

He went in. Not to the gate itself yet, but to the anchor chains bolted into the bank stone that held
it against the current's full pressure — chains that broke the surface, half wet and half dry, close
enough to touch with a hand rather than a broadcast. Direct contact carried the true signature past
the water's interference entirely, the way a hand on a wall had always told him more than a reading
thrown across open air ever could.

"The gate itself, I still can't read clean through the current," he said, tracing the chain's tension
down toward the submerged fitting. "But the chain doesn't care what's between us. It only cares that
I'm touching it."

The anchor points gave up the gate's real weak seam — a fitting rushed during construction, tensioned
unevenly against the current's steady push. Obsidian Malice's discharge went into the chain rather
than the gate directly, the resonance carried down through solid contact into the ironwork beneath the
waterline, and the fitting gave all at once, the gate swinging wide on the current's own force before
the garrison upstream had finished registering that anything had happened at all.

Three settlements' water traffic opened again within the hour. Kanja stood on the bank afterward,
still dripping, turning the lesson over more than the victory.

"Every structure I've ever read, I read through whatever carries the sound to me," he said. "Water
just meant I had to get closer than I wanted to, instead of trusting the distance I'm used to." He
looked back at the current, already smoothing itself over where the gate had stood. "I don't think
that's a weakness in the doctrine. I think it's the doctrine, working exactly the way it always has,
somewhere I simply hadn't stood yet."
