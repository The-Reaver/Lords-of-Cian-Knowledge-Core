# The Village That Walked Out Singing

*Locked canon, Batch 201, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-852`). The Crow King Alias Chronicle XXXVII, wave 13 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The village of Kettren sat directly in the path of a punitive sweep ordered against a district
suspected, wrongly, of sheltering rebel supply lines — three days' warning before the Directorate
column arrived, not nearly enough time to evacuate several hundred residents across open country
without the column's own scouts noticing a mass departure and treating it as confirmation of
exactly the guilt they'd already assumed.

An ordinary evacuation would have looked like flight. Flight would have looked like guilt.

"They need to leave in a way that doesn't look like leaving at all," Kanja told the village elders,
who had spent the three days growing more frightened rather than less. "Kettren holds a harvest
procession every year around this season, doesn't it? Singing, a route out past the eastern fields,
half the district usually turns out for it."

"Not for another two months."

"The column doesn't know that."

Over two days, word spread quietly through Kettren and three neighboring settlements that the
harvest procession had been moved early this year, an explanation ordinary enough that nobody
outside the district questioned it — and on the morning the column's scouts finally sighted the
village, they saw exactly what the explanation promised: several hundred people walking openly,
singing, carrying baskets and banners down the eastern road in broad daylight, nothing furtive about
any of it, nothing resembling the frightened, silent flight a guilty village would attempt.

The column's commander, watching an apparent festival pass in full view rather than any attempt at
concealment, concluded the district's suspected involvement had been overstated and redirected his
sweep elsewhere — the entire population of Kettren clear of the punitive action by the time anyone
thought to question a procession that had, in fact, never once tried to hide.

"We didn't disguise the leaving," the village's own elder said afterward, still working out how
something so open had worked at all. "Everyone could see us."

"That's exactly why it worked. Nobody hides from something they're not ashamed of. You walked out in
full view, singing, because that's what an innocent village actually does. I just made sure the
column arrived already believing that's what they were about to see."
