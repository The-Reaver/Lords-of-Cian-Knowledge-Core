# The Report That Named the Wrong Man

*Locked canon, Batch 201, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-849`). The Crow King Alias Chronicle XXXIV, wave 12 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

An unrelated rebel cell, operating entirely independently and unknown to Kanja's own crew, had
sabotaged a Directorate supply depot using timing and misdirection crude enough to superficially
resemble the Hymn-Engine's reporting-network method — and Directorate intelligence, primed for years
to see the Crow King's hand in anything sufficiently clever, attributed the operation to him within
a day, with reprisals against the surrounding villages already being drafted before anyone checked
the claim against anything resembling evidence.

Nobody had framed him deliberately. The mistake was simpler and more dangerous than that: his own
reputation had grown large enough to absorb credit for things he'd had no part in at all, and the
absorption was about to cost people who'd never met him.

"We can't prove a negative quickly enough to stop the reprisal order," the crew's own intelligence
officer said, laying out the problem plainly. "Denying it does nothing. Everyone already assumes a
denial is exactly what he'd say."

Kanja didn't deny it publicly at all. Instead, within two days, he arranged for a very different,
genuinely verifiable operation — a small, low-risk supply diversion, carried out openly enough and
documented carefully enough that its timing and method couldn't possibly be confused with the
original sabotage, and delivered it directly into Directorate hands as unmistakable, dated proof that
the two operations were plainly not the same craft at all.

"You committed an actual operation just to prove you didn't commit a different one," the officer
said, still working through the strangeness of it.

"A denial is just another claim, and claims are cheap next to what's already been decided about me.
Proof is different. If I show them exactly what my own method actually looks like, dated and
undeniable, right next to the sabotage they've already attributed to me, the mismatch does the
denying for me far better than my own word ever could." The reprisal order was quietly withdrawn
within the week, the actual cell responsible never identified, never pursued by either side. "I
don't know who did the original work. I hope they're careful. But I wasn't going to let a village pay
for a reputation I built getting credited for something it never earned."
