# The Fever That Moved Faster Than the Column

*Locked canon, Batch 254, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1101`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXXI, wave 24. Pure
humanitarian entry, no combat -- a fever outbreak forces a genuine no-good-options quarantine
decision inside a refugee camp.*

---

The fever moved through the camp faster than anyone could count the sick, and by the time Bane's
column reached it the camp's own healer had already run out of anywhere clean to put the newest
cases.

He didn't take over her ward. He asked her what she needed and then spent the next two days doing
exactly what she told him to, nothing more — hauling water to be boiled, clearing a second structure
for isolation, standing watch on a quarantine line so no one crossed it out of grief or panic rather
than because the sickness allowed it.

The decision that actually mattered wasn't his to make and he didn't pretend otherwise. The healer
came to him on the second night with the number that made it real: moving the whole camp toward the
column's own protected corridor, as planned, would carry the fever into a population of six hundred
healthy refugees who hadn't been exposed yet. Leaving the sickest under guarded isolation, with a
skeleton crew to tend them, gave the rest a real chance and left perhaps a dozen people behind who
might not survive either way.

"You don't need my permission," Bane told her. "You need someone to carry the decision with you so
it isn't only yours."

"That's not nothing."

They left six people behind with two volunteers and enough supplies to last a week, and moved the
rest of the camp out at first light. A Directorate patrol was two days out and closing, and there was
no version of the choice that let everyone win.

Efa Gol, walking the column's edge that morning, asked him plainly whether it was the hardest call
he'd sat in on yet.

"I didn't make it," Bane said. "That's most of what made it bearable to sit in on. She's the one who
has to live with whether it was right. All I did was make sure she didn't have to decide it alone."
