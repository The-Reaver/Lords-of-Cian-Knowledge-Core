# Lauris Chronicle LX: The Century That Learned to Sit Still

*Locked canon, Batch 300, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1677`). Sixtieth entry in Lauris Letitia's own Chronicle
series, the thirteenth entry of Strand K (Kares Prime / deep past), drafted as part of a second
50-entry wave across eight strand-agents under Abad's blanket authorization. Opens the
previously-undramatized ~1,100-year span between the close of the no-ceiling calibration period
(age 114, Chronicle XIII, `MCD-1630`) and Velith's death (age ~1,200, Chronicle XIV, `MCD-1631`).
Dramatizes the Sister-Hold's formal transition from crisis-rotation training to a standing,
permanent instructor roster, and Tiramen's earliest private observations that would, decades later,
begin the work locked at `MCD-169`. No new named characters. Full text below.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Vask Karth-Ven, age approximately one hundred thirty: "Voreth was not relieved
today. Neither was I. I have decided this means the century is over, though no one told me so
directly."*

I have set down already the century that made Voreth's own account of being relieved four times in
a single session, and Veska's line about a child who does not stop, the two facts by which most
people who have heard of Lauris Letitia's early training understand it at all. What I set down now
is quieter, and I think, in its way, more difficult to write plainly, because nothing in it is
dramatic enough to have survived in the Vask's own oral record the way those two facts did. It is
the account of how a household reorganizes itself around an emergency once the emergency stops
being new.

The change did not arrive as an announcement. I have looked for one in the Karth-Ven training log
and found none -- no formal session in which Veska, Tiramen, and Voreth sat down and declared the
calibration period concluded. What I have found instead is smaller and, I think, truer to how
institutions actually change: a gradual thinning of the word "anomalous" from beside Lauris's
density readings in Tiramen's own hand, until by her hundred-and-thirtieth year it appears nowhere
at all, replaced only by the number itself, recorded with the same flat regularity any other
figure in the archive received.

*

The eleven-substitution days Voreth once described did not stop happening because Lauris's capacity
had changed. They stopped happening because the Vask stopped treating each one as an emergency
requiring improvised coverage, and built, instead, a standing roster: three instructors, sometimes
four when Karth-Ven could spare a fourth, rotating through fixed shifts on a fixed calendar rather
than being pulled from whatever else they had been doing when a session ran long. It is, I think,
the least glamorous fact I have yet transcribed from her Kares Prime record, and also one of the
more instructive -- a civilization thirty-one thousand years into the practice of remembering
everything it does had, in Lauris, finally found something it could not remember its way into a
category for, and had solved the problem the way such civilizations solve most problems: not with
awe, but with scheduling.

I have the entry for the day the fragment above records, the first full session in longer than
either Voreth or Lauris could specifically recall in which no substitution was required at all --
not because Lauris had tired, which she had not and would not, by every account, ever again in her
recorded life, but because Voreth herself had, over the preceding decade, built enough of her own
stamina and technique around the problem to hold a full session unrelieved for the first time. Voreth
records the moment with unusual plainness for a woman whose earlier entries lean toward dry humor:
*I did not need relieving today. I do not know whether this means I have become adequate to her, or
whether it only means I have finally stopped expecting to fail before the session ends. I suspect it
is the second, and I have decided I do not mind.*

*

Tiramen's own entries from this period are the ones I find myself returning to most, not because
they record anything eventful, but because I can see, reading them in sequence, the first quiet
motions of the woman who would spend her hundred-and-fortieth through two-hundred-and-twentieth
years building something the Vask's existing methodology had no name for. There is no single entry
in which this begins. There is only a pattern -- Tiramen, across a run of otherwise ordinary
sessions, beginning to keep a second set of notes alongside the official log, not about what Lauris
could currently do, but about what the shape of her progress across four separate variables
suggested about what she might, eventually, need a training architecture capable of holding. I will
not claim, transcribing this now, that Tiramen already knew what she was building toward. I do not
believe she did. I believe only that a careful woman had started asking a question the existing
archive had no answer for, and that careful women, on Kares Prime, tended eventually to write the
answer down themselves.

Veska's own contribution to this period is a single line, recorded on an evening she and Voreth
shared training-floor cleanup after the roster's first full month, and I have chosen to close this
account with it because I think it says, in fewer words than I have managed across this entire
entry, exactly what the century's ending actually amounted to: *We do not know what we are
calibrating for. We said that when she arrived. We are still saying it. I find I no longer say it as
a question.*
