# When the Plan Broke Mid-Song

*Locked canon, Batch 201, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-841`). The Crow King Alias Chronicle XXVI, wave 9 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Every prior deployment of the Braid had been the product of weeks of rehearsal, its timing fixed
and its structure settled long before a single voice was raised in the field — which was precisely
why nothing in six weeks of preparation had accounted for a wind shift strong enough to carry the
third interleaved chant twenty degrees off the angle it needed to hold, halfway through the second
hour of a four-hour deployment.

The apprentice caught it first, the deviation audible to her trained ear seconds before it would
have become audible to the sensor grid it was meant to fool.

"Frequency's drifting on the third line," she said, low, to the two singers nearest her, already
adjusting her own pitch to compensate before she'd finished the sentence. "Follow me down a
half-step. Now."

There was no time to signal the full company, no rehearsed correction built for exactly this failure
— only a live, unrehearsed judgment call, made in the middle of a four-hour performance, with three
hundred people's safety resting on whether eleven singers scattered across a mile of exposed ground
could hear an adjustment through wind and distance and follow it without a single audible break in
the pattern the sensors were already reading as true.

They did. Not perfectly — the deviation left a faint, momentary irregularity that a sharper ear than
any listening that night might have caught, had one been present — but close enough that the false
signature held, the compound emptied on schedule, and no one beyond the singers themselves ever knew
how close six weeks of preparation had come to unraveling over a wind nobody had planned for.

"We've never had to fix it live before," the apprentice admitted afterward, hoarse in a way that had
nothing to do with the usual physical cost of the Braid. "Every other time, we rehearsed until the
mistake couldn't happen. Tonight the mistake happened anyway, and there wasn't a rehearsal answer
for it."

"There never is, eventually," Kanja told her. "You can prepare for everything you can imagine going
wrong, and something will still go wrong that nobody imagined. Tonight you found out you can catch
it live, under real pressure, without me standing next to you telling you what to do. That's worth
more than another six weeks of rehearsal would have been."
