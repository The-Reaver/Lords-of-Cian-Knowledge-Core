# What Callum Breck Remembered of the Water

*Locked canon, Batch 185, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-542`). The Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias
Chronicle XV, closing the fifth wave. Rebellion era, age 21, some months after Ghost Harbor. Not a
territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters beyond the already-locked Callum Breck.*

---

Callum Breck had said the words that saved the fleet at Ghost Harbor after four months of silence
following Nev Torr's death at the Black Trench — a fact the crew knew in outline, repeated often
enough that it had started to harden into legend rather than memory. He'd never once told anyone what
the water itself had actually meant to him in that moment, until a younger sailor, newly assigned to
the fleet, asked him directly.

"Everyone tells the story like the words were the whole thing," Breck said, watching the same waters
that had once carried his silence and then finally broken it. "The words mattered. I don't think
they were the actual turn, though. I think the turn happened earlier, out here, before I ever spoke."

"What happened out here?"

"I used to come stand at this rail during the silent months, not to think, just to be somewhere Nev
would have liked. The water doesn't ask anything of you. It doesn't need you to talk. I think that's
the only reason I could stand to be near people at all, those months — the water let me be silent
without anyone needing it fixed." Breck's voice carried the same deliberate, functional care it always
had, four months of rebuilding still audible in every measured word. "The night I finally spoke, it
wasn't because I'd decided to. It was because the ship needed the words to survive, and by then I'd
spent enough months at this same rail that speaking, when it actually mattered, didn't feel like
betraying the silence. It felt like the water had finally given me back what it had been holding for
me the whole time."

"Nobody's ever told that part."

"Because I never told it. I don't know that it changes the story much, in the end. The fleet still
needed the words, and I still gave them. I think it matters to me, though, that people understand the
words didn't come from nowhere. They came from months of this exact water, this exact rail, holding a
silence I needed until I didn't anymore." Breck looked out over Ghost Harbor's own waters a final
moment before returning to his duties. "I don't think I'd have found my voice anywhere else. I think
the sea gave it back to me as much as I found it myself."
