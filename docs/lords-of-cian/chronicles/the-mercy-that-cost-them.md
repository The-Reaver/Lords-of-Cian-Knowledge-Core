# The Mercy That Cost Them

*Locked canon, Batch 210, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-912`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle XLVI, wave 16. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The Trust supply officer captured aboard the requisition sloop was young, frightened, and by every
account Dol Maren could find in the ship's own papers, honest — a quartermaster's clerk who'd signed
manifests he hadn't written and followed orders he hadn't chosen, caught in the middle of a war he
kept insisting, credibly, he'd never wanted any part of.

Kanja had heard versions of that story before, and believed most of them. He paroled the clerk on his
word alone, no ransom, no hostage-taking, the same way he'd released the coerced conscripts months
earlier — a name and a promise, set ashore with his own boat and told to go home.

"You don't know that he meant it," Dol Maren said afterward, watching the boat pull away.

"I don't. I know what believing people costs less than assuming the worst of all of them costs. That's
the trade we've been making since Ghost Harbor." Kanja watched the boat shrink toward the coast. "It's
worked more times than it hasn't."

It hadn't worked this time. Three weeks later, a coastal fishing settlement that had quietly supplied
the fleet with charts and provisions was raided at dawn by a Trust punitive column that moved with
exact knowledge of the settlement's layout, its warning signals, and the names of the two families who
coordinated with the ghost fleet directly — knowledge the column could only have had from someone who'd
seen the arrangement from the inside.

Six people died before the column withdrew. The clerk was never found, never confirmed, never proven —
Kanja had no evidence beyond the shape of what a frightened, grateful-sounding young man might do under
pressure from officers who'd have known exactly how to make gratitude cheap next to fear.

"You can't know it was him," Garren Hask said, laying the settlement's own account beside the ship's
manifest from three weeks prior, the timeline too clean to be entirely comfortable and too uncertain to
be entirely damning.

"I can't. That's the part that doesn't let me put it down." Kanja stood at *The Audit*'s rail a long
while, the six names Hask had already begun entering into his ledger sitting unresolved between them.
"If it wasn't him, then trusting him cost us nothing and something else entirely killed those six
people. If it was him, then I handed a column exactly what it needed and called it mercy."

"Which do you believe?"

"I don't get to believe either one comfortably enough to stop asking the question. That's the whole
cost, Garren. Not that mercy sometimes gets people killed — I already knew that arithmetic. It's that
I'll never get to know, cleanly, whether this specific mercy was the one that did it, and I still have
to decide tomorrow whether to make the same trade again with the next frightened clerk who says he
never wanted any of this."

He made the same trade again, three months later, with a different prisoner — not because the answer
had gotten any clearer, but because he'd concluded, deliberately and without comfort, that a doctrine
that only held when it was safe wasn't a doctrine at all.
