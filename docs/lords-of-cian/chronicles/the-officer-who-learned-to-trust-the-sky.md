# The Officer Who Learned to Trust the Sky

*Locked canon, Batch 137, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-460`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle IX, closing
the third wave. Rebellion era, age 29, the Gale Straits. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone
material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters beyond the already-locked
Sephtis.*

---

The fleet's newest naval officer had transferred in from a conventional command where storm
predictions were treated as rough estimates at best, and Sephtis's own particular confidence — precise
timings, exact landfall projections, decisions staked entirely on numbers most captains would have
hedged against — struck him initially as the kind of overconfidence that got crews killed.

"You're betting the whole fleet's position on a prediction that could be wrong," he said, three weeks
into his transfer, watching preparations begin for a maneuver timed entirely around Sephtis's latest
forecast. "I've served under commanders who trusted their instincts that completely. Most of them
learned the hard way that the sea doesn't care how confident you sound."

"Sephtis has been wrong before. Two hours late, once, on a prediction that mattered." Kanja didn't
dispute the officer's caution, didn't dismiss it either. "I don't trust the doctrine because it's
never failed. I trust it because when it does fail, the fleet survives the failure, and every mission
between those failures runs cleaner than anything a hedge-your-bets approach would manage. That's not
overconfidence. That's a different kind of caution than the one you're used to."

The officer watched three engagements unfold exactly as predicted before the maneuver he'd doubted
most directly proved itself — a merchant-fleet rescue that would have been impossible to execute in
time without the precision he'd initially distrusted, forty ships saved on a timeline that left no
margin for the hedged, conservative approach he'd have defaulted to himself.

"I owe you an apology," he told Sephtis afterward, genuine in it. "I judged the confidence before I'd
actually seen what it was built on."

"Most people do, the first time. The confidence looks like recklessness until you've watched it work
enough times to see it's actually the opposite." Sephtis accepted the apology without needing to make
more of it than it was. "Trust the sky less than I do, if it helps you sleep. Just don't mistake my
certainty for carelessness. Everything I predict costs me something to get right. I wouldn't spend
that cost if I weren't confident it was worth it."
