# The Debt They Held With Chains

*Locked canon, Batch 277, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1433`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XCIV, wave 32,
first entry. Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench -- Onyx of Oblivion solo, Mafesto dormant and Obsidian
Malice undeployed (`MCD-232`). The alias's first offensive infiltration rather than a defensive or
reactive engagement, and a detailed battle-intense combat showcase per standing craft instruction.*

---

Every prior fight the tally method had drawn to Kanja's door had come looking for him first --
enforcers on the dredge line, an assassin sent after an organizer, a squad dispatched to a hearing.
This one he went looking for.

A private lockup two districts over held eleven men and women against debts none of them had
signed for -- inherited from dead fathers, invented outright by owners who kept the only copies of
the paperwork, enforced by a standing guard of six behind walls nobody outside the operation had
ever mapped. Garren Hask found the pattern first, in a column of names that never quite matched the
column of hours worked. Kanja didn't send a delegation. He went himself, after dark, with nothing
but Onyx and the knowledge Pell Ostra had traded for over three patient days -- guard rotations,
a locked inner door, the specific weight of the outer gate's bar.

Whisper of Shadows carried him over the outer wall without a sound, the courtyard crossed in the
gap between two guards' rounds measured to the second. The inner door was iron, barred from within;
Veil Piercer found the seam in it anyway, the bar parting clean rather than the door itself, so that
nothing announced the breach until the first guard inside was already disarmed and folded to the
floor, breath knocked from him before he could shout. Two more came at the noise he hadn't quite
managed to make. Onyx read the first one's weight shift half a second before the blow landed and
turned it aside with the flat rather than the edge; the second, faster and better trained, forced a
real exchange -- three strikes, a feint Kanja let land against his shoulder rather than lose the
tempo correcting for it, and a disarming cut that ended the fight without ending the man holding the
blade.

The remaining three guards, alerted by then, found the inner yard already cleared and eleven people
already moving toward the gate under Cadence Ruin's read of exactly which of them would panic and
which wouldn't -- Kanja placed himself last in the line, between the freed and whatever came through
the door behind them, and nothing did. The owner who'd built the operation was gone before dawn,
the debts he'd invented burned in his own ledger room with Pell Ostra's help, and the eleven walked
out owing nothing to anyone, least of all to the man who'd freed them.

"You didn't wait for us," Corren Halst said afterward, not quite a complaint.

"There wasn't a version of waiting that didn't cost someone another night in there." Kanja checked
the shoulder where the feint had landed, already bruising. "I'd rather explain a bruise than explain
why I let it run another day because the plan looked cleaner with more hands on it."
