# What They Carved Into the New Threshold

*Locked canon, Batch 261, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1297`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LXXVIII, wave 26, closing the wave. A legacy/memory closer years after the earthquake's rebuilding. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The rooming house that had come closest to killing six people before the Trinity's full sequence saved all of them stood rebuilt three years later, its own new threshold stone carved with a line none of its residents had asked Kanja's opinion on before choosing.

He hadn't known about it until he passed back through the district on other business and saw it plain above the doorway: a date, no name attached to it, and beneath the date a single line — *the ground moved, and something else held.*

"We debated putting your name on it," the district's rebuilding overseer told him, catching him reading it. "Most of the council wanted to. I was the one who argued against it, if you want to know who to be annoyed with."

"I'm not annoyed. I'd rather know why."

"Because the day it happened, it wasn't really about you, even though you were the reason six people walked out alive. It was about a ground that lied to everyone, all at once, and a district that had to decide together how much time it was willing to trade against how many people it might otherwise lose. Your name on the stone would have made it a story about one man's hands. What actually happened was six buildings' worth of people making impossible choices fast enough to matter, and you were the sharpest instrument any of them had, not the whole of what saved them."

Kanja read the line again — *something else held* — turning it over the way he might once have turned over a structure's own tension signature, listening for what was actually being said underneath the words chosen to say it.

"I've spent years being credited with things I did alone," he said. "Or worse — things a doctrine that's since passed through six other pairs of hands gets credited entirely to me, as if none of them learned it as honestly as I did. This is the first threshold I've stood under that got it exactly right without my having to argue for it."

"That's the whole point of a threshold, I'd think. You don't put a man's name over a door people are going to walk through every day for the rest of their lives. You put the truth of what happened there instead, and let whoever built it into their lives after that be a rumor they hear from a neighbor, if they ever hear it at all."

He didn't ask them to change it. He stood under it a moment longer than the errand that had brought him through the district required, then walked on, the carved line staying exactly where the district had decided it belonged — not his name, just the honest account of a morning the ground had lied to everyone, and something, for reasons the stone itself declined to specify, had held anyway.
