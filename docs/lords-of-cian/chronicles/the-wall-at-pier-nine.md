# The Wall at Pier Nine

*Locked canon, Batch 264, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1372`). Captain Alias Chronicle LXXII, wave 24, closing the wave. Mira's answer resolves the council's deadlock; a physical memorial wall is built at Pier Nine, Joran's name carved first.*

---

Mira's answer, when it came, wasn't complicated. Asked what her father would have wanted, she said he'd never cared much for speeches — he'd cared about being remembered by people who'd actually known him, not mourned by strangers performing grief they didn't feel. What he would have wanted, she thought, was somewhere real, something you could put a hand on, not a ceremony that ended the moment everyone went back to their own lives.

Garren Hask took that back to the council and it broke the deadlock inside a single meeting. Not a day of mourning, not a spoken eulogy repeated on schedule — a wall. A stretch of stone along Pier Nine's own seawall, plain and unmarked until someone had a name that belonged on it, each one carved by whoever in the crew had known them best rather than by any single official hand. Joran's name went up first, cut by Mira's own small, careful, unpracticed strokes with Pell Ostra's steady hand guiding hers on the chisel, followed by names already lost to the crew across its long history, cut by whichever hand had known them — Tam Sullen among them, lost at the Black Trench long before Pier Nine itself existed, carved by Efa Gol's own steady hand for the first time in all the years since.

"It's not for the crew," Corren Halst said, watching Mira step back from her father's freshly cut name. "It's for anyone Pier Nine ever failed to save in time, or anyone it lost trying. The crew's just the ones who keep the stone standing."

Kanja ran a hand along the cold, salt-worn rock afterward, reading names some of the newer hands wouldn't recognize and some he'd carry the rest of his own vastly longer life. "This outlasts the ship's own memory," he said. "Longer than any of us, probably. Longer than me, if the stone holds."

"That's rather the point," Hask said. "A wall doesn't need anyone to keep telling its story out loud. It just has to still be standing when the next person needs to find a name on it."
