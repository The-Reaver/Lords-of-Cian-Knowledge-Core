# The Word They Wouldn't Let Him Mock

*Locked canon, Batch 193, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-619`). Captain Alias Chronicle XLIV, wave 15 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The captured Directorate officer said it as an insult, meant to needle, the way prisoners sometimes
tried when they had nothing else left to fight with: "Captain. Cute little title your people gave
you. Makes you sound like a harbor pilot instead of whatever it is you actually are underneath it."

He said it in front of a dozen crew members, clearly hoping it would land as a wedge — that hearing
their name for him dismissed would sting either him or them. It landed nowhere close to what he'd
intended.

"You say that like it's small," Danne Sok said, before Kanja had said anything at all. "It isn't.
Every other name he's ever worn, your own people gave him — Bane, the Trench Monarch, whatever else
the Directorate's coined trying to catalogue what scares them about him. This one's ours. We made
it, we kept it, and it means something none of your classifications ever bothered to measure."

"It means he checks the names on the lists," Garren Hask added, not looking up from the ledger he
was working. "It means he sat on a flooded deck all night for a fever that wasn't even his own
crisis to fix. It means thirty feet of open ground he wouldn't give up, and a trade he offered
himself for, and a name your intelligence reports have apparently never once bothered to explain
properly, because you clearly don't understand what it costs to earn it."

The officer, visibly recalculating, tried once more. "It's still just a word."

"It's the word thousands of people chose, freely, over years, for the one man among all of us who
never once demanded it," Efa Gol said. "Your titles get handed down from officers who've never met
the person they're classifying. Ours got built one kept promise at a time. You can mock it if you
want. Won't change what it actually holds."

Kanja hadn't said a word through any of it, letting the crew answer an insult aimed at him with far
more conviction than he'd have managed defending himself. When the officer finally fell silent, out
of arguments, Kanja spoke only once, quietly.

"They said it better than I could have. That's the whole answer, really."
