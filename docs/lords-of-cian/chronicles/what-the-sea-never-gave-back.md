# What the Sea Never Gave Back

*Locked canon, Batch 258, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1227`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
LXXXIX, wave 30. A genuine, permanent loss with no body recovered and no closure. Not a territory
Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The rescue had gone right in every way that mattered until the very end — a capsized fishing fleet
caught in a sudden squall, eleven boats overturned, and the ghost fleet's crew pulling survivor after
survivor from water cold enough to kill in under twenty minutes, Kanja himself diving repeatedly past
the point most bodies could sustain, Onyx of Oblivion's Whisper of Shadows the only thing letting him
track a drifting shape through black water and worse visibility than the depth alone accounted for.

Thirty-one people came out of that water alive. The thirty-second, a young deckhand whose crewmates
swore they'd seen him surface once, briefly, calling for help before a wave took him under again,
never did.

They searched for six hours past the point any reasonable measure of hope would have called it. Danne
Sok ran the currents backward, calculating every plausible drift pattern the body could have taken.
Pell Ostra and two others dove the wreck site itself long after the squall passed, finding nothing. By
nightfall, with thirty-one survivors needing care the crew could no longer delay giving them, Kanja
called the search, and it was the first time in this fleet's recorded history that a rescue attempt
had ended without a body recovered either way — not saved, not confirmed dead, simply gone,
unaccounted for, with no closure the crew or the family could be given.

He went to the man's family himself rather than send word through anyone else, and told them plainly
what he could and couldn't offer: thirty-one others alive who would not have been without the search,
and one man the sea had simply kept, with no explanation and no certainty either way to hand back.

"You did everything," the man's mother said, and there was no comfort in it for either of them, only
fact. "I believe that. It doesn't make the not-knowing easier."

"No," Kanja admitted. "It doesn't. I wish I had something better to give you than the truth."

Garren Hask logged it as he logged everything, refusing the small mercy of rounding the account up to
a clean success or down to a clean tragedy — thirty-one survivors, one name never confirmed either
way, the first entry in the fleet's history that the ledger itself could not close.
