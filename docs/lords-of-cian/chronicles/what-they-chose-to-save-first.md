# What They Chose to Save First

*Locked canon, Batch 202, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-865`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XX, wave 7 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The raiders split their force deliberately, one column feinting toward the forge terrace while the
larger, better-equipped second column struck straight for the granary barges moored downriver — a
season's grain for four settlements, gathered and stored against a coming lean month, undefended
compared to the forge's own standing watch.

Kanja read the feint within the first minute and did not take the bait.

He left the forge terrace to its ordinary watch and moved the Trinity downriver at speed, Mafesto's
Kinetic Transfer System converting the hard run into stored charge that fed straight into the fight
waiting at the barges. Obsidian Malice discharged across the lead raiders' boarding line before a
single hull was breached, the shockwave rolling flat across open water to capsize two skiffs outright.
Onyx read the second wave's approach through the grain barges' own crowded rigging, Whisper of
Shadows closing distance the raiders never registered until Cadence Ruin had already unmade their
formation from the inside.

Not one sack of grain reached open water. The feint column, finding the forge terrace calmly held and
no reinforcement pulled to answer their diversion, broke off without ever pressing the attack at all.

"You left the forge lighter-guarded than I'd have liked," the settlement's granary keeper admitted
afterward, watching the barges secured and intact. "Wasn't sure that was the right call, from where I
was standing."

"Tools we can rebuild. We've proven that a dozen times over this campaign alone." Kanja checked the
grain sacks himself, satisfied, before turning back toward the terrace. "Nobody rebuilds a season's
food once it's gone to the bottom of a river. I'll spend every forge in this campaign before I let
that happen instead. The metal's replaceable. What people eat this winter isn't."
