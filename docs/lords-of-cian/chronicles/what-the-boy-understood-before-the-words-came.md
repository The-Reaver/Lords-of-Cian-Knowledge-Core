# What the Boy Understood Before the Words Came

*Locked canon, Batch 197, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-737`). The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XLII, wave 14 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The rescue site was still settling when the boy found him — a collapsed storehouse wall brought down
deliberately, precisely, to free the family trapped in the cellar beneath it, the surrounding
structure left standing exactly as Kanja had intended.

The boy, seven or eight, hadn't been one of the trapped. He'd been outside the whole time, watching,
and now stood at the edge of the settling debris with his palm pressed flat against an undamaged
section of the same wall, head tilted slightly, listening to something Kanja hadn't asked him to
listen for.

"What are you doing?" Kanja asked, more curious than concerned.

"You did that without touching most of it." The boy didn't look up from the wall. "I wanted to know
what it feels like. The part you didn't break. It's different than the part you did."

"How do you mean, different?"

"Quieter, maybe. I don't have the word for it." He finally looked up. "Is that what you were doing?
Listening to which part was quiet and which part wasn't?"

Kanja crouched down to the boy's level, no longer entirely sure whether the question deserved the
simple answer or the true one. "Something close to that, yes. Most people don't think to ask that
question at all, let alone answer it with their hands."

"It seemed obvious. The wall that came down felt loud, even before. The wall still standing doesn't."
The boy shrugged, entirely unaware of how unusual the observation actually was, already turning his
attention back to his rescued family being helped clear of the debris. "I don't know why everyone
doesn't just feel that."

Kanja watched him go, saying nothing further, filing the moment away without fully deciding what to
make of it — a child, untrained, unprompted, reaching toward the same basic intuition it had taken him
years of deliberate practice to sharpen into a doctrine capable of unmaking Crawlers. Whether it meant
anything beyond a curious child's passing observation, he didn't know. He found, walking away from the
settling wall, that he wanted to.
