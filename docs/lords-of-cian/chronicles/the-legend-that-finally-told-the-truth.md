# The Legend That Finally Told the Truth

*Locked canon, Batch 260, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1282`). Crow King Alias Chronicle XC, wave 30, closing
it. A valedictory closer drawing together the scholar's record, the commandant's report, and the
still-unresolved fifth generation. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The scholar's record and the commandant's report reached the fourth generation within the same
season, by pure coincidence, each arriving through an entirely different channel -- one carried by a
crew courier, the other found its way to him secondhand through a captured Directorate officer who'd
thought it worth passing along rather than burning. He read them both in the same sitting, side by
side, two accounts of the same decades written by people who had never met each other and had no
reason to agree, and found that they matched almost everywhere it mattered.

"Two people who never spoke to each other," he told the third generation, "one of them chasing us for
years and the other one just trying to write down the truth, and they ended up in almost the same
place. That's strange, if you think about it."

"It's not strange," she said. "It just means the truth was actually there to find, the whole time,
underneath every version that got it wrong. It just took people willing to look past the crown and
the smoke and the seven-foot man made of shadow to find it."

The runner's son -- still unanswered, still watched rather than taught, patience being tested on both
sides of that question now -- asked, when he heard the two records described to him, whether the
legend would eventually just become the truth anyway, worn down by enough retellings that nobody would
bother checking the written records against it.

"Maybe," Kanja said, when the question reached him. "Legends usually win that fight, eventually.
I've made my peace with that. What I care about is smaller than the legend surviving accurately. I
care about the part underneath it -- the listening, the restraint, the willingness to let a plan work
slowly instead of loudly -- surviving at all, in whatever shape it takes, whether anyone remembers to
call it by this name or not."

Thirty waves and ninety Chronicles after a scared young man invented a trick in under an hour to save
three hundred lives, the craft was four generations deep, written into two separate institutional
archives, carried in shapes he'd never taught and couldn't have predicted, and still, somewhere, an
unanswered question sitting quietly between three people who trusted the process more than they
needed the answer right away.

"What happens when someone finally does ask to be the fifth?" the apprentice asked him once, not long
after.

"I don't know," Kanja said. "I didn't know what the second would look like, either, until she was
already there. I've learned to trust that the craft finds its own next shape better than I could plan
one for it."
