# What Corren Halst Remembered

*Locked canon, Batch 129, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-436`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle IX, closing the
third wave. Rebellion era, age 18, before the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. New
standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters beyond the
already-locked Corren Halst.*

---

Corren Halst had already been standing beside Kanja for a year by the time the Black Trench made
either of their names mean anything to people who hadn't lived through Warehouse Twelve themselves —
one of the three, alongside Danne Sok and Maret Vos, who'd fought there before Maw-9 ever added
newer recruits to a core that already existed. Decades later, older and long since past the point of
needing to prove anything to anyone, he was still the one people asked when they wanted to know what
the Trench Monarch had actually been like before the title became something the whole world
recognized.

"Everyone wants the story to start with something dramatic," he told a younger dredge-line worker,
long after the war had moved on to names neither of them had imagined at eighteen. "A single act, a
single speech, something you could point to and say, there, that's the moment he became something.
It wasn't like that. It was a hundred small things, none of them dramatic on their own. Showing up
early. Staying late. Carrying his own share of the mud instead of directing someone else to carry it.
None of it would have made a story worth telling if you'd watched any single day of it in isolation."

"So when did it become something worth a title?"

"I don't think there was a single day. I think it was more that one day, enough small things had
happened that everyone on the line looked around and realized none of us doubted him anymore, and
somebody had to put a word to that, so somebody did." Halst's expression carried the particular
warmth of someone who'd watched the whole thing build from the ground up rather than arrived after
the fact to admire the finished structure. "I was there for most of the small things. I couldn't
tell you which specific one tipped it. I don't think it works that way. I don't think trust like that
ever comes from one moment. It comes from every moment nobody bothered writing down."
