# Ijoko Chronicle III: The Criticism She Couldn't Dismiss

*Locked canon, Batch 174, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-521`). Homage-era Los Angeles, Ijoko/Adwoa. Territory
Chronicle — Adwoa as protagonist, Kanja an unnamed background guest with no command, credit, or
resolution authorship. New standalone material. No new named characters.*

---

The critique arrived from a genuine ally rather than a council rival, delivered privately and without
malice — a real, well-reasoned argument that the cooperative lending system Adwoa had spent three years
building was quietly favoring established businesses over the newest, most marginal ones it had been
meant to serve first.

Adwoa's first instinct was the same iron resilience that had carried her through years of deniable
council-chamber mockery.

"I nearly answered you the way I answer everyone who criticizes the work," she admitted, a full day
after first hearing the concern, having sat with it rather than dismiss it on reflex. "I caught myself
doing it — treating a genuine critique the same way I've learned to treat an attack that isn't. That's
not fair to you, and it isn't good for the work either."

"I wasn't attacking it. I believe in what you built. I just think it's not doing what you meant it to
do, for the people who need it most." The ally hadn't expected the reconsideration, had braced for
the same deflection Adwoa had perfected against genuine hostility. "I wasn't sure you'd actually hear
it, honestly."

"I nearly didn't. The gift that's kept me standing through years of mockery is very good at making me
resilient. It's not actually built to tell me when resilience is the wrong response." Adwoa reviewed
the cooperative's own lending data, the pattern the ally had flagged now undeniable once she looked
for it honestly. "Deniable mockery and honest criticism feel similar in the moment, if I'm not careful
to actually tell them apart. I owe you an apology for almost failing to make that distinction."

She restructured the lending priority within the month, the newest and most marginal businesses moved
to the front of the queue exactly as the critique had argued — a real correction, made possible only
by recognizing that her own hard-won resilience needed a discernment to go alongside it.

"Most people in your position would have just kept doing it the old way," the ally observed
afterward, watching the correction take effect. "The old way wasn't costing you anything visible."

"It was costing the people it was supposed to help most. That's not a cost I was willing to keep
paying just because my own resilience made it easy not to notice." Adwoa watched the newly reprioritized
list take shape. "The iron hand's useful. It's not a substitute for actually listening, and I nearly
let it become one."

An unnamed man near the council chamber's edge, present for the exchange, left once the correction had
been announced.
