# The Tunnels He Sealed Behind Him

*Locked canon, Batch 195, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-678`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XLIII, wave 15 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

With the siege won and the Trust's garrison withdrawn, the question of what to do with six weeks'
worth of newly opened, newly mapped tunnel network fell, by informal consensus, to the one person
who'd read every section of it more carefully than anyone else alive.

Kanja recommended sealing most of it.

"You spent six weeks fighting for access to these tunnels," the crew chief said, genuinely
surprised. "Now you want to close them back up?"

"I want to close the sections that were never safe to begin with, the ones we only used because we
had no other choice under fire. Leaving them open past the war just because we fought hard for the
access isn't honoring the fight. It's letting the next unlucky person inherit a risk we only
accepted because the alternative was worse." He marked the sections on the crew's own survey,
methodical, the same care he'd brought to every assessment across the whole siege. "The sections
that are genuinely sound stay open — proper municipal thoroughfares now, not a war's improvised
shortcut. Everything else gets sealed properly, not abandoned carelessly, so it fails safely instead
of failing on someone who never knew what they were standing under."

The sealing work took nearly as long as some of the original digging had, timber and stone set with
the same unhurried precision as any shoring brace, each closure checked twice before he considered it
finished.

"Most people would want the tunnels to stay open," a crew member said, watching him seal a passage
that had, three weeks earlier, saved a dozen lives during the worst of the fighting. "As a monument,
if nothing else. Proof of what happened down here."

"The monument isn't the tunnel. It's the twelve people who walked out of it alive, and they don't
need the passage left open and unstable to remember that it happened." Kanja set the final brace of
the closure, satisfied. "I'd rather leave this district safer than I found it than leave it a
museum to a war it's finally allowed to stop fighting. The people who need to remember what this
tunnel did already do. That's enough of a monument for me."

By the time he left Killane for good, the district's underground network was smaller, safer, and
quieter than it had been at the siege's height — a deliberate diminishment nobody but the crew who'd
built it ever fully understood as its own kind of respect.
