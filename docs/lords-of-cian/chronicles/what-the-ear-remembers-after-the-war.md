# What the Ear Remembers After the War

*Locked canon, Batch 197, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-740`). The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XLV, wave 15 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The Trust scholar, his once-shelved research now published and, slowly, gaining the kind of
institutional attention it had spent years being denied, sought Kanja out one final time before the
Rebellion's own momentum carried them both toward whatever came after it — a war nearing an ending
neither of them could yet fully picture.

"I've spent years documenting what you built," he said, settling beside a fire neither of them
needed for warmth so much as for the ordinary human comfort of one. "I don't think I ever asked you
plainly what it looks like from where you're standing, rather than where I've been standing,
studying it from outside."

Kanja considered the question for a long moment, turning the Aegis-Talisman over once in his hands
before answering. "It looks like nine bridges in one night, taught well enough that I didn't have to
be at six of them myself. It looks like a boy pressing his palm against a wall, asking a question he
didn't have the words for yet. It looks like a widow who never forgave what it cost her, and was
right not to. It's not one thing. I don't think it ever was."

"Do you think it outlasts you? The doctrine, I mean — not the war."

"I think it already has, in the small ways that matter most. A student who learned to be honest with
himself before he learned to be fast. Sapper teams working from written notes at crossings I never
reached myself. A smith in a village who was already listening the way I do, long before she ever met
me." He set the Talisman down. "I built a technique for reading what holds things together. I didn't
expect it would end up teaching me the same thing about people, a little more, every time I got it
wrong as much as every time I got it right."

The scholar had no further question ready, and didn't reach for one. They sat with the fire a while
longer, an artificer's instrument and an honest account of it, both finally being heard by more people
than either had once believed possible.
