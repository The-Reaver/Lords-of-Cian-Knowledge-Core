# The Winter the Stores Ran Thin

No new named characters beyond already-locked crew.*

The winter the supply lines ran thin, nobody starved, but everyone felt it — rationed portions,
worn-out gear nobody could afford to replace, three weeks where the crew's mood soured under the
particular strain of a problem that no amount of skill in a fight could solve.

Kanja didn't call a meeting to announce a solution. He called one to ask for ideas.

"I could push us further from safe harbor chasing a richer supply line," he said, "and some of you
have said that's the answer. I want to hear the case against it before I decide anything, because
I don't actually know this problem as well as the people managing it day to day."

Garren Hask went first, with the numbers: current stores, burn rate, the real margin they had before
rationing became dangerous rather than uncomfortable. Pell Ostra suggested repurposing scrap metal
they'd been hoarding into trade goods for a nearby settlement that had timber and grain but no smith
of its own. Efa Gol, who'd quietly kept a mental map of every settlement the crew had ever helped
without asking for payment, suggested calling in exactly none of those debts directly — but making
their presence known nearby, in case anyone remembered on their own and offered.

They did. Word reached three settlements within a week, each sending what they could spare — not
charity, but memory, the specific kind that only forms when someone helped once and never mentioned
it again.

"You didn't decide any of this," Danne Sok observed afterward, watching supplies come aboard from
people who owed the crew nothing on paper.

"I didn't need to. Every idea that actually worked came from someone who understood the problem
better than I did. My job wasn't to solve it. It was to ask the right people and then get out of
the way." Kanja checked the new stores against Hask's ledger, satisfied the margin had moved back
into safety. "That's most of what commanding a crew actually is, when it isn't a fight. Knowing who
to ask, and trusting the answer enough not to second-guess it just because it wasn't mine."
