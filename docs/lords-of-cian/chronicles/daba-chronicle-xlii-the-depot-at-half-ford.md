# Daba Chronicle XLII: The Depot at Half-Ford

*Locked canon, Batch 296, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1612`). Entry 42 of 50 in Daba's own Chronicle series.

doctrine in its own right, per `MCD-1567`'s framing of it as "the smallest standing force of any

Half-Ford was not the kind of place that made it into anyone's dispatches. It was a labor depot on a
tributary too shallow for real barges, where the Trust's regional office had taken to warehousing
debt-bonded children between contracts -- not a policy anyone would admit to in writing, just an
arrangement of convenience between three overseers who had each individually decided it was easier
than housing them properly. Daba had learned of it from a cell three provinces removed, in a report
that mentioned it the way a tired man mentions weather: in passing, as a condition of the world
rather than a target.

He read the report twice and then sent for Orsk Dresk.

"Forty-one children," Orsk said, when Daba laid it out for him. "Six guards on rotation, none of them
Trust regulars -- contracted men, the kind who'll run before they'll die for a wage. No walls to
speak of. Just distance and the assumption that no one's counting."

"Someone's counting now."

Orsk had run smaller things before this -- a granary, once, and a single overseer's private ledger
that had gotten four families out from under a debt that would have outlived them -- but nothing at
this scale, and Daba watched him understand that before he said it aloud. "This isn't Trust
suppression. This isn't the Rookery." His voice didn't change when he said it, which was its own
kind of discipline. "Why does this one matter to us specifically?"

"Because no one else is going to notice it," Daba said. "That's the whole of it. That's the only
qualification anything needs."

He gave Orsk four cells and eleven days and did not tell him how to spend either, because that was
the doctrine and the doctrine did not bend for how much a thing mattered to him personally. What
came back, eleven days later, was not a battle. It was a barge, rerouted under a cargo manifest three
layers removed from anything that would survive scrutiny, forty-one children and two of the
overseers' own clerks who had quietly decided they preferred not to have been there, and a fire set
in the depot's own record room after everything worth taking had already been taken -- not to punish
anyone, Orsk reported afterward, but because a debt ledger that no longer exists cannot be reissued
under a different overseer's name next season.

"No casualties," Orsk said, when he gave his account in person, and Daba heard the pride under the
flatness of it and let him have it. "Two of the contracted men ran the moment we lit the perimeter
lamps. The rest never got the chance to decide."

"And the clerks?"

"Placed with a cell that can use honest bookkeeping more than it can use another set of hands that
know how to hold a blade." Orsk hesitated, the only hesitation in the whole report. "One of them
asked who we were. I told her we didn't have a name that meant anything outside of a number, and she
didn't ask again."

Daba turned that over for a long moment after Orsk had gone, the way he turned over every operation
that had cost him nothing directly and everything indirectly -- the resources, the exposure, the
eleven days four cells had not spent doing something else. There was no ledger anywhere that would
ever record Half-Ford as a battle, because it had never been one, and there was no single figure
whose name would attach to it the way names attached to the things the Trust chose to notice. That
absence was the entire design. Forty-one children existed now somewhere the depot's own paperwork
would never think to look for them, and the only cost of that had been a fire in a record room and
eleven days no one outside 1804 would ever know had happened at all.

He thought, not for the first time, that this was closer to what he had actually built than any of
the larger, sharper engagements ever were -- not the doctrine that made a column collapse into a
ravine, but the slower, patient version of the same lesson: that a thing too small and too quiet to
be seen could still, given enough of them, add up to something the world's ledgers would never be
able to properly total.
