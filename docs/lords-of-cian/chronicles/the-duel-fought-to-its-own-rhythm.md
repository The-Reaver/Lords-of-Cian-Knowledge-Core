# The Duel Fought to Its Own Rhythm

*Locked canon, Batch 255, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1133`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle LXXVI, wave 26.
Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench -- Onyx of Oblivion solo, Mafesto dormant and Obsidian Malice
undeployed (`MCD-232`). A dedicated Cadence Ruin deep-dive, and its first shown cost.*

---

The duelist the Trust sent that season had trained in a formal southern discipline built entirely
around ceremony — every strike, every parry, every advance and retreat bound to a spoken cadence he
recited under his breath as he fought, a rhythm so deliberately consistent that his own instructors
had called it unbreakable, a fighting style with no gaps in it for an opponent to exploit because it
had been built, generation over generation, to have none.

Onyx read it in the first four seconds.

Where the arrhythmic duelist months before had defeated Cadence Ruin entirely by refusing to keep any
rhythm the gift could latch onto, this was the opposite case — a rhythm so complete, so internally
consistent, that Cadence Ruin didn't need to find a gap in it at all. It found the rhythm itself, and
inhabited it, matching the duelist's own recited cadence beat for beat and then, degree by degree,
compressing the half-beats between his words until the ceremony that had always protected him started
working against him instead — his own trained certainty about when the next strike was supposed to
arrive becoming the exact thing Onyx used to make sure it never arrived when he expected it to.

The duel ended in under two minutes, the duelist disarmed and staring at his own fallen blade with the
particular confusion of a man whose lifelong discipline had just been turned, seamlessly, into a
weapon against him.

What came after wasn't in any prior account of the gift. Walking back to Warehouse Twelve, Kanja found
his own instincts oddly unreliable for the better part of an hour — a passing cart's ordinary,
arrhythmic clatter nearly drew a reaction from him it had no business drawing, his trained sense for
an attack's internal timing still calibrated to the duelist's precise, ceremonial cadence rather than
to the genuinely random noise of an ordinary street. It passed on its own, the way a held breath
eventually released itself, but it was the first time the gift had left him narrower rather than
sharper in its immediate aftermath.

"You flinched at a fruit cart," Danne Sok said, the closest thing to a joke he ever offered.

"I know." Kanja didn't elaborate further, but he filed the fact away with the same care he gave any
lesson the sword taught him. Cadence Ruin, at its fullest and cleanest expression, cost him something
in the minutes immediately after — a narrow window where his own reflexes were still tuned to a
rhythm that no longer existed, and an ordinary threat arriving in that window would have found him
slower to answer it than he should have been.
