# What Four Generations Now Agree On

*Locked canon, Batch 263, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1345`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LXXII, wave 24, closing the wave. The retired successor, the third-generation senior student, and the new fourth-generation apprentice sit together for the first time to formally write down the doctrine's core principles as an explicit creed.*

---

It was the retired successor's idea, though she insisted afterward it had really been Sephtis's, decades unfinished. "He always meant to write it down plainly, once," she said. "Not the technique. The reasons underneath the technique. He ran out of time before he ran out of things to say about it."

So the three of them sat at the same chart table Sephtis had worn pale at one corner, and argued, gently, about what belonged on a single page meant to outlast all of them.

"Read the body before the instrument," the successor offered, and the student wrote it down as the boy watched.

"Credit whoever taught you the piece that isn't yours," the student added, thinking of the northern pilot's name in the ledger rather than folded into the school's own. The successor nodded at that one without needing to be convinced.

"Say the unsure part out loud," the boy offered, quieter than the other two, testing whether a fourth generation's voice belonged in a document three generations had built without him. The student looked at him a long moment, then wrote it down exactly as he'd said it, no softening, no correction.

"Log the misses the same as the hits," the successor said, "or the whole thing is just a way of hiding how often you're wrong behind how loudly you're right when you're not." That one went down word for word too.

They argued longer over the fifth line than the first four combined, and it was Kanja -- present, as ever, without having been asked to weigh in -- who finally offered it, not as instruction but as something he'd watched all three of them do without naming it.

"The work outlasting the one who did it isn't a loss," he said. "It's the only kind of winning that holds."

The student looked at him for a moment. "That's not weather," she said.

"No," Kanja agreed. "But you've been living it since before I ever said it out loud. Sephtis lived it. She's living it now, handing this table to the two of you instead of keeping it." He nodded at the successor, who didn't argue the point.

Five lines, in the end, on a single page, copied that same afternoon into the front of the school's official record where every future student would read it before anything else -- ahead of pressure, ahead of wind, ahead of gulls.

"Four generations," the successor said, reading it back once more before it went into the archive. "First time we've all agreed on anything at the same table at the same time."

"We didn't agree," the student said. "We argued until it was true enough that none of us wanted to change it anymore. That's different. That's better."
