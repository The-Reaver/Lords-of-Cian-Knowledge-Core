# What Fell When the Tower Did

*Locked canon, Batch 199, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-790`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle XXXV, wave 12 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The watchtower guarded the only navigable channel through a strait the rebellion needed open and the
Trust had spent two years fortifying against exactly this kind of attempt — stone walls rising sheer
from a rock islet, no beach to land on, no approach that didn't run directly under its own garrison's
sightlines.

"No boarding action's going to work here," Dol Maren said, studying the tower through a glass. "There's
no deck to board. It's a fortress, not a ship."

"Then we don't fight it like a ship." Kanja was already planning the approach differently — not a
broadside, not a boarding rush, but the same patient, layered use of the gear system the fleet had
built for exactly this kind of asymmetric problem. "Onyx first. Then us."

Onyx went in alone under cover of full dark, Veil Piercer finding the seam in the tower's own iron
gate where two centuries of salt air had done what no siege engine could — a weakness in the metal
itself that a sighted strike widened into an opening no garrison had ever thought to reinforce. Whisper
of Shadows carried him past the first watch rotation entirely undetected, disabling the alarm bell's
own mechanism before a single hand could reach it.

By the time the garrison understood an attack was underway, Kanja and the boarding crew were already
scaling the rock face itself, Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System converting each controlled fall between
handholds into stored charge rather than strain, until the full assault force stood inside the tower's
own walls with a speed the garrison's defenses had never been built to answer.

The fighting that followed was close, vertical, fought through narrow stone corridors never designed
for a battle — Obsidian Malice's discharge cycle clearing choke points one at a time, the war club's
reach suited exactly to corridors too tight for a garrison to bring its own numbers fully to bear.

The tower fell before dawn, its garrison disarmed rather than slaughtered, its own commander surrendering
once the alarm he'd never gotten to ring made clear how thoroughly the assault had already succeeded
before his men even understood it had begun.

"Two years fortifying against a ship attack," Kanja said, surveying the captured tower as the sun came
up over a channel finally open again, "and nobody ever fortified against the possibility that we'd
simply climb the rock instead of sail at the walls."

"Will they learn from this one?" Dol Maren asked.

"Eventually. Everyone eventually learns the lesson we just taught them. The trick isn't teaching it
once. It's teaching a new one before they've finished learning the last."
