# What They Wouldn't Let Him Take

*Locked canon, Batch 264, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1386`). Captain Alias Chronicle LXXXVI, wave 29. A detailed full-Trinity combat showcase defends the charter's free-membership clause against a rival faction trying to coerce crew members away.*

---

The men who came to Pier Nine calling themselves a labor guild weren't raiders in any sense the crew had fought before — they carried contracts instead of weapons, offering coin and protection to any crew member willing to sign on with an outfit that ran cargo routes the same way the crew once ran resistance operations, except this one made its recruits sign binding terms with penalty clauses for leaving. Three junior hands had already signed before anyone senior noticed, drawn by a signing bonus larger than anything Pier Nine's equal-share ledger had ever offered any single person.

Corren Halst wanted to simply forbid it. Kanja stopped her.

"We don't get to override anyone's choice to leave. That's the whole first clause of the charter, and it doesn't stop applying just because the choice looks bad to us." He read the contract terms himself, though, and found the actual problem fast: buried in language none of the three had understood when they signed, a clause forfeiting any wage already owed if they tried to leave inside the first year — coercion dressed as opportunity. "What we get to do is make sure the choice they made was actually informed, and give them room to make it again with the truth in front of them."

He didn't fight the guild's recruiters. He brought Garren Hask instead, ledger in hand, and had him walk the contract clause by clause in front of the three recruits and the guild's own representative both, exposing exactly what the fine print actually cost against exactly what Pier Nine's own equal-risk-equal-share policy already guaranteed for free. Two of the three tore up their contracts on the spot. The third, humiliated at having been caught out, stayed only long enough to save face before he too walked it back.

The guild representative didn't take the loss well, and it was only when Obsidian Malice's low warning discharge scattered his hired muscle across the pier without touching a single bystander that the argument ended entirely.

"That wasn't about the fight," Corren Halst said afterward. "That was won an hour before it started, the moment Hask started reading."
