# What the Marshal Couldn't Take Back

He came alone, in the end, which nobody aboard expected. No squadron, no roving pairs, a single unarmed
launch rowed out from a supply cutter under a flag of parley toward *The Audit* at anchor in open water
— a meeting he'd requested through channels slow and formal enough that Corren Halst had verified it
three separate ways before Kanja agreed to it.

"I built a trap out of your own decency and it cost you two wounded," the Fleet-Marshal said, once he
stood on *The Audit*'s deck, making no move toward the blade at his hip. "I've thought about it every
week since. I told myself it was strategy. It wasn't. I wanted you to have to choose between being what
everyone says you are and being safe, and I wanted to be the one who finally made that cost real."

"It was real," Kanja said. "Two of my people bled for it."

"I know. That's why I'm here instead of building a second one." He looked out at the three flagships
riding at anchor, close enough now to name each one by silhouette alone. "I lost a command at Ghost
Harbor because a tide did what my own judgment should have accounted for, and I spent a year deciding
that was your fault instead of mine. It wasn't. I've had a year to sit with that, and I don't like what
I built while I was avoiding it."

Efa Gol, standing close enough to intervene if the parley turned, watched him say it and didn't relax
her stance even slightly. Dol Maren watched the launch behind him for a second boat that never came.

"I'm not asking forgiveness," the Marshal went on. "I know what I cost you, and I know an apology
doesn't put it back. I'm telling you I'm requesting reassignment away from this coast, effective the
season's end, because I don't trust myself not to build a third one if I stay, and the two people who
bled for the second one don't deserve to find out whether I would."

Kanja studied him a long moment — not with Mafesto's reading, not with anything the crew could see, just
the plain attention of one man deciding what another man standing unarmed in front of him actually was.

"You could have just gone quietly," he said finally. "Nobody here would ever have known why."

"I'd have known." The Marshal met his eyes without flinching from it. "I built something out of your
mercy because I decided somewhere along the way that mercy was a weakness I was entitled to exploit. I
don't think that anymore. I don't expect you to believe that just because I said it. I just needed to
say it to the person it cost, not to my own conscience in private, where it's easy."

He left the way he'd come, alone, unescorted back to his own launch. His reassignment was confirmed
within the month — Corren Halst verified that too — and no third trap ever came from the coast he'd
held.

"Do you believe him?" Dol Maren asked, once the launch was well clear.

"I believe he meant it standing here," Kanja said. "Whether it holds once he's somewhere the temptation
isn't in front of him — I don't know. I've let go of men I wasn't sure about before. This is just the
first one who came to tell me himself, instead of making me guess."
