# What Pell Ostra Couldn't Keep Safe

*Locked canon, Batch 255, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1143`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle LXXXVI, wave 29.
Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. Pell Ostra's second dedicated entry, and the first genuine limit of
her care.*

---

Pell Ostra had never lost a set of records to a raid since the first time she'd saved them, and the
district had come to treat that fact the way it treated most things Ostra did well — as simply true,
unremarkable, the sort of competence nobody bothered praising because it had never once failed.

It failed on a night she'd spent four straight days preparing for, when a professional records-thief
— hired, she worked out afterward, by an owner with considerably more resources than the district's
usual desperate men — bypassed every countermeasure she'd built specifically because he'd studied her
own prior methods the way the mercenary duelist had studied Onyx's. He didn't force a lock she'd
reinforced. He didn't trigger the small, deniable hazards she'd learned to leave for anyone careless
enough to rush. He simply waited three extra nights past when she'd expected any attempt, until her
own vigilance, honed for urgency rather than patience, had quietly relaxed.

The records he took were recoverable — Hask kept independent copies of everything material, a
redundancy Ostra herself had insisted on years before specifically against a failure like this one —
but the theft itself landed on her harder than the actual loss warranted.

"I built for someone rushing," she told Kanja, turning the picked lock over in her hands afterward,
addressing it the way she addressed all her materials, half question and half accusation. "I never
built for someone patient enough to wait me out."

"Nobody had, before him."

"That's not the same as it being fine." She set the lock down, precise, the way she set down
everything. "I talk to my materials because they don't lie to me about what they can and can't do. I
should have asked mine what happens to a countermeasure built for urgency when somebody refuses to be
urgent."

She rebuilt the entire protection system over the following two weeks, this time layering patience-
resistant elements alongside the rush-countermeasures she'd always relied on — nothing dramatic,
mostly small, deniable checks spaced across days rather than hours, designed to catch exactly the kind
of methodical patience that had beaten her once. She never spoke of the theft again unprompted, and
the district, for the first time in the crew's short history, understood that even Pell Ostra's
record-keeping had a limit somebody, eventually, had found.
