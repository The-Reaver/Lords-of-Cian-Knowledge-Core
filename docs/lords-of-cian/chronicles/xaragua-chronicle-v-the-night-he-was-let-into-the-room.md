# Xaragua Chronicle V: The Night He Was Let Into the Room

*Locked canon, Batch 225, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1024`). Fifth Xaragua Chronicle, set after Xaragua
Chronicle IV (`MCD-362`) in the "modern" Arturo era. Protagonist Arturo Salvatierra Duho (`PH2-061`).
A meaningful step in the flagged Kanja/Arturo long-arc, deliberately not the payoff: introduces a
previously-undramatized standing private annual remembrance for Arturo's lost dock-boy cohort
(extends `PH2-061` and `MCD-361`'s named dead -- Nzila, Tunde, Bendu -- and Yaisa's established
unique standing, `PH2-062`), with Kanja invited into it for the first time. Arturo explicitly denies
this grants unguarded-banter parity with Yaisa. No new named characters.*

---

Once a year, on a night he never announced beforehand, Arturo closed the Five Families' business
entirely and did not open his door to anyone.

The stranger had heard of the night only secondhand, the way most things about Arturo eventually
reached him — not explained, just observed, a gap in the calendar the captains had learned not to
ask about. He had never expected to see the inside of it, and did not, this year, expect anything
different, until Yaisa found him near the docks an hour before sundown and told him, without
ceremony, that Arturo wanted him at the house that night.

"He didn't say why," Yaisa said, which was, the stranger had come to understand, the closest thing
to an explanation Yaisa ever offered on Arturo's behalf.

The house that night held no captains, no disputes, no table set for business. Three empty chairs
sat at one end of the room, unused and clearly meant to stay that way, and Arturo sat across from
them with two cups of something he did not usually drink, one already poured for Yaisa before either
of them had arrived.

"Nzila. Tunde. Bendu," Arturo said, when the stranger had taken the seat offered him, nodding once
toward the empty chairs without looking at them directly. "You won't know the names. There's no
reason you should. I don't say them anywhere else, and I don't explain this night to anyone who asks
— Yaisa's the only one who's ever needed to be in this room, because she's the only one left who
remembers what those chairs used to hold."

"Then why tonight."

"Because you've been walking through five territories on my say-so for years now, and every year
I've told myself there was no reason to let it be more than that." Arturo turned his cup once
without drinking from it. "I'm not going to pretend this makes you Yaisa. It doesn't, and I won't
insult either of you by saying it does — she remembers who I was before any of this, and that's not
a debt you can pay your way into no matter how long you stand at my table. But I've spent long
enough deciding what this night is for to know it's not only about memory. It's about who I trust
enough to let watch me be a man with something to grieve instead of a man everyone's afraid of.
Tonight, that's two people instead of one. That's not nothing."

The stranger said nothing for a while, turning his own untouched cup slowly, weighing something
Arturo did not press him on.

"I had people once too," he said finally. "Before any of the names I've carried since. I don't say
who, and I won't tonight — that's not a debt I'm ready to pay either, not to you, not to anyone yet.
But I know what those three chairs are for. I've sat across from my own version of them more times
than I've told a living soul."

Arturo studied him a long moment, the particular stillness of a man recalculating something he'd
thought he already understood. "That's the most you've ever given me. I notice it. I won't ask for
more than that tonight."

"I know you won't. That's most of why I said it."

They sat past midnight, the three chairs unused and Yaisa quiet between them, and when the stranger
finally rose to leave, Arturo walked him to the door himself rather than let Naya or any other hand
do it, which the stranger understood, without needing it explained, was its own kind of answer.

"Same time, next year?" Arturo asked, and there was something almost light in it, the nearest thing
to the banter Yaisa alone usually earned, though neither of them named it that.

"If you'll have me."

"I think I just did."
