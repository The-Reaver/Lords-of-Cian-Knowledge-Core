# The Vote to Turn Back

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XL, wave 13.
Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Word reached The Anvil mid-crossing that a settlement two days behind the campaign's current route had
come under threat of a punitive strike — not yet burned, but close enough to it that reaching them in
time would mean abandoning the next scheduled stop entirely, a settlement that had been waiting nine
months for its own turn.

The crew split on what to do almost evenly, and said so, openly, in a way Kanja did not shut down or
overrule.

"We promised that settlement ahead of us nine months ago," Garren Hask argued, laying both ledgers
side by side. "We turn back now, we're telling them a threat somewhere else always outweighs a promise
already made."

"We turn back now, we might actually stop a burning before it happens instead of rebuilding after one
again," Pell Ostra countered, already calculating the fastest route back. "That's not nothing. That's
the whole difference between the job we usually do and the job we could do, once, if we moved fast
enough."

Kanja let the argument run its full course before he spoke, deliberately not settling it with his own
preference first. "I don't have a clean answer that satisfies both of you, and I'm not going to pretend
I do. I want to hear where this actually lands before I decide anything."

It took the better part of an hour, argued openly across the deck, before the crew itself reached a
answer neither side had proposed outright: split the response, a smaller detachment turning back under
Callum Breck's command to reach the threatened settlement while the main crossing continued on schedule
toward the settlement already waiting.

"That's not the clean choice either of us wanted," Hask admitted, once the vote had settled.

"No. But it's the one that doesn't fully abandon either promise." Kanja signed off on the split without
further argument, satisfied the decision belonged to the crew as much as to him. "I don't always know
the right call faster than the people standing on this deck with me. Today I didn't pretend to. That's
not a weakness in how this campaign runs. I think it might be the actual reason it's held together as
well as it has."
