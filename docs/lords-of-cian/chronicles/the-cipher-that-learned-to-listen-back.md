# The Cipher That Learned to Listen Back

*Locked canon, Batch 157, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-496`). The Crow King Alias Chronicle XII, closing the
fourth wave. Rebellion era, age 23. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters beyond the already-locked apprentice singer from
"The Apprentice Who Learned to Listen" (`MCD-451`).*

---

A Directorate cryptographer, methodical enough to have studied years of intercepted Hymn-Engine
patterns rather than any single engagement, finally cracked a genuine partial signature — not the
full method, but enough to flag roughly a third of future false signals as suspect before they could
fully take effect.

The apprentice caught it first.

"Something's different about how the last two attempts landed," she told Kanja, her own trained ear
picking up a hesitation in the garrison's response that hadn't been there before. "They're not
falling for it as completely. Something in how they're listening has changed."

She was right. The cryptographer's partial breakthrough had taught a growing number of garrisons to
listen for a specific structural tell in roughly a third of the Hymn-Engine's constructions — not
enough to defeat the method outright, but enough that continuing unchanged would mean a slow, steady
erosion of its effectiveness.

"You found this before I did," Kanja said, genuine credit in it rather than surprise. "What do you
think we do about it?"

"Vary the structure more than we have been. We've relied on a few reliable patterns because they
work. That reliability is exactly what gave the cryptographer something consistent to study." She'd
already begun sketching alternate constructions, drawing on lessons from her own apprenticeship
rather than simply reporting the problem and waiting for Kanja to solve it. "I don't think this ends
the method. I think it means the craft has to keep growing the same way any craft does when someone
smart enough finally starts studying it seriously."

"Then let's grow it." Kanja reviewed her sketched alternatives with real attention, several genuinely
better than anything he'd have built alone. "You've moved past being taught the method. You're
building the next version of it. That's not a small thing to watch happen."

The new constructions entered use within weeks, the cryptographer's partial breakthrough rendered
obsolete before it could fully mature — the Hymn-Engine's first real evolution, driven as much by the
apprentice's own growing mastery as by Kanja's original craft.
