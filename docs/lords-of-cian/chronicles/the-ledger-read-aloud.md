# The Ledger Read Aloud

*Locked canon, Batch 198, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-745`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XX, wave 7 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The magistrate's chamber was not built for a man like Kanja to stand in — no weapon permitted past
the door, which suited him fine, and no precedent either for a rebel's ledger to be entered as
testimony in a formal Trust hearing, which suited nobody in the room.

"You understand," the presiding magistrate said, "that this chamber does not simply accept a private
account as fact because it is thick and well organized."

"I don't need you to accept it as fact." Ezio set the ledger on the rail before the bench himself,
since Kanja had been told, correctly, that an unarmed man standing too close to the bench made the
guards nervous regardless of what he was or wasn't carrying. "I need you to test it as fact. Every
entry is sourced to a named worker and cross-checked against the district's own production records,
which your own clerks are welcome to pull and compare, page by page, in front of this chamber, right
now."

"And if we find discrepancies?"

"Then strike those entries and keep the rest. I'm not asking you to trust the book. I'm asking you to
read it."

The magistrate assigned two clerks to the cross-check, expecting, by the visible set of his jaw, to
find enough error to dismiss the whole submission by lunch. They did not finish by lunch. They did
not finish by evening. Three clerks working two full days against the district's own sealed
production logs found four entries requiring correction out of four hundred and twelve, each
correction moving a figure by a matter of coin, not principle, and none of them in the district's
favor.

"The account stands," the magistrate said finally, reading the clerks' own findings into the record
with visible reluctance, "as substantially accurate."

"Then it's entered." Ezio didn't ask for more than that, because he'd learned by then not to expect
it in one sitting. "What happens with it after that is this chamber's business, not mine."

The chamber granted no immediate remedy that day — no back wages ordered, no administrator censured,
the matter formally "taken under advisement" in the dry language such chambers used when they meant
to do nothing quickly. But the ledger was in the record now, tested and unbroken, in a room where
rebel testimony had never before survived cross-examination intact, and that alone outlasted the
magistrate's reluctance to act on it.
