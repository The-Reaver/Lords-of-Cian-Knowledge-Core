# The Page That Went Missing

*Locked canon, Batch 260, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1257`). Crow King Alias Chronicle LXV, wave 22. The
single written page of doctrine is lost during a raid, testing whether the craft's real
vulnerability was ever a document at all. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The raid on the supply cache went wrong in the ordinary way raids go wrong -- a patrol arrived early,
the crew scattered rather than fought, and in the scramble the third generation came out with two
packs instead of one and neither of them, when she finally stopped running long enough to check, was
hers.

The page was in the other one.

She didn't sleep that night. Not from fear of what the page could tell an enemy about tactics --
there was nothing in it a checkpoint captain could use, nothing about drainage channels or thermal
readings or the Braid's real mechanics. What kept her awake was smaller and harder to name: it had
been hers, in her own hand, the one copy, and now it was gone into a pack some Directorate quartermaster
would empty onto a table without understanding a word of what he was holding.

She told Kanja everything, expecting him to be angrier than he was.

"What does it actually cost us if they read it?" he asked.

She turned the question over for longer than she wanted to admit needing. "Nothing," she said
finally, surprised to hear herself say it. "It tells them to listen before they act. To value people
over plans. If a Directorate officer reads that and takes it to heart, that's not a loss for us."

"Then what were you afraid of losing?"

"That it was mine. That I wrote it and now it's just -- out there, with no way to know who reads it or
whether they understand it or laugh at it."

Three weeks later, word came back through an unrelated channel: a defecting Directorate clerk, asked
why he'd finally walked away from his post, produced a folded, well-read page and said it had been
circulating among a small handful of officers for weeks -- copied by hand, re-copied, discussed in
low voices. Someone had added a line of their own at the bottom, in a different hand: *We already
knew this. We just needed someone to write it down first.*

The third generation read the addition three times before she said anything. "It wasn't a secret,"
she said finally. "It was never a secret. I don't think it can be stolen, because it was never
something only we had."
