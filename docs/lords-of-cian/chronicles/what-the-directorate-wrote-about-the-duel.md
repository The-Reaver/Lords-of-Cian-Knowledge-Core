# What the Directorate Wrote About the Duel

*Locked canon, Batch 280, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1468`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
CII, wave 34, closing the wave. Closes the arc opened at the wave's own start three entries back
(`MCD-1460`): the First Blade's honest report of his defeat and the mercy shown him is disbelieved and
quietly buried by his own institution. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose.*

---

The First Blade wrote his report the same night he returned, before anyone could tell him what he was
supposed to have seen instead of what he had. He wrote that he had lost, cleanly, to a swordsman whose
technique read intention before it committed. He wrote that his own life had been in the man's hand and
the man had chosen not to take it, asking only for an honest account carried home. He signed it with his
full name and rank, and delivered it himself.

His superiors read it twice. The second reading was worse than the first.

"A First Blade doesn't lose," the reviewing officer told him, not unkindly, "and if one somehow did, he
certainly doesn't come back to write a report thanking the pirate who spared him. Do you understand what
this document does to twenty years of training records if it's filed as written?"

"It's the truth," the First Blade said.

"It's inconvenient," the officer said, "which in this line of work amounts to the same problem." The
report was filed, eventually, but reclassified — the encounter recorded as inconclusive, the duel itself
scrubbed from any record that might reach a training curriculum built on the idea that the Directorate's
best had no equal. The First Blade himself was quietly reassigned to a coastal registry post three
provinces removed from open water, his career not ended, exactly, only permanently redirected somewhere
it would never again be tested against anything that mattered.

Garren Hask heard the shape of it months later, through channels the fleet kept for exactly this kind of
thing, and logged it beside the original duel entry without editorializing.

"They didn't believe him either," Kanja said, when Hask read it to him. "Same as the officer who lost
his commission for telling the truth about a sighting."

"Different truth," Hask said. "Same wall."

"Then he knows exactly where he stands with people who'll actually listen," Kanja said, "which is more
than most men in that uniform ever get to know about themselves."
