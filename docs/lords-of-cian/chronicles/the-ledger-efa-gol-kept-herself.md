# The Ledger Efa Gol Kept Herself

*Locked canon, Batch 257, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1192`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle LXXXI, wave 27, closing the wave. Efa Gol presents her own independent accounting of the alias's toll -- a second-voice legacy entry paying off her wave-19 introduction.*

---

Efa Gol had been running diversions and coordinating evacuations for this alias since the flooding fight that first brought her into its orbit, and it turned out she'd been keeping her own tally the entire time, separate from Garren Hask's official ledger, in a hand nobody had asked her to write in.

She brought it to Kanja unprompted, a worn notebook thick with dates and names. "Hask counts the money. Somebody should count the rest."

It wasn't a duplicate of anything already kept. Hask's ledger tracked hazard funds, wages, material costs -- the honest financial accounting the crew's own economy needed to function. Gol's tracked something else: every worker's name who'd come through a rescue she'd helped stage, every diversion that had cost her people nothing because the timing held, and, in a separate section near the back, every name from the diversions that hadn't gone perfectly, the near-misses and the two outright losses she carried without ever mentioning them in the moment.

"You've never told me about half of this," Kanja said, reading a page recording a surface diversion three years back he remembered only from her own account at the time -- an account that, he now realized, had left out exactly how close it had come.

"You had enough weighing on you already." She didn't apologize for the omission. "Didn't seem like it would've changed anything, telling you in the moment. Seemed like it should be written down somewhere, though. Not forgotten. Just not yours to carry on top of everything else."

He asked to see the full notebook, and she let him, watching him read through names he recognized and some he didn't -- people her diversions had protected that he'd never met because the whole point of her work was that he never had to. It was a different kind of accounting than Hask's cost tally or his own memory of the sieges themselves: a ground-level record of exactly what the work had actually cost and saved, kept by someone who'd never once asked for credit for keeping it.

"This should be part of the real record," Kanja said. "Not just yours."

"It is now," Gol said. "I just needed you to read it before I said that out loud."
