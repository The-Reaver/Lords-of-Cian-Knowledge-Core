# What Danne Sok Didn't Believe

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle III, wave 6,
closing the wave. Rebellion era (age 19-21), unarmed and non-combat throughout, matching the alias's
established ethos. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters.*

---

Danne Sok had fought at the Black Trench and half a dozen battles since, and he did not understand
why he'd been assigned to stand outside a tent full of paperwork instead of somewhere his hands were
useful.

"You don't need a guard for a book," he said, the second night, watching Kanja work by lamplight
through the tent flap. "You need a guard for a man they want dead. Nobody's tried to kill a ledger
yet."

"They've tried to burn a few." Kanja didn't look up from the page. "Stay anyway."

Danne stayed, unconvinced, through three uneventful nights, until the fourth, when a dozen men came
down from the district's outer settlement carrying torches and a grievance of their own — not against
Kanja, but against a warehouse they meant to burn that night regardless of what any ledger said,
convinced that fire would move the administrators faster than four days of patient tallying ever
would.

Danne's hand went to where a blade would have been if he'd been let carry one. There was nothing
there. He stood in front of the tent flap anyway, because that was the order, and braced for a fight
he had no way to win.

Kanja came out instead, unarmed, and did not raise his voice over the crowd's. He simply asked them
the same four words he'd been asking everyone else for four days, and when they answered — furious,
disbelieving, certain fire was the only language anyone above them understood — he wrote it down.
Every grievance. Every name. He read the growing tally back to them once it was done, out loud, in
front of the warehouse they'd meant to burn, and told them the number was already large enough to be
undeniable without a single building lost to prove it.

They didn't burn it. They went home instead, still angry, but carrying a receipt instead of a torch.

Danne watched the whole exchange from four feet away, close enough to have done nothing if it had
gone wrong, and said nothing himself until the crowd had gone.

"I still don't believe it," he said finally. "I don't believe a book stops men who've already decided
to burn something."

"You don't have to believe it." Kanja went back inside to finish the entry. "You watched it work.
That's enough for tonight."

Danne didn't answer that either, but he took up the same post the next night without being told to,
and didn't ask again why a ledger needed a guard.
