# The Fight He Couldn't Walk Away From

*Locked canon, Batch 151, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-476`). Bane Alias Chronicle X, first entry in the fourth
wave. Rebellion era, age 19, shortly after the Black Trench. Not a territory Chronicle. New
standalone material — a detailed armor-and-weapon combat showcase per Abad's craft instruction, this
time against a single peer-level opponent rather than an overwhelmed garrison. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The Directorate had finally sent someone who wasn't afraid of the reputation — a duelist trained
outside conventional garrison doctrine entirely, chosen specifically because his record showed no
sign of the hesitation Bane's presence usually produced in an opponent. He met Bane on an open plaza
at midday, in full view of both sides, and made clear from his opening stance that this would be
decided by skill alone, not by whatever the stories claimed Bane actually was.

It was the first fight since the Black Trench that Bane couldn't simply end early.

Onyx of Oblivion met a blade handled with genuine mastery, Cadence Ruin finding no disrupted rhythm
to exploit because the duelist fought without any rhythm to break — pure, trained improvisation,
adjusting to every attempted disruption before it could fully land. Whisper of Shadows closed
distance that closed again a heartbeat later, the duelist's own footwork matching pace for pace
rather than being caught flat-footed the way every prior opponent had been. For the first several
minutes, neither Mafesto's stored charge nor Obsidian Malice's held cycle found an opening worth
spending on — this wasn't a fight decided by equipment. It was decided by two skilled fighters
reading each other in real time.

The turn came narrow. The duelist, pressing an advantage that had genuinely worked twice already,
overcommitted a third time on the same angle — a pattern Cadence Ruin finally had something real to
disrupt, breaking the duelist's confidence in his own read a half-second before Veil Piercer found
the gap that confidence had been covering. The fight ended there, close enough that either side
watching honestly would call it closer than any Bane engagement before it.

"You could have used the equipment from the start," the duelist said afterward, disarmed but
unhumiliated, something like respect in it rather than the usual fear. "Ended this in the opening
exchange, the way every account says you always do."

"I could have. I didn't want to, this time. You came here on skill alone, refusing to be afraid of
anything but the fight itself. That deserved an answer in kind." Bane offered a hand up, genuinely
meant. "You're the first opponent in a long while who made me work for it instead of simply ending
it. I don't intend to forget that."
