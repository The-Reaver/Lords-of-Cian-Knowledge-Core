# What Efa Gol Taught the New Ones

*Locked canon, Batch 254, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1105`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXXV, wave 25, closing wave
25. Efa Gol personally trains new recruits in decoy and diversion tactics -- generational
transmission through her rather than Bane, tied to her own established loss of Tam Sullen.*

---

Efa Gol didn't teach the way Corren Halst did. Halst built discipline through review and correction,
a plan checked and re-checked until it held. Efa Gol taught by putting people directly into the
problem and watching how they moved through it, the same way she'd learned it herself, running the
decoy line at Iron Shallows with nothing but instinct and a partner she trusted to be exactly where he
said he'd be.

The six recruits she had this time were all younger than the war, none of them old enough to remember
a world before it. She ran them through a mock diversion three times before she let any of them near
a real one — a false line of noise and movement meant to pull a garrison's attention away from the
actual breach point, timed to the second, everyone's position depending on everyone else's holding
theirs.

"The whole thing only works if you trust the person next to you is where they said they'd be," she
told them, the third time through, when one recruit had drifted a few yards from his marked position
out of nerves and nearly collapsed the whole line's timing. "That's not a suggestion. It's the entire
tactic. The moment one of you decides you know better than the plan, in the moment, without saying so
first — that's the moment someone doesn't come back."

She didn't tell them about Tam Sullen. She didn't need to; the column's older members already knew,
and the way she said it left no doubt she was speaking from something specific rather than a general
principle. The recruit who'd drifted understood it anyway, in the way people usually understand the
things said to them most plainly.

They ran the real diversion four days later, and it held — six new names doing exactly what the plan
asked of them, a garrison's attention pulled cleanly away from the breach, no one lost.

Bane found her afterward, watching the recruits break down their gear with the particular quiet of
people who'd just done something right for the first time.

"You never make it about him," Bane said.

"It doesn't need to be about him for it to be because of him." Efa Gol didn't look away from the
recruits. "I teach it this carefully because I know exactly what it costs when someone doesn't learn
it carefully enough. That's reason enough. They don't need the whole story to get the lesson right."
