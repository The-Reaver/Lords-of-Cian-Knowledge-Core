# The Smith Who Resented the Crown

*Locked canon, Batch 125, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-424`). Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle VI, closing the
second wave. Rebellion era, during the Rolling Foundry Campaign (`MCD-241`). Not a territory
Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters.*

---

The settlement's own senior smith had spent thirty years earning his standing the ordinary way, one
commission and one apprentice at a time, and resented, quietly but completely, the arrival of a
title-bearing outsider whose reputation as "Lord of Embers" threatened to make thirty years of
unglamorous local work look small by comparison.

"You've got a name that travels ahead of you," he said, watching Kanja work at a borrowed forge with
the settlement's own apprentices gathered to observe rather than learn from their actual master. "I
built this forge with my own hands. Trained every smith in this settlement personally. And in walks a
war hero with a title, and suddenly my own apprentices are more interested in watching him true a
hinge than finishing the commissions I've actually assigned them."

"That's a fair complaint." Kanja didn't argue the point, didn't minimize it. "I didn't come here to
replace thirty years of your work with one afternoon of mine. If my being here is undermining what
you've built, that's a real cost, and I should have thought about it before I set up at a forge in
your own settlement without asking you first."

The smith hadn't expected the concession, and it cost him some of the resentment's momentum. "What
would you have done differently?"

"Asked you to teach the lesson instead. I could demonstrate a technique. You could actually train
them in it, the way you've trained every other skill they've got. The title gets their attention for
an afternoon. Your thirty years is what actually makes them smiths." Kanja stepped back from the
forge, offering it over without ceremony. "I don't need the credit. I need the apprentices to
actually learn something, and you're a better teacher of your own settlement than I'll ever be, no
matter what any Directorate report calls me."

The smith took the forge, and the lesson, and spent the rest of the afternoon teaching his own
apprentices a technique he'd have gladly credited to a passing stranger's demonstration — except that
the stranger had deliberately declined the credit, leaving it entirely with the man who'd actually
earned thirty years of it.

"You didn't need to do that," the smith admitted, once the lesson was done and the apprentices had
dispersed, visibly more engaged with their own master's teaching than they'd been in months. "Most
men with a name like yours would have let the moment be about the name."

"Most days I probably would have too, without thinking about it." Kanja began packing his own gear.
"You reminded me it wasn't the right call today. That's worth more to me than the afternoon's
credit ever would have been."
