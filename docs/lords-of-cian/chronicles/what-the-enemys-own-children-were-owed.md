# What the Enemy's Own Children Were Owed

*Locked canon, Batch 276, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1426`). Bane Alias Chronicle XCVI, wave 32, closing wave
32. First entry extending "the fear only works if it's true" (`MCD-431`) to the dependents of enemy
soldiers rather than to enemies, civilians, or captives themselves.*

---

The supply-chain campaign had done exactly what it was built to do: three weeks of quiet, deniable
sabotage (the same method that had starved the Threshbend depot into surrender, `MCD-700`) had cut
the garrison town's grain wagons off at the root, and the garrison itself was down to half-rations and
short tempers. It was working. Corren Halst said so with something close to satisfaction when the
column passed close enough to the town's outer quarter to see it.

What they saw first was children.

Not the garrison's — the garrison ate before anyone else did, same as any occupying force — but the
soldiers' own families, quartered in the civilian rows behind the walls, drawing on the same cut
supply the campaign had aimed at the men hunting Bane's column. A woman at a well told Danne Sok,
without knowing who was asking, that her husband hadn't eaten a full meal in nine days and neither had
their two children, and that she didn't blame anyone for the war, she just wanted it to stop taking
things from people who hadn't started it.

Bane had a wagon of captured grain, taken from the same garrison's own stores two days earlier, meant
to feed the column through the next stretch of road. He redirected half of it into the civilian
quarter that night, in the dark, no announcement, no name attached.

Corren Halst caught up with him before it finished. "You're feeding the families of the men who'll be
shooting at us again inside a month, once their supply line's patched."

"I'm feeding children whose fathers didn't ask them what side to be born on," Bane said. "The campaign
was never against them. If it starts costing them specifically, it's not pressure anymore, it's
punishment with worse aim than the garrison itself has."

"That's a distinction the garrison won't make."

"It's not their distinction to make. It's mine." He watched the last sack come off the wagon. "The
reputation only holds if it's actually true that I'm not the thing they're afraid I am. The day it
stops being true for someone just because they're standing on the wrong side of a wall, it stops being
true at all."

The woman from the well found the marked crates at her door the next morning and never learned who
left them, and never asked the column's escort — moving through at dawn, ordinary and unremarkable —
a single question about it.
