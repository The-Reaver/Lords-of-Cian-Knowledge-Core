# What the Quarry Wall Held Back

*Locked canon, Batch 202, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-877`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XXXII, wave 11 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The quarry cut deep into a hillside the campaign had been working for six days, extracting stone for
the next four rebuild sites, its narrow access cut and stacked stone blocks offering a Trust
demolition team exactly the kind of confined, collapsible terrain a coordinated strike could turn
against everyone working inside it.

The charges went in high, along the quarry's own load-bearing cut face, timed to bring the entire
working wall down on the crews below rather than simply attack them directly.

Kanja was already moving before the first charge finished settling into its seat. Mafesto's Kinetic
Transfer System read the cut face's own structural strain the way it had once read a resonating hull,
converting the demolition team's detonation timing into a countdown he could actually beat. Obsidian
Malice discharged directly into the primary charge's own housing, the shockwave disrupting its
detonation sequence before the wall above it could fully destabilize. Onyx cleared the demolition
team's secondary charges in a sequence brief enough that the quarry's remaining crews never fully
understood, until well after, how close the whole cut face had come to burying them.

The wall held. Two charges detonated harmlessly against stone already braced against the collapse they
were meant to cause; the rest never fired at all.

"You read that collapse before we even heard the charges going in," the quarry's own foreman said,
still pale, surveying the intact cut face. "I've worked this stone twenty years. Didn't hear a thing
until you were already moving."

"I wasn't listening for the charges. I was listening for what the stone itself was about to do."
Kanja checked the cut face's bracing, satisfied it had held true. "Same principle as anything else this
campaign's built. You don't wait for the break to happen and then answer it. You read what's about to
give, and you're already there before it does."
