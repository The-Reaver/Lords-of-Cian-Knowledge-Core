# The Student Who Chose Restraint

*Locked canon, Batch 158, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-499`). The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XII, closing the
fourth wave. Rebellion era, age 25. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

A young engineer attached to the crew, gifted enough with structural mechanics to have picked up the
rudiments of diagnostic listening faster than Kanja had expected anyone could, found himself alone
one evening beside a Trust granary his own resentment over a family debt made genuinely tempting to
bring down — the granary's owner among the men who'd profited from the debt that had cost his family
their land.

He had the listening skill to find the frequency. He didn't use it.

"I stood there long enough to actually feel it," he admitted to Kanja the next day, plainly still
unsettled by how close he'd come. "I could have brought that granary down and told myself it was
justice. I don't think it would have been. I think it would have just been me, angry, with a tool
you taught me for something else entirely."

"What stopped you?"

"You did, honestly — not standing over my shoulder, just what you taught alongside the technique
itself. You never once used this doctrine for anything that wasn't already a fight already chosen for
good reasons. I kept hearing that in my head, the whole time I was standing there." The young man met
Kanja's eyes, some real weight lifted by the admission. "I don't know if I'd have stopped without
that. I'd rather not find out by testing it again."

"I'm glad you told me, rather than let me find out some other way." Kanja didn't diminish how close
the moment had actually come, didn't pretend the danger wasn't real. "The doctrine isn't just a
skill. It's a responsibility, and I've clearly done enough teaching the skill that I need to spend
more time teaching the responsibility that has to travel with it. That's on me as much as it's on
you."

"What happens to my training now?"

"It continues. You told me the truth about a moment you didn't have to tell me about at all. That
matters more to whether I trust you with this than the fact that the moment happened in the first
place." Kanja set a hand on the young man's shoulder. "Everyone who's ever held real power has stood
exactly where you stood last night. What separates the ones worth teaching from the ones who
shouldn't be taught at all is whether they walk away from that edge, and whether they're honest about
having stood on it."
