# The Ones Who Called Him That First

*Locked canon, Batch 107, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-370`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle III. Rebellion
era, closing the alias's three-Chronicle wave. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material.
Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters -- the old dredge worker and the
apprentice are both unnamed.*

---

The old woman who ran the tally-shack at the fourth dredge site had never seen the Trench Monarch,
not once, not even at a distance, and had opinions about him anyway that she was perfectly willing to
share with anyone young enough to still be asking.

"You're new," she said to the apprentice tallying beside her, a boy of maybe fifteen who'd been
sent down from the upper canal to learn the manifest work. "So you don't know yet that half of what
you'll hear about him this year is going to be wrong, and the other half is going to be true in a way
that doesn't sound like it should be."

"Wrong how?"

"They'll tell you he's eight feet tall. He isn't. They'll tell you he doesn't bleed. He bled plenty at
the third site, I heard it from a man who was there — just didn't stop for it, which people mistake for
the same thing." She marked a figure in her ledger without looking up. "The part they get right,
without ever meaning to, is that he doesn't come here for us. Not the way you'd think. Every crew boss
before him came through this canal wanting something — a cut, a favor, a story to carry back to
whoever paid him. This one wants the number. Just the number. What's owed, and who's not paying it."

"That's all?"

"That's everything." She set the pen down and looked at him properly for the first time. "You know
what it does to a man, being lied to about his own wages for thirty years, when somebody finally
walks up and says the number out loud in front of everyone who's been lying to him? It doesn't feel
like rescue. It feels like being told you weren't crazy. That's a harder thing to give somebody than a
sword fight, and nobody chalks a nickname on a captured officer's forehead for a man who does that —
they chalk it for somebody who fights. But it's the number that earns it. The fighting's just what
they remember, because fighting's easier to tell."

The apprentice thought about that for a while, watching the barges move sludge-slow along the canal
that three sites down had been deliberately flooded to drown a punitive column's advantage in mud.
"Do you think he knows that? That it's the number, not the sword?"

"I think somebody told him once, or he worked it out himself, because I hear the name still bothers
him. That's the part that tells me it's real." She picked the pen back up. "A king who's comfortable
being called one, you should worry about. A king who keeps flinching at it every time somebody says
it out loud — that's a man who still remembers he didn't ask for the crown, and hasn't decided yet
he's earned the right to stop questioning it. That's the one worth trusting. Write the number down,
boy. That's the whole job. Everything after that is just what people call you for doing it."
