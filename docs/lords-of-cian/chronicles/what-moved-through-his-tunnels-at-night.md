# What Moved Through His Tunnels at Night

*Locked canon, Batch 195, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-660`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XXV, wave 9 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Someone was using the crew's own cleared sections of tunnel to move contraband — salvaged goods,
skimmed rations, nothing tied to the Trust or the war at all, just opportunists who'd noticed a
newly reopened stretch of underground passage and decided it was worth exploiting before anyone
official thought to check it.

Kanja found the evidence before he found the people: wheel-ruts from a cart too narrow for any of
the crew's own equipment, a discarded wrapper from rations the tunnel crew didn't carry, boot prints
crossing a section that should have shown only their own traffic.

He waited for them the next night, unarmed, sitting in plain sight at the junction their route had
to pass through.

"You're using tunnels people are dying to keep secret," he said, when the cart and its two handlers
came around the bend and found him waiting. "Every trip you make down here is a trail someone
official could follow straight back to a war effort you have nothing to do with."

"We're not hurting anyone. Just moving goods nobody's using this route for anyway."

"You're risking everyone who is using this route, whether you mean to or not. One patrol finds your
wheel-ruts, they don't stop to ask whose they are. They just know someone's moving traffic down
here, and they start watching every tunnel in the district a lot more closely than they were
before." Kanja didn't move to block the cart, didn't reach for a weapon, simply kept talking. "I'm
not interested in punishing you for trying to survive a war the same as everyone else down here. I
am interested in you finding a different route, tonight, before your traffic gets people killed who
never signed up to carry your risk alongside their own."

The two handlers exchanged a look, weighing threat against reason, and found the second read more
useful than the first.

"There's an older drainage line two streets over. Slower, but nobody's watching it."

"Then use that one." Kanja finally stood, letting them pass. "I'd rather solve this with a
conversation than a confrontation neither of us actually wants. You're not my enemy. You're just
inconvenient to a war you didn't ask to be part of. That's not the same thing."

The wheel-ruts never reappeared in the crew's sections again, and the two handlers, for reasons that
never made it into any report, began leaving a portion of their salvage at a marked junction each
week — an unspoken toll neither side ever formally negotiated, paid anyway.
