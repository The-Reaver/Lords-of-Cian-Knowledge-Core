# When the Crow Could Not Fly

*Locked canon, Batch 123, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-417`). Crow King Alias Chronicle V. Rebellion era, a
detailed Trinity combat showcase for an occasion when evasion genuinely fails and Kanja must fight
his way clear instead. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The private's accurate report reached a garrison commander fast enough, once, to close a
counter-encirclement before the Hymn-Engine's Braid could finish its own coordinated resolution — a
genuine trap, this time, sprung on a compound only half-evacuated, with no clean evasion left to
execute.

Kanja stopped singing and started fighting.

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System took the opening volley meant to pin him in place, converting three
seconds of concentrated fire into stored charge before the garrison's own forward line had finished
reloading. Obsidian Malice discharged directly into the counter-encirclement's weakest seam — not the
strongest point, which any tactician would have reinforced first, but the specific gap between two
units that had never trained together and didn't yet trust each other's timing, the war club's single
strike collapsing the one place two separate chains of command had failed to coordinate cleanly.
Onyx moved through the opening that created, Cadence Ruin unmaking the pursuit's own coordinated
tightening before it could close, buying the last ninety seconds the compound's final evacuees needed
to clear the perimeter.

"You're not supposed to fight," the garrison commander said, over the chaos, genuinely startled — his
entire operation had been built around a doctrine that treated the Crow King as a pure evasion threat,
never once war-gaming what happened if the trick failed and the man behind it simply turned and
fought instead. "Every report says you run."

"Every report's about the Hymn-Engine. That's a tool, not the whole of what I am." Kanja closed the
last of the gap Obsidian Malice had opened, Onyx already quiet again in his hand as the compound's
last evacuees cleared behind him. "You built a doctrine to counter a trick. You never built one to
counter the man who invented it, because you assumed the trick was all there was to counter. That's
the actual lesson here, and it's a more expensive one than the marsh ever taught you."

The compound emptied in full within the following twenty minutes, the counter-encirclement broken
not through deception this time but through plain, decisive combat — and the garrison's own doctrine
review, filed weeks later, would flag the gap explicitly: every existing countermeasure assumed the
Crow King's only weapon was escape, leaving nothing on file for the rare occasions escape simply
wasn't available to him.
