# The Word Before the Alias

*Locked canon, Batch 116, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-395`). Captain Alias Chronicle I. Rebellion era, the
naval campaigns (`MCD-242`, ages 21-22), from Garren Hask's perspective -- already a locked named
character (`CC-115`/`CC-116`). Not a territory Chronicle. No new named characters beyond the
already-locked Garren Hask.*

---

Garren Hask had been the first person to call Kanja by his bare name instead of "boy" or "son," back
at the Dredge-Line Ambush when the boy was still eighteen and hadn't yet earned a single alias the
Directorate would bother classifying. It took him three more years, and two captured flagships he'd
personally named himself, to settle on the word that would eventually outlast every title the
Directorate ever gave him.

"The Audit," he said, the morning after Iron Shallows, standing on the newly captured deck with his
tally ledger already open, "and I'm calling the next one The Receipt, when we take her. Somebody's
got to keep count of what this war actually costs and what it actually recovers, and I've decided
that's going to be my job, same as it's always been."

"You could have named them something with more fight in it," Kanja said, reading over his shoulder.
"Vengeance. Reckoning. Something the crew can shout."

"I could have. I didn't, because a ship's name isn't for shouting. It's for remembering what the
number's supposed to mean when you're not looking at it anymore." Hask closed the ledger, satisfied
with the day's tally. "Same reason I don't call you Bane, or the Trench Monarch, or whatever name's
currently making the Directorate nervous. Those are titles for what you do to people who deserve to
be afraid of you. I'm not afraid of you. I've watched you personally check every name on every
casualty list since Dredge-Line, and I've never once seen you skip one because the number was
inconvenient. That's not a Trench Monarch's job. That's a captain's job — the person responsible for
everyone aboard, whose name goes on what happens to them either way."

"So that's what you're going to call me."

"That's what I already call you, in my own head, every time I write your orders into this ledger.
I'm just telling you out loud now, so you know it's not an accident." Hask handed the ledger over,
letting Kanja check the day's count himself the way he always did. "The Directorate can keep coining
names for what scares them. I'd rather have one word that just means the person I trust to bring
everyone home, or at least to be honest with me when he can't."

The name spread through the crew the way names born of genuine respect usually do — quietly, without
ceremony, until nobody could quite remember hearing it for the first time, only that it had somehow
always been the word that felt true.
