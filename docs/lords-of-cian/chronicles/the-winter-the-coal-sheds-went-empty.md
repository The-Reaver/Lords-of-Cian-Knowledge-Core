# The Winter the Coal Sheds Went Empty

*Locked canon, Batch 273, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1415`). Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XCI, first entry
in the thirty-first wave. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241). The alias's
first entry testing "metabolizes punishment" against natural deprivation with no enemy involved at
all — an early hard freeze, not a Directorate act. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose.*

---

The freeze came three weeks ahead of any almanac's prediction and stayed a full month past when the
coastal shipping lanes should have thawed, and by the time The Anvil reached the third of the three
settlements caught inside it, every coal shed along the route was already scraped down to floor
dust. No Directorate hand had done this. No embargo, no burning, no seized convoy. Just weather,
indifferent to who it starved.

Garren Hask had the numbers within a day of arriving — the exact carry-forward each settlement still
held, the exact rate each forge was burning through it, and the exact date each one would go cold if
nothing changed. He laid the tally out for Kanja the way he always did, without embellishment. "Site
one's got eleven days. Site two's got six. Site three, the one we're standing in, has three."

"Then we don't burn evenly," Kanja said. "We burn where three days actually means something."

It wasn't a popular answer at first. The settlement smith at the second site, a broad-shouldered man
who'd banked his own private reserve against exactly this kind of winter, refused outright to
release it to a rationing pool that would see his own forge go quiet two days sooner than it needed
to. It was his apprentice, not Kanja, who finally moved him — a girl no older than fifteen, asking
flatly whether he'd rather keep his own fire lit and watch the settlement down the coast lose theirs
entirely, or bank the coal and know exactly what it bought.

He released it before the day was out, and said nothing further about it, which Garren Hask noted in
the ledger anyway: *given freely, second asking.*

What actually carried the three settlements through wasn't more fuel — there wasn't any more to find
— but smarter use of what existed. Kanja walked each forge floor personally, teaching a
waste-heat-recovery trick learned off a Karesian foundry text decades out of print: venting a
working forge's exhaust through a second, unlit hearth to pre-warm green stock before it ever touched
the coal fire at all, cutting the fuel a full smelt required by nearly a third. Staggered shifts kept
every forge burning on alternating days rather than all three guttering out together. By the time the
thaw finally broke, all three sheds were empty and all three forges were still standing, and not one
day of work had actually been lost across the whole month.

"We didn't beat the winter," Kanja said, watching the first supply barge finally make it through the
ice-choked channel. "We just made sure it never got to choose which of us it starved first."

Garren Hask closed the ledger on the month with a single line, the kind he only wrote when a number
actually meant something beyond itself: *nobody went cold who didn't have to.*
