# The First Time He Taught Them to Fight

*Locked canon, Batch 277, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1441`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle CII, wave 34,
closing the wave. Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. Kanja directly teaches bladework to his three
earliest crew members for the first time, distinct from his training of Nev Torr (`MCD-1063`) and
of an unrelated bullied bystander (`MCD-948`).*

---

He had trained a frightened conscript's boy and a fourteen-year-old stranger's footwork before he
ever taught Corren Halst, Danne Sok, or Maret Vos a single deliberate thing about how to hold a
blade -- an oversight none of the three had ever raised, and one Kanja only noticed himself the
morning after the tannery ambush left him bound and blind for the better part of an hour. The three
men who'd been with him since before the name existed had learned to fight the way most of the
Rebellion learned anything: by surviving it, not by being taught it.

He corrected that with the same plainness he brought to everything else, three sessions a week on
the Warehouse Twelve loading yard once the day's tally work closed, no Onyx involved at all -- only
an ordinary blade, ordinary footwork, and the same patience he'd shown the fourteen-year-old stretched
now across three grown men who each, in their own way, resisted being taught something they'd
already survived without it.

Corren Halst, methodical in everything else, fought like a man solving a puzzle rather than reading
an opponent, and took three weeks to unlearn the habit of watching his own feet instead of the blade
in front of him. Danne Sok needed almost nothing corrected in his guard and everything corrected in
his silence -- he fought exactly as quietly as he spoke, giving no tell at all until the moment he
committed, which worked precisely until Kanja taught him that reading an opponent required, at
minimum, glancing at one. Maret Vos, freed before the Black Trench with nothing but his own two
hands and whatever he'd learned surviving worse than any of them, turned out to already carry
instincts none of the drilling had put there -- Kanja spent more of Maret's sessions asking questions
about where those instincts came from than correcting anything about them.

None of the three would carry a blade the way Onyx carried one for years yet, if ever. That wasn't
the point, and Kanja said so plainly the evening he ended the formal sessions, satisfied rather than
finished. "I'm not building three more of me. I already have one of those, and it isn't always
enough." He handed each man back his own blade rather than a copy of anyone else's. "I only wanted
you three to stop needing me standing between you and the worst of it, the way I needed someone to
stand between me and mine, once, before there was anyone who could."
