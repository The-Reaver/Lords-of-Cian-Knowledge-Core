# The Harvest That Wouldn't Wait

*Locked canon, Batch 243, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1066`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle LXII, wave 21. The method's first agricultural-domain application, forcing its established four-day patient-documentation pacing to bend against a hard natural deadline -- a harvest window measured in fruit going soft on the branch -- rather than an administrator's artificial stalling. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Every district the crew had worked so far ran on a stalling clock that belonged to someone. An administrator could delay a day, a week, a month, and the only cost was the workers' patience and whatever leverage time gave the crew to build a fuller case. The orchard estate ran on a different clock entirely, and it belonged to nobody. The stone-fruit crop across four hundred rows was three days from turning, maybe four if the weather held, and every hour spent on testimony instead of picking was fruit that would hit the ground soft and unsellable — which meant every hour spent on testimony instead of picking was also the wage the pickers were owed, shrinking in real time, no administrator's hand on the scale at all.

Ezio's instinct was to do what the method always did: take full testimony, cross-reference it properly, build the case before acting on it. Kanja stopped him before the first sheet was laid out. "The numbers don't care if we're right in four days if there's nothing left worth being right about."

What they built instead was the method's first deliberately compressed pass — testimony taken in the field, between rows, a line of workers answering three essential questions each without breaking rhythm, while Ezio cross-checked names against the estate's own harvest-quota boards rather than against separately gathered corroborating statements. It was less rigorous than any tally the crew had ever put its name to, and Ezio said so out loud, more than once, uncomfortable with his own handwriting.

It held anyway, because the estate owner's underpayment wasn't hidden behind fabricated records or staged testimony the way city administrators had learned to manage — it was a flat, quota-based shortfall visible in the estate's own books the moment anyone bothered to add the columns honestly, and there was no time for anyone to doctor them before the crop came in. By the fourth morning the full crew was picking alongside four hundred workers to save what could still be saved, Ezio's ledger balanced and closed before the last crate was loaded, and the settlement paid out of that season's own sale rather than months later out of some future one.

"We didn't do this the right way," Ezio said afterward, meaning the compressed testimony, the field cross-checks, all of it.

"We did it the way that mattered this time," Kanja said. "The right way's still there for every district that isn't rotting while we use it."
