# What the Sea Kept Between Them

*Locked canon, Batch 217, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-959`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle LVII, wave 19. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Garren Hask closed the season's ledger the way he closed every season's ledger, with the full account
laid out plain: ships disabled rather than sunk, captives freed rather than ransomed, a Fleet-Marshal
who'd come to apologize instead of attack, a hand who'd left in disagreement and another who'd stayed
until his knees gave out, a food line run through a blockade, a debt paid to something that lived in
water no chart had ever properly named. He read it aloud to the assembled crew at anchor, the way he
did once a year, less for their benefit than for his own certainty that the true record still matched
the legend.

"It doesn't match," Danne Sok said, when he'd finished. "Not really. The legend's cleaner than any of
this. The legend doesn't have a season where we nearly starved doing nothing dramatic at all."

"The legend was never going to have that in it," Kanja said. "Nobody tells a story about grain being
rationed evenly. They tell the one about the ghost ship and the six warships it slipped past. That's
fine. I never asked the legend to be accurate. I asked us to be, and Hask's ledger is where that
actually lives."

Dol Maren, patching a section of hull that had taken damage from the season's last engagement, didn't
look up from the work. "Forty years from now, when half of us aren't here to remember it firsthand,
that ledger's the only place any of this survives honestly. The rest turns into whatever story serves
whoever's telling it."

"Then we keep making the ledger worth reading," Kanja said, "and let the legend do whatever work it's
going to do on its own. I've stopped trying to control which one people believe. I just make sure both
of them are still true, as much as it's in my power to keep them that way."

Efa Gol raised a cup, the closest the fleet came to ceremony. "To the ones who left because they
couldn't hold the doctrine anymore, and the ones who held it until the sea took the choice from them
instead."

Corren Halst raised his own. "To the ones who tried to hurt us and changed their minds before they had
to be made to."

"To the ones the ledger doesn't have a story dramatic enough for," Garren Hask said, closing the book,
"which is most of what actually keeps a fleet like this one alive."

Kanja didn't add a toast of his own. He watched the three flagships riding together at anchor instead —
*The Audit*, *The Receipt*, *The Ledger* — and the crew gathered across all three decks in the kind of
quiet that came only after a season that had asked something real of everyone aboard and mostly gotten
it. Whatever came next — another trap, another storm, another season thin enough to test what the
doctrine actually cost to keep — would come when it came. Tonight the ledger balanced, and that, more
than any legend, was the thing worth sitting with a while longer before the next watch began.
