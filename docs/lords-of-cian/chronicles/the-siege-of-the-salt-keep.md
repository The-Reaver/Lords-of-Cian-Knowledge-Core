# The Siege of the Salt Keep

*Locked canon, Batch 133, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-446`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle VII, first entry in
the third wave. Long Mask era, age ~140, roughly a decade into the Golden Terror period (ages
80-180). Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material — a detailed armor-and-weapon combat
showcase per Abad's craft instruction. Onyx of Oblivion remains sealed at L9 throughout the Long
Mask per VB-060-adjacent precedent (`MCD-413`); this showcase uses Mafesto, Obsidian Malice, and the
built gear system only. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The Salt Keep had stood as a fortified slaving depot for three generations, its walls thick enough
that no single raiding party had ever breached them, and its garrison confident enough in that
history that they'd stopped believing any threat, ghost story or otherwise, actually warranted more
than a token watch.

The Scourge came over the seaward wall in the deep hours before dawn, the Forge-Coat's V2 grounding
weave absorbing the garrison's first alarmed volley of crossbow bolts and conducting the impact
harmlessly into the coat's own structure rather than his body, while the Sovereign Eyes' amber-
calibrated lenses read the keep's interior layout through smoke and darkness that would have blinded
any ordinary intruder completely. Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System absorbed every subsequent
impact — falling masonry when a guard tower's alarm bell brought a hail of dislodged stone down, a
volley from the inner bailey — converting each into stored charge that built steadily as he moved
deeper into the keep.

The inner gate held against everything but a full discharge from Obsidian Malice, its two-year
dormant cycle spent in a single blow that didn't merely breach the gate but collapsed the section of
wall it was set into, opening the slave holds beneath the keep to open air for the first time in
years. The Ironhand Gauntlets tore the holding cages' iron latticework apart by hand where Obsidian
Malice's charge wasn't yet ready to spend again, freeing the first of what would become one hundred
and eighty captives before the garrison's commander even reached the lower level.

The commander met him there with a blade already drawn and no illusions left about the outcome.
"You're really him," the man said, the reputation finally made solid in front of him. "I didn't
believe the stories were literal."

"Most of them aren't. This part is." The Scourge didn't draw a weapon to answer the blade — the
Ironfall Boots simply closed the distance faster than the commander's swing could track, and the
fight ended before it properly began, the man disarmed and pinned without a single cut landing on
either side. "The Salt Keep's held for three generations because nobody who could stop it ever tried
hard enough. I intend for that history to end tonight."

By sunrise, one hundred and eighty captives stood in open air on a keep that had never once, in
three generations, expected to fall.
