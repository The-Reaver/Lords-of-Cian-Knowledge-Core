# The Vault That Held No Light

*Locked canon, Batch 236, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1045`). Crow King Alias Chronicle LIX, wave 20. A detailed
full-Trinity combat showcase fought in total darkness. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The garrison commander at Hollowmere Keep had done his reading. Somewhere in the captured fragments
of doctrine that had circulated since the cryptographer's breakthrough and the spy's counter-signals,
he had found a line that convinced him the Crow King's every advantage began with what the man could
see, and he had built his answer to that on a single, absolute premise: strip every torch, brazier,
and reflective surface from the Keep's lower vault, seal the shaft that let in what little daylight
reached it, and let whoever came for the prisoners chained down there come in blind.

He was half right about the vault. He was entirely wrong about what it would blind.

Kanja went down the shaft first, into a dark so complete it had no gradient to it — no shape of a wall,
no edge of a door, nothing for the eye to fail at gently. He did not slow down. The Hymn-Engine had
never been built on sight; every signal in it lived in breath, rhythm, and the particular way a voice
carried through stone, and Onyx of Oblivion's own senses ran the same way, tuned to weight and motion
and the changed pressure of a body entering a room rather than to light reflecting off it. Cadence Ruin
read the vault's whole layout in the first four seconds through nothing but the way sound folded back
off its walls — a long corridor, three side chambers, a guard rotation of six men whose footsteps gave
their positions away more precisely than a torch ever could have. Veil Piercer went to work next,
tracing not through stone this time but through absolute black, confirming what Cadence Ruin had
already mapped and catching the one guard standing motionless enough to have nearly gone unheard.

The garrison fought the only way blind men fight when they believe their enemy is blind too: loudly,
clustered, calling positions to each other across a dark that carried every word straight back to the
one man in the vault who didn't need the words to know where they stood. Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer
System took the first rush whole, redirecting three men's own momentum into the wall they'd been
calling their position from. Obsidian Malice discharged twice in the second chamber, not aimed by sight
but by the same pressure-reading Onyx had already mapped, dropping two more before either finished
shouting a warning to the third. The last guard standing, the one Veil Piercer had caught holding
still, made the mistake of staying silent a second time — and found that silence, in a vault built to
deny sight, was no protection at all against a craft built to need none.

It took less than two minutes. The commander, watching from the shaft's mouth with a lantern he hadn't
dared bring down, found Kanja waiting for him at the top with all six of his men disarmed and breathing
and the prisoners already being carried up past them both.

"You took the torches," Kanja said, "from the one part of this Keep where they were never going to
matter." He nodded back down the shaft. "Everything that finds you down there was built to work in the
dark before it ever had to. You gave us the one room in the building where that wasn't a disadvantage.
It was home ground."
