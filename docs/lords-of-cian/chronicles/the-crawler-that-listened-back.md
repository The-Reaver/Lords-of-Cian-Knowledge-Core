# The Crawler That Listened Back

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle XXXVIII. Rebellion
era, age 25. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material — a real-risk escalation entry, the
first countermeasure capable of injuring Kanja through the Talisman itself. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The engineering corps' newest variant didn't try to resist the read or deceive it. It answered — a
purpose-built feedback emitter wired into the Crawler's own fittings, designed to return a resonance
signal actively amplified rather than passively diagnosed, aimed back along the exact channel the
Aegis-Talisman's lens used to receive one.

Kanja felt it the instant contact was made: not the familiar, controlled information of a structure's
own tension, but a deliberately overdriven pulse traveling back up the same pathway, enough force
behind it to drop him to one knee, the Talisman's housing hot against his forearm in a way it had
never been before.

"They built something that hits back," he said, forcing the words out through what felt, disorienting
and unfamiliar, like the same kind of structural shock he'd spent years inflicting on others.

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System absorbed what came next — the second Crawler in the formation
closing to finish what the feedback emitter had started — while Onyx pulled him back from direct
contact with the lead vehicle, the diagnostic connection broken before a second pulse could follow the
first.

It took several minutes, longer than any engagement in recent memory, before the shock cleared enough
for him to think clearly again.

"I can't read it directly," he said finally, studying the Crawler from a safer distance. "Not without
paying for it the way I just did. But it can only answer what it's built to answer — a single
emitter, tuned to feed back exactly what the lens sends it. If I don't send the expected signal, it
has nothing to amplify."

The second approach used an irregular, deliberately unpredictable broadcast pattern rather than the
lens's usual steady tone — the feedback emitter, built to answer a consistent signal, returning
nothing coherent enough to weaponize against an input it hadn't been designed to expect. The Crawler
came apart the ordinary way, several minutes and one genuine injury later than it should have taken.

"They finally built something that could actually hurt me through this," Kanja said afterward, the
ache in his forearm still present. "That's worth remembering, longer than most of what they've tried
before it."
