Garren Hask arrived at the ledger office with his own books under his arm and a look on his face that
Ezio had learned, over the better part of a year working near him, meant a discrepancy had been found
and not yet explained.

"The provisions requisitioned for this district campaign don't match what the flagship's stores show
leaving port," Hask said, setting his ledger beside Ezio's on the table without preamble. "Difference
of about eleven days' rations, unaccounted for. I'm not accusing anyone of anything yet. I'm telling
you what the numbers say, because that's the only thing I've ever found worth telling anyone."

Ezio didn't take it personally, because he'd have done exactly the same thing in Hask's position, and
the two of them spent the better part of a day walking the discrepancy backward through both books —
requisition slips, delivery dates, the ledger office's own consumption records — the same cold,
patient method turned, for the first time, on the crew's own accounts instead of a district's.

The gap closed itself before either of them found anything to be alarmed about. A shipment recorded
as delivered on the ledger office's books had actually arrived two days later than logged, split
across two smaller loads instead of the single delivery the requisition form assumed, and a clerk
new to the supply chain had double-counted the split as two separate shipments before correcting his
own error in a margin note neither book had cross-referenced against the other.

"Honest mistake," Hask said, once the trail ran out at exactly that. "Costly one, if it had gone
uncaught — eleven days of rations look like theft if nobody ever checks the arithmetic behind them."

"That's the whole reason to check it," Kanja said, reading the reconciled figures once they were
done. "I've had this crew running an unarmed campaign for a year on the promise that nobody's numbers
get special treatment — not an administrator's, not a moneylender's, not mine. If I let our own
books slide because we trust each other, I've already broken the only thing that makes any of the
other ledgers mean something."

Hask closed both books, satisfied, and left the reconciled figures with Ezio to file alongside every
other district's account — not flagged as an error corrected, just as a number now agreed upon, the
same treatment given to every entry in every ledger the campaign had produced.
