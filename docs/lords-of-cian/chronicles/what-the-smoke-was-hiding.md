# What the Smoke Was Hiding

*Locked canon, Batch 233, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1035`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle LVIII, wave
20. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The first sign was the smell, not the smoke — pitch-oil, wrong for a tunnel that ran nothing but
groundwater and stone dust. By the time the smoke itself reached the western gallery, it was already
too thick to see a raised hand through, and the fire behind it was climbing timber supports that had
never been built to burn.

"They didn't collapse it. They lit it." Kanja had a palm flat against the gallery wall before the
first cough reached him, reading heat through stone the way he read load. "Somebody carried enough
oil down here to do this on purpose, and they timed it for the shift change, when the most people are
in the one corridor that isn't cleared yet."

Twenty-six workers were behind that fire, not ahead of it — the arson had been set to cut off retreat,
not to kill outright, driving them deeper into a network with one known exit already burning. Mafesto's
kinetic transfer, tuned for redirecting force rather than generating it, found an unlikely use pulling
displaced air backward through a side shaft Kanja had shored two years earlier, thinning the smoke
along the escape line by inches at a time instead of clearing it outright — not enough to see by, but
enough to breathe by, which was the only version of the problem that mattered in the next four minutes.

Obsidian Malice took longer to matter and mattered more when it did. Kanja held the discharge until he
had the shaft's true geometry in his head — where a controlled strike would open a vent to the surface
without opening a new path for the fire to follow the fresh air down — and then split the wall in one
motion, a chimney where a moment before there had been none. The fire found the new draft and climbed
toward the surface instead of toward the workers, exactly as read. Onyx of Oblivion's Cadence Ruin took
the two men waiting at the exit to make sure nobody who wasn't supposed to survive the smoke did — both
down before either registered a shape had come through the haze at all, Veil Piercer finding a third
posted deeper who never got to raise the alarm.

Twenty-six people came out through a vent that hadn't existed twenty minutes earlier, coughing, several
badly burned, all of them alive. Danne Sok, who'd led the counter-sweep above ground and found the
empty oil casks the arsonists had left behind, was the one who said what the whole crew was already
thinking once the count came back whole.

"That wasn't a collapse dressed up as an accident. That was somebody who'd watched us handle floods and
cave-ins and decided fire was the one thing our reading couldn't answer."

"It answered." Kanja was still tracing the new vent's edge with his hand, checking it wouldn't
destabilize anything above it before calling the work finished. "Just not the way they were betting on.
Fire moves like anything else that's looking for the easiest way out. You give it one, and it stops
looking for people instead."
