# The Ledger Line He Made Them Correct

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. Captain Alias Chronicle L, wave 17. Rebellion-adjacent era. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters — reuses already-locked Garren Hask.*

---

Garren Hask had kept the crew's ledgers for long enough that Kanja rarely checked them line by line
anymore — a matter of trust earned the same way everything else on the ship was earned, slowly and
without being asked for. So when he did open the quarterly accounting, it was mostly out of habit,
and it was habit alone that caught the discrepancy: his own listed share of the prize-take from the
last three engagements sat noticeably higher than anyone else's, padded by what the ledger's own
margin notes called, in Hask's careful hand, "risk premium, command differential."

He found Hask at the counting table that evening. "What's this."

"What's what."

"You've been paying me more than the others. For the same work."

Hask didn't look up from the coin stacks. "You take more risk than the others. You're first through
every breach, first into every collapse, first to eat a blade meant for somebody else. That's not
sentiment, that's arithmetic. A captain's share has always run heavier than a hand's. It's standard on
every honest ship that ever put to sea."

"I'm not asking you to justify it. I'm asking you to stop."

"Why."

"Because the day this crew decided to call me that instead of something the Directorate handed me,
it wasn't because I take more risk than anyone else on this ship. Ostra's walked into more live
charges than I have. Halst's held a line alone more times than either of us could count. Breck went
four months without his own voice and came back to save a fleet with what was left of it. If the
name means anything, it means I don't get counted differently than they do. Pay me what you pay a
deckhand who pulled a full watch. If you want to argue the totals, argue them with the whole crew
present, not just quietly in a margin note where nobody has to see it."

Hask set his pen down slowly. "You understand most captains would take the premium and never think
twice."

"Most captains didn't get the name from people who'd have called them something else if they thought
it was earned that way."

It took Hask three days to redo the quarter's accounting from scratch, and he did it publicly, at the
counting table, in front of whoever wandered past — not because Kanja demanded the audience, but
because Hask decided on his own that a correction made quietly was only half a correction. When he
finished, the new totals put Kanja's share exactly level with every other hand who'd stood the same
watches, no premium, no differential, and Hask amended the ledger's own standing policy to read, in
the same careful hand: *equal risk, equal share, regardless of rank or reputation, until this crew
says otherwise.*

"There," he said. "Now it's not just your preference. It's the rule."

"Good," Kanja said. "Rules outlast preferences."
