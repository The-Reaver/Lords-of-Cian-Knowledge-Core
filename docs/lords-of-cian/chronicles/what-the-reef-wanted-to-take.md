# What the Reef Wanted to Take

*Locked canon, Batch 199, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-772`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle XVII, wave 6 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The lights were exactly wrong in a way that took Dol Maren most of a minute to name, standing at the
rail beside Kanja as *The Audit* closed on what should have been open water off a stretch of coast
he'd charted himself, twice, in better weather than this.

"Channel markers," he said finally. "Four of them, spaced like the real ones at Kothrane Narrows. But
we're forty miles from Kothrane Narrows."

"Somebody built false ones. Here."

"Somebody built false ones *here* — meaning somebody knew we'd recognize the real spacing and trust
it without rechecking the water underneath." He was already moving toward the wheel. "Which means
whatever's under those lights isn't open water."

The reef, when the lead's line finally found it, sat exactly where a captain trusting the false
markers would have driven a hull at full speed — close enough to the surface to gut a keel, far enough
below to stay invisible until the moment it wasn't. Three Trust gunships waited a half-mile off in the
dark, engines cold, sails struck, counting on the wreck to do their work before they ever had to fire
a shot.

"They studied us," Kanja said, watching the false lights with something closer to respect than anger.
"Somebody spent real time learning which markers we'd trust on sight."

Dol Maren brought them about with four points of clearance to spare, close enough that the reef's
shallow water scraped audibly along the hull's underside without ever catching it — the flexible-frame
trim he'd spent two years refining doing exactly what he'd built it to do, flexing where a rigid keel
would have caught and torn.

"They're going to realize the trap failed in about thirty seconds," he said. "What do we do with
that?"

"Give them a second trap. One that actually works." Kanja was already signaling the boarding crews.
"They committed three ships to sit dark and silent waiting on a wreck. That means they're not
expecting a fight. Let's not disappoint the effort they put into pretending we'd never notice."

*The Audit* came about a second time, engines dark, running the same silent approach the gunships had
planned to use on a drowning crew — and took the lead vessel's rigging apart in the same confusion its
own captain had built to trap someone else, boarded and disabled before the other two ships had
finished understanding the reef hadn't done what it was supposed to.

"Reef didn't take us," Dol Maren said afterward, checking the hull for damage and finding none worth
logging. "Took them instead. Feels like it evens out."
