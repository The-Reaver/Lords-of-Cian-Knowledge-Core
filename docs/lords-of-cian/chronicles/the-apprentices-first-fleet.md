# The Apprentice's First Fleet

*Locked canon, Batch 274, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1419`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XCII, wave 31. The student formally cedes full forecasting authority to the fourth-generation apprentice for a major fleet engagement -- not a supervised test, a real handoff -- and a full Trinity combat showcase is committed entirely on the apprentice's own unconfirmed call for the first time.*

---

The student could have made the call herself. She was there, on the flagship's deck, close enough to the chart that no one would have questioned it if she'd simply taken it back at the last moment the way she'd taken back harder calls before. Instead she stepped away from the table entirely, hands folded behind her, and made the apprentice stand at it alone.

"This one's yours," she said. "Not mine to check behind you. Yours."

The engagement was real enough to matter: a Trust flotilla moving to relieve a besieged coastal garrison, timed against a squall line the apprentice alone had read three days out, using the overlap-window method she and the student had committed to the creed's book together after the strait crossing weeks before. No second reading. No scout confirming it. The fleet's captains had been told plainly, by the student herself, that the call was the apprentice's and only the apprentice's, and had sailed on it anyway -- a trust extended not to the school's reputation in the abstract, but to one specific young forecaster they'd mostly watched grow up on these decks.

Kanja stood the deployment with the crew, Mafesto and Obsidian Malice settled and quiet against him, Onyx of Oblivion resting easy at his hip, waiting on a window that would either open exactly when a nineteen-year-old said it would or leave a relief fleet and a besieged garrison both exposed to open water in the worst possible hour.

It opened within a quarter-hour of her call.

The squall broke behind the flotilla rather than ahead of it, holding the Directorate's own blockade squadron pinned against weather it hadn't been warned about, while the relief fleet came through clean water into the garrison's harbor mouth. Kanja went in with the boarding parties on the lead blockade vessel, Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System redirecting a boarding pike's full force back through the man who'd thrown it, Obsidian Malice discharging in a tight arc that cleared the quarterdeck without touching the garrison-side rigging six feet behind it, Onyx's Whisper of Shadows carrying him below decks ahead of any alarm reaching the powder stores he needed sealed rather than lit. It was fast work, coordinated the way three generations of Trinity use had taught him to coordinate it, and none of it would have mattered at all if the window it depended on hadn't opened exactly where a girl still two years from her majority had said it would.

The garrison held. The relief fleet anchored inside the harbor before the squall's true weight arrived. The blockade squadron, scattered and half-flooded, struck its colors rather than fight blind in weather it had never been given the chance to prepare for.

Afterward, on the flagship's deck, the apprentice looked less triumphant than simply exhausted, the particular exhaustion of having held something alone that she'd always previously held with someone standing just behind her.

"You didn't check it," she said to the student. Not quite a question.

"I didn't need to," the student said. "I trained you the way Sephtis's successor trained me -- to be checked until the checking stopped teaching either of us anything. It stopped teaching me anything about six months ago. I just hadn't told you yet."

Kanja, cleaning Obsidian Malice's housing at the rail, said nothing for a while, watching the two of them -- one who remembered clearly what it had cost her to make her own first unconfirmed call, one who was only now learning what it cost. "Sephtis used to say the whole doctrine only really mattered once it could outlive him," he said finally. "I don't think he meant it would take one handoff. I think he meant it would take exactly this many, for as long as the coast keeps needing storms read."

"How many is that?" the apprentice asked.

"However many it takes," he said, "before the next one's ready to be told the same thing you just were."
