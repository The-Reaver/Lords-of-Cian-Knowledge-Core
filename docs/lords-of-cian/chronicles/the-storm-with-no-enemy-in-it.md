# The Storm With No Enemy in It

*Locked canon, Batch 193, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-617`). Captain Alias Chronicle XLII, wave 14 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The storm that struck the coastal settlement wasn't sent by any enemy, called by no tactician, tied
to no campaign — just weather, violent and indifferent, tearing through a fishing village the crew
happened to be anchored near when it broke well ahead of any warning anyone could have given.

There was no fight to be had. Kanja spent the night doing the same physical labor as every other
person who could lift a beam or brace a collapsing wall — hauling debris, shoring up a roof that
was one gust from caving in on a family still sheltering beneath it, wading into flooded lanes to
carry people to higher ground the same way he'd have carried them from a battlefield, except the
enemy here was only water and wind.

Dol Maren worked beside him most of the night, applying the same structural instincts that kept
ships afloat to buildings that had never been built to withstand what was hitting them. Pell Ostra
rigged makeshift pulleys from salvaged ship's rope to move debris too heavy for hands alone. None of
it required Mafesto, or Obsidian Malice, or Onyx, or any part of the Trinity at all — just strength,
coordination, and the same stubborn refusal to stop that the crew brought to any engagement that
actually mattered to them.

By dawn, the worst of it had passed, the settlement battered but standing, and not a single life lost
that the crew's presence could have prevented.

"You didn't have to stay for this," the settlement's headman said, exhausted, watching Kanja help
right an overturned cart with nothing more than his own hands. "There's no glory in a storm. No
story worth telling afterward."

"There doesn't need to be glory in it for it to matter. A roof that doesn't collapse on a family
matters the same whether the thing threatening it wears armor or just blows through at night. I've
never needed an enemy in the story to know when people need help."
