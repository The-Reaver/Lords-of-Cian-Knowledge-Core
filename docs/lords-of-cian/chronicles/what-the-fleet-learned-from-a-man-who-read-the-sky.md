# What the Fleet Learned From a Man Who Read the Sky

*Locked canon, Batch 192, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-590`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XLV, wave 15 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

By the time an old sailor, decades removed from active service, sat retelling the legend to a younger
crew that had never known a navy without storm-forecasting doctrine built into its standard practice,
Sephtis's name had passed into something closer to institutional memory than personal recollection —
a founder's name, attached to a method so thoroughly absorbed into how fleets planned engagements that
few serving sailors could say precisely where the doctrine ended and ordinary naval practice began.

"There was a time," the old sailor said, "when a fleet went into a storm blind, and came out however
the weather decided. Nobody planned around it. Nobody thought you could. Then a man with nothing but
years of patient watching and a folding chart table showed a whole navy that the sky wasn't
random — it just hadn't been read carefully enough yet."

"Is it true he trained his own successor before he died," one of the younger sailors asked, "and she
trained hers, and it just kept going?"

"That part's true. That's the part that outlasted the alias itself, if you ask me. The storm-timed
raids, the flagships boarded instead of sunk — that's the part people remember first. But the
doctrine didn't end when he did, or when she eventually stepped back the same way he had. It became
something a fleet just does now. Reads the sky, trusts the reading, plans around it honestly instead
of hoping the weather stays out of the way. That's not a small thing to leave behind. Most legends
fade into stories. His turned into a habit an entire navy still keeps, whether or not anyone telling
it tonight remembers his name while they're doing it."
