# What the Soulbound Edge Bound

*Locked canon, Batch 196, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-696`). Bane Alias Chronicle XXXI, wave 11 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The refugee column had been moving for three days before Bane's escort caught up with it, two
hundred people freed from a labor conscription camp and walking the last stretch toward safe
ground, and nothing about the column read as anything but exactly what it appeared to be — until
Onyx of Oblivion, sheathed at his hip, went cold against his leg the way it never had on open,
uncomplicated ground.

Soulbound Edge did not announce itself the way Cadence Ruin unmade an opponent's footing, loud and
immediate. It worked slower, quieter — a tether the blade could feel between a person and whatever
that person was truly bound to, oath or loyalty or a debt not yet called in, and it had fixed,
somewhere in that column of two hundred exhausted, grateful strangers, on one man walking near the
rear who carried nothing that looked like a weapon and nothing that looked like a threat.

Bane didn't confront him in front of the column. He simply walked the rear line for the better part
of an hour, unhurried, letting the blade's cold certainty narrow itself down from "somewhere in this
column" to a single face, before he finally stepped beside the man and matched his pace.

"You're not bound to anyone in this column," Bane said quietly, low enough that no one nearby would
hear it as anything but conversation. "You're bound to someone who isn't here. I'd like to know who,
before we reach ground where two hundred people are meant to finally feel safe."

The man's composure held for exactly as long as it took him to understand that composure wasn't
going to be enough. He was an informant, planted three weeks earlier among the camp's conscripts
specifically to be freed alongside the others and carried, unsuspected, straight to whatever safe
ground the rebellion meant to use — his real bond a standing debt to a Directorate handler who'd bought
his family's protection in exchange for exactly this delivery.

"I didn't want this," he said, once the pretense collapsed, no fight left in him at all. "They have
my sister. I didn't have a choice they left me able to refuse."

"I believe you." Bane's voice didn't harden the way it might have for a willing enemy. "That doesn't
change what the bond was going to cost two hundred people who trusted you were exactly who you
looked like. It does change what happens to you now." He didn't hand the man over to be punished as
a spy. He separated him quietly from the column, arranged, through channels the man never fully
understood, for his sister's own extraction from the leverage holding her — and let him go free of
the debt entirely, the true bond finally broken, long before the column ever reached its safe
ground.
