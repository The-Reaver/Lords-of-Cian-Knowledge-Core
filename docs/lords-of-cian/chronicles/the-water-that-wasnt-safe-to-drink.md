# The Water That Wasn't Safe to Drink

Three dredgers fell ill within an hour of each other at the Undertide flats, and the site's own
water steward swore the cistern had been checked that morning, same as every morning, and found
clean.

Kanja didn't call for a healer first. He called for the cistern's intake log, the delivery manifest
for the week's water, and every hand who'd been near the cistern in the last two days.

"You're treating this like a tally discrepancy," the steward said, watching him work through the
records instead of the sick men.

"It is a tally discrepancy. Somewhere between clean water going in and three men falling sick, a
number went wrong, and I trust numbers to tell me where faster than I trust guessing." Kanja found
it on the third pass — a delivery two nights prior, logged as routine, from a supplier who'd never
serviced this cistern before and hadn't serviced it since. "This delivery doesn't match the pattern.
Every other week, the same two suppliers, alternating. This one's neither."

He traced the supplier through the district's own registry — a name that led, after two more hours
of questioning and cross-referencing manifests against known Compliance contractors, to a
Directorate-linked concern with no legitimate reason to have delivered anything to an independent
dredge site's cistern at all.

"They didn't attack the site," Kanja said, once the picture was fully assembled. "They poisoned three
men slowly enough to look natural, hoping it would spread before anyone thought to check the water
instead of blaming exhaustion or bad luck. If I hadn't pulled the intake log, we'd have kept drinking
from that cistern for days, assuming it was just sickness making its way down the line."

"How did you know to check the water at all, instead of just treating the men?"

"Because sickness that hits three men in the same hour, all healthy the day before, isn't usually
sickness. It's usually a number that doesn't add up somewhere upstream of the men themselves. I've
learned to look for the number before I look for the symptom." He was already drafting the warning
to send to the other four districts, the shared standard from Marrow Landing making the cross-check
fast in a way it wouldn't have been a month earlier. "Whoever ordered this wanted it to look like bad
luck. It only worked because nobody was checking the water the way they check the tallies. That
changes today, on every site we can reach."

The three men recovered. The cistern was flushed and resealed under watch from two of the newly
cross-checking districts, and the poisoned-delivery attempt, never repeated in quite the same form
again, became the reason every site on the canal started keeping a water log with the same rigor
they kept a wage tally.
