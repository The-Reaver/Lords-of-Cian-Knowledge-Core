# The Alloy She Wouldn't Share

*Locked canon, Batch 202, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-878`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XXXIII, wave 11 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

She had learned the reheat's patience during a single afternoon aboard The Anvil, one of a hundred and
twenty thousand residents the tour eventually reached, and had spent the years since taking that one
lesson somewhere no lesson had ever been intended to lead her — years of her own quiet experiments,
alloying salvaged Dead Drakma fragments with local ore ratios no established guild technique had ever
documented, chasing a temper she couldn't fully explain even to herself until she finally achieved it.

The result held an edge longer than anything her settlement's own smiths had ever produced, and she
told almost no one how she'd made it.

"You could teach that method to every smith on this coast," a traveling trader told her, examining a
blade she'd been persuaded, reluctantly, to sell. "Could change how half this region forges."

"Maybe. I'm not ready to give it away yet." She wasn't apologetic about it, turning back to her own
work. "He taught me the reheat for free, on a beach, in an afternoon, and told me to give it away the
same way, freely, to whoever wanted it. This isn't that lesson anymore. This is mine. I built it past
anything he ever showed me. I get to decide when, and whether, it stops being only mine."

Word of the alloy reached the campaign's own senior smith eventually, secondhand, years after The
Anvil had moved on from that coast entirely.

"Sounds like your student outgrew the lesson," someone remarked, half in warning, expecting the news
to trouble her.

It didn't. "Good. That was always the actual point of teaching it — not that everyone stays exactly
where the lesson left them, but that some of them go somewhere the lesson never could have reached on
its own." She meant it without reservation. "She doesn't owe the method back to us just because it
started with something we gave her. If she's built something new out of it, that's hers to keep or
share, on her own timeline. I'd rather have raised one genuine inventor than a hundred people who never
moved past copying what I showed them."
