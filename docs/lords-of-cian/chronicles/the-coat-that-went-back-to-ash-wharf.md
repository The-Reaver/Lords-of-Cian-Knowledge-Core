# The Coat That Went Back to Ash-Wharf

*Locked canon, Batch 281, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1477`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle CII, wave 34, closing
the wave. Age 258, V4 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Two hundred and thirty-six years had done what fire and Trust bombardment had failed to: the harbor at
Ash-Wharf was unrecognizable. The breakwater where two cruisers had run aground and burned had long
since been rebuilt into a working seawall; the scorched ground where four thousand people had once
waited to learn whether they'd survive the night was a market square now, loud with ordinary commerce
that had no memory of what the ground beneath it had cost. Nothing in the harbor as it stood would have
told a stranger that a persona had been born here, standing in a ruined coat, on a morning that felt,
even now, like the last clear thing he remembered before this name became the rest of his life.

He hadn't come back once in all that time. There had never been a reason to — no captives here, no
threat, nothing the reputation was built to answer. He came this time for no reason he could name
cleanly even to himself, docking under no disguise worth the name and walking the market square in the
Forge-Coat's civilian undercoat rather than its full silhouette, unremarked by anyone buying fish or
rope a few feet from where he'd once detonated eight hundred tons of Dead Drakma to save their
grandparents.

He found the old harbor edge eventually, past the market's noise, where the water still met the stone
the same way it had the morning Efa Gol found him in his ruined coat and he'd told her, for the first
time, that he didn't yet know what this one would end up costing before it was finished. He knew now,
or knew as much of it as two hundred and thirty-six years could add up to: freed captives numbering
past two hundred thousand, a ledger Garren Hask still kept, a persona that had outlived every other
alias he'd ever carried and would outlive this visit too, by however many years remained before the
coat came off for good.

He didn't tell anyone he'd gone. He stood at the harbor edge the way he had the first morning, a while
longer than the visit strictly needed, and then he left the same way he'd arrived — unrecognized, by a
harbor that had rebuilt itself into something that no longer needed to remember him standing there at
all.
