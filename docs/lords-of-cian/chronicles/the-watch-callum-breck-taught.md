# The Watch Callum Breck Taught

*Locked canon, Batch 264, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1378`). Captain Alias Chronicle LXXVIII, wave 26, closing the wave. Callum Breck trains a successor in the silent-signal shore-watch craft his own four months of post-Black-Trench silence first built.*

---

Callum Breck had never planned to teach anyone the signal-language he'd built for himself out of necessity, four months of silence after the Black Trench turned into a system of hand-code and rope-tension nobody else had ever needed the way he had. It had simply become the crew's own shore-watch standard over the years without ever being written down anywhere — passed hand to hand, learned by watching him work rather than by any formal instruction, because the man who'd built it had never once explained the why behind any of it out loud.

The deckhand who finally asked him to teach it properly wasn't Mira, and wasn't the grown deckhand who'd once learned to read Hask's ledgers — it was one of the newer hands, quiet in a way that made the other crew nervous around her the same way Breck himself once made people nervous, before they learned that quiet wasn't the same thing as absent.

"You don't have to have gone through what I went through to learn this," Breck told her, the first time he'd said the words out loud to anyone. "I built it because I couldn't talk for a while and needed a way to still be useful. You don't need a reason like mine. You just need to want a way to say something without needing your voice to carry it."

He taught her over the better part of a year, patient in a register nobody who'd known him only from the Ghost Harbor legend would have recognized — not the silent, functional efficiency of his crew reputation, but something warmer, a man teaching a craft he'd built out of grief now handing it forward as a gift instead. When she ran her first full shore-watch alone, flawless, reading Kanja's own signals back correctly across a hundred yards of dark pier without a word passing between them, Breck watched from the shadows and said nothing, which everyone aboard by then understood as his highest form of praise.

"You built something out of the worst months of your life," Kanja told him afterward, "and now it's going to outlive both of us."

"That's the only kind of legacy worth having," Breck said. "Not the thing that happened to you. The thing you made out of it, that someone else gets to use without ever having to live through what you did to build it."
