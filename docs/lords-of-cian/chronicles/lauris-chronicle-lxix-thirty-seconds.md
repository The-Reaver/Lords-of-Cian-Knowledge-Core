# Lauris Chronicle LXIX: Thirty Seconds

*Locked canon, Batch 300, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1686`). Sixty-ninth entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, the twelfth entry of Strand K. Set at age ~3,972, roughly 28 years before her
scheduled departure and following the Iron-Speaker agreement in Chronicle LXVIII (`MCD-1685`).
Dramatizes directly, for the first time, Selene's death in a Vask Threnarr defensive action already
locked at `MCD-174` -- Lauris fighting at her side, killing the attacker within thirty seconds of
Selene's death, too late -- and the writing of the archive line already locked at `MCD-1558` ("Thirty
seconds. The Hold continues. Selene does not.") in its full original context for the first time. No
new named characters; reuses Selene Aldreth-Vorr, already locked. No contradictions.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Vask Threnarr, age ~3,972: "Thirty seconds. The Hold continues. Selene does not."*

I have known, since I first began assembling this account of her deep past, that I would eventually
have to write this entry. I have put it off longer than the strict chronology required, transcribing
Chronicles further along the sequence before returning to complete this one, and I will admit that
plainly rather than pretend otherwise. There are entries in this archive that ask only to be recorded.
This one asked, of me, something closer to permission.

The attack itself was, by every surviving account, unremarkable in its shape — a raiding party larger
than any that had reached Threnarr's own walls in centuries, drawn, the Iron-Speakers later
concluded, by rumor of a departure ceremony already being quietly prepared, though the party itself
seems to have known nothing of Lauris specifically and to have chosen Threnarr for its size and its
archives rather than for who defended it. I note this because I think it matters: Selene did not die
in some engagement built around her daughter's fame. She died defending the same Vask she had
relocated to nearly four thousand years earlier, in the same kind of action Threnarr's thinned
trade-points had been absorbing, with diminishing margin, across the whole of the Long Operational
Period.

*

Lauris was present from the engagement's opening minute — she had returned to Threnarr for what her
own entry calls an unremarkable visit, the kind she had made regularly across the preceding decades
as her own departure drew nearer, wishing, I believe, though she does not say so directly, to spend
what time remained to her near the Vask that had raised her. She and Selene fought together that
day, which her archive notes almost as an aside, as though it required no separate comment: two
Karesians, mother and daughter by every meaning of the word that had ever mattered between them,
holding a line neither had held together in this configuration in centuries.

I do not know, and Lauris's own entry does not resolve, whether the engagement's outcome would have
differed had she been standing three paces closer, or had she read the collapsing formation on
Selene's flank one half-second sooner than she did. I have chosen, in the years I have spent
narrating this family, not to speculate further than her own record permits, because I think the
speculation is a kind of cruelty her archive itself refuses to indulge in. What she records is only
this: a Karesian veteran twice Selene's raw density, breaking through a formation gap that should
not have existed, closing the distance faster than the defensive line's own coordination could
compensate for. Selene fell. Lauris killed the attacker within thirty seconds of the blow landing.

Thirty seconds. I have sat with that number longer than any other in this entire chronicle. It is,
by every standard this world measures combat against, an extraordinarily short interval — a
testament, if one wished to read it that way, to how completely and how swiftly Lauris answered what
had been done. She has never read it that way herself. In her own words, recorded some hours later,
after the engagement had ended and the Hold had begun its own accounting of what the day had cost
it: *Thirty seconds is not fast. Thirty seconds is the length of time I was too late by.*

*

I want to be careful here, narrating what followed, because I do not believe Lauris herself would
want it rendered as spectacle, and I have tried, across this whole account, to give her the same
restraint she gives herself. She did not, by any surviving record, weep, or rage, or withdraw from
the Hold's own accounting of its dead. She did what she has always done, in every loss this
chronicle has asked me to transcribe, going back to Velith: she recorded it. Carefully. Completely.
Without softening any part of it for her own comfort or anyone else's.

The entry she wrote that night is the one already preserved elsewhere in this family's record, three
lines, which I have chosen to place here, finally, in the context that gives them their full weight:
*Thirty seconds. The Hold continues. Selene does not.*

I have read those nine words perhaps a hundred times since I first found them, long before I
understood what stood behind them. I understand it now. The Hold continues is not, I have come to
believe, a statement of indifference. It is the same principle she gave to Doreth at Aldreth, eight
hundred and fifty years before this — that the people inside a thing matter independently of whether
it survives them — turned, for the first time in this whole long chronicle, against the one person
whose survival she had never once, in over two thousand years, allowed herself to imagine
questioning.

*

She stayed at Threnarr eleven days after the funeral rites — longer, by some margin, than any other
visit recorded in this period — and returned to her preparations for departure only when the Hold's
own Iron-Speaker asked her, gently, whether she intended to let this delay the promise three hundred
and eighty years of deliberation had already settled. Her answer, recorded in the same flat hand as
everything else in this entry, is the one I have chosen to close this account with, because I believe
it says more about who she is than any single act of combat this whole chronicle has yet asked me to
narrate: *No. She spent four thousand years arguing that I was also a person, not only a record. I
think the truest way to honor that is to continue being one. I will leave when I said I would leave.
I will simply leave with one fewer person to say goodbye to than I expected.*
