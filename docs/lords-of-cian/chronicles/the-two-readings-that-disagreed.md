# The Two Readings That Disagreed

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle L, wave 17. Rebellion-to-Long-Mask transition era, shortly after the founding of the school. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. Names Sephtis and his successor; no new named characters.*

---

It had never happened before, not once in years of working the same sky from the same deck: Sephtis
and his successor read the same gathering weather and came back with two different calls.

He read a hard storm, full strength, arriving within the day. She read the same system stalling
offshore, weakening before it reached the fleet, safe to hold position through.

Neither of them budged.

"I've read a thousand systems like this," Sephtis said. "This one doesn't stall. It's carrying too
much water to stall."

"I've read the same thousand," she said, "from underneath you, watching everything you watched. This
one's different from the others in one way you're not weighing enough — the wind's shifted twice in
the last hour, not once. That's a system running out of push, not building it."

Kanja, waiting on the command decision the two of them were supposed to hand him together, watched
them argue it out in full view of the crew and made no move to settle it himself.

"You're not going to choose for us," Sephtis said, half a question.

"No," Kanja said. "Because if I choose, I'm choosing which of you I trust more, and that's not a call
I have any right making. You built this method between the two of you. You'll settle this between the
two of you, or we hold position until the sky settles it for you."

Neither reading could be tested by argument. So they didn't argue further. They held the fleet at
anchor, sent a single fast scout vessel forward to observe the system's leading edge directly, and
waited two hours for an answer neither of their instruments could give them from where they stood.

The scout confirmed the successor's reading. The system was already losing strength.

Sephtis took the correction the way he'd always taken them — without complaint, and with a question
immediately behind it.

"Where did I lose it?"

"You didn't lose anything," she said. "You read it the way you've always read it, and it's always
been right before. I read it differently because I've spent ten years reading it standing where you
stood, not where you stand now. That's not a better eye. It's just a newer one."

"Then the method survives disagreement," Sephtis said, "as long as neither of us mistakes conviction
for certainty."
