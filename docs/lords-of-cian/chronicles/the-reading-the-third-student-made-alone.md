# The Reading the Third Student Made Alone

*Locked canon, Batch 239, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1054`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LIX, wave 20. The third-generation student (established `MCD-983`) makes her first fully independent storm call, with the successor deliberately absent rather than unavailable. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The successor was three days south, mediating the disagreement-resolution protocol she'd built years
before at a rival school that had finally asked to adopt it. That left the third student -- the one
Sephtis's successor had spent a decade training, the one who'd never yet made a fleet-scale call without
someone senior standing close enough to catch it -- alone at the chart table when the pressure reading
came in wrong.

She could have waited. The successor would be back inside two days, well within the window before the
system that was building offshore made landfall. Waiting was the safe answer, the answer nobody could
fault her for giving.

She didn't wait.

"Twenty-six hours," she told the harbor-master, "not the forty the almanac's seasonal pattern would
predict. The pressure's dropping faster than the season allows for. I don't have a teacher's confirmation
on this. I have my own reading, and I'm telling you what it says."

Kanja was in the harbor when she said it -- there for an unrelated matter, a supply run, nothing to do
with the school -- and watched the harbor-master weigh a young woman's unconfirmed word against forty
years of seasonal almanac and choose to move his fleet early anyway, because the name behind the reading,
even unproven on its own, had never once been wrong when it came bundled with that particular tone of
certainty.

The storm came in at twenty-four hours, two ahead of even her own number, hard enough that a fleet still
in open water would have lost ships. The one that had moved early lost nothing.

She found Kanja afterward, not the successor, because the successor was still three days south and
wouldn't hear about any of it until the harbor-master's letter reached her.

"I didn't have anyone to check my work," she said.

"You had thirty years of two people's work behind you," Kanja said. "She learned it from a man who
learned it from nobody at all, the first time he ever called one right without knowing if he could. You
weren't reading a sky nobody had ever read blind before. You were reading one three generations of
people already knew how to trust their own eyes on."

"That's not the same as being right on my own."

"No," Kanja agreed. "It's better. You didn't inherit a gift. You inherited a way of being brave about
being wrong, and you used it before anyone told you it was time to."
