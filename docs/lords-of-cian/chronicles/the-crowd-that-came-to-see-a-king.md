# The Crowd That Came to See a King

*Locked canon, Batch 216, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-946`). The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle LIII, wave 18. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Nobody at Coldrace Basin had a dispute for him to settle. That was the problem.

Word had spread far enough past the five districts by then that people had started arriving simply to
see him — dockhands from towns with no tally crew of their own, a handful of pilgrims who'd walked
three days on the strength of a rumor, curious onlookers who'd heard "king" attached to a boy not much
older than their own sons and wanted to judge for themselves whether it was true. By midday there were
close to two hundred people standing along the Coldrace embankment with nothing to do but wait for him
to appear, and no site work anywhere close to getting done with that many bodies crowding the paths.

Danne Sok, who'd been managing crowds of angry owners and frightened workers for months, found this
harder. An angry crowd had a shape you could read. A crowd that wanted nothing but to have seen
someone didn't behave the way threats behaved, and there was no tally sheet that fixed it.

Kanja tried, at first, simply working through it — walking the embankment on his usual rounds and
letting people watch if they wanted to. It didn't help. Every step drew more of the crowd along
behind him, and by the second hour the actual dredge work, the reason anyone was supposed to be on
that embankment at all, had stopped entirely.

He stopped, finally, on a low stone landing where most of the crowd could see him, and said the
plainest thing he had.

"I don't know what most of you came to find. If it's a king, I'd rather save you the walk back
disappointed — I dig ditches for a living, same as everyone working this embankment, and the only
thing I've ever actually done that anyone should bother repeating is check a number against another
number and refuse to let someone lie about it. That's the whole of it. If any of you have a real
grievance — a debt, a wage, a lie someone's telling you about what you're owed — I'll hear it, today,
right here. If you came just to look, I understand it, but I'd rather you let these people get their
work done while you do the looking."

More than half the crowd left within the hour, some visibly disappointed, some quietly relieved to
have been told plainly there was nothing more dramatic waiting for them. Of those who stayed, six
turned out to have genuine grievances nobody local had been willing to hear — a wage theft from three
towns over, a debt dispute that had nowhere else to go. He worked through all six before the light
gave out.

"You could have just refused to come out at all," Sok said afterward.

"I could have. But some of the ones who left with nothing would've stayed and turned into exactly the
kind of trouble that does need managing, if I'd made them feel like they weren't allowed to ask. Better
to tell people plainly what I am and let them decide for themselves whether it was worth the walk."

Garren Hask logged Coldrace Basin's day not as a site correction but as its own separate category, one
he hadn't needed before: *Crowd, no grievance. Six real grievances found inside it. Cost: a full day's
work, at every site within a day's walk of here.*
