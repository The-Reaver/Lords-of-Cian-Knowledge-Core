# The Grandchildren of the Freed

*Locked canon, Batch 186, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-545`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle XV, closing the fifth
wave. Long Mask era, age ~200, generations into the persona's 284-year span. Not a territory
Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters.*

---

A settlement founded by captives freed decades earlier from the Salt Keep had grown into a genuine
community, its founding generation mostly gone now, their grandchildren raised entirely on the
account passed down rather than any memory of their own — the Scourge's own visit, unannounced and
unrecognized, arriving in the guise of an ordinary trader rather than the legend the settlement's own
stories had built around the name.

"They still tell it exactly the way it happened," the quartermaster observed quietly, watching a
grandmother recount the Salt Keep's liberation to a circle of children too young to have known anyone
who'd actually been there. "The details haven't drifted the way most legends do."

"Some of that's luck. Some of it's Hask's ledger — I made sure an accurate account reached this
settlement early, before the story had the chance to grow past what actually happened." The Scourge
listened from the circle's edge, unrecognized, exactly as intended. "I'd rather they remember this
accurately than remember something more dramatic. The truth was already worth telling. It didn't need
embellishing."

A child, finishing the story, asked the grandmother the question every generation eventually asked. "Do
you think he's still out there?"

"I don't know, child. Nobody's seen him in years, and the stories say he doesn't stay anywhere long
enough to be found on purpose." The grandmother smiled, unaware of who stood listening at the circle's
edge. "I like to think he's still out there somewhere, doing exactly what he did for us, for someone
else who needs it just as badly."

The Scourge said nothing, offered no correction, and moved on from the settlement within the hour —
the accurate account intact, the legend's warmth undiminished, and the man underneath both entirely
unrecognized by the very community his one act, generations before, had made possible.

"You could have told them," the quartermaster said, once they'd put the settlement behind them.
"Just once. They'd have wanted to know."

"They already know everything that actually matters. Who I am specifically was never the point. What
happened, and what it built since, was." The Scourge looked back once at the settlement's own lights.
"I'd rather leave them with the story intact than complicate it with a stranger's face they'd have to
reconcile against decades of their own grandmother's telling. Some things are better left exactly as
they've been passed down."
