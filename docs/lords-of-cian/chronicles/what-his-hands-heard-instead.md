# What His Hands Heard Instead

*Locked canon, Batch 283, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1493`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle C, wave 34, first entry. The doctrine's first fully tactile curriculum, built for a stonemason deaf since birth. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The stonemason came to Kanja through a letter someone else had to read aloud to him, because he'd never once been able to hear the words for himself, and asked — through the same intermediary — to be taught to listen to stone the way the Iron Bastard listened to iron. It took Kanja longer than he liked to admit to understand that the request wasn't a misunderstanding of what deafness meant. The man had spent thirty years running his palms and his tools over cut stone, feeling for a flaw in the grain the way every mason eventually learned to, and had heard about the doctrine only as "a man who feels a wall the way I feel one," which was, Kanja realized, a description closer to the truth than most of the doctrine's own students ever managed.

"I've always taught this partly by sound," Kanja admitted, honestly, once the mason stood in front of him with his tools set aside and his palms already resting against a test span of stacked masonry. "The vibration under a strained beam, yes — but also the sound of it, the particular note a thing makes right before it tells you the truth. I don't know how much of what I teach translates if the sound half of it was never available to you in the first place."

The mason answered by pressing both palms flat to the stone and holding perfectly still for longer than Kanja had ever seen a first-time student hold anything, and when he finally spoke — his voice careful, evenly pitched, the voice of a man who'd learned to speak by feeling his own throat rather than hearing it — it wasn't a question.

"Then teach me the half that isn't sound."

It took the better part of two months, and it was, by Kanja's own honest account afterward, some of the doctrine's clearest teaching he'd ever done — every cue that had always ridden alongside an audible tone had to be isolated, named, and handed over as pure touch: the specific quality of tremor that meant *sound*, the different quality that meant *failing*, the third that meant *nothing, ignore it, every structure hums a little*. The mason, never once distracted by an actual sound to second-guess his palms against, read some signatures cleaner on his first honest attempt than most hearing students managed on their tenth.

His first solo test came without ceremony: a well cover in his own village, judged safe by every eye that had ever looked at it, until his palms found what no eye could — a hairline give in the cap-stone's seating that would have dropped a child through within the season. He fixed it himself, the plain mason's way, no discharge needed at all.

"You didn't need me for that one," Kanja told him afterward.

"No. But I needed you for every lesson that got me to the day I didn't."
