# The Worst Cast He Ever Tied

*Locked canon, Batch 306, 2026-09-23 (`MCD-1785`). Ozmund Verehimu Character Chronicle series, Chronicle LVI, Draconis strand wave 3.*

---

Not every story the General told me needed a reason to be told, and this one, he admitted, he mostly told because it made him laugh to remember it, which was reason enough. It was the lake again — the same lower lake, the same half-forgotten chest of lines, years on from the first idle afternoon the two of them had ever spent beside it.

It came about the same way the first had, more or less — a stretch of days with nothing urgent standing in either man's roster, Draconis appearing without much ceremony to ask whether the General had any particular use for the hours, and the two of them riding out before midday with no purpose grander than the lake itself and whatever the kitchens had packed along. The General told me Draconis had aged into the habit properly by then, the way a man does with a thing he's let himself keep — no longer half-forgotten in a chest, but maintained, mended, brought out with something close to ceremony each time the chance arose.

What made this one worth telling, the General said, wasn't the fishing, which was exactly as unproductive as it had always been, but that for once Draconis let himself be openly amused by him rather than merely patient with him. The General had improved his cast over the years, by his own account, though only from genuinely dreadful to reliably mediocre, and Draconis, watching him foul his third line in a row on the same stubborn stand of reeds, finally set down his own rod entirely and informed him, with a gravity the General said was somehow more insulting than if he'd simply laughed outright, that he had trained recruits out of worse habits in a single afternoon and was beginning to suspect the General's difficulty wasn't technique at all but a kind of principled refusal to improve.

The General told me he'd protested this at some length, and that Draconis had listened to the whole of the protest with the exact expression he wore when a soldier under his command was explaining, unconvincingly, why a roster had gone wrong, and had then reached over without further comment and untangled the line himself for what the General insisted, telling it to me, was at minimum the ninth time that particular afternoon. He said Draconis's hands, doing it, had none of the crispness he brought to a blade or a rein — slower, more deliberate, a craftsman's patience rather than a soldier's — and that watching him work a snarled line free with the same total unhurried attention he gave to reading a poisoned seal across a crowded hall was, in its own small way, as strange a thing to witness as anything else the man had ever done in front of him.

He returned the favor as best he could, the General told me, by pointing out, once Draconis's own line finally went in clean, that a man who'd spent thirty years teaching recruits to hold a formation under fire seemed to have taught himself nothing at all about holding still for a fish, and that the reeds Draconis kept blaming for every lost catch had, as far as the General could tell, never once moved of their own accord in either man's direction. Draconis took the jab the way the General said he took very little else offered to him in jest — without deflecting it, without the small courteous laugh a man gives a lord's attempt at humor, but with something closer to real delight, as though being teased badly by a young man he'd half-raised was a pleasure he hadn't expected to still be owed at his age and had no intention of correcting.

They caught nothing worth naming again, exactly as before, and ate their way through whatever the kitchens had sent and dozed through the worst of the afternoon heat the way the General said men do who trust the ground they're lying on completely. What stayed with him, telling it to me, wasn't any single exchange from the day so much as the plain fact of it — that somewhere in the accumulated years between one lake afternoon and the next, a boundary neither of them had ever named out loud had quietly worn thin enough to let something almost like equal footing through it, for exactly as long as neither of them looked at it too closely.
