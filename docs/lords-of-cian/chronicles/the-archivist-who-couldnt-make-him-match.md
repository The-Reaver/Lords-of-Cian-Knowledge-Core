# The Archivist Who Couldn't Make Him Match

*Locked canon, Batch 196, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-683`). Bane Alias Chronicle XVIII, wave 6 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The Directorate records clerk assigned to compile a physical description of Bane for the standing
threat file had, by the end of her third month on the task, filled two full folders with statements
that refused to agree with each other on a single detail worth building a likeness from.

A garrison survivor from the Kessic salt-flat rumor's actual origin — the fever outbreak, not the
invented massacre — swore Bane stood taller than any man she'd ever seen and never once raised his
voice above a murmur. A Suppression Brigade officer who'd faced him at the ravine wash swore he was
unremarkable in height, ordinary-looking enough to have passed for a laborer, and that he'd spoken
at length, calmly, for most of a minute. A freed conscript said he never spoke at all. A merchant who
claimed to have sold him rope at a market three towns from any confirmed engagement described a scar
across one cheek that appeared in no other account anywhere in either folder.

"They can't all be describing the same man," her supervisor said, reviewing the accumulated
contradictions with the particular irritation of someone who needed a clean file and kept getting a
mess instead. "Pick the most-repeated details and standardize the rest as unreliable."

"That's the problem. Nothing repeats." She laid the statements out side by side, height against
height, voice against voice, scar against unmarked cheek. "Either forty different witnesses are all
independently unreliable in forty different specific ways, or he's not holding still long enough
for any one description to become the description. I've cross-checked timing on eleven of these.
Some of them can't both be true — he can't have been murmuring in a fog at the ravine and speaking
at length calmly at Kessic in the same season if the timing's right, and the timing looks right on
both."

Her supervisor had no answer for that beyond ordering the file closed as "description unreliable,
multiple contradictory accounts" and moving to threat pattern instead, which was its own separate
mess of a different kind. The clerk kept her own copy of the contradictions long after the official
file stopped trying to resolve them, not because she expected to ever finish the portrait, but
because she had started to suspect, quietly, that the portrait was never the point — that whatever
this was, it had learned early that a story nobody could pin down traveled further, and lasted
longer, and frightened more effectively than any accurate likeness ever would have.
