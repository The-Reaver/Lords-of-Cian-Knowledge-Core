# Lauris Chronicle CII: The Silence They Shared

*Locked canon, Batch 300, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1719`). One-hundred-second entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, closing the sixth Strand W wave. A stakes-free entry pairing Lauris with Danne
Sok, one of the three earliest crew members freed before the Black Trench (`MCD-234`) and already
established across the Alias Chronicle track for his silent watchfulness -- a night watch shared in
near-total silence, extending `MCD-215`'s "adequate-but-undemonstrative ties to the rest of the
crew" clause into its own scene. Puts `CC-134`'s combat-joy trait on the page as ease taken in
shared, wordless company rather than in any exertion at all. No new named characters.*

-----

*Archive fragment, present-day, undated by her own habit: "Danne Sok and I kept watch together and
said perhaps a dozen words across six hours. I have spent six thousand years learning to be
comfortable in silence. I do not think I have often shared it with someone who chose it as
deliberately as he does."*

Danne Sok has been, since the years before the Black Trench, one of this crew's quietest presences
in the most literal sense available to that word -- not withholding, so far as I have ever observed,
but simply economical, a man who has told me himself, on the rare occasions he tells me anything at
length, that he learned young to make his silences count for more than most men's speeches, and has
never since found sufficient reason to unlearn it. He keeps watch the way other men keep an old
promise: without complaint, without needing to be asked twice, and without, in my long experience of
him, feeling any particular need to fill the hours of it with talk.

Lauris took the watch beside him one otherwise unremarkable night, at his own quiet invitation rather
than her own request, which I record as notable in itself, since Danne Sok extends few invitations of
any kind and I have come to understand he does not extend them carelessly.

What followed was, by his own later account and by her own archive entry, very nearly six hours of
silence, broken by perhaps a dozen words total between them across the whole of it -- a shift in
position, a quiet observation about the wind changing, once, deep into the night, a low remark from
him that the stars over this particular stretch of coast reminded him of a night early in the war he
did not elaborate on and she did not ask him to. I was present for perhaps the first hour of it,
sent along on the pretense of relieving a lamp that did not, in fact, need relieving, and I confess I
found the silence itself, once I had settled into it, rather more companionable than I had expected
watching two people share six hours of near-total wordlessness to be.

I asked her afterward, since an account built almost entirely of silence is a genuine challenge for
me to render honestly, what those six hours had actually consisted of for her, and she told me, with
the same plainness she has given me for every entry in this strand, that the question rather missed
the point of the evening. "He does not keep silence because he has nothing to say," she told me. "He
keeps it because he has learned, I think, that most of what needs saying does not actually require
saying, if the people sharing the silence already understand each other well enough. I have spent
six thousand years cultivating a similar discipline, for rather different reasons than his own -- but
I do not think I have often sat with someone who chose the same silence as deliberately, and for
company rather than for solitude."

Danne Sok, for his own part, told me only a little of what the evening meant to him, which I record
as entirely in keeping with everything I know of him. He said that he has spent this entire war
watching, first for the young man who freed him and now for the crew that man built, and that he has
found, across the years, that most people mistake his quiet for absence -- for a man simply not
present in the conversation rather than a man choosing, carefully, which parts of himself he extends
and to whom. "She did not mistake it," he said. "She sat with me the whole night and never once tried
to make the silence into something it was not."

I have come, across the many evenings I have now recorded for this strand, to think that this account
is among the truest I have set down of what `CC-134`'s epithet actually means when it is not spent on
a boarding action or an unresolved research question or forty-one crates of neglected shelving. She
carries joy, the source material tells me, as her defining register against the burdens the rest of
this crew carry -- and I have come to understand, watching her share six near-wordless hours with a
man who has spent this entire war learning to say more with his silence than most men manage with
their speeches, that the joy she carries does not require exertion, or company that talks, or even
much of anything at all beyond a person willing to sit beside her and let the night be exactly what
it is. She wrote, in her own archive, only a single further line for that evening, and I include it
because it closes this account better than anything further I might add to it: *Some company does not
need to be filled. I am glad, after six thousand years, that I have finally found some that is not
afraid to leave it empty.*
