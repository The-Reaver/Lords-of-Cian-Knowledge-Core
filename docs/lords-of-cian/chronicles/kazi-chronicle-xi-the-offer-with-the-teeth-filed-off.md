# Kazi Chronicle XI: The Offer With the Teeth Filed Off

*Locked canon, Batch 289, 2026-09-12 (`MCD-1530`). Sixth entry in Kazi's own Chronicles, Femi
(`PH2-066`) as protagonist, not a Kanja Chronicle. Kanja appears only as a guest, present but
granted no command, intervention, or resolution credit. Narration is close-third on Femi throughout.
Set some months after the fund audit (Kazi Chronicle X, `MCD-1529`), dramatizing an attempt to
neutralize Femi's seat from inside the institution itself rather than oppose it from outside.*

---

The audit had cost the fund's administrator his position and cost two other men their signatures'
good standing, and it had cost the plant nothing directly — no fine, no ruling, nothing that touched
the company's own ledgers — which was, Femi understood clearly enough by then, exactly why the offer
that followed came from the union's own regional office and not from plant management at all. It was
easier, and cheaper, to spend the movement's own institution than to fight it.

The offer had a title attached to it that sounded, on its surface, like exactly the kind of thing
Femi had spent a year proving himself capable of: regional benefits coordinator, a paid staff
position, full-time, off the floor entirely, overseeing fund matters across every local in the
district rather than just his own. It came with a salary no trustee's seat paid and a office two
buildings from where he currently punched a clock.

"It's a promotion," the regional officer said, across a desk clean enough that Femi doubted much work
crossed it. "You'd be doing exactly what you did with our own fund, at ten times the scale, with
real resources behind you instead of a bylaw nobody enforces."

"I'd be doing it from an office two buildings from the floor, answering to men who didn't vote me
into anything, on a fund I no longer stand to lose my own pension into if it fails." Femi turned the
offer over slowly, the way he'd have turned over any file with a gap in its dates. "The seat I hold
now, I hold because eleven men more than the other fellow decided I'd read their books straight. This
one, I'd hold because your office decided I'd read them quietly instead."

"That's an uncharitable read of a fair offer."

"Maybe. I've found the charitable read and the accurate one aren't always the same thing, and I've
learned to check which one I'm being handed before I decide how grateful to be for it." He didn't
raise his voice; he had learned that much from watching Kunle work a hearing room. "If the district's
funds genuinely need the kind of look I gave our own, I'll help you build a way to do that from
inside a seat men actually voted for, same as mine. I won't take a desk that removes the one thing
that made the first seat worth anything: that the men who put me there can still vote me back out of
it if I stop being useful to them specifically."

The offer was not repeated. Within the month, two of Femi's own committee assignments — minor ones,
easily justified on paper, impossible to challenge as retaliation without looking as though he were
protecting his own turf — were quietly reassigned to men the regional office trusted more. He said
nothing about it publicly. He kept doing the work of the assignments he still held, thoroughly enough
that within a season three of them had grown back to him on their own, requested by name by the local
trustees who'd lost patience with whoever had been handed them instead.

"They tried to buy the part of you that could be moved," Tunji said, watching him work through a
newly returned case file that evening, "and when that didn't take, they tried to starve the part that
couldn't."

"Both were the same offer, really. Just made with different currency." Femi didn't look up. "The
seat only means anything as long as it stays exactly as small and exactly as accountable as it was
the day eleven votes put me in it. The moment it gets bigger by someone else's hand instead of the
plant's own vote, it isn't mine to hold anymore, however much it might still say my name on the
door."

A stranger delivered the returned committee files that evening, unremarked, gone before Femi
finished reading the first one — one more unglamorous errand in a season made up of nothing else.
