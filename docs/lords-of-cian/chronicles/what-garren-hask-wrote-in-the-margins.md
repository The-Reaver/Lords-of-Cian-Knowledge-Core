# What Garren Hask Wrote in the Margins

*Locked canon, Batch 195, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-680`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle XLV, wave 15 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Garren Hask kept the crew's true accounting the way he kept every accounting — honestly, in full,
whether or not the numbers flattered anyone, including the man the district was already starting to
call a titan.

The tally, finished weeks after the siege ended, ran longer than any single battle's own count could
have suggested: timber consumed and timber salvaged, Obsidian Malice housing repaired twice through
genuine smithing rather than replaced, injuries sustained by crew members across six weeks of
tunnel work, the one death the digging itself couldn't prevent, the wall that failed under a rushed
assessment, the days lost rebuilding what a mistake had cost.

"You've kept every failure in here alongside every victory," Kanja said, reading the finished
ledger with the same unhurried attention Hask had always known him to bring to any honest
accounting. "Most records of a siege leave the failures out."

"Most records aren't meant to be useful. This one is." Hask didn't look up from the next page he
was already drafting. "I've watched you tell a guild hall your failures were worth more to them
than a perfect record. I believe you meant it. This ledger's the proof, in case anyone ever needs
it — not a story about a titan who never lost anything, but an honest count of what it actually cost
to hold this district, failures included, because the failures are as much a part of what you built
here as anything that went right."

Kanja turned another page — the false ledger caught in materials fraud, the boy who chose pipe
fittings over a blade, the honorary mark from a guild that trusted honest hands over perfect
records — each entry recorded in Hask's own careful accounting, plain and unembellished.

"What happens to this?"

"It stays with the crew's own records. Not for anyone's legend. For the next crew that has to hold
a district like this one, so they know what it actually costs, not what the stories will eventually
claim it cost once enough time's passed to round the edges off." Hask finally looked up, the closest
thing to warmth he generally allowed himself. "I've kept your ledgers a long time now. I don't
think I've ever kept one that told the truth about a man as plainly as this one does."

Kanja closed the tally without argument, trusting, as he generally did, that Garren Hask's own
accounting would outlast whatever version of the siege eventually made its way into legend instead.
