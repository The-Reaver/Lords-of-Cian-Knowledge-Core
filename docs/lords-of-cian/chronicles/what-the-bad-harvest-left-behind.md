# What the Bad Harvest Left Behind

*Locked canon, Batch 255, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1135`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle LXXVIII, wave 26,
closing the wave. Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. A failed harvest, and a logistics problem no
tally figure can solve.*

---

The grain shortfall wasn't theft. Garren Hask confirmed that within a day of the first reports
reaching Warehouse Twelve — three districts' worth of upriver farms had lost most of a season's crop
to a blight nobody had caused and nobody could have prevented, and the food that should have carried
the canal district through winter simply wasn't going to arrive in the quantities anyone had planned
for. There was no owner hoarding it. There was no ledger to correct. There was only less food than
there were mouths, spread unevenly across districts that had never needed to coordinate with each
other before.

"This isn't a dispute," Hask said. "I don't have a figure for it."

Kanja didn't try to make one. What the shortfall needed wasn't verification or restitution; it needed
someone willing to spend weeks doing the unglamorous work of counting exactly how much grain existed,
where, and moving it to where the need was worst before pride or fear made any district try to hoard
what little it had. He used the same cross-district trust the tally method had spent two years
building — the five foremen who'd once been brokered into a shared standard, the districts that had
learned to believe Hask's figures over their own suspicion of each other — and turned it toward
something the method had never been built for: an honest, voluntary accounting of who had enough and
who didn't, followed by the harder work of convincing districts with a little surplus to part with it
before winter made the decision for them.

It wasn't clean. Two districts refused outright, citing their own thin margins, and Kanja didn't force
either of them, understanding a coerced sacrifice would poison every future request the same way a
coerced restitution would have. What moved, moved because the districts that gave it had come to
trust, over two years of honest figures, that the same discipline asking them for grain now would
account for every measure of it honestly and would remember, when their own turn to need something
came.

Efa Gol organized the physical movement — carts, routes, timing, the same logistics instinct she'd
once turned toward decoy operations now turned toward getting sacks of grain across district lines
before the roads iced over. Nobody starved that winter. Nobody was made whole, either; every district
that gave ended the season thinner than it started, and every district that received still went
without more than it should have.

"You can't verify your way out of a bad harvest," Kanja said, once the worst of it had passed.

"No," Hask agreed. "But it turns out two years of people trusting your figures is worth something
even when there's no figure to give them."
