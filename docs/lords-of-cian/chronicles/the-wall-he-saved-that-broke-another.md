# The Wall He Saved That Broke Another

*Locked canon, Batch 248, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1081`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle LXII, wave 21. A genuine new limit: a correct, verified read still causes unintended harm to a structure Kanja didn't know was load-sharing with the one he targeted. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The retaining wall above the terraced quarter at Sennow Crossing was exactly what it looked like: a
Directorate strongpoint, iron-braced, holding back the slope so a garrison could shelter under it. Kanja
read it twice, the way he'd read a hundred walls like it — confirmed, confirmed again, the signature
clean both times. He brought it down in nine seconds, the braces shearing exactly where he'd told them
to, the garrison scattering out from under a wall that dropped precisely where he'd aimed it and nowhere
else.

Forty yards downslope, a footbridge over the drainage cut collapsed eleven seconds later. Three
washerwomen were on it. Two got clear. One didn't.

He hadn't touched the bridge. He hadn't read the bridge. It took the second student two days of walking
the ground with him, tracing drainage channels and old foundation lines under a records office's
half-ruined survey maps, to find what the doubled verification had never been built to catch: the
retaining wall and the footbridge shared a single buried footing, laid a century before either structure
above it existed, and the wall's tension had been quietly propping the footing's far side the entire
time. Remove the wall's load and the footing shifted, and the bridge that had stood on it without complaint
for decades lost the support it never knew it was borrowing.

"The read was right," the second student said, laying it out plainly rather than softening it. "The wall
was exactly what you said it was. You just didn't know there was a second thing listening to the first
one."

Kanja didn't argue the point and didn't excuse it either. "Doubled verification tells me I heard the
target correctly," he said. "It never told me to ask what else was standing on the same ground." He
walked out to the drainage cut himself and stood where the bridge had been for a long time before he
spoke again. "That's the gap now. Not whether the read is true. Whether the thing I'm reading is alone."

He didn't invent a new instrument for it — no third verification pass, no additional tool. What changed
was the question asked before any discharge at all: what else, above or below ground, shares a footing,
a brace, a load path with the target, and has anyone actually looked. It cost more time before every
future reading, time he'd have once spent moving faster, and he accepted the cost without complaint. The
washerwoman's name was recorded, quietly, in the same reckoning that had once held Tam Sullen's — not
folded into a report, not turned into a lesson recited at students, just kept.
