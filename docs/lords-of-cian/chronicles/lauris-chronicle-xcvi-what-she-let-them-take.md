# Lauris Chronicle XCVI: What She Let Them Take

*Locked canon, Batch 300, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1713`). Ninety-sixth entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, closing this Strand L run. Opens a new debt concerning Lauris's own body rather
than any facility or population: her original Directorate intake, decades before Operation 28's
engineered subject whose combat capability closely mirrored her own (`MCD-186`, "the Copy"),
included routine biological sampling she did not think to question at the time. Deliberately left
unconfirmed whether the sample was ever used. No new named characters. Narrated by Fermand
Aurelias.*

-----

*Archive fragment, present-day: "I have spent this season finding what the Directorate lost. I
did not expect to find something the Directorate was simply given, by me, without my having once
asked what would be done with it. I am recording the oversight honestly. It has taken me
considerably longer to notice than I would like to admit."*

I do not think I have watched Lauris review her own archive with quite the particular stillness she
brought to this entry, and I want to record, before the account itself, that she did not raise this
matter to me in the way she has raised every other debt across this season -- not as something
newly discovered in the field, but as something she found sitting, unremarked, in her own earliest
recorded pages, where it had apparently sat the entire time without her once returning to it with
the eyes she has now.

The occasion for the review was unrelated. She had been cross-referencing her Apprentice
Contracts-era intake records for a separate, minor administrative purpose -- confirming a contract
date against her own memory, nothing more -- when she encountered, in the standard processing
documentation attached to her very first Directorate engagement, a routine notation she had signed
without particular attention at the time: biological sampling, conducted as part of the ordinary
intake procedure the institution applied to every new external asset, contractor and constructed
subject alike, regardless of provenance.

"I was newly arrived on Cian," she told me, when she finally spoke of it. "I did not yet understand
what kind of institution I had contracted with, or what the word 'sampling' might come to mean in
an institution of that particular kind. I signed what was placed in front of me because signing it
was the fastest route to the work I had actually come to Cian to do. I do not believe I gave the
matter a second thought for perhaps three thousand years."

*

What returned it to her attention, she told me plainly, was not the discovery itself but its
placement against everything else this season has surfaced. She has long held a working theory
about Operation 28's engineered subject -- the one whose combat capability and bearing so closely
mirrored her own development around age 2,400 that she concluded, at the time, the tradition
beneath the Directorate must have been observing her operations closely enough to reverse-engineer
her methodology from behavior alone, having no independent copy of the Karth-Sera curriculum from
Kares Prime itself. She has never fully resolved, in writing, how she feels about that subject or
that conclusion. What she confronted this evening, for the first time, is that behavioral
observation may never have been the tradition's only available avenue.

"I assumed they built what fought me at Operation 28 by watching what I did," she said. "I did not
consider, until tonight, that they may also have simply had what I am, on file, since the very
first year of my career -- long before Operation 28, long before I understood enough to be careful
about what an institution like that one could do with a sample it never told me it intended to
keep."

*

I asked, because I believed it the honest question, whether she had any reason to think the sample
had actually been used for anything at all, rather than filed and forgotten in the ordinary,
indifferent way institutions file most of what passes through their hands. She did not claim
certainty either way. "I have no evidence it was used," she said. "I also have no evidence it was
destroyed, returned, or even retained under a classification I could locate if I searched for it
tonight -- and I have spent this entire season learning exactly how little an institution's own
paperwork can be trusted to answer that question honestly. I am not asserting the Copy was built
from more than observation. I am recording, for the first time, that I no longer have grounds to
assert it was built from observation alone."

She has not decided whether to pursue the sample's fate the way she has pursued Sample K-403's
missing siblings. She told me only that she intends to raise the possibility with Sephtis, whose
own archive may hold a cross-reference she has not yet thought to check, before deciding whether
this is a debt worth actively chasing or one she must simply learn to carry, unresolved, alongside
everything else this season has widened rather than closed.

Her closing note, the last entry of this account: *I gave them something before I understood what
I was giving. I do not know what it became. I am no longer willing to assume it became nothing,
simply because assuming that would be easier to carry than the alternative.*
