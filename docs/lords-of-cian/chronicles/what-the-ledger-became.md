# What the Ledger Became

*Locked canon, Batch 256, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1174`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XC, wave 30, closing the wave. A legacy closer bookending "The First Book He Filled" (`MCD-932`) at a larger scale: Ezio and Kanja look over the now-many-volume archive of ledgers and reckon with its size.*

---

There were eleven volumes by then, not counting the first one Ezio had carefully recopied years earlier when its spine finally gave out. They sat in a crate that took two men to lift, each book filled edge to edge in Ezio's own hand or, in a growing number of pages across the later volumes, in the hands of the district auditors his method had trained and left behind to keep their own books honestly — the second and third generations of a practice that had started as one man's careful arithmetic in a single recording tent.

Kanja hadn't set out to build an archive. He said as much, turning through the eleventh volume's early pages, cases he barely remembered clearly anymore against ones that still sat with him the way MCD-928's exhaustion or MCD-930's forced restraint still did years later. What he'd set out to do was find out, one district at a time, whether numbers patient enough and honest enough could move something that fear and force alone hadn't been able to move on their own. He hadn't thought, at nineteen, about what eleven volumes of it would eventually look like stacked in a crate.

"You could stop counting cases and just count districts," Ezio said, running a finger down a tally he'd started keeping himself, unprompted, somewhere around the fortieth entry. "Might be easier to hold in your head."

"I don't need it easier to hold. I need it true." Kanja's own reckoning with the crate wasn't pride, exactly, though there was something adjacent to it in how carefully he set the eleventh volume back among the others. It was closer to the unsettled feeling of watching something outgrow the hands that started it — a method built for one furnace district's strike, still running years and dozens of districts later, no longer entirely dependent on him or Ezio to function, carried now by auditors in districts neither of them had personally visited in over a year.

"What happens to all of it," Ezio asked, "once we're not the ones carrying it district to district anymore?"

Kanja didn't have a clean answer, and didn't pretend to one. "I don't know if it stays a crate somebody has to lift, or turns into something else entirely — copied, spread wider than either of us could carry by hand. I know it doesn't stop being true just because we're not the ones holding it. A number that was honest the day Ezio wrote it down stays honest whether it's read next week or a hundred years from now, by whoever's still asking the same question we were asking that first morning — not who's owed the most sympathy, just who's owed what, and how much."

He closed the crate himself, the way he'd once closed a single worn book he couldn't bring himself to discard. Whatever it eventually became — read again, carried forward, outlasting both of them entirely — the discipline that had filled it, one patiently cross-referenced figure at a time, was the part he trusted to still be true no matter how far past this crate the ledger ever traveled.
