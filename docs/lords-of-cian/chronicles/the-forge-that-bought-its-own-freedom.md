# The Forge That Bought Its Own Freedom

*Locked canon, Batch 259, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1247`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle LXXXII, wave 28,
first entry. Age 180, V3 gear. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

He arrived expecting a raid and found a finished conversion instead. The forge-town's ore trade had
fed three separate slaving routes for two generations, buying its iron cheap from operations that
paid in captive labor rather than coin — the kind of arrangement that made the town's own owner
complicit without ever making him a slaver himself, one profitable step removed from the chains his
metal helped forge.

He'd heard the reputation two years earlier, secondhand, the way most people heard it — a merchant
elsewhere who'd converted under direct pressure, a story retold at three removes until it barely
resembled the original. He hadn't waited for a visit to decide what it meant for him. Two years of
quiet, unannounced restructuring — new suppliers found, old contracts allowed to lapse rather than
renewed, workers who'd have been bought cheap instead hired at a wage that actually competed for
their labor — had already converted the forge over by the time Kanja's crew arrived on an entirely
unrelated supply run and discovered, almost by accident, that there was nothing left here to raid.

The owner met him without fear, mostly out of curiosity: he'd built his whole conversion around a
reputation he'd never verified in person, betting two years of restructured contracts on rumor alone
being close enough to true. It had been. He wanted, mostly, to know if he'd guessed right.

Kanja confirmed what the man had already built for himself needed no confirming, and stayed only
long enough to see the operation firsthand — genuine, thorough, no performance for a visitor's
benefit since no visitor had been expected. Garren Hask logged it as a first: not a conversion under
pressure, not even a conversion following a visit, but reputation alone, unconfirmed and acted on
anyway, doing the entire job before the crew had known there was a job to do.
