# What Broke and What He Fixed

*Locked canon, Batch 120, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-408`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle V. Rebellion
era, a field-repair showcase distinct from any prior Trinity combat sequence. Not a territory
Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters.*

---

Obsidian Malice's housing cracked along its forward edge during an ambush extraction, a hairline
fracture from a strike that had exceeded even the war club's considerable tolerances, and the crew's
field armorer delivered the diagnosis with the flat certainty of a man who had seen this exact kind
of damage end other fighters' careers.

"That's not a field repair. That needs the forge it was made at, or something close to it. You keep
using it cracked like that, next full discharge is as likely to shatter the housing as hit anything."

"Then I'm not doing a full discharge until it's fixed." Kanja was already examining the fracture
himself, turning the war club in the firelight, reading the crack's depth and direction the way he'd
once read a bent hinge for a fourteen-year-old apprentice. "But I'm not waiting on a forge three
weeks away either. Get me a portable kiln, the finest Dead Drakma filing stock we've got, and about
six hours nobody's going to interrupt."

The armorer watched, first skeptical and then genuinely absorbed, as Kanja worked the fracture by
hand — not the Trinity's own regenerative properties, which had never applied to this kind of
structural damage, but plain smithing technique, heating the housing's forward edge just past the
point of malleability, working fine Dead Drakma filings into the crack's full depth, and cooling it
in a controlled sequence that mimicked, as closely as improvised field conditions allowed, the
original forging process at the Mao Volcano itself.

"You actually know how to do this," the armorer said, several hours in, watching the fracture line
slowly disappear under careful, repeated work. "Not just watched it done once. Actually know it."

"I forged my share of ordinary steel before I ever touched anything that came out of Mao. The
principles aren't as different as people assume — you're still just convincing metal to become one
thing instead of two, and metal's always been more patient about that than people expect, if you're
patient with it in return." He checked the repair's surface, satisfied, the housing whole again
under close inspection. "A war club that only ever gets fixed by the one forge that made it is a war
club with a single point of failure built into its entire existence. I'd rather know how to keep it
alive myself, in case that forge is ever three weeks away when it matters most."

The repair held through the following eleven engagements without incident, and the field armorer,
years later, would cite it as the moment he stopped thinking of the Trinity as something mystical and
started thinking of it as, at bottom, extraordinarily well-made equipment — maintained, like anything
else worth keeping, by someone who understood exactly how it was built.
