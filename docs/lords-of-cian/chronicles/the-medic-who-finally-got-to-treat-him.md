# The Medic Who Finally Got to Treat Him

*Locked canon, Batch 196, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-701`). Bane Alias Chronicle XXXVI, wave 12 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The column's medic had treated every kind of injury the crew's ordinary fighters accumulated —
broken hands, burns, the slow grinding wear of a war fought mostly on foot — and had never once, in
a year attached to the column, been allowed to lay hands on Bane himself. He healed fast, faster
than anyone she treated, and he'd made a quiet habit of tending his own minor injuries before anyone
could offer, or simply outlasting them without treatment at all.

The wound at Corren's Ford was not minor. A glancing strike had found a seam in the plate the Trinity
hadn't fully covered, and he'd fought the rest of the engagement on it anyway, the way he fought
through most things, until the column was clear and there was no longer an engagement left to
justify ignoring it.

"You're going to let me look at this," she said, more statement than request, when he finally sat
still long enough for her to see it properly. "Not because I think you can't handle it. Because I've
spent a year watching you handle everyone else's wounds and never once let me do my actual job on
you, and I'd like to understand why before I let you talk me out of it again."

He let her work this time, quiet through most of it, and answered honestly when she finally asked
the question plainly.

"It's not that I don't trust you. You're better at this than I am at treating myself, and I know
that." He held still under her hands, the admission costing him something visible. "It's that the
column needs to believe I don't need this the way everyone else does. The moment they watch me
bleed and stay down long enough to be properly treated, some of what holds the line together on the
worst days goes with it. I know that's not entirely fair to you."

"It's not fair to you either." She finished binding the wound, plainer about it than the reputation
usually allowed anyone to be. "You don't have to be the reason the line holds every single time.
Some of us are allowed to actually do our jobs on you occasionally."

He didn't fully concede the point. He did let her finish, and came to her directly, unprompted, the
next time an injury was bad enough to need it — a small change, unremarked by most of the column,
that she counted, privately, as the closest thing to a real answer she was going to get.
