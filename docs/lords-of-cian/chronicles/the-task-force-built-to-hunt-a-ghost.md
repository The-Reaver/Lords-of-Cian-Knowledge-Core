# The Task Force Built to Hunt a Ghost

*Locked canon, Batch 132, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-443`). The Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias
Chronicle VII, first entry in the third wave. Rebellion era, age 21, Ghost Harbor. Not a territory
Chronicle. New standalone material — a detailed armor-and-weapon combat showcase per Abad's craft
instruction. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The Directorate had finally stopped treating the ghost fleet as folklore and assembled a task force
purpose-built to counter it — five ironclad hunters running dark, coordinated by signal-lantern
patterns designed specifically to deny the fleet's usual approach of appearing only long enough to be
feared before vanishing. They'd studied every prior encounter. They knew about the anchor-chain
weapon. They came prepared for a fight that never fully arrived the way they'd planned it.

Kanja brought the ghost fleet in low and fast under cover of a moonless swell, Mafesto's Kinetic
Transfer System reading the hunters' hull displacement through the water itself long before any
lookout could have sighted them. The lead hunter's own chain-net defense — rigged specifically to
foul the anchor-chain trick that had disabled a gunship's ironwork before — caught nothing but open
water, since Obsidian Malice struck the hunter's rudder assembly directly instead, a single
discharge concentrated into disabling steering rather than repeating a tactic they'd already
countered.

The second and third hunters turned to flank, exactly as their coordinated doctrine specified — and
found Onyx of Oblivion already among their boarding crews, Whisper of Shadows closing distance faster
than lantern signals could relay a warning, Cadence Ruin breaking their drilled coordination into
five captains each reacting alone to a threat none of them could locate in time to organize against.
The fourth hunter's captain, watching two ships disabled and a third's crew disarmed without a single
ghost-fleet vessel ever coming fully into view, made the call the task force's own doctrine hadn't
accounted for: he struck his own colors before engagement, refusing to commit his crew to a fight the
data in front of him said was already lost.

The fifth hunter fled. Kanja let it go.

"They built an entire doctrine around everything we'd already shown them," Dol Maren said afterward,
surveying the disabled hunters from the flagship's deck. "And it still wasn't enough."

"It wasn't supposed to be enough. It was supposed to prove something." Kanja watched the fleeing
fifth ship's lanterns shrink toward the horizon. "Whatever report reaches the Directorate from that
ship will say the task force failed despite doing everything right by their own doctrine. That's a
harder thing to recover from than a single lost battle. That's a reason to doubt the doctrine itself."
