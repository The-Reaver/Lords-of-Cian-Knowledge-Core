# The Market That Rebuilt Itself

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Scourge Alias Chronicle, wave 13. Long Mask era,

The Kessara slaving hub had taken three months of patient surveillance and one decisive night to
dismantle entirely — every warehouse burned, every ledger seized, every buyer's name passed to a Trust
magistrate willing to act on it — and by every measure the crew's own records used to judge a
victory, it had been total.

A season later, a merchant sailing the same strait brought word that troubled him more than any
account of a fight lost ever had: the trade hadn't ended. It had simply moved forty miles down the
coast, rebuilt under new names, serving the exact same demand that had made Kessara profitable in the
first place, as though the hub itself had never been more than a convenient address for something far
harder to actually destroy.

"You burned the building," Efa Gol's successor said, laying the report out plainly rather than
softening it. "You didn't burn the reason the building existed. That's a different fight, and I don't
think it's one the coat and the blade were ever built to win."

"Then what wins it?"

"I don't know that anything does, cleanly. Buyers keep buying as long as buying stays profitable
enough to be worth the risk. Every hub we burn just raises the price of the next one, and someone
always decides the price is still worth paying." The successor didn't offer comfort in the assessment,
only honesty. "You can keep making it expensive. You can't make it stop being wanted. That part isn't
a fight anyone wins with force, however good the force is."

He turned the assessment over a long while, standing at the rail of a ship bound for the new hub's
location, forty miles and one season removed from a victory that had, in the plainest accounting, not
actually solved anything.

"So we go again," he said finally, "and again after that, for as long as it keeps moving, because
making it expensive is still worth doing even if it isn't the same as ending it."

"That's the only answer I've got too. I just didn't want you sailing into this one thinking Kessara
taught you the fight was over. It taught you the opposite — that the fight doesn't have an over,
not from this direction."

The new hub fell in turn, weeks later, exactly as thoroughly as Kessara had, and within a season a
third one had already begun taking shape somewhere neither of them had yet located — a market that,
however many times its address burned, kept finding a way to rebuild itself from demand that no coat,
however well made, had ever been built to burn along with it.
