# Lauris Chronicle LXIV: The Point Where a Body Would Fail First

*Locked canon, Batch 300, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1681`). Sixty-fourth entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, the seventeenth entry of Strand K, drafted as part of a second 50-entry wave.
Dramatizes a training session at age ~950, within the age-600-1,100 window already locked at
`MCD-170` for the structural-breach strike trained under Tiramen -- shown here as a still-unnamed
precision technique rather than as "Spine of Dagon," consistent with `MCD-176`'s locked fact that
her four disciplines take on their weapon-names only later, on Cian. Extends her Hexa-Lamellar
Lattice physiology (`MCD-157`/`159`) and Tiramen's established role as "adaptive-methodology
specialist" (`MCD-167`). No new named characters. Full text below.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Vask Karth-Ven, age approximately nine hundred fifty: "Tiramen no longer asks me
to strike hard. She asks me to strike correctly, and tells me the hardness will follow on its own if
I am right about where. I am not yet reliably right about where."*

I have written of Veska's stance and of the method she built for rebuilding it faster than Lauris
could outgrow it. I turn now to Tiramen, whose own contribution to this long, undramatic stretch of
training I have found harder to set down plainly, because what she was building had no single visible
form the way a stance does -- no fixed position I can describe someone standing in. What she was
building was a way of looking, and I have needed, transcribing this entry, to work harder than usual
to make a way of looking read as something that happened, on a particular day, to a particular
person.

By Lauris's ninth century at Karth-Ven, her density had continued its long, slow climb past every
figure the Vask's own instrumentation had once treated as remarkable, though I will not attempt to
give a precise number here -- her own archive from this stretch of years is characteristically sparse
on exact figures, more concerned with what the density could do than with what it measured, and I
have chosen to respect that reticence rather than invent a precision she herself did not record. What
I can say plainly is that ordinary sparring had, by this period, become genuinely dangerous to any
partner who was not Lauris herself, and that Tiramen's own solution -- training her against
increasingly dense forged-Drakma constructs rather than living opponents, for the sessions where
precision rather than reaction mattered most -- is the setting for the exercise this entry records.

*

The construct itself, by Tiramen's own description in the training log, was built to fail in exactly
one location, hidden among a dozen points that looked, to any ordinary strike, identical to it. The
exercise was not to break the construct. It was to find, before striking at all, the single point at
which the construct's own internal structure would give way first -- a discipline Tiramen had begun
building around a simple, repeated observation: that Lauris, even as a child recording a training
bout at eight, had shown an instinct for reading where a structure's weight had stopped agreeing with
itself, long before anyone had trained her to strike anything at all.

I have the record of the session behind the fragment above, and it is, by the standard of everything
else I have transcribed from this stretch of her life, a record of failure more than of triumph --
seven attempts across the afternoon, four of them striking a point adjacent to the true failure point
rather than the point itself, close enough to crack the construct's surface without breaking it
cleanly. Tiramen's own note on the session does not read as disappointed, and I think this is worth
setting down plainly, because it tells me something about how she understood the work she was doing:
*Four near misses today, out of seven. A year ago it would have been seven near misses out of seven.
I am not training her to be right immediately. I am training her to notice, with increasing speed,
exactly how far from right she was, and to close that distance a little further each time we do
this. That is the whole of the method. It has no shortcut, and I have stopped looking for one.*

*

Lauris's own entry for the day, shorter, closes on an observation I have chosen to end this account
with, because I think it shows something of how the discipline was beginning, even this early, to
shape the way she thought about combat more broadly, decades and centuries before any weapon would
ever carry a name for it: *I struck the wrong point four times today. Each time, I understood
afterward exactly why it was wrong before Tiramen told me. I have decided this understanding is worth
more than a correct strike I did not understand. Tiramen agrees. She says this is the entire
argument she has been making to me, without saying it directly, since I arrived here at fourteen.*

I do not know, reading this now, whether Lauris herself understood, at nine hundred fifty, how
directly this quiet, repetitive work would one day be channeled through a weapon she had not yet
commissioned, on a world she had not yet heard the name of. I suspect she did not. I suspect, knowing
her as I now do, that she would not have found the knowledge especially useful even if she had.
