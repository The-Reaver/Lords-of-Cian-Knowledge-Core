# The Storm That Came Early

*Locked canon, Batch 126, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-425`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle IV, first entry
in the second wave. Rebellion era, a new Gale Straits engagement testing Sephtis's storm-prediction
reliability. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose. No new named characters beyond the already-locked Sephtis (referenced, not appearing
on-page).*

---

Sephtis's prediction had never failed across three prior engagements, close enough to the hour each
time that the fleet had come to plan around it with the same confidence they'd extend to any charted
tide — which made the fourth prediction's two-hour error, the storm system closing far sooner than
forecast, the first genuine crisis the Storm That Walks doctrine had faced from its own supporting
infrastructure rather than the enemy.

"We're not going to make the timing," the flagship's navigator reported, watching the weather system
darken faster than any prior engagement's countdown had allowed for. "Two hours short, maybe less.
We planned the whole approach around having that margin."

Kanja didn't hesitate long enough for the crew to see him hesitate at all. "Then we don't need the
margin. We need to close the engagement faster than the plan assumed, and take the storm at its
actual arrival instead of its predicted one."

The arrowhead formation compressed rather than held its planned approach speed, driving through the
Trust envelopment's center at a pace that sacrificed some of the formation's usual precision for raw
urgency — the friendly-fire crossfire still triggered, still effective, but rougher, costlier, two of
the fleet's own smaller vessels taking damage the original timing would have avoided entirely. The
storm arrived exactly as its revised, hastily recalculated window suggested, close enough behind the
fleet's own escape that the heaviest Trust pursuers were still caught in open water when it broke.

"We made it," the navigator said, once the fleet was clear, watching the storm swallow the pursuit
behind them. "But that was closer than any engagement I've been part of under this doctrine."

"It was. And it's going to happen again eventually, because Sephtis is good, not infallible, and I'd
rather build a doctrine that survives an honest miscalculation than one that only works when every
prediction lands perfectly." Kanja checked the damage reports from the two struck vessels, already
planning their repair. "The margin was never the point. The point was always having a plan flexible
enough to survive losing it."

Sephtis's own account of the miscalculation, delivered without defensiveness once the fleet was
safely clear, would become required study for every subsequent operation built around the timing —
not as a failure to be hidden, but as the proof the doctrine needed that it could bend without
breaking.
