# The Officer Who Wasn't Lying

*Locked canon, Batch 230, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1027`). Bane Alias Chronicle LIX, wave 20. Extends
`VB-060`'s "Already-Finished Negotiation" presence trait in reverse -- an opponent whose sincerity,
not deception, is what unsettles Bane. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The Directorate captain came alone, unarmed, in daylight, and said the thing Bane had heard a hundred
variations of from a hundred men trying to buy time, save their own skins, or set a trap: that he
wanted to defect.

Bane had spent years learning to hear the shape of a lie inside a sentence like that — the practiced
pause before the plea, the eyes that checked the treeline instead of his face, the offer that arrived
a half-second too eager. He listened for all of it now, out of habit more than expectation, and found
none of it. The captain's account of what had turned him — six months watching Scrip-Tether quotas
override medical triage in a border camp, one specific child's name he gave without being asked —
didn't have the shape of a story built to be believed. It had the shape of a man who'd simply stopped
being able to do the job.

"Already-finished negotiation" was Bane's own private name for what he did to rooms like this one,
the thing Callum Breck had once told him he did without seeming to try — arriving at a conclusion
somewhere the other man hadn't been invited and only announcing the result. It worked because most
men brought him something to see through. This one hadn't brought anything. He'd simply stood there,
plainly, and let himself be read, and there was nothing underneath the surface to find, which
unsettled Bane in a way no trap ever had.

"You're not lying," Bane said finally, and watched something in the captain's shoulders release that
had nothing to do with fear of being caught.

"No."

"That's the problem. I know exactly what to do with a man who's lying to me. I've never had to work
out what a defector who isn't costs the rest of you when your own side finds out he's gone."

He took the captain in — not on faith, not without six weeks of the same careful verification any
Directorate defector earned, Danne Sok's own network confirming every claim the man made piece by
piece — but he took him in having already believed him from the first sentence, which was the part
that stayed with him longer than the verification did. Every alias he'd built, every reputation he'd
let the Directorate write for him, ran on being unreadable. He hadn't built anything for being the one
doing the reading of a man with nothing to hide, and he suspected, walking away from that first
conversation, that he was going to need it more often than the years ahead of him would be
comfortable with.
