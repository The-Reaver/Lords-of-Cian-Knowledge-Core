# What Can't Be Burned

*Locked canon, Batch 125, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-422`). Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle IV, first entry
in the second wave. Rebellion era. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The Trust administrator who studied the failure of two punitive burnings concluded, correctly, that
fire itself had become a losing strategy, and proposed instead a subtler punishment no rebuild could
answer directly: a formal blacklist, barring any merchant guild in the region from trading with a
district that had received the Rolling Foundry Campaign's assistance, enforced through Scrip-Tether
debt leverage against the guilds themselves.

It worked, for the first six weeks, in a way burning never had. The district that had rebuilt in
eleven days now sat with full workshops and no legal buyers for anything they produced, the entire
population employed and starving at once, a punishment with no fire to put out and no wall to raise
back up.

"You can't out-build this one," the district's own guild representative told Kanja, on the seventh
week, exhausted. "There's nothing physical to fix. The problem isn't material. It's paper."

"Then the answer isn't material either." Kanja spent the following two weeks doing something the
alias had never done before — not rebuilding, not fighting, but personally traveling the region's
merchant routes, meeting guild leaders directly, offering the Rolling Foundry's own smithing and
repair services at cost to any guild willing to defy the blacklist and trade with the district
anyway, absorbing the financial risk the guilds themselves couldn't afford to take alone.

Eleven guilds took the offer within a month, enough to break the blacklist's effectiveness entirely
— not through confrontation, but through simply making defiance cheaper than compliance, the same
underlying principle that had powered every physical rebuild turned toward a problem no physical
rebuild could touch.

"You called it punishment that couldn't be burned," Kanja told the administrator, once the
blacklist's failure was undeniable, "because you were still thinking about it as something I'd have
to fight directly, the way I fight fire. I didn't fight it. I just made it more expensive to enforce
than to ignore, the same as I've always done. The district was never actually starving because of
paper. It was starving because compliance with your paper was cheaper than defiance, until I changed
that arithmetic. That's not a different method. That's the only method I've ever actually used."
