# The General Who Stopped Fighting Fair

*Locked canon, Batch 135, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-454`). The Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle IX, closing the
third wave. Rebellion era, age 25. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The Directorate general who'd lost four consecutive engagements to the Iron Bastard's resonance
doctrine — the original Stand, the four-alloy Crawler variant, the bridge collapse, and finally the
berm that had cost him the outpost despite denying the doctrine entirely — submitted his resignation
from active field command the week after the border engagement, a decision that surprised his own
superiors more than it surprised him.

"You could have kept fighting," his adjutant said, packing the general's own effects for the transfer
back to garrison duty. "The berm proved the doctrine has limits. That's exactly the kind of weakness
a determined commander could keep exploiting."

"I found the one weakness it has, and it cost me an outpost and six hours of tunneling to prove it
existed at all." The general didn't look up from the report he was finishing, his own account of
every engagement laid out with a precision he hoped his eventual successor would actually read. "Four
engagements. Four different approaches from my side, each one built on what I'd learned from the
last failure. And he adapted to every single one of them faster than I could adapt to him. That's not
a weakness a determined commander exploits. That's a weakness a determined commander spends his whole
career failing to exploit fast enough to matter."

"So you're simply conceding the doctrine can't be beaten?"

"I'm conceding it can't be beaten by fighting it the way I've been trained to fight anything else —
with more force, more preparation, more certainty that the next engagement will go differently if I
just study harder." He set the finished report aside. "Somebody smarter than me, or luckier, might
still find a way. I've spent four engagements and most of my career finding out I'm neither. I'd
rather step aside for someone who might be than keep losing soldiers proving I'm not."

The report reached the Directorate's own archives within the month, filed alongside every other
account of the doctrine's engagements — the clearest, most honest assessment any opposing commander
had yet produced, precisely because it came from someone who'd finally stopped trying to win and
started trying only to understand.
