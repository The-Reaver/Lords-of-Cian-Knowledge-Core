# What No One Owed

*Locked canon, Batch 278, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1447`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle XCIX, wave 33, closing the wave. The method's first case where the finding is genuinely, cleanly no fault at all — extending the "Recorded. Unrecoverable. True." documentation category (MCD-926) into a death with no debt attached.*

---

The mine-roof collapse at the eastern gallery killed six men in a district that had, by every record Ezio could find, followed every safety practice the crew had ever seen honored anywhere. The support timbers were sound and recently inspected. The seam had shown no warning signs any comparable operation would have caught. There was no owner to blame, no warning ignored, no chain left unreplaced.

"There's nothing here," Ezio said, three days into an audit that kept turning up the same clean answer no matter which direction he pressed it. "Not a hidden shortfall, not a corner cut. It just fell."

It was the first time either of them had reached the end of a case with genuinely nothing to write against anyone's name, and it left Ezio uncertain what the ledger was even for in a finding like that.

The families didn't ask for money. They asked for the six names to be written down somewhere that would still exist after the district itself had moved past grieving them — not a claim of negligence, since there was none, just a record that six specific men had gone into that gallery and hadn't come back out of it, and that the world had noticed.

"We've written 'unrecoverable' before," Kanja said, "when a debt was real but couldn't be paid. This is different. There's no debt here at all. Just six men who deserve to be more than a line in someone else's accident report."

Ezio wrote it that way — six names, the date, the gallery, the plain fact of the collapse, no figure attached anywhere on the page, no fault assigned because none existed to assign. It sat strangely in a ledger built for finding what was owed and to whom.

"Doesn't feel like it belongs next to the rest of these," Ezio said, setting the page beside a volume full of fraud and shortfall and negligence.

"Maybe it doesn't," Kanja said. "Maybe that's exactly why it has to go in anyway. The ledger was never only for proving someone failed these men. Sometimes it's just for proving they were here, and that no one's forgetting it just because there's no one left to blame."
