# The One Who Almost Sold It

*Locked canon, Batch 260, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1273`). Crow King Alias Chronicle LXXXI, wave 27, closing
it. One of the twenty trained scouts is offered money to sell craft knowledge to a rival power, and
the established consent safeguards are tested from within. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

She was one of the twenty scouts trained years before, back when a regional commander had first asked
the apprentice and Kanja to run the craft as a structured curriculum rather than personal mentorship.
She'd used what she learned well and honestly for years since, and no one on the crew's side had ever
had reason to doubt her -- which was exactly why the offer, when it came, was aimed at her specifically.

A rival power's agent found her three days' ride from anywhere the crew would hear about it and named
a sum large enough to change the rest of her life, in exchange for everything she remembered of the
curriculum's real methods -- not the philosophy, which everyone already knew wasn't secret, but the
actual mechanics: the Braid's interleaving, the authentication markers built to catch imitators, the
private phrase meant to separate genuine lineage practitioners from anyone who'd merely picked up
fragments.

She didn't take it. She also didn't report it immediately -- she sat with the offer for four days
first, turning it over the way she turned over any hard decision, before she rode back and told the
apprentice everything, unprompted, including how close she'd come to saying yes before she'd said no.

"I want you to know it wasn't an easy no," she said. "I thought about what that money could do for my
family for longer than I'm proud of."

"That's exactly why I believe you now more than I would have if you'd said it was never a question at
all," the apprentice said. "The authentication marker we built after the last scout was captured and
coerced -- it was built to catch someone lying about what they knew. It was never built to catch
someone telling the truth about being tempted. That's not the same failure. That's the safeguard
working exactly as intended, on someone it was never really meant to catch."

The scout kept training others after that, and the crew never treated the four days of hesitation as
anything other than what it was: a hard choice, honestly made, in the direction that mattered.
