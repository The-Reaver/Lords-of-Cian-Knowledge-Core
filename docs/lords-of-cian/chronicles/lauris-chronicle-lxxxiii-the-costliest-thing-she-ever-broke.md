# Lauris Chronicle LXXXIII: The Costliest Thing She Ever Broke

*Locked canon, Batch 300, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1700`). Eighty-third entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, a Strand D entry giving full-scene treatment to the Operation 32 half of `MCD-190`
(previously only summarized there alongside the separate, later Operation 34, which this entry
deliberately does not touch, reserved), deep in the Disillusionment sub-period. Dramatizes the
destruction of a Living-Drakma-adjacent biological raw-material production substrate off the
Voskharen coast -- `MCD-190`'s own framing as "the single most damaging act of sabotage against the
engineering tradition in Lauris's contractor career." Extends the trajectory from Chronicle
XXXIII's (`MCD-1650`) Operation 31 alliance with Aerelin into a first unilateral strike against the
supply chain itself, distinct from that operation's rescue-and-extraction register. Does not
reference Site K-Theta, the Anu Un Ra name reveal, or any Operation 34 content. No new named
characters. Zero collisions.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Disillusionment designation LL-001-S-032: "The contract asked me to neutralize a
hazardous marine installation. I neutralized considerably more than the contract asked for. I have
stopped pretending, even to myself, that this was an accident of thoroughness."*

Operation 32 reached her, on the Directorate's own paperwork, as a hazard-clearance assignment of
modest and unremarkable scope: an unregistered marine installation off the Voskharen coast, detected
by routine patrol rather than any specific intelligence, suspected of processing biological material
under conditions the Directorate's own maritime safety code would have condemned outright had it ever
been formally surveyed. The brief instructed neutralization and, where practical, salvage of any
recoverable material for the Directorate's own archive.

She has told me, and I believe her without reservation, that she read the brief once and understood
within the same reading precisely what she was looking at, and precisely what she intended to do
about it — a certainty that would not have been available to the woman who took Operation 3, or even
the woman who took Operation 18, but had become, by Operation 32, simply how she now approached any
site her accumulating private catalogue had taught her to recognize on sight.

The installation itself, submerged and partially built into a natural reef formation, proved on
approach to be considerably larger than the Directorate's own preliminary survey suggested: a
production substrate, Living-Drakma-adjacent in composition by every reading her own instrumentation
returned, generating the raw biological material she had by then learned, across the better part of
three decades of gathered fragments — Sample K-403's origins, the Iron-Halls of Velkar's
instrumentation, the third kind of body she had examined at Operation 24 — to associate directly with
the engineering tradition's supply chain. Not a facility that processed finished subjects. A source.
The kind of installation that fed every other kind she had ever cleared.

*

She engaged the installation's modest defensive presence — she has described it to me as barely
worth the term, a handful of automated deterrents rather than any living guard — and disabled it
within the hour. What followed occupied the greater part of two additional days, and it is this
portion of the account, rather than the brief engagement, that she has always treated as the
operation's true substance.

Rather than neutralize the substrate to the minimum standard the contract required — sufficient to end
the immediate hazard, leaving the underlying architecture intact for whatever salvage or future study
the Directorate's own archive might eventually authorize — she methodically destroyed it in full: the
generative architecture itself, its accumulated stores, and the deep structural anchoring that had let
it operate undetected for however many years it had already run. She has told me she understood, doing
it, that she was destroying something the Directorate would likely have wanted preserved, and that she
filed her report afterward describing the destruction as an unavoidable consequence of the
installation's own structural instability during neutralization — a characterization she has admitted
to me, without defensiveness, was not strictly honest.

"I did not accidentally break it," she wrote, in the entry she has since shown me in full. "I decided,
somewhere in the first hour, that a source this far upstream of everything I have spent thirty-one
operations clearing did not deserve the courtesy of survival simply because destroying it exceeded my
contract's stated terms. I have broken a great many things in this career. I do not believe I have
broken anything this costly to the apparatus beneath before, and I do not expect, honestly, to break
anything costlier again before I am finished serving it."

I have asked her whether Operation 32 felt different, in the moment, from the quieter omissions and
discretions she had been practicing since Operation 22 — the unreported nodes, the falsified subject
counts, the staged termination that had freed Aerelin one operation prior. She told me, without
hesitation, that it did: those earlier acts had all been acts of withholding, of choosing what the
institution would not learn. Operation 32 was the first act of hers, in this stretch of her career,
built entirely around causing loss — a deliberate strike against the apparatus's own capacity, rather
than a mercy extended to something already within her power to spare.

Her closing note for Operation 32: *I have spent this career learning what this apparatus is. I have
spent this operation, for the first time, costing it something it cannot simply regenerate elsewhere.
I intend to learn whether that is a habit I can afford to keep.*
