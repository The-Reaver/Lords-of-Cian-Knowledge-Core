# What the Floodwater Couldn't Take

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XX, wave 6.
Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign. A detailed showcase of the Trinity operating
through storm-surge flood conditions. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose. No new named characters.*

---

The storm surge came in faster than the settlement's own watchers had ever recorded, swallowing the
forge site's lower terrace under three feet of churning brown water within the hour — and a raiding
detachment had timed its approach to arrive in the same window, wagering correctly that a flooded
site, its footing gone and its defenders scattered to higher ground, would be easier to finish than
one standing dry.

Kanja met them in water to his knees, Mafesto already online, the Kinetic Transfer System absorbing
not just the raiders' strikes but the surge's own relentless push, converting the current's drag into
stored charge the way it had once converted an incendiary blast — the water fighting him and feeding
him in the same motion.

Obsidian Malice discharged low, at the waterline, a strike that used the flood itself as a conductive
medium, the shockwave traveling through submerged ground to buckle the raiders' footing before they
ever closed the distance. Onyx read the fight through wind-driven rain and breaking surf that would
have blinded an ordinary combatant — Cadence Ruin tracking six attackers by the water's own
disturbance patterns as much as by sight, Veil Piercer finding gaps in a formation half-swallowed by
its own flooded ground.

By the time the surge crested and began to recede, the raiders had broken and fled for higher terrain
of their own, and the forge site's upper works — the parts that mattered, the stock, the tools, the
half-finished season's work — stood untouched above the water line.

"They read the flood as an opening," the site's foreman said afterward, watching the water drain back
toward the tideline it had come from. "Reckoned nobody could fight properly standing in that."

"They weren't wrong that it's harder." Kanja checked Obsidian Malice's housing, the charge already
bleeding down. "They were wrong thinking harder meant impossible. The water didn't take anything from
us today it wasn't already prepared to give back. That's most of what the whole method's ever been."

"Even the water."

"Even the water." He looked out at the tideline, already retreating. "Everything's material if you're
willing to let it be, instead of just letting it be a problem."
