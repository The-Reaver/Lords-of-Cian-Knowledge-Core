# The Strait That Ate the Light

*Locked canon, Batch 199, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-784`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle XXIX, wave 10 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The fog off Kothrane Strait had a local reputation for swallowing ships whole even without a war to
help it, and the Trust patrol squadron that pursued *The Audit* into it on the second day of a running
engagement seemed determined to prove the reputation earned, firing blind at sounds and shadows that
might as easily have been driftwood as an enemy hull.

"We can't out-run them in this," Dol Maren said, on the first night, visibility down to a length of
rope's worth of deck in every direction. "But they can't find us in it either. Question is which of us
runs out of patience first."

It ran three full days — no single decisive engagement, no clean victory, just a running, grinding
contest of nerve fought almost entirely by sound and instinct. Kanja took the wheel himself for long
stretches, reading the fog's own thinning and thickening the way Dol Maren read weather, threading
between patrol vessels close enough to hear their crews' voices without ever being seen by them,
striking twice — brief, disabling engagements against isolated pursuers who'd drifted too far from
their own formation to call for support before Obsidian Malice's discharge took their rigging apart in
the dark.

By the second night the squadron's own captains had started firing at each other's shadows, unable to
distinguish friendly hulls from the enemy they'd been chasing for two days without a single confirmed
sighting, the fog doing more damage to their formation than *The Audit* had managed directly.

"They're going to break their own squadron apart before they ever find us," Kanja said, listening to a
distant, panicked exchange of signal horns from somewhere in the white.

"Do we let them?" Dol Maren asked.

"We let them. We're not trying to sink a squadron blind in fog thick enough to kill our own crew by
accident. We're trying to survive three days and let their own confusion do the rest."

It did. By dawn on the third day, two Trust vessels had run aground on shoals their own charts should
have kept them clear of, a third had collided with a fourth in the confusion of a friendly-fire
exchange, and the remaining ships broke off the pursuit entirely rather than risk losing more of their
own squadron to a fog they'd stopped trusting themselves to navigate.

"Three days," Dol Maren said, finally bringing them out into clear water and clean sunlight, "and we
never won a single fight worth calling a battle."

"We won three days of them being more afraid of the fog than they were of us. That's not nothing."
Kanja finally stepped back from the wheel, hands aching from three days gripping it. "Sometimes the
strait does the fighting for you. You just have to be willing to trust it longer than the other side
can stand to."
