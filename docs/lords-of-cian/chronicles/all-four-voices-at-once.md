# All Four Voices at Once

*Locked canon, Batch 260, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1264`). Crow King Alias Chronicle LXXII, wave 24, closing
it. The first operation run by Kanja and all three generations of the direct teaching lineage
together in a single coordinated action. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

It had never happened before, not because anyone avoided it but because the years had never lined up
that way -- Kanja usually elsewhere, the apprentice running her own crews, her own student building a
name apart from her, and his own student newer still. This time all four of them ended up in the same
valley on the same night for reasons that had nothing to do with wanting to prove a point, and
someone -- no one could agree afterward who first said it out loud -- suggested they simply run the
operation together instead of splitting it four ways.

It wasn't the largest deception of the alias's run. Five provinces at once, years before, had moved
more ground and more lives with more hands doing the work. This was one garrison, one night, ordinary
in every way except who was holding it together.

Kanja took the opening false signal, the same worn, plain-voiced relay he'd used for two decades. The
apprentice -- rasp-voiced still, the cost of the original marsh trick never healed and never regretted
-- carried the Braid's interleaving through the middle of it, three patterns held steady against a
sensor grid that had never faced her particular version of the trick. Her own student, the third
generation, read the garrison's guard rotation the way she'd learned to read almost anything: patient,
listening longer than felt comfortable before acting at all. And when the garrison's logistics officer
grew suspicious of a supply discrepancy the Braid alone wouldn't have explained, the fourth generation
produced a fabricated requisition trail on the spot, seamless enough that the suspicion resolved
itself without anyone ever needing to know it had existed. Four different methods, four different
shapes of the same discipline, moving through one night without anyone needing to hand off control to
anyone else.

Afterward, walking out, the fourth generation said what none of the other three had quite put into
words yet. "That's the first time I've watched all three of you work at once and understood, all the
way through, that we're not doing versions of the same trick. We're four different crafts that happen
to share one reason for existing."

"That's what I hoped it would become," Kanja said, "the day I told her" -- a nod toward the apprentice
-- "to teach the way she needed to, not the way I had. I didn't know it would ever look like tonight.
I'm glad it did."
