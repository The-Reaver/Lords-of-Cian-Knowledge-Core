# The Second Envelopment

*Locked canon, Batch 115, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-392`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle I. Rebellion
era, weeks after the Battle of the Gale Straits (`MCD-242`, age 29). Not a territory Chronicle. New
standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters beyond the
already-locked Sephtis (referenced, not appearing on-page).*

---

Admiral Krael's replacement inherited a fleet still recovering from the loss of twenty warships and
a standing order, issued from the capital itself, never to attempt an envelopment against the same
commander again. He built his new doctrine instead around dispersal — a loose net of independent
squadrons across sixty miles of open water, no single formation large enough to be split down the
middle the way Krael's had been.

The 22-ship arrowhead found the net's exact weakness within four hours.

It did not attempt to break through a single line this time; there was no single line left to
break. Instead it ran the seam between two dispersed squadrons at flank speed, close enough to draw
fire from both without ever presenting a target either squadron could commit to fully — each captain
forced, in the confusion of low visibility and a target crossing their mutual boundary at speed, to
choose between firing across the seam at a risk to the other squadron's own hulls, or holding fire
and letting the arrowhead pass untouched. Most held. Two didn't, and the same friendly-fire confusion
that had broken Krael's formation months earlier repeated itself in miniature, smaller in scale but
identical in shape.

"He's not fighting our ships," the replacement admiral said, watching the report come in from the
flagship's own chart table. "He's fighting the seams between them. Doesn't matter how we arrange the
fleet. Wherever two commands meet and have to decide, in the same second, whether the other one's
going to fire — that's the door he's going to walk through."

By the time the arrowhead reached open water and the storm system Sephtis had once again predicted
to the hour closed in behind it, the dispersal doctrine had cost the Trust four more hulls than the
tight formation it had replaced, and the replacement admiral filed a report recommending, with a
frankness his predecessor had never quite managed, that no fleet architecture the navy currently
possessed could close the specific gap being exploited.

"It's not a formation problem," the report read. "It's a trust problem. Every seam in our fleet is
two captains who have to decide, faster than doctrine allows, whether to trust the ship next to them
enough not to fire into their own line. He's found the one door that opens no matter how we rearrange
the walls around it."
