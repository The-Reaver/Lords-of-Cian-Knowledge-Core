# The Reef He Didn't Know

*Locked canon, Batch 200, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-812`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle XXVII, wave 9 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Fourteen years into the V4 coat's service, the shoulder locks had long since been retuned and the
lenses recalibrated twice more, and the gear that had stumbled through its first real fight now
answered as cleanly as a hundred years of its predecessors ever had — which was fortunate, because the
reef system guarding the slaving depot at the edge of the known charts had never once appeared in any
account he'd gathered in two and a half centuries of following chains across every water he knew.

The Sovereign Eyes' recalibrated lenses read the reef's structure through water clouded with silt no
chart had recorded, picking out channels wide enough for a launch boat between coral formations that
would have gutted anything larger on the first pass. Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System, tuned across
years to naval boarding impacts, adapted without complaint to a new kind of shock entirely — the jolt
of a launch boat scraping coral rather than taking cannon fire, converted and stored exactly the same.

The depot's guards, confident the reef itself was their true defense, had grown careless in a way
generations of Salt-Keep-trained crews back home would never have permitted, and the Forge-Coat's
redesigned grounding weave absorbed their answering volley with the same steady hum it had finally,
properly, earned back.

"You don't know these waters," the depot's overseer said afterward, disarmed on his own dock, more
baffled than afraid. "Nobody outside three generations of my own family has ever read that reef
clean on a first pass. How."

"I didn't read the reef. My eyes did — gear built for exactly this, refined for exactly this long,
because the water doesn't stay the same everywhere I go, and pretending it does gets people killed."
He gestured toward the freed captives already being ferried clear through the same channels that had
just proven the depot's own confidence wrong. "You built a hundred years of safety on the assumption
that unfamiliar water would always stay unfamiliar to whoever came looking for you. That was never
going to hold forever."

Two hundred and thirty captives cleared the reef by nightfall, guided out through channels marked now
for the first time on a chart the crew would carry home and never sell, and the depot's overseer,
questioned weeks later by a Trust patrol that finally found the place using the same channels, could
offer nothing but the same disbelieving account: a stranger who had read water no outsider had ever
read, on the first try, gear doing calmly what three generations of his own family had called
impossible for anyone but themselves.
