# The Soldier Who Almost Didn't Come Back

*Locked canon, Batch 196, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-689`). Bane Alias Chronicle XXIV, wave 8 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

He came to the camp's outer picket line an hour past midnight, unarmed, hands visibly empty the
whole approach, and said only that he wanted to speak to Bane before he lost whatever nerve had
carried him that far. The picket nearly killed him twice before believing that was the whole of his
intention.

He was a rank Suppression Brigade conscript, three years in, and he laid it out plainly once Bane
came to hear it himself: he'd watched his own company's officers falsify a supply report to cover
theft that had left his squad rationed thin for a month, had said nothing because saying something
meant a tribunal that punished the reporter as often as the thief, and had spent every night since
turning over the same unbearable question — whether staying loyal to an institution that would let
that happen to its own men was worth anything at all.

"I'm not asking you to take me," he said, before Bane could answer anything. "I don't know that I'm
brave enough to actually desert. I don't even know that I believe in whatever you're fighting for
instead. I just needed to say it to someone who wasn't going to report me for saying it, and you're
the only person on either side of this war I could think of who might actually listen instead of
using it."

Bane didn't press him toward a decision. Didn't offer him a place in the column, didn't ask for
anything the soldier hadn't already given by walking to the picket line unarmed in the dark. He
listened the whole account through, asked two questions about the falsified report specifically —
who signed it, whether the theft was still ongoing — and then let the silence sit a long moment
before answering.

"I'm not going to tell you what to do with what you know. That's not mine to decide for you, and I
don't think you'd trust the answer if I gave it to you easily." Bane's voice carried none of the
weight it carried toward garrisons. "What I will tell you is that you already did something tonight
most men on either side never manage — you looked straight at something wrong and didn't let
yourself pretend you hadn't seen it. Whatever you decide next, that part's already true. Nobody can
take it back from you."

The soldier left before dawn, the same unarmed way he'd come, and never returned to the camp again.
Word reached the crew, months later, secondhand and unconfirmed, that the falsified report had
surfaced anyway — through a route nobody could trace back to a single source — and that the officers
responsible had been quietly reassigned rather than punished, the theft itself never fully
addressed. Whether the soldier himself had sent it, or simply found the courage the conversation had
left him with, no one on Bane's side ever learned for certain.
