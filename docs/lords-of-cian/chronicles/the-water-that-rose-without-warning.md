# The Water That Rose Without Warning

*Locked canon, Batch 255, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1125`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle LXVIII, wave 23.
Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. A spring flood, pure evacuation, no dispute and no blade.*

---

Nobody had built the low-lying rows along the eastern canal bend to survive a flood that came
without a storm to announce it — the snowmelt further upriver arrived three days early that year, and
the water rose through the night while the district slept, no wind, no rain, no warning past the
sound of it finally reaching the first threshold.

Kanja was at the rows within the hour, and what waited for him there had nothing to do with the tally
method or a stolen wage. Forty families, waist-deep water climbing by the minute, no owner to name
and no theft to expose — just people who needed to be carried, and carts that needed to be floated
rather than pulled, and a narrow window before the canal's own current picked up enough to make the
crossing to higher ground genuinely dangerous rather than merely unpleasant.

He didn't draw Onyx once. There was nothing in the flood a blade could cut that would matter. What
the night asked for instead was the same thing the Dredge-Line Ambush's flood-timing math had once
asked for — reading how fast water actually moves against how fast frightened people actually move —
except this time there was no enemy on the other side of the calculation, only forty families and a
narrowing amount of dry ground.

Corren Halst organized the carrying lines. Danne Sok, saying nothing as always, found the two
shallowest crossing points before anyone else had finished arguing about which route was safest, and
simply started using them. Maret Vos ferried the youngest children across on his own back, three and
four at a time, his face giving away nothing about how many trips that actually required. Kanja
carried an elderly dredge hand who refused every offer of help until the water reached her collarbone
and refusing stopped being a choice she still had.

By dawn all forty families stood on the higher ground above the bend, cold, soaked, and short exactly
nothing — no drowned child, no lost elder, nothing the district would have to bury this week. The
rows themselves were a loss; three days of standing water would ruin most of what the flood hadn't
simply carried off. Nobody at the tally table that morning had a figure for what had been saved,
because nothing about it had ever been a theft to correct in the first place.

"You didn't fight anything last night," Hask observed, watching him wring out a soaked sleeve.

"There wasn't anything to fight." Kanja accepted a dry blanket someone pressed into his hands without
looking to see who. "Sometimes the whole job is just being fast enough, and strong enough, and
there long enough before the water decides for everyone."

rising flood with no structure to save and no repair to perform, purely people-carrying against a

toward nature at its most indifferent. Reuses Corren Halst, Danne Sok, and Maret Vos in their
established pre-Black-Trench crew roles. No new named characters -- the elderly dredge hand and the
forty families are unnamed. Second entry in the Trench Monarch's twenty-third wave.*
