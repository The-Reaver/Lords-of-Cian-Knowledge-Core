# The Apprentice Who Heard Too Much

---

The volunteer had asked to learn the doctrine's basics, not to master it, just enough to help
identify structural weaknesses during rebel sabotage operations, and Kanja had agreed to a limited
teaching — the listening technique alone, none of the discharge mechanics that required the Trinity's
own equipment.

Within a week the volunteer stopped sleeping properly.

"It doesn't turn off," she admitted, when Kanja finally pressed her on the exhaustion visibly
wearing through her. "Once you've learned to actually hear what a structure's telling you, every
building starts talking. The support beam in the room I sleep in. The wagon wheel on the cart outside.
I can't unlearn how to listen, and I didn't understand, before I started, how much quieter the world
used to sound."

"I should have warned you about that part."

"Would it have changed anything? I still wanted to learn it."

"Maybe not. It still should have been said out loud before you started, not discovered a week in
while you're losing sleep over a wagon wheel that isn't actually in danger of anything." He considered
her carefully. "This is part of the doctrine I don't think I've ever explained to anyone, because it's
not something I struggled with the same way — I built this technique slowly enough, over years, that
it grew into background noise I could set down when I chose to. You learned the core of it in a week.
That's not the same thing as learning to live with it."

"How do you set it down?"

"Honestly — most days, I don't fully. I've just had longer to make peace with what that costs. I don't
think I can teach you that part quickly, and I don't think I should have let you learn the listening
without also teaching you, first, what carrying it actually feels like."

She kept the skill. She also, at his insistence, stopped practicing it for a month afterward, learning
first how to rest inside a world that had stopped being quiet.
