# What the Districts Kept After Him

*Locked canon, Batch 255, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1147`). Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XC, wave 30,
closing the wave. Rebellion era, pre-Black-Trench. An ensemble walk down the length of the dredge
line, not the eve of the Black Trench.*

---

He walked the full length of the dredge line one ordinary afternoon, for no reason he gave anyone in
advance, past every site this alliance of districts had touched in the two years since a flood first
put the crew's name in the district's mouth. It wasn't the night before anything in particular. There
was no battle waiting at the end of the walk, no coming shift anyone had named out loud the way they
had, once, at the last ordinary site before a change nobody could quite define. It was simply a day
with nothing urgent enough to fill it, and he'd decided to spend it looking at what two years had
actually built.

Corren Halst was overseeing a new plank-bridge crossing at the third canal bend, competent and steady
and entirely unbothered by Kanja's presence, the way he'd been unbothered by most things since the day
he'd decided to trust a ledger before he trusted a man. Danne Sok stood watch nearby without being
asked to, the same wordless positioning he'd given the crew since before anyone had a name for what he
was doing. Maret Vos was teaching two newer recruits a knot he'd learned from nobody living, patient
in a way his own past had never taught him to expect from himself.

Garren Hask sat at a tally table that had moved three times in two years and always looked, somehow,
exactly the same — ledgers stacked in the same order, backup copies filed in the same undisclosed
places, a district's entire honest history kept in one man's careful hand. Callum Breck passed
through mid-afternoon with his infant daughter balanced on one hip, four years older than the boy who
had chalked three words on a captured officer's forehead without asking anyone's permission first, and
nodded once, unhurried, the way he nodded at most things now. Efa Gol and Pell Ostra worked side by
side at the edge of a site neither of them commanded that day, one reading the ground and one
addressing her materials, neither needing him there to keep doing exactly what they'd learned to do
without him.

He didn't linger at any single stop long enough to interrupt the work. What struck him, walking the
line end to end, wasn't any single site or any single person — it was how little any of it seemed to
need him standing in the middle of it anymore. The method ran itself. The bridges held themselves up.
The people who'd built all of it around a name he'd never asked for kept doing the work whether he
watched them do it or not.

"You're not usually just walking," Hask said, when he finally circled back to the tally table.

"No." Kanja sat across from him, picked up a sheet neither of them had looked at yet. "I wanted to see
it before whatever comes next decides it's seen enough of me for one lifetime."

Hask didn't ask what he meant by that, and Kanja didn't explain it any further than he had. They
worked the rest of the afternoon in the same unhurried quiet the whole dredge line seemed to have
settled into, the district's two years of honest figures laid out between them exactly the way they
always had been.
