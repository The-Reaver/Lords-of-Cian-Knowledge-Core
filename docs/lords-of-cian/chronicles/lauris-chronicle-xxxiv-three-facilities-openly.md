# Lauris Chronicle XXXIV: Three Facilities, Openly

*Locked canon, Batch 299, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1651`). Thirty-fourth entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, closing this wave's run through "Strand D," deep in the Disillusionment
sub-period. Full-scene treatment of Operation 37 (previously only summarized within `MCD-191`):
Lauris's first openly joint operation with Aerelin's Kareth War-Order network -- three coordinated
facility clearances conducted together rather than covertly in parallel, resulting in roughly 280
engineered Karesian subjects rescued to concealment rather than left to Directorate processing.
Deliberately stops at the edge of Operation 38 (reserved, not drafted here) and does not touch
Operation 40 (the Defection, reserved). No new named characters; Aerelin already locked. Zero
collisions.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Disillusionment designation LL-001-S-037: "Three facilities. One report, filed
honestly as to method for the first time in years, though not as to result. I no longer work alone
because I no longer believe alone is sufficient to the size of what I am finding."*

By Operation 37, the working relationship between Lauris and Kareth-Vassen Aerelin had matured, over
six years and Lauris's own intervening operations, from an informal exchange of intelligence into
something closer to a standing partnership — Aerelin's network, by then expanded to roughly eighteen
autonomous operatives across the Korren Highlands and its coastal approaches, and Lauris's own
Directorate cover, still formally intact, now serving that network's purposes at least as often as it
served the institution's. Operation 37 is the first occasion on which the two women chose to act
together openly rather than in the parallel, mutually deniable fashion they had maintained since
Operation 31.

The contract that brought her to the region was, on its Directorate face, unremarkable: a routine
Site Recovery assignment covering a cluster of three suspected engineering-tradition facilities her
own earlier reconnaissance — conducted, though her official report did not specify this, on
Aerelin-network intelligence rather than Directorate tasking — had already located and partially
mapped. What the contract's brief did not anticipate, because Lauris had deliberately shaped her own
report to prevent it from anticipating, was the scale of what those three facilities actually held:
combined, roughly 280 engineered Karesian subjects, ranging by her own assessment from newly
generated to several centuries established, secured across the three sites in conditions she
describes in her archive with a restraint that does not fully conceal how close that restraint comes
to failing her.

The clearance itself, conducted jointly across nine days with eleven of Aerelin's operatives working
alongside her rather than at a discreet remove, proceeded with a coordination Lauris notes, almost
with surprise, as more effective than anything her solo career had produced — proof, she wrote, that
the arithmetic she had spent so many operations solving alone had never actually required her to
solve it alone; it had simply never before occurred to her that it might not need to. The facilities'
active-duty defenders, engineered subjects of a lower tier than the "Copy" she had terminated at
Operation 28, were neutralized within the first two days. The remaining seven days were spent doing
something her career had, until this operation, never once required of her at this scale: not
termination, not reburial, not the quiet, solitary discretion of Operation 22's unreported resonance
node, but *extraction* — moving 280 living subjects, most of whom had never known any existence
outside containment, to concealment sites Aerelin's network had spent months preparing in advance of
an operation neither woman had yet told the Directorate was coming.

Her report to the Directorate, filed on schedule and with a precision that would satisfy any
reviewing officer, described the three facilities' locations, their defensive architecture, and their
successful neutralization accurately in every operational particular. It did not mention the 280
subjects at all. Her archive entry, by contrast, is the longest of this entire stretch, and I have
chosen to close this account with the passage from it I consider most revealing of where she now
stood, six operations from the end of a career she had not yet consciously decided to end:

*"I have spent thirty-six operations learning what this apparatus is. I have spent this one, for the
first time, actively building something to stand against it — not alone, not secretly hedging my own
private ledger against a debt I intended to discharge eventually, but openly, with allies who share
the accounting. I do not yet know what this makes me to the Directorate, should it ever learn. I know
what it has made me to myself: no longer a contractor who occasionally withholds a finding. Something
closer, now, to what Aerelin has been calling herself for sixty years, and what I am only now
beginning to have the honesty to call myself as well."*

She did not, in that entry or any that followed it in this stretch of her archive, complete the
sentence with the word itself. I have chosen not to supply it for her. Some accounts, I have learned
across thirty years of transcribing hers, are more faithfully rendered by what the ledger-keeper
chooses to leave, for the moment, unwritten.
