# What the Colonel Understood Too Late

*Locked canon, Batch 254, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1115`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXXXV, wave 29. Closes the
three-entry Colonel Serrin Draeth thread -- resolved not through combat but through his own direct
recognition of the "Already-Finished Negotiation" presence trait, matching the sub-series' preference
for nuanced closures over clean villain defeats.*

---

Draeth came himself this time, finally, and Bane understood before a word passed between them that
something in the man had changed since the lieutenant's visit — not calmer, not more prepared, just
tired in a way that hadn't been there the first two times.

"I drilled for months," Draeth said, before Bane could speak. "Told myself if I never stood in front
of you again, whatever it is you do wouldn't have anything to work on. Sent men instead. Told myself
that was strategy."

"It wasn't."

"No." Draeth's hand wasn't on his weapon. "I know that now. I came here today to finally fight the
thing I've been building in my head for months, the version of this where I finally win the argument
I never actually had with you the first time. And standing here, I understand there was never an
argument to win. You'd already finished whatever this was between us the day you let my men go home
instead of killing them. Everything since has just been me finding out, slowly, what you already
knew."

Bane didn't answer that the way Draeth's earlier visits might have led him to expect — no correction,
no confirmation, nothing that gave the moment more weight than it already carried on its own.

"I'm not going to tell you that's wrong," he said. "I'm also not going to tell you it means anything
about you that it took this long to see it. Most people never do."

Draeth stood there a moment longer, and then did the thing none of it had ever actually been leading
toward in his own head: he turned and walked back the way he'd come, without a fight, without a final
word meant to land, without anything that would have made the story he'd have told about this day if
he'd planned it out in advance. Bane's column never saw him again. Word reached them months later,
secondhand, that he'd requested reassignment to a rear post and stayed there.

"No death," Corren Halst said, watching the empty road Draeth had left by. "No court-martial. Just...
gone."

"That's usually what it actually looks like when someone stops fighting something that was never
there to be won," Bane said. "It's not as satisfying as the version where it ends in a fight. It's a
lot more honest about what the fight was actually for."
