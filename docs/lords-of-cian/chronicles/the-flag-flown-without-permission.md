# The Flag Flown Without Permission

*Locked canon, Batch 200, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-819`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle XXXIV, wave 12 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The banner flying over the resistance camp's stockade bore a crude approximation of black sails
against amber eyes, stitched by hands that had clearly never seen the real coat up close, and he
stood at the tree line studying it for a long moment before announcing himself, because he had never
once set foot in this region, and had certainly never given anyone permission to fly his likeness over
anything.

The camp's leader met him at the gate with the wary confidence of a man who'd built something real on
borrowed reputation and wasn't entirely sure how it would survive contact with the original.

"You're going to tell me we've got no right to it," the man said, before any accusation had actually
been made. "I know. We made the banner two years ago, after word reached us that you'd cleared the
Salt Keep. Figured if slavers believed you might be watching this strait too, they'd think twice
before running it. It worked. Three transports rerouted around us rather than risk finding out if the
rumor was true."

"I've never been within four hundred miles of this strait."

"I know that now. Didn't then, and honestly, still don't know it in any way that matters to the men
who avoid us because of that flag." The leader didn't flinch from the admission, laying it out plainly.
"You want it down, it comes down. But I'd ask you to think about what comes back into this strait the
day it does."

He studied the banner a while longer, turning the problem over honestly rather than reaching for the
easy answer. The fear it traded on was real fear, built on real things he actually had done, however
far from here — and the transports it had turned away really had stayed turned away, whether or not
he'd ever lifted a hand toward this particular water.

"Leave it up," he said finally. "But you tell your people the truth of it, privately, among
yourselves — that it's borrowed, not earned, and that whatever protection it's bought you came from
fear doing work I never asked it to do. I don't need credit for a strait I've never sailed. I just
need slavers to keep believing they might be watched everywhere, because eventually, if enough camps
like yours fly enough banners like that one, it stops mattering whether any particular rumor is true."

The leader considered that a long moment, then nodded, something like respect settling into the
gesture. "That's a stranger kind of victory than I expected to hand you."

"It's not mine to claim credit for either way. You built this one yourselves, out of fear I only
happened to be the shape of." He turned back toward the tree line, done with the conversation. "Keep
it standing. Just keep it honest."
