# The First Book He Filled

*Locked canon, Batch 214, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-932`). The Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle LVII, wave 19. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The first ledger book finally gave out on an ordinary evening, its spine splitting clean along a
crease that had been threatening to go for months, and Kanja sat with the two halves of it in his lap
longer than the moment strictly required.

It was the book he'd started with — before Ezio, before the corrective method had a name for itself,
before there was a crew at all beyond a handful of people willing to sit at a table and ask a
district's worst-off workers a question no one had thought to ask them first. The early entries in it
were rougher than anything that had gone into a ledger in over a year: figures crossed out and
re-added when he'd learned, too late for that page, what a fabricated counter-tally actually looked
like; testimony recorded in a hand that hadn't yet learned to write fast enough to keep pace with
someone speaking; whole sections with no cross-referencing at all, because there'd been no one yet who
knew how to do it.

"You could just start a new one," Ezio said, watching him turn the split pages. "It's not going to
hold together much longer no matter how careful you are with it."

"I know. I'm not trying to keep using it." He found the entry he'd been looking for without much
searching — the very first, a dockhand's testimony from a district neither of them had set foot in
since. "I wanted to look at this again before it went into whatever we keep the old ones in now."

"What's different about it?"

"Everything. I didn't know to cross-check anything yet. I didn't know a false claim could hurt someone
who never made it. I didn't know districts could train their own people, or that the method could
outrun both of us on its own. None of that's in this book. This book is just a man asking questions
he wasn't sure would matter."

He had the pages copied over the following week — every entry transcribed into a fresh volume by a
scribe trained in the method that hadn't existed when the original was written, the fabricated
figures corrected in a note beside the original rather than erased, the whole worn first book set
aside afterward rather than discarded.

"It's not going anywhere," he told Ezio, once the copy was done and the original wrapped and shelved
among the district's own kept records. "I just wanted it kept as what it actually was. Not the start
of something that already knew what it was going to become. The start of something that didn't know
yet, and got it wrong plenty, and kept going anyway."
