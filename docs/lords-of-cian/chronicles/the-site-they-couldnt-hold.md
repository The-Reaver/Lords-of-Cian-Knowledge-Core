# The Site They Couldn't Hold

*Locked canon, Batch 194, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-633`). The Trench Monarch Alias Chronicle XXVIII, wave 10 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The Compliance response to the five-district agreement was larger than anything the canal had seen
before it — two full companies, moved overnight, converging on the Deepcut site specifically because
it was the newest of the five to sign on and the least fortified against a genuine military answer
rather than a punitive raid.

Kanja saw the numbers before he saw the companies themselves, watching from the Deepcut loading dock
as the first ranks came into view along the canal road, and understood within seconds what six
months of dredge-site skirmishes had not prepared anyone in his crew to face.

"We can't win this one," he said, flatly, to Corren Halst beside him. "Not here. Not today. Two
companies, open ground, no ravine to seal and no ambush terrain to use. This is exactly the fight
they've been trying to force since Silt Row, and they finally built the conditions for it."

"So what do we do?"

"We leave. Now, before they're close enough to cut off the canal-side retreat. Every site we've held,
we've held because the ground favored us or the numbers favored us. Today neither one does, and
pretending otherwise gets people killed for a site we can retake later instead of a battle we'd lose
today."

It was not a popular order. Several of the newer recruits wanted to hold the line on principle alone,
and it took Halst's own flat repetition of the command, twice, before the retreat actually began in
earnest. Onyx, for all five of its gifts, offered nothing against two companies in open ground that
patience and a sealed ravine hadn't already proven were the only real answers the crew possessed — a
single blade, however gifted, was not an army, and Kanja did not pretend otherwise to anyone watching
him make the call.

They cleared the site with minutes to spare, the tally books and the five-district agreement's
signed copies carried out ahead of anything else, the Deepcut dredge line itself left standing,
undefended, exactly as the companies had come to take it.

It fell within the hour. Kanja watched from a ridge a mile off, and did not pretend the loss didn't
cost something real — weeks of organizing, the fifth district's hard-won trust in a standard that
now looked, to Deepcut's own workers, like a promise the Trench Monarch hadn't been able to keep.

"We'll rebuild it," Halst said, watching the same distant smoke.

"We will. But I want everyone to remember today exactly as it happened, not as a story where we found
a clever way to win anyway. We didn't win today. We survived today, and we kept the people and the
records that let us come back. Those aren't the same thing, and I don't want the difference lost the
first time somebody tells this story without me in the room to correct it."
