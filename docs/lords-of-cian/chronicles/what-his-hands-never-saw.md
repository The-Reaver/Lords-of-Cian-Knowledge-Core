# What His Hands Never Saw

*Locked canon, Batch 256, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1154`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle LXX, wave 24. The method's first case centering a blind worker's testimony, dismissed by his employer as unreliable and vindicated through the same tracking discipline the method itself depends on.*

---

The overseer said it before Ezio had finished taking the testimony down, loud enough that half the yard heard it: the man was blind, had been blind twelve years, and could no more count what crossed the quarry scales than count the stars. It was meant to end the claim before it started. Ezio kept writing.

The worker — a scale-tender, not a hauler, his blindness the reason he'd been given the job in the first place, since the overseer who'd hired him years earlier had assumed a blind man would be easier to cheat at a post that seemed to require sight — explained his method without being asked to defend it. He couldn't see the weights posted on the scale face. He didn't need to. Twelve years at the same post had taught him the exact sound and give of the beam settling at true weight, the particular hitch in its swing when a thumb pressed down on the pan to shave a false reading light, and the count of cart-loads by ear and by the specific creak of each cart's own axle, which he'd learned to tell apart the way a sighted man might learn faces. He gave Ezio a tally, cart by cart, covering four months, entirely from memory and touch, more precise in places than the yard's own written log.

Ezio cross-referenced it against three separate records — the quarry's official weigh-book, a rival buyer's own receiving log for the same stone, and the wear pattern on the scale mechanism itself, which a specialist Ezio brought in confirmed showed exactly the kind of thumb-pressure interference the worker had described, worn into the beam's pivot in a pattern no honest scale would show. Every figure the blind man had given matched, cart for cart, a fraud the overseer had been running against sighted buyers for years, confident that a blind scale-tender's testimony would never be taken seriously enough to check.

"You said he couldn't count what crossed the scale," Ezio told the overseer, laying the specialist's report beside the tally. "He counted it better than your own written book did."

Kanja didn't lecture the overseer about the worker's competence — the numbers had already said everything that needed saying. What he made sure happened, before the crew left the district, was that the finding named the scale-tender's own method explicitly in the record, not as a curiosity but as the actual investigative technique that had cracked a fraud the crew's own eyes would have needed the same specialist to catch. The quarry's new management, forced to replace the overseer, kept the scale-tender at his post at a corrected wage and, at Ezio's specific recommendation, began routing every future scale dispute through him first.
