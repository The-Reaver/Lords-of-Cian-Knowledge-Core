# The Village That Never Needed Saving

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Scourge Alias Chronicle, wave 12. Long Mask era,
age 100, V3 gear (Golden Terror era). Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose. No new named characters.*

---

He passed through the fishing village on ordinary business, restocking water ahead of a longer
crossing, and noticed only in passing the elaborate signal-fire arrangement built into the headland
above the harbor — three separate beacon stacks, each color-coded with dyed kindling, positioned to be
visible from any approach a raiding ship might realistically take.

The village elder, unbothered by a stranger's curiosity, explained the system without much ceremony
when he asked.

"Built it forty years back, after the stories about the Scourge started reaching this far south. Never
seen a slaving ship ourselves — never even seen the man, if half of what's said about him is even
true. But we figured, if there's someone out there clearing straits of that trade, the least we could
do was make sure we weren't the reason a raid ever got the chance to happen in the first place."

"Forty years and never used it."

"Forty years and never needed it. That's rather the point." The elder said it plainly, without any
sense of having wasted the effort. "My grandmother always said the smartest villages weren't the ones
that got rescued. They were the ones that made themselves too much trouble to bother raiding in the
first place, so nobody ever had to come save them at all."

He studied the beacon stacks a while longer, turning over a thought he hadn't quite had cause to have
before — every liberation the crew's ledgers recorded had happened after the fact, a rescue answering
a harm already done. This village had built an answer to a harm that had simply never arrived, its
whole defense constructed from nothing but rumor and a grandmother's good sense, decades before any
of it had ever been tested.

"You never even confirmed the stories were true before building all this."

"Didn't need to. True or not, the fear was doing useful work either way — made us careful, made us
ready, made every raiding captain who ever scouted this coast decide there were easier villages
further on." The elder shrugged, entirely at peace with a defense built on a legend she'd never
personally verified. "Whoever he is, if he's real, I hope he knows some of what he started never
needed him to finish it. We finished this part ourselves, just from hearing he might be out there."

He left without correcting her, water casks refilled and nothing about the encounter worth adding to
any ledger beyond a quiet private satisfaction — a village saved by nothing but its own fear turned
useful, in a strait he had, in fact, genuinely never sailed.
