# Borikén Annals III: The Men Who Claimed His Name

*Locked canon, Batch 165, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-512`). Homage-era Batey, Borikén/Guaní. Territory
Annals entry — Guaní as protagonist, Kanja an unnamed background guest with no command, credit, or
resolution authorship. New standalone material. No new named characters.*

*Corrected Batch 339, 2026-10-02: narrative prose no longer quotes "No Single Point" as an in-world
phrase, in two places.*

---

Three separate men, unconnected to each other and unconnected to Guaní, began independently claiming
his identity across different corners of Borikén — small-time extortion, mostly, threats made in his
name to collect debts he'd never authorized, exploiting the very ambiguity that kept him alive.

The community's trust, built over years on the certainty that the real Guaní never appeared where his
name was invoked carelessly, began fraying under three impostors nobody could immediately distinguish
from the truth.

"They're using the mystery against the mystery itself," his closest lieutenant said, watching
confusion spread through neighborhoods that had once trusted the name completely. "If nobody can prove
who's real, people stop trusting any of it."

"Then we don't try to prove who's real. We make the fakes prove they aren't." Guaní didn't reveal
himself to correct the record directly — doing so would have undone the very protection the ambiguity
provided. Instead, each impostor was quietly, individually confronted by ordinary residents Guaní's own
network briefed beforehand, asked specific questions only the real man's actual conduct could answer
consistently. All three impostors, tested this way, contradicted themselves within days, their
extortion collapsing not because anyone unmasked the truth, but because the lies couldn't survive
close, patient questioning.

"You never once stepped forward yourself," the lieutenant observed afterward, the community's trust
already rebuilding as the impostors' schemes fell apart on their own. "I half expected you to."

"Stepping forward would have solved this problem and created the next one. The moment there's a
confirmed face, the whole of what's kept me breathing stops working the way it's always worked — for
real threats, not just petty extortionists riding my reputation." Guaní watched the neighborhoods
settle back into their
old, careful trust. "The ambiguity that let three men briefly exploit my name is the same ambiguity
that's kept five raids from ever finding me. I'm not willing to trade the second protection for a
faster fix to the first problem."

An unnamed man who had quietly helped brief two of the residents on which questions to ask left the
district once the last impostor's scheme had unraveled.
