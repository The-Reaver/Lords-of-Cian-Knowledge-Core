# The Wreck They Named for Him

*Locked canon, Batch 121, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-410`). Sovereign Ghost Alias Chronicle IV, first entry
in the second wave. Rebellion era. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The merchant convoy that went down in the Salt Reach during a genuine, unremarkable storm had
nothing to do with Kanja's fleet at all, but by the time survivors reached shore and told their
story, three separate secondhand retellings had already attached black sails to the weather that
sank them, and a fourth had the Sovereign Ghost personally boarding the lead vessel before it went
under.

The Trust seized on the story before anyone could correct it, and within a month a formal diplomatic
complaint had been filed against "known rebel naval forces" for an act of piracy that had, in truth,
been entirely the work of an ordinary squall.

"You could let it stand," Dol Maren said, laying out the complaint's language for Kanja to read
aboard *The Audit*. "It's not costing us anything real. If anything, it adds to the reputation."

"It's costing the actual survivors something real. Their insurers are refusing every claim, citing
an act of piracy instead of a weather loss, because the story that reached the capital first was the
wrong one." Kanja read the complaint twice, checking dates against the fleet's own confirmed
positions that week. "We were two hundred miles north the night that convoy went down. I can prove
it. I'm not going to let a storm's own work get pinned on us just because it makes a better story
than the truth."

He surrendered the fleet's own navigation logs to a neutral Astral Archipelago maritime tribunal —
an unprecedented move for a rebel commander with every reason to guard his own positions — cross-
checked in full against the convoy's last known heading and the storm's independently recorded
track. The tribunal's ruling, delivered six weeks later, cleared the fleet of any involvement and
forced the insurers to honor the original claims.

"You gave up operational secrecy to clear your name over a storm," Maren said afterward, still
faintly incredulous. "Most commanders would have let the legend eat the blame and called it good
publicity."

"A legend that gets credit for things I didn't do is a legend that'll eventually get blamed for
things I didn't do either, and next time the people paying for that story might not survive it to
tell the tribunal otherwise." Kanja watched the logs being sealed and returned by the tribunal's own
courier. "I don't control what people believe about the ghost. I can at least make sure the ghost
doesn't get credit for a dead man's storm."
