# The Spy Who Read the Signals Back

*Locked canon, Batch 187, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-547`). The Crow King Alias Chronicle XIV. Rebellion era,
age 23. Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.
No new named characters.*

---

A Directorate intelligence officer, trained in the same category of deception the Crow King had spent
years perfecting, began feeding false signals of his own into the same reporting channels — not to
counter the Hymn-Engine directly, but to seed doubt about which signals, real or false, could be
trusted at all, turning the deception war into a contest between two people who both understood
exactly what the other was doing.

For the first time, Kanja faced an opponent playing his own game rather than simply falling for it.

"He's not trying to catch the lie," the apprentice observed, watching the pattern of counter-signals
develop over several days. "He's trying to make every signal, real or fake, equally unreliable. If
nobody trusts any report anymore, the whole method stops working, whether or not he ever identifies a
single false one."

"That's the more dangerous version of the attack, and the harder one to counter." Kanja studied the
officer's own counter-signals carefully, recognizing craft he respected even while working against it.
"He's not trying to out-deceive me. He's trying to poison trust itself. I can't out-lie someone who's
trying to make lying meaningless. I need a different answer."

The answer, when it came, mirrored the trust-based resolution once used against a different attacker
entirely: Kanja stopped relying on the reporting network altogether for the operation's critical
moment, delivering the real signal in person, the same unforgeable method that had once defeated an
attack on his own crew's trust. The officer's careful campaign of doubt had nowhere left to attach
itself once the actual information never touched the compromised channel at all.

"You beat him by refusing to fight him where he was strongest," the apprentice said afterward, genuine
respect in it. "That's not something I'd have thought to try."

"Because he understood the craft as well as I do. The only way to beat someone who knows your own game
completely is to occasionally decline to play it." Kanja reflected on the officer's skill without
diminishing it. "I don't know that I've faced anyone since who understood the Hymn-Engine's actual
mechanism as clearly as he did. I'd rather respect that than pretend the win was easy."
