# Sankofa Chronicle I: The Man Who Turned Against Himself

*DRAFT, pending Abad's explicit approval. First entry in Sankofa's own Chronicles, its protagonist
Baálé (`PH2-021`), not a Kanja Chronicle. Kanja appears only as a guest, present but granted no
command, no intervention, and no resolution credit, matching the established convention from
Xaragua Chronicle I (`MCD-334`), Umoja Chronicle I (`MCD-335`), Yara Chronicle I (`MCD-336`), Areíto
Chronicle I (`MCD-339`), Guanín Chronicle I (`MCD-340`), Borikén Chronicle I (`MCD-341`), Ide
Chronicle I (`MCD-342`), Kwan Chronicle I (`MCD-343`), Jibaro Chronicle I (`MCD-344`), and Uhuru
Chronicle I (`MCD-345`). Narration is close-third on Baálé throughout, not Onyx of Oblivion. Opens
Los Angeles's own run of territory Chronicles. Slots into no existing mainline battle — original
homage-era material set in Sankofa itself.*

---

Kojo came to kill him in broad daylight, which was, Baálé would think afterward, the single
smartest and single dumbest decision the man ever made in the same breath. Smartest because a
daylight killing in front of forty witnesses was the only kind that could actually settle who ran
Sankofa's western blocks once and for all. Dumbest because it meant Kojo had to do it himself,
close enough to see Baálé's face, and Baálé had never once, in three years of men trying exactly
this, found a version of close enough that worked out the way the other man intended.

He came with a blade and a grudge three months old, over territory that had belonged to neither of
them a year before either grudge existed. Baálé didn't draw first. He rarely did; drawing first
had a way of making a man's decision for him before Baálé got the chance to watch what the man
actually decided once the moment arrived.

Kojo got one real cut in — a shallow line along Baálé's forearm, the kind of wound a man carries
for the rest of his life as the only physical proof he'd ever come close. It was, by any ordinary
measure, the best either man would ever do against the other, and it changed nothing about how the
fight actually ended: Baálé had him on the ground inside another ten seconds, blade gone, breath
gone, alive only because Baálé had never once, in three years of this, found a reason to make
alive optional.

The stranger watched from across the street, the way he'd watched most of the morning without
anyone quite noticing when he'd arrived — a face at the edge of a crowd that had gathered fast and
would scatter faster, the way crowds in Sankofa always did the moment the outcome stopped being in
doubt.

Kojo did not run once Baálé let him up. That was the part the crowd hadn't seen coming, and the
part Baálé himself, three years into this particular gift, had stopped being surprised by.

"You should be walking away right now," Baálé said, not moving to stop him from doing exactly that.

"I know." Kojo didn't look like a man who understood what was happening to him, because he wasn't.
"I don't want to. I came here to put you in the ground and I don't — " He stopped, tried again,
found the sentence no more available the second time. "I don't want to be anywhere but standing
right here anymore. That doesn't make sense. Three minutes ago I wanted you dead."

"I know exactly how that feels, because I've watched it happen to eleven other men before you, and
I still don't have a better way to explain it than the truth: it isn't magic and it isn't
brainwashing, whatever your friends are going to tell you tonight when you don't come home for
dinner. You came at me with everything you actually had. I answered with everything I actually
had. Something in a man recognizes that, once it's happened to him for real, and doesn't forget it
just because the recognizing is inconvenient." Baálé held out a hand, not to help him up, simply to
be there if Kojo decided to take it. "I can't make that happen without you swinging first. I've
tried. It only works the one way."

Kojo took the hand. By the following week he was running the same western blocks he'd tried to
take by killing the man who held them — for Baálé now, not against him, with a loyalty that made
no more sense to Kojo on the seventh day than it had on the first, and that he had, by then, mostly
stopped trying to argue himself out of.

"That's twelve," the stranger said, once the crowd had thinned and it was just the two of them and
Kojo's abandoned blade still lying in the street. "Twelve men who came to kill you and left serving
you instead."

"Twelve I know about. There's a version of this that doesn't announce itself with a blade in
daylight — men who never once raise a hand against me themselves, who just point other men at me
from somewhere I can't see and call it strategy instead of cowardice." Baálé picked up the blade,
turned it once in his hand, set it back down exactly where it had fallen. "That version doesn't
care how many men like Kojo I turn. It was never going to walk up to me in the street and let me
answer it face to face. I don't have anything for that kind, and I've stopped pretending otherwise
to anyone who asks."

"You think it's coming."

"I think it's always coming, the same way weather is always coming. I just don't know the day." He
looked at Kojo, already walking the western blocks like he'd held them his whole life instead of
his first week. "Until it does, I intend to keep doing the only thing I actually know how to do,
which is stand here and let anyone who wants to try their hand find out what happens when they
do."

The stranger was gone before Kojo came back that evening to formally report in, the way he always
seemed to be. He left nothing carved, no phrase repeated hand to hand — only, the following
morning, Kojo's abandoned blade, cleaned and left leaning against Baálé's own door, exactly where
anyone walking up to it would have to see it first.

Kanja is the unnamed guest, present at the edge of the crowd and inactive throughout, matching the
Umoja/Yara/Areíto pure-witness end of the established range. The scene puts Baálé's signature
ability (`PH2-021`, "The Turn") directly on the page for the first time via a fresh one-on-one
attacker (Kojo), deliberately distinct from the ability's own already-locked backstory event (the
COINTELPRO-orchestrated ambush that nearly killed Baálé and Kra, which per `PH2-021` remains
backstory only and is not restaged here, matching the Toussaint-Louverture/Ogoun-Xarey precedent
for events referenced rather than dramatized): a direct, face-to-face attack that Baálé survives
converts the attacker into a bound, loyal follower, exactly matching the ability's stated mechanic.
The ability's cost is honored explicitly and left as live, unresolved tension rather than a settled
fact: Baálé states plainly that a faceless, coordinated threat -- the same shape as the COINTELPRO
conspiracy already established in his backstory -- is exactly what his gift cannot reach, foreshadowing
rather than restaging that already-locked near-death event. Introduces one new named character,
Kojo (Akan Monday-born day-name, per the standing Taíno/Yoruba/Akan/Kikongo/Nahuatl/Swahili naming
convention -- deliberately not a real-world surname, since this world's own established reading of
Spanish colonial surnames as imposed identity, per Arturo Salvatierra Duho's backstory, makes a real
Latino surname the wrong register for an organically-named convert into Baálé's own following): a
rival lieutenant who attacks Baálé and, surviving, becomes one of his loyal followers, left open as
a minor recurring figure for future entries rather than a one-scene antagonist. No other new proper
