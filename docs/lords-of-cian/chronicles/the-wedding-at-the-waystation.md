# The Wedding at the Waystation

*Locked canon, Batch 276, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1429`). Bane Alias Chronicle XCIX, wave 33, closing wave
33. Bane's first purely celebratory, conflict-free entry: Toran (already locked, `MCD-1099`) marries,
and the column halts a full evening for it.*

---

Toran asked permission for exactly one thing in the whole planning of it, and it wasn't time off or
an escort or a delay in the column's schedule — it was whether Bane himself would stand as witness.

"You don't need my permission for any of it," Bane told him. "Least of all that part."

The bride was from a settlement the column had passed through three months earlier, freed of a small
garrison outpost in an operation that hadn't made anyone's list of the war's important battles. She
and Toran had exchanged perhaps a dozen letters since, carried by whatever runner happened to be
going her way, and neither of them had wasted any of those letters pretending the war left room for a
long courtship.

The column stopped for one evening at a waystation nobody would remember as anything but the place
Toran got married. Efa Gol organized it in an afternoon with the same competence she brought to decoy
routes and diversion timing, which meant it came together faster and better than anyone expected.
Danne Sok, who almost never sang, sang an old dock-work song at the reception that half the column
somehow already knew the second verse to. Corren Halst gave a toast that started as an assessment of
Toran's tactical judgment and ended, to everyone's surprise including his own, half in tears.

Bane stood where he was asked to stand and said the words the ceremony called for, and for one evening
nobody in the column was Bane's crew or the Trench Monarch's old fighters or anyone's reputation at
all — they were people at a wedding, eating better than they had in a month because Efa Gol had traded
for it specially, and nobody once brought up the war until the fires were banked low and it was time
to sleep.

"Feels strange," Toran admitted to him near the end of the night, "being happy in the middle of all
this."

"It's not strange," Bane said. "It's the whole reason any of this is worth fighting. If we forget how
to do this, we've already lost something the war can't give back." He looked at the fires, at the
column asleep around them, ordinary and unafraid for one night. "Don't apologize for it. Just don't
forget to notice it while it's happening."
