# The Hearing They Couldn't Hold Without Him

*Locked canon, Batch 262, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1319`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LXXIII, first
entry in the twenty-fifth wave. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241), a
Sovereign Trust magistrate's court. A legal/institutional register, distinct from the earlier
diplomat's territorial offer (MCD-879). Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person
prose.*

---

The summons came the ordinary way a summons came to a man the Trust couldn't reliably locate — nailed
to a dozen public boards across three districts, addressed to "the person or persons operating under
the designation the Lord of Embers," demanding an accounting for property damages the Directorate's
own reports attributed to punitive burnings the campaign had answered, not started. It set a date. It
set a magistrate's chamber. Nobody genuinely expected the accused to appear.

Kanja appeared anyway, unannounced and unarmed in anything but the plainest working clothes, and
sat through a bailiff's confusion about how to seat a defendant nobody had a description matching
until the man simply said his own name and asked where he should stand.

The magistrate, to his credit, recovered quickly. "The charge concerns damages to Trust property
across six settlements," he said, reading from a ledger thick enough that reading it in full would
have taken the rest of the day. "Burnings the Directorate attributes to reprisal actions following
your interventions."

"Every burning on that ledger happened before I arrived," Kanja said. "I didn't start a single fire
listed in it. I rebuilt what the Trust burned as punishment for people asking to be treated
decently. If the court wants an accounting of who did the damage, I'd welcome one — I think it
would go very differently than this ledger currently reads."

It was Garren Hask, brought as the only witness Kanja called, who did the actual arithmetic in
front of the room — settlement by settlement, date by date, cross-referencing the Directorate's own
supply requisitions against the fires they claimed as reprisals, until the chronology the Trust's
case depended on came apart in the magistrate's own hands. Hask read numbers the way he always had,
without performance, letting the ledger itself make an argument no speech could have made as
cleanly.

The magistrate didn't rule in Kanja's favor, exactly — the court had no real mechanism for
acquitting a man it couldn't prove had committed any of the acts charged, and no appetite for
formally blaming the Trust's own forces in open session. He adjourned the matter indefinitely
instead, the charge neither dropped nor pursued, a quiet institutional shrug that let everyone in
the room draw their own conclusion about whose ledger had actually told the truth.

"That's not a victory," Hask said, once they were clear of the chamber.

"It's not a defeat either," Kanja said. "Sometimes the honest arithmetic is all you get to leave
with. It's usually enough."
