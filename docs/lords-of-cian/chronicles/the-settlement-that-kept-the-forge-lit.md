# The Settlement That Kept the Forge Lit

*Locked canon, Batch 136, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-457`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle IX, closing the
third wave. Rebellion era, age 27, several years after the events of "An Apprenticeship That
Outlasted the War" (`MCD-391`). Not a territory Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The settlement resident who'd learned hinge-truing aboard The Anvil during the campaign's earliest
months had kept teaching it, in the years since, to anyone in the settlement willing to learn a trade
that hadn't existed there before the Rolling Foundry Campaign passed through — and by the time Kanja
returned, years later, on an unrelated errand that happened to route him near enough to visit, the
settlement had its own small forge running independently, staffed by people who'd never once met him
in person.

"You started something you probably didn't mean to start," the resident said, showing him the
settlement's own modest works — smaller than anything The Anvil could produce, but steady, self-
sustaining, entirely theirs. "I taught the lesson you taught me. Some of them taught it to others.
Nobody here remembers exactly which hands it started with anymore, three generations of apprentices
in."

"That's the whole point of teaching something properly. It stops needing you." Kanja examined a piece
of the settlement's own work, competent, unhurried, carrying none of the urgency of a campaign forge
but all of its genuine skill. "I taught you a hinge-truing technique during a war, expecting maybe it
would help this settlement survive the campaign. I didn't expect it to become something that outlasted
the war itself, running on its own, teaching people who weren't even born when I passed through the
first time."

"Most of what the campaign left behind was rebuilding what got burned. This is the one thing it left
that wasn't a repair. It was something new that just kept going after you moved on." The resident set
the tool back on its rack. "I think that's worth more than anything you actually built while you were
here. Everything you built with your own hands, we still had to maintain ourselves eventually. This,
you handed us the ability to keep building on our own."

Kanja stayed the evening, working alongside apprentices who knew him only as a skilled stranger
passing through, and left the following morning without correcting the impression — the settlement's
forge, and the quiet, self-sustaining thing it had become, exactly the kind of legacy the alias had
never set out to leave but had, somehow, left anyway.
