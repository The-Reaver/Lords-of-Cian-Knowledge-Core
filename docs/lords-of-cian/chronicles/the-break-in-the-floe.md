# The Break in the Floe

*Locked canon, Batch 263, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1341`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LXVIII, wave 23. A detailed Long Mask-era gear rescue-and-combat showcase freeing a fleet trapped as pack ice closes in, using the northern pilot's method rather than storm-timing doctrine to time the intervention. Corrected Batch 321, 2026-10-02: an earlier draft staged this as a full-Trinity showcase, despite this Chronicle's placement well within the Long Mask era of just over 283 years, after Kanja's already-locked age-30 surrender of the Trinity to its sealed vault (`MCD-246`); replaced with his correct Long-Mask-era kit (`ARS-344` through `ARS-356`, plus the Rexmar Machete). Also corrects a mischaracterization that attributed Obsidian Malice to Onyx of Oblivion -- it is a war club (`ARS-030`) and Kanja's own weapon, not one of Onyx's named powers.*

---

Three ships had been careless, or unlucky, or simply trusting a chart that hadn't accounted for how early the ice was setting this year, and now they sat locked in a channel that had been navigable water four days earlier, the floe closing around their hulls a hand's width at a time.

It was the pilot's reading that gave Kanja the window, not the student's -- a specific stillness in the gulls an hour before dawn that meant the temperature was about to drop hard enough to finish the job the ice had started, crushing three hulls that could still, right now, be freed. "You have maybe ninety minutes," the pilot said. "After that I can't promise the ice waits for anyone."

The Forge-Coat and Ironfall Boots went to work on the floe itself rather than any enemy -- grounding the ship's own trapped momentum and redirecting it outward in controlled pulses, breaking a channel ice-width by ice-width instead of trying to shatter the whole sheet at once, since a single massive strike risked sending fractures the wrong way, straight into a hull already under pressure. The Ironhand Gauntlets struck along carefully mapped fracture lines the pilot herself had pointed out from twenty years of watching this exact strait break apart in spring, precision over power, opening seams rather than caving them. The Smoke System carried Kanja hull to hull across ice too thin to trust and too thick to swim, checking each ship's timbers by hand for the specific groan that meant a hull was about to go before the ice did. His own instinctive Rexmar-Mar tactical sense, with no storm-gust to time against, timed the Rexmar Machete instead to the ice's own settling rhythm -- the pilot's method, borrowed whole and applied to a fight rather than a forecast for the first time.

The third ship's hull cracked with eleven minutes left on the pilot's own count, water flooding a hold faster than any pump could answer. The Ironhand Gauntlets' last blow didn't break ice at all -- it threw a wall of displaced slush and seawater beneath the sinking hull, buying just enough buoyancy for the crew to abandon in open water instead of under it.

All three ships freed. All crews accounted for. The channel, by the pilot's own prediction, closed solid an hour after the last ship cleared it.

"That's not a sky-reading," the pilot said afterward, watching the student log the whole event in the running ledger, "that's mine, credited proper."

"Then it goes in as yours," the student said, and wrote it exactly that way -- the pilot's name on the reading, not the school's, not Sephtis's tradition claiming a method it hadn't built.
