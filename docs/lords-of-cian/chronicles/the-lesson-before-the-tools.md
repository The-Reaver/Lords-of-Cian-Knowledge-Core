# The Lesson Before the Tools

Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

Every new recruit assigned to the tunnel crews arrived expecting to be handed a shovel or a pry bar
within the hour. Kanja had stopped letting that happen three weeks into the siege, after the third
close call that traced back to someone working a section they didn't understand well enough to
recognize its own warning signs.

"Before anyone touches a tool on this crew, you sit through this," he told the latest group of
six, none of them older than nineteen, gathered around a cross-section diagram scratched into a
flattened patch of clay. "Not because I don't trust you to work hard. Because hard work in a tunnel
that's about to fail doesn't save anyone. It just means more people are close to it when it does."

He walked them through the same fundamentals every time, unhurried: how a joint fails before it
fails, the specific sound settling stone makes in the minutes before it moves, the difference
between a crack that's cosmetic and one that's structural, when to stop and call for a second
opinion instead of pushing through on confidence alone.

"This feels like it's slowing us down," one of the recruits said, glancing toward the section
they'd actually been sent to shore.

"It is slowing you down. By about an hour, once, at the start." Kanja tapped the diagram's marked
failure points, each one drawn from an actual close call the crew had survived. "Every hour I spend
teaching this now is an hour I don't spend digging someone out later, or telling their family why I
didn't teach them sooner. I'd rather lose the hour."

By the time the six recruits finished the lesson and picked up their first tools, every one of them
could name the three warning signs Kanja considered non-negotiable before that section's own senior
crew would let them work unsupervised — a standard that hadn't existed before the third close call,
and that outlasted the siege itself, carried forward by every crew member who'd sat through it and
later taught it to someone new in turn.

"You're building more than tunnels down here," the crew chief observed once, watching the lesson
repeat itself for what must have been the twentieth new group.

"I'm building people who don't need me standing over their shoulder to know when to stop." Kanja
rolled the diagram back up for the next group. "That matters more than anything I could dig myself.
I won't always be the one underground with them."
