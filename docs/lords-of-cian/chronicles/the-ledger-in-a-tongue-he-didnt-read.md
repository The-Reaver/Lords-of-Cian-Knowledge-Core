# The Ledger in a Tongue He Didn't Read

*Locked canon, Batch 256, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1166`). Industrial Myth Alias Chronicle LXXXII, wave 28. The method's first language-barrier case, worked through phonetic recording and independent bilingual verification -- a domain untouched by any prior entry.*

---

The dye-works district sat close enough to the old border that half its workforce had come from beyond it two generations back and still spoke their own tongue at home, in the yard, and — critically for Ezio's purposes — in every testimony they gave, since the district's own written records were kept exclusively in the Trust's administrative language by clerks who'd never learned to read the workers' own script at all.

Ezio didn't speak it either. His first instinct, take the testimony as best he could and hope enough overlapped with the little he understood, would have produced a case built on guesswork dressed up as documentation, and he said so to Kanja before he'd write down a single line that way.

What they did instead was slower and, Ezio admitted afterward, better for the slowness. A young worker who'd grown up bilingual, fluent in both the district's home tongue and the Trust's administrative language from years running errands between the two, agreed to sit through every testimony as an interpreter — not translating loosely for speed, but rendering each worker's account phonetically first, word for word as spoken, before rendering it again in full sense afterward, so that anyone checking the record later could verify the second version actually matched the first rather than trusting a single pass of memory and interpretation.

It doubled the time every testimony took. Ezio insisted on it anyway, and insisted further on a second, independent bilingual worker — found three days in, a retired dockhand with no stake in the outcome — cross-checking a sample of the first interpreter's phonetic transcriptions against her own memory of the same conversations, catching two small discrepancies that turned out to matter: a number the first interpreter had rendered as an estimate where the original testimony had actually given an exact figure, the kind of small softening an interpreter working fast might not even notice doing.

"We could've gone faster trusting one interpreter," Ezio said, once the finding was built.

"Faster wasn't the risk. Getting someone's exact words turned into someone else's approximate meaning was." The administrator's shortfall, once fully documented, was substantial and undisputed — he'd relied for years on the language gap itself as a kind of protection, correctly betting that few outside auditors would ever take the time to verify testimony he knew most officials couldn't read without help. The finding, delivered in both languages side by side at Kanja's insistence, closed that particular protection for good: every worker in the district could now read, in their own script, in their own words as they'd actually spoken them, exactly what had been found and why.
