# What the Trust Wrote Into the Manual

*Locked canon, Batch 250, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1088`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LXIII, wave 21, closing the wave. The Sovereign Trust formally codifies the storm-timing doctrine into official naval regulation, crediting it to the school rather than to any one name -- the sub-series' first entry showing the doctrine outlive personal reputation as written law rather than only living memory. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

The document that reached the school was three pages long, dense with the particular flatness of Trust
naval prose, and it took the third student longer to find her own institution's name in it than it had
taken the liaison to read her ledger on the dock two seasons before.

*Storm-interval verification,* the new regulation called it, *to be consulted before any fleet movement
inside a fifty-league radius of a certified reading station, per the attached protocol.* No mention of
Sephtis. No mention of the successor. No mention of the student who had signed the report the regulation
was built from, and no mention of Kanja at all, in any of his names.

"They took it," the student said, not quite sure yet whether she meant it as a complaint.

"They didn't take it," the retired successor said, reading over her shoulder. "They kept it. There's a
difference. A name can die with the person who carried it. A law in a fleet manual gets copied into the
next one, and the next, whether anyone remembers who taught the first captain to read a sky or not."

Kanja, present as he so often was for this alias without ever once being asked to be, thought of how little
of his own name appeared anywhere that mattered most -- Bane unclaimed in a parley tent, the Trench Monarch
worn by a man who'd never sanctioned it, a title his own crew had given him that no Directorate file had
ever recorded. He had spent most of a very long life learning that the work outlasting the worker's name
was not a loss. It was, if anything, the only kind of winning that held.

"Sephtis would have hated the prose," he said, of the document.

"Sephtis would have read it twice, complained about the prose, and then made every captain under him
memorize it anyway," the successor said. "That was the whole method. Complain, and comply, and teach the
next one to do both."

The student read the regulation a third time, more slowly, until she found the one line that mattered to
her -- not a name, but a requirement: that any officer disputing a certified reading was obligated to
request the reader's full record before overriding the call, not merely their rank or their years. Written
now, permanently, where no single skeptical liaison could ever again make her prove it from scratch.

She folded the document and set it beside the ledger the successor had handed her a season ago, two
different kinds of inheritance on the same table.

"Three generations," she said, "and now it doesn't even need us in the room."

"It never did, once it was true enough," the successor said. "That was always where this was going. We
were only ever the part that had to be believed until the part that didn't need believing caught up."
