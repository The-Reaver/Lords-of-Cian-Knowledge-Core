# The Convoy That Wouldn't Stop Moving

*Locked canon, Batch 136, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-455`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle VII, first
entry in the third wave. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign. Not a territory
Chronicle. New standalone material — a detailed armor-and-weapon combat showcase per Abad's craft
instruction. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The Anvil rarely stayed anchored long enough to make a fixed target, and the Directorate had finally
adjusted its own tactics accordingly — striking not at a settled forge but at the mobile convoy
itself, mid-relocation between sites, when the moving forge's own defenses were spread thin across a
strung-out column of wagons rather than concentrated around a single stationary position.

Kanja took the rear of the column personally the moment the ambush began.

Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System absorbed the opening volley aimed at the rearmost wagons, converting
impact into stored charge while he moved forward along the column's exposed flank faster than the
ambushing force's own repositioning could track him. Obsidian Malice discharged twice in close
succession — once to collapse a felled-tree roadblock meant to stall the convoy's lead wagons, once
to break the ambush's own flanking cavalry formation before it could complete its encirclement — each
strike timed to keep the column moving rather than stopping it, since a stopped convoy was a
vulnerable convoy in a way a moving one simply wasn't.

Onyx of Oblivion cleared the wagons entirely, Whisper of Shadows carrying him the length of the
column in the time it took the ambushing commander to realize his own force was being disassembled
from the inside rather than repelled from without. Cadence Ruin broke the ambush's coordination into
isolated skirmishes his own convoy guards could handle without him, freeing Kanja to return to the
column's most exposed point — the mobile forge itself, where two apprentices had kept the bellows
running the entire engagement rather than abandon a half-finished casting mid-pour.

"You kept working through an ambush," Kanja said, arriving at the forge wagon to find the pour
already complete, cooling in its mold despite the chaos around it.

"You've never once stopped for an interruption when the metal was ready. We figured you wouldn't want
us stopping either." The younger apprentice didn't look up from banking the forge fire properly before
the wagon resumed its roll. "Besides. A half-finished pour's wasted metal. Wasted metal's worse than
a little danger, this deep into the campaign."

The convoy reached its next site by nightfall, forge fire still lit, the ambush broken and scattered
behind it.
