# What the Smoke Said

*Locked canon, Batch 284, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1500`). Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XCVIII, wave 33.
Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241). Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in
neutral third-person prose.*

---

Every forge on the tour already burned something. Callum Breck's idea, once he'd finally said it out
loud after turning it over for the better part of a week, was that a fire nobody was hiding had no
reason not to be read.

"We've got twelve sites running at once some weeks," he said, laying a rough map across the counting
table with each forge chimney marked. "Word of trouble at any one of them takes a rider half a day to
reach the next. By the time it does, the trouble's usually already decided itself one way or the
other." What he proposed instead used the forges' own chimneys — banked, opened, banked again in
patterns too plain to mistake for an accident and too crude for anyone outside the campaign to bother
decoding, since nobody outside it knew there was anything to decode.

Building the code took longer than building any single tool the campaign had made. Kanja insisted on
patterns simple enough that a frightened fourteen-year-old apprentice, alone on a night watch, could
send one correctly without hesitating over which puff meant what: three short releases for a raid in
progress, one long one for a wound needing more than the site's own hands could manage, a slow
repeating pulse for nothing wrong at all, sent every hour specifically so its sudden absence would
mean something on its own.

It went live across nine linked sites on a morning nobody expected to test it, and it was tested
within the week regardless. A site two valleys over stopped sending its hourly pulse mid-morning, no
raid signal ahead of it, nothing but silence where a signal should have been. Kanja reached it inside
two hours instead of the half-day a rider would have needed, in time to find a support collapse had
buried three apprentices under rubble with no fire left standing to send any signal at all — the
absence itself the only warning the system had been built to carry, and the one it carried loudest.

"You built a code for trouble announcing itself," Breck said afterward, watching the rescued
apprentices carried clear. "It just saved three lives by going quiet instead."

"That's the part I didn't think to plan for," Kanja admitted. "You did." He had the silent-pulse
protocol written into the code formally that same night, credited in the campaign ledger the way
every genuine improvement to the method was credited — to the name of whoever had actually seen it,
not to his own.
