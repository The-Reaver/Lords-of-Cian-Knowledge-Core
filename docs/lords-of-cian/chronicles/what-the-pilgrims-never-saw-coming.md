# What the Pilgrims Never Saw Coming

*Locked canon, Batch 262, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1332`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LXXXVI, wave
29. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241), a settlement's annual pilgrimage
festival. A detailed, battle-intense Trinity combat showcase protecting a dense civilian crowd. Not
a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The pilgrimage brought four thousand people through a settlement that normally held four hundred,
a once-a-year procession to a coastal shrine that had nothing to do with the campaign at all until
a Directorate strike planner decided the crowd itself was the opportunity — cover dense enough to
hide an approach, and a target too large and too distracted to organize any real defense of its
own before the first strike landed.

Kanja felt the wrongness of the crowd's rhythm before he understood its cause, Onyx of Oblivion's
Cadence Ruin picking out a hard, purposeful thread of movement cutting against four thousand
unhurried, ceremonial footsteps — soldiers moving through a pilgrimage the way water moves through
a crowd that doesn't know to part for it yet.

"This isn't a fight I can win by being fast," he said, feeling the scale of the problem settle
over him. "Fast in a crowd this size gets someone trampled before a single blow lands. I need
them contained, not just beaten."

He fought differently than any prior showcase because the crowd itself demanded it — Mafesto's
Kinetic Transfer System used less for absorbing or redirecting force and more for physically
shepherding panicked bodies clear of a widening fight, a hand on a shoulder here, a shoulder turned
to block a stampede's start there, defense of people who had no idea what was actually happening
around them taking precedence over the fight's own tempo. Obsidian Malice's discharge ran in tight,
narrow bands rather than open pulses, cutting lanes through the crowd's own density that soldiers
couldn't cross without exposing themselves, while Onyx of Oblivion's Whisper of Shadows moved
through gaps between bodies too tight for any soldier in full gear to follow.

It took longer than a fight this size against a force this modest should have taken, precisely
because every choice Kanja made cost speed in exchange for making sure four thousand pilgrims left
the shrine that day having barely understood anything dangerous had happened around them at all.
The strike force was contained, disarmed, and marched off well before the procession's own
ceremony reached its close, most of the crowd learning what had actually occurred only afterward,
secondhand, from settlement guards rather than from anything they'd witnessed themselves.

"You could have ended that faster," the settlement's own procession-master said, once the shrine
rites had finished undisturbed. "Nobody would have blamed you for it."

"Faster wasn't the actual job today," Kanja said. "Nobody getting hurt was."
