# What the Fifth Generation Chose to Notice

*Locked canon, Batch 282, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1483`). Crow King Alias Chronicle XCIX, wave 33, closing
it. The fifth generation's first independent field test reveals a specialty built on neither sound nor
numbers. Not a territory Chronicle.*

---

He didn't sing, and he didn't falsify a single ledger entry. When the fourth generation asked him
afterward how he'd done it, the runner's son seemed almost embarrassed by how little there was to
explain.

He'd been sent to confirm a rumor -- a quartermaster suspected of skimming grain meant for a besieged
town -- and told to report back before anyone decided what, if anything, the craft should do about it.
Instead of listening for a lie in the man's voice the way the apprentice would have, or auditing his
paperwork the way the fourth generation would have, the fifth generation had simply watched him for
two days: which hand he reached for a ledger with when he thought no one important was looking, how
long he held eye contact when the numbers came up versus when they didn't, the half-second delay
before he answered any question about the grain that wasn't there when he answered questions about
anything else.

"He wasn't lying with his voice," the fifth generation reported. "He was lying with everything except
his voice. His voice was the only part of him telling the truth the whole time, because he'd
rehearsed exactly what to say. Nobody rehearses their own hands."

The apprentice and the fourth generation looked at each other the way people look at each other when
they've just watched the same trick land twice from two completely different directions.

"That's not what either of us does," the fourth generation said, not as a complaint.

"It never had to be," the apprentice said. "I read a voice. You read a page. He reads a person
standing in front of him not saying anything at all." She looked back at the fifth generation. "You
found the same door we've always found. You just walked up to it from a side none of us thought to
check."

When Kanja heard the report, he said only that this was the clearest proof yet of something he'd told
the fourth generation once and only half-believed himself at the time: that the craft was never
built around any one sense, or any one gift, or any one kind of mind. It was built around a single
instruction simple enough that four completely different people had now each found their own way to
follow it.
