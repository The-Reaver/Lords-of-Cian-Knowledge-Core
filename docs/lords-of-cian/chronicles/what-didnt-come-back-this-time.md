# What Didn't Come Back This Time

*Locked canon, Batch 159, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-500`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XIII, first
entry in the fourth wave. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign. Not a territory
Chronicle. New standalone material — the first genuine limit of "metabolizes punishment" in this
alias's run. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The third punitive burning in as many months finally outpaced what the campaign's stretched salvage
network could answer — a settlement forge reduced to slag with no reclaimable scrap within reach, no
marginal ore left unclaimed by the two prior rebuilds, the embargo-era resourcefulness that had once
turned scarcity into a source of strength finally running against a scarcity it genuinely couldn't
metabolize.

The forge didn't come back.

"We've rebuilt worse than this," the settlement's own smith said, watching Kanja survey the wreckage
with none of the usual certainty that preceded a rebuild. "What's different this time?"

"What's different is I've run out of the things that made the prior rebuilds possible. The scrap's
gone. The marginal ore's gone. I could bring in material from further afield, but that costs time this
settlement doesn't have before winter, and I won't pretend otherwise to make either of us feel
better about it." Kanja said it plainly, no attempt to spin the outcome into another victory. "I've
built a reputation on metabolizing everything that's been thrown at this campaign. I think I need to
be honest that the reputation has a floor, and we've finally found it."

"So what happens to us?"

"You relocate, for now — to a settlement with an intact forge, until this one can be properly rebuilt
once the campaign has resources to spare again. That's not nothing. It's also not the clean victory
every other burning's aftermath has been." He didn't dress the loss up as anything but what it was.
"I'd rather tell you honestly that this one cost us something real than pretend I found another way
to metabolize it. I didn't. Some punishments genuinely outpace what any single method can answer,
however resourceful. That's worth remembering before the next one, not after."

The settlement relocated within the week, the forge's rebuilding deferred rather than achieved — the
first genuine, unresolved loss the alias's entire ethos had encountered, held honestly rather than
folded into the pattern of prior successes.
