# The One Who Didn't Want Freedom

*Locked canon, Batch 156, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-491`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle X, first entry in the
fourth wave. Long Mask era, age ~110, the Golden Terror period. Not a territory Chronicle. New
standalone material — a moral-complexity entry testing the limits of the alias's rescue pattern.
Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The indentured works ran on a legitimate, if harsh, debt-bondage contract rather than outright
slavery — years of labor traded against a real debt, terms harder than fair but not the manifest-
fiction cruelty the Scourge usually broke apart without hesitation. He boarded expecting the same
clean liberation he'd delivered at the Salt Keep and a dozen sites like it.

One worker refused to leave.

"I've got four years left on this debt, and a wife and two children on the mainland this contract is
paying to keep fed while I work it off," the man said, plainly unintimidated by the reputation
standing in front of him. "Free me today, and I've got no debt paid, no wage owed, and a family that
starves waiting for whatever comes next. I didn't ask to be rescued from a life I chose with my eyes
open."

The Scourge hadn't accounted for this. "The terms of the contract are harsh enough to look like
bondage from outside it."

"Harsh isn't the same as unchosen. I signed it knowing exactly what it cost, because the alternative
cost my family more." The man held his ground, respectful but unmoved. "You've built a reputation on
freeing people who never agreed to what was done to them. I agreed to this. Take that away without
asking first, and you're not freeing me. You're deciding for me, the same as every other authority
that's ever assumed it knew better than the person actually living the choice."

The Scourge left the man's contract untouched, taking instead only the operation's genuinely coerced
captives, a smaller liberation than intended but a more honest one.

"I nearly made a mistake today," he told the crew afterward, unusually reflective. "I've spent decades
assuming every bonded worker wanted exactly what I was offering. Today someone with more courage than
most told me plainly that wasn't true, and made me actually listen instead of simply acting on
instinct."

"Does that change how you approach the next one?"

"It changes that I ask, now, before I assume." He set the ledger of the day's smaller, more honest
count aside. "The whole point was always freeing people from what was taken from them without
consent. I nearly took something from a man who'd given his consent freely, because it looked the
same from outside. That's a mistake worth remembering."
