# The Site That Burned Twice

*[DRAFT — awaiting batch/rule-ID assignment]. The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle XLI, wave 13.
Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign. A detailed showcase compressing the alias's core
mechanic across two punitive strikes in one season. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral
third-person prose. No new named characters.*

---

The garrison commander responsible for the district had watched the first burning fail to accomplish
anything lasting — an eleven-day rebuild, the crew gone within the month, the district standing exactly
as it had before — and concluded, wrongly but not unreasonably, that a second strike, launched before
the campaign could fully withdraw its attention elsewhere, might succeed where the first hadn't.

He burned the same quarter a second time, six weeks after the first, while the campaign's crew was
still finishing peripheral work nearby rather than fully departed.

Kanja arrived within hours rather than days, the crew's own continued presence in the district cutting
the response time the first burning had never had the benefit of. The rebuild that followed drew on
lessons the first one hadn't needed — bracing techniques proven against the earlier reheat failure,
material staging refined across a dozen sites since, a crew that now included district residents who
had rebuilt once already and needed no retraining to do it again.

The second rebuild finished in seven days — four faster than the first.

"He thought hitting us again, faster, before we'd finished elsewhere, would catch us thinner than the
first time," the district's own foreman said, watching the second rebuild's final beams go up faster
than anyone had thought possible. "Instead he just proved we're faster the second time than the first."

"That's not an accident. That's the actual mechanism." Kanja checked the new framework against the
old, the district's second rebuild already indistinguishable from ground that had never burned at all.
"Every burning teaches this district something the last one didn't. Every rebuild banks that lesson
somewhere it doesn't go away again. He didn't find the district's weak point by hitting it twice. He
just proved it doesn't have one that punishment alone can reach."

The garrison commander did not attempt a third strike. His own report, filed afterward, would note
that the district's second rebuild had outpaced its first by nearly forty percent — a figure his
superiors read, correctly, as confirmation that repetition itself had become the district's own
weapon.
