# Lauris Chronicle LXX: Everyone She Had Time Left to See

*Locked canon, Batch 300, 2026-09-18 (`MCD-1687`). Seventieth entry in Lauris Letitia's own
Chronicle series, the thirteenth entry of Strand K. Set across the final months before departure, age
~3,999, in the interval `MCD-174` establishes between Selene's death (Chronicle LXIX, `MCD-1686`) and
the departure itself. Dramatizes her farewell circuit of her cohort at Vask Threnarr and her
instructors at Vask Karth-Ven, closing on a final private visit to Karth-Ven's central training floor
-- the same floor Chronicle XX (`MCD-1637`) showed failing to measure her -- before she leaves the Vask
for the last time. Reuses Veska Karth-Ven, Tiramen Karth-Ven, and Voreth Karth-Ven, all already
locked. Introduces one new minor named character, Rassa (collision-checked clean, zero prior hits), a
surviving member of Lauris's original 47-child cohort (`MCD-165`), for a single farewell scene. No
contradictions.*

-----

*Archive fragment, Vask Threnarr, age ~3,999: "There are forty-six of them left who remember being
children with me, though none of us remember it quite the same way. I have decided to see as many as
I can before I go. I do not think I will manage all of them. I have decided that is acceptable."*

I want to open this entry by noting something Lauris's own archive never states outright but which
becomes unmistakable once one reads the whole of this final year against everything that precedes
it: this is the only sustained period, in over three thousand nine hundred years of recorded life,
in which she deliberately set her own schedule around other people's convenience rather than the
reverse. She had, by this point, spent centuries being summoned wherever Kares Prime needed her.
For the span of this final year, she summoned herself, one Vask and one name at a time, to say what
she plainly understood might be the last thing she would ever say to each of them.

She did not manage all forty-six. Her own accounting, kept with the same discipline she brings to
every ledger, records that she reached thirty-one in person, corresponded briefly with nine more
whose own duties kept them from Threnarr that year, and left the remaining six without contact
beyond a single shared entry in the Hold's own departure record, which she asked be read to them
whenever they next returned. I do not believe this failure troubled her the way I once assumed it
would. She writes, of the six she could not reach: *I would have liked to see them. I do not think
either of us would have known quite what to say that the record cannot already say for us. We were
children together once. That remains true whether or not I see them again before I leave.*

*

The farewell I have chosen to preserve in full belongs to Rassa, one of the thirty-one, a stoneworker
at Threnarr who had shared Lauris's earliest cohort classes under Olda before either of them knew
what either would become. Their exchange survives because Rassa herself, unusually, kept a private
record of it and later added it to the Hold's own archive — the only farewell from this whole
circuit preserved in a second hand rather than Lauris's alone, which I have come to treasure for
precisely that reason: it lets me see her, for once, as someone else in the room saw her, rather than
only as she saw herself.

Rassa's own account records asking her, plainly, whether she was frightened. Lauris's answer, by
Rassa's transcription: *I do not think frightened is the right word for what I feel. I think it is
closer to what the training floor felt, the day it could no longer read me. I am going somewhere
nothing here can calibrate for. I find that I am curious what it will read instead.* Rassa's own
closing note, appended to the exchange rather than part of it, is the detail I return to most: *She
held my hand before she left. She had never done that before, in all the years I knew her as a
child. I do not think she knew how much that would matter to me. I have decided to tell her, in this
record, in case she never learns it any other way.*

*

Her final visit to Vask Karth-Ven came in the last month before departure, and I have chosen to close
this entry there rather than at Threnarr, because I believe it is where the truest shape of her
farewell circuit reveals itself. Veska, Tiramen, and Voreth — the three instructors who had, alongside
her original Threnarr Sister-Hold, built the eight-member household that raised her from age fourteen
— met her together in the same training hall where her earliest sessions had been conducted, nearly
three thousand nine hundred and eighty-six years before.

Tiramen, who had spent eighty years of her own life building the Karth-Sera specifically for a
student the existing curriculum could not contain, said the least of the three and, by Lauris's own
account, the thing she has returned to most often in the years since: *I built a discipline for a
girl I did not fully understand. I am glad, now, that I never finished understanding you. I think
that is what let you outgrow it as thoroughly as you have.* Voreth, closest to her in relative age
and her first sparring partner, said only that he expected to still be training students on this same
floor when word of whatever she became eventually reached them, and that he intended to tell every one
of them her name. Veska, who had carried her request to the Iron-Speakers three hundred and eighty
years earlier and waited out the whole of that deliberation beside her, said the most, and I have
chosen it as the entry's true closing line, because I believe it is the one Lauris herself carried
furthest: *I do not think this Vask, or this floor, or any of us standing on it, taught you everything
you are. I think we simply had the good fortune to be standing near you while you became it. Go and
find out what the rest of the world has to teach you. We will still be here, continuing, the way you
taught us that word ought to mean.*

*

She stayed alone on the training floor after they had gone, by her own brief account, for perhaps an
hour — the same sixty meters of continuously calibrated Drakma that had, twenty years before her
own arrival there, begun failing to measure her, and had never fully recovered the capacity to. She
does not record what she thought, standing on it a final time. She records only that she left it
before dawn, walked the mountain passage out of Karth-Ven without looking back a second time, and
did not return to it again before the day, some weeks later, that she departed Kares Prime for good.
