# The Collapse They Meant to Cause

*Locked canon, Batch 249, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1083`). The Lord of Embers Alias Chronicle LXI, first
entry in the twenty-first wave. Rebellion era, age 27, the Rolling Foundry Campaign (MCD-241),
one of the marginal reopened ore drifts brought back into use during the embargo (MCD-456). A
detailed, battle-intense Trinity combat showcase fought on two fronts at once. Not a territory
Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The charge wasn't meant to kill anyone. That was the part the demolitions specialist was proudest
of, reading it back afterward: a single timed placement against the drift's second support beam,
calculated to seal eleven apprentices into a dead-end gallery without touching them at all,
followed forty minutes later — once the floor's full attention was necessarily underground — by a
second team hitting the surface terrace where the ore came up to be weighed.

The beam went first, a low grinding crack apprentices belowground felt through their boots before
they heard it. Rock dust rolled down the gallery in a slow gray wave, and the drift's own echo
turned a controlled partial collapse into eleven people sealed behind forty feet of shifted stone
with the air already starting to thin.

Kanja was down the shaft before the dust finished settling, one hand already flat against a
straining prop timber. Mafesto's Kinetic Transfer System didn't absorb the load so much as
redirect it — the exoskeleton grounding the beam's shifting weight through his own frame and out
into the drift floor, buying the timber minutes it didn't have on its own rather than pulling the
weight into stored charge the way it would against a blow. "Grounding, not swallowing it whole,"
he said to no one, feeling for the difference the way he'd feel for a hot spot in an unfamiliar
forge — the mechanic he'd built the coat's whole vocabulary around, applied now to holding a
mountain still instead of a punch.

Onyx of Oblivion went ahead of him into the dark, Cadence Ruin reading the rock itself for
rhythm — not sound so much as the faint, arrhythmic disturbance eleven frightened heartbeats made
against stone that otherwise held nothing but its own slow settling. It found them in under a
minute, sealed behind the worst of the fall rather than under it. Obsidian Malice went into the
rubble face next, not as a blunt discharge but a series of precisely placed pulses timed to the
gallery's own resonant frequency, shearing the choke point apart in controlled sections rather
than bringing down what was left of the ceiling.

The gallery opened onto eleven apprentices standing in a tight, frightened knot around a single
lantern, uninjured and already forming a bucket line for the last of the rubble before Kanja
finished clearing the gap — nobody had told them to.

He was back on the surface in time for the second team, who had planned their assault around a
floor they expected to still be underground managing a crisis they'd caused on purpose. Onyx's
Whisper of Shadows closed the distance before the ambush's own lookout finished raising the alarm,
and Veil Piercer found the seams in armor built to survive a fight against a distracted, divided
opponent rather than one who'd already solved the first half of the problem in under fifteen
minutes.

The specialist, taken at the tree line with the second timer still unset in his pack, had built
his whole plan on a true premise — that a man managing a cave-in couldn't also be managing an
ambush — and simply hadn't accounted for how fast the first half of that math could be finished.

"You weren't wrong that I'd have to choose," Kanja told him, no performance in it. "You were wrong
about how long the choosing would take."

*Continuity notes (not narrative): the first Lord of Embers entry set underground, and the first
to show the alias defending two simultaneous, geographically separated fronts (a sabotaged mine
collapse and a surface ambush timed to exploit it) with the full three-piece Trinity split
deliberately between them — Mafesto's grounding/conductance function (MCD-291) applied to
structural load-bearing rather than absorbing a blow, Obsidian Malice used for precision
controlled demolition rather than a discharge into an enemy line, and Onyx of Oblivion's Cadence
Ruin used for the first time to locate people by rhythm through stone rather than against an
opponent, followed by Whisper of Shadows and Veil Piercer resolving the ambush. Distinct from the
Trench Monarch's own established mine-rescue precedent (MCD-636, Veil Piercer freeing miners from
natural collapse) — this collapse is deliberately enemy-caused, paired with a coordinated surface
attack, and resolved through split-front timing rather than a single rescue act. No new named
characters; the demolitions specialist is unnamed and one-scene, matching established convention.
First entry in the Lord of Embers' twenty-first three-Chronicle wave.*
