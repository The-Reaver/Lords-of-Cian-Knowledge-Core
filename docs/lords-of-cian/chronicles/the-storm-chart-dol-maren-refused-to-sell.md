# The Storm-Chart Dol Maren Refused to Sell

*Locked canon, Batch 258, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1218`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
LXXX, wave 27. Dol Maren declines a fortune to keep weather-reading knowledge free. Not a territory
Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The merchant house's representative found Dol Maren ashore rather than aboard ship, having tracked
down the fleet's shipwright specifically rather than approach Kanja or Garren Hask, correctly guessing
the man who'd once given away real weather-reading methods to a village of fisherfolk for free might
be the easiest point of entry for what he had in mind.

"Your storm-reading, your hull technique — we've had three separate captains describe encounters
where your ships weathered conditions that should have sunk them," the representative said, laying out
a genuinely generous offer: exclusive licensing rights, a standing annual payment, enough coin over a
decade to refit every ship the fleet would ever need. "We'd pay handsomely to have that knowledge
formalized, taught to our own house captains under contract."

Dol Maren turned it down before the man had finished laying out the terms, and did it without the
anger the representative had plainly braced for. "I gave the same method to a fishing village for
nothing, because knowing how to read a storm shouldn't depend on who can afford to buy it. Selling it
to your house exclusively would mean every captain who can't pay your fee still drowns in weather your
captains sail through safely. That's not a trade I'll make."

"We could offer more."

"It's not about the price," Maren said. "It's about who gets left out once there's a price at all."

He offered instead what he'd apparently been considering before the representative ever arrived — an
open method, written plainly enough for any working shipwright to learn, distributed freely through
the same trade networks Garren Hask's ledger already touched, no house holding exclusive rights over
it. The representative left without the contract he'd come for, and Dol Maren spent the following
weeks doing the actual work: writing the flexible-hull principle and the weather-reading method out in
full, in language any dockyard apprentice could follow, and giving copies to every port the fleet
passed through afterward.

"You just gave away a fortune," Efa Gol pointed out, watching him work.

"I gave away the thing that keeps people alive," Maren said. "The fortune was never really mine to
sell."
