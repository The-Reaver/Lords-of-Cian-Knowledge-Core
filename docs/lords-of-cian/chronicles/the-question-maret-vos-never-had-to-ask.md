# The Question Maret Vos Never Had to Ask

*Locked canon, Batch 223, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1013`). The Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle LVII, wave 19. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Maret Vos had been running shoring rigs on the western gallery for eleven straight days by the time
the question finally came out, apparently sat on since the siege began.

"Every other crew chief I've worked under tells you what to build and expects you to build it." Maret
Vos watched Kanja read a support column's load-bearing capacity by hand before committing a single beam
to a brace he could have simply ordered installed. "You keep asking the ground what it'll actually
hold before you tell anyone what to build at all. Slower way to run a siege. Why do it that way?"

"Because the ground doesn't care what I ordered. It only cares what's actually true about it, and if I
skip asking that question to save time, the ground answers it for me later, usually at the worst
possible moment to find out I guessed wrong." Kanja finished the read and nodded the brace forward, the
column holding exactly as he'd found it would. "I've seen what happens when someone builds from what
they expect to be true instead of what they checked. I'd rather be the slower crew chief than the one
explaining a collapse afterward."

Maret Vos considered that for the length of the lift, then offered something back — not a challenge to
the method, but an addition to it, drawn from years spent freed and working since the Black Trench,
watching more than one confident method eventually meet ground that didn't cooperate with it. "I've
watched engineers who thought asking the ground a question meant it owed them an honest answer. It
doesn't. Sometimes a column reads sound and fails anyway. Sometimes it reads compromised and holds
another twenty years out of spite. You can ask the right question and still get an answer that lies to
you."

"That's fair. I've had that happen too." Kanja hadn't considered it from quite that angle before —
not that his method was wrong, but that it wasn't as complete an answer as he sometimes treated it as
being. "So what do you do with that? Stop asking?"

"No. You ask anyway, because asking wrong is worse than asking and still getting fooled sometimes. But
you build in slack for the times the ground lies to you — a second support the calculations say you
don't need, margin the honest read didn't call for." Maret Vos nodded toward the column Kanja had just
cleared, itself carrying more redundant bracing than his own load figures strictly required. "You're
already doing it. Just didn't have the words for why yet."

It was, Kanja realized, watching the brace go in without incident, the first time in eleven days
anyone on this crew had improved his own method rather than simply trusted it — not a rival engineer's
challenge, not a crew chief's order, but a fellow survivor's hard-won caution folded quietly into a
system he'd have kept treating as complete otherwise.

"I've been asking the ground the right questions this whole siege," he said, once the shaft's work was
done for the day. "I don't think I'd thought as carefully as I should have about what happens when it
answers me honestly and still gets it wrong."

"Most men who build things as well as you do never get told that. They just get complimented until
something fails and it's too late to hear it." Maret Vos began securing the rig for the night, already
thinking about the next lift. "You asked me a real question back, though, instead of just thanking me
for the thought. That's rarer than the redundant bracing."
