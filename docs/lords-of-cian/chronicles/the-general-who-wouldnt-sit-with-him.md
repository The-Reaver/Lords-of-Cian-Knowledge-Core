# The General Who Wouldn't Sit With Him

*Locked canon, Batch 254, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1095`). Bane Alias Chronicle LXV, wave 22. A principled
ideological rival refuses to coordinate with Bane's methods, and the disagreement is never resolved
-- both approaches succeed independently, side by side, in the same district and the same week.*

---

The commander of the district's other partisan network sent word through a neutral courier before
Bane's column had even finished making camp: she knew he was there, she wasn't going to fight beside
him, and she wanted that stated plainly before either of them did anything that might look like an
alliance neither of them intended.

Bane went to her camp alone, unarmed but for what Onyx never truly left him without, and sat across a
cold fire from a woman who did not offer him tea and did not apologize for it.

"Your name means force," she said. "Mine means the opposite. I've spent six years teaching people
that the moment you pick up what he's carrying" — she didn't gesture at Onyx, but they both knew what
she meant — "you've already lost the argument you were making by refusing to pick it up in the first
place. I won't have my people confused about which one of us they're following."

"You don't have to have them confused. Work the district your way. I'll work it mine."

"Together we'd take the garrison in half the time."

"Maybe. And every person who joined you because you never once asked them to hold a weapon would
learn that the promise had an asterisk on it. I'm not interested in costing you that." He stood.
"I'm not here to convince you I'm right. I already know we disagree, and I don't think either of us
is going to talk the other one out of it tonight."

She didn't offer a truce, and he didn't ask for one. They ran the district in the same week without a
single shared plan between them: Bane's column took the garrison by force in four days, freed sixty
captives, and moved on. Her network organized a total boycott of the garrison's supply contracts that
starved its resupply lines dry over the following month, without a blow struck on either side, and
converted forty local families to open resistance who'd never have joined a column carrying weapons
through their fields.

Corren Halst asked him afterward, watching the smoke from the taken garrison settle, whether it
bothered him that she'd never thank him for the opening his own raid gave her boycott room to work.

"She's not wrong about the asterisk," Bane said. "I'd rather she keep doing what she does without
owing me anything for it. The district's better off with both of us in it disagreeing than it would
be with either of us alone."
