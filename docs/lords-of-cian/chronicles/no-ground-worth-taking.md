# No Ground Worth Taking

*Locked canon, Batch 113, 2026-09-10 (`MCD-386`). Iron Bastard Alias Chronicle I. Rebellion era, a
new solo stand distinct from the original Iron Bastard's Stand (`MCD-238`, age 25). Not a territory
Chronicle. New standalone material. Narrated in neutral third-person prose. No new named
characters.*

---

The second column came prepared for terrain tricks, having studied the flooded canals and collapsed
ravines that had swallowed every other engagement the Directorate's files described. They chose open
salt-pan for the confrontation specifically because nothing could be flooded, collapsed, or hidden
across forty square miles of hard-baked flat, and because their own commanding general had staked
his reputation on proving that the rebel commander's tactics only worked where the ground cooperated.

Kanja walked out onto the salt-pan alone, unhurried, and made no attempt to find cover that did not
exist.

"No ravine this time," the general said over the open channel, close enough now to be heard without
amplification. "No canal. No trick of ground left to hide behind. You picked a fight you can't win
on terms you don't control."

"I didn't pick the ground. You did." He kept walking, closing the distance rather than holding it,
which unsettled the column's forward officers more than retreat would have. "You've spent this whole
war assuming my advantage was always the terrain. It never was. The terrain just made the actual
advantage easier to hide. Out here, with nothing left to hide behind, you're about to find out what
the real one always was."

The general did not have an answer ready for that, because the report he'd built his confidence on
had never actually distinguished between the two. What the column discovered, over the ninety minutes
that followed, was that an advantage built on structural vulnerability rather than geography traveled
with the man carrying it regardless of what ground he stood on — the salt-pan offered no cover,
because none had ever been the point.

By the time the column withdrew, having gained no ground and lost the confidence that had brought
them to open terrain in the first place, the general understood something his own report had failed
to capture: Kanja had never fought for territory in any engagement the Directorate had on file. He
fought until the fight itself became structurally impossible to continue, on whatever ground happened
to be under his feet when that moment arrived, and the salt-pan's total absence of cover had simply
made that truth impossible to misread as anything else.

"There was no ground worth taking," the general admitted, in the report he filed afterward, the one
that ended his career for what his superiors read as an admission of tactical failure rather than
the accurate account it actually was. "I gave him open terrain because I thought it would strip his
advantage bare. It did. It just turned out the advantage was never the terrain to begin with."
