# The Signal They Almost Answered

*Locked canon, Batch 258, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1214`). Sovereign Ghost of the Great Sea Alias Chronicle
LXXVI, wave 26, first entry in the wave. A growth/adaptation callback to the Fleet-Marshal's staged
distress-call ambush. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The distress flare went up an hour before dusk, three miles off a heading the fleet had no particular
reason to be watching, and for the length of a single held breath aboard *The Audit*, nobody moved to
answer it.

That pause was new. Years back, before the Fleet-Marshal had rigged an answered distress call into the
fleet's first real ambush, the flare would have had ships turning toward it inside a minute. Now Efa
Gol simply watched, glass to her eye, counting things that shouldn't have taken counting if this were
genuine — no listing to the hull, no visible smoke, a crew moving with a discipline no panicked ship's
crew ever managed.

"Could be nothing," Danne Sok said, chart already out, cross-referencing the position against known
shipping lanes. "Could be exactly what it looks like."

Kanja didn't order the fleet toward it and didn't order it away either. He sent a single fast launch,
Pell Ostra aboard for exactly the reason her eye for rigged constructions made her the right person to
look closely before anyone closer, and held the flagships back at a distance that let them respond
fast if it was real and stay clear if it wasn't.

It was neither, in the end — not the Fleet-Marshal's old trap reborn, but a common raiding crew who'd
heard enough ghost-fleet legend to try borrowing the tactic themselves, counting on reputation and
instinct to pull a rescue-minded ship into range. Pell Ostra spotted the towline rigged beneath the
"distressed" hull's waterline from thirty yards out and turned the launch before it ever closed the
distance, no shots fired, no trap sprung, the whole attempt wasted the moment it became clear the
fleet wasn't answering blind anymore.

"They learned the wrong lesson from what happened to us," Efa Gol said afterward, watching the raiding
crew's ship give up and turn away rather than press an ambush with no target left to spring it on.
"Rigged distress calls work because people don't check. We check now."

"We check because it cost us something to learn we had to," Kanja said. "That's the only good that
came out of what the Marshal did to us. We don't forget it."
