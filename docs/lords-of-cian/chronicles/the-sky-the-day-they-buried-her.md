# The Sky the Day They Buried Her

*Locked canon, Batch 263, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1356`). Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle LXXXIII, wave 28. The retired successor dies peacefully; the senior third-generation student, having done this once before for Sephtis, makes the burial-rite call herself, this time without the hesitation of a first time.*

---

She died the way she'd lived the last several years of her life -- quietly, without asking for anything she hadn't already earned the right to ask for, in her sleep, in the small harbor-side room she'd kept since her retirement, the running ledger she'd handed over years earlier close enough that the student, finding her, understood she must have been reading it again the night before.

The sea-burial rites required a clear reading the same way they had for Sephtis -- a window of calm water and settled sky in which to carry her out and commit her properly, a call that fell, by the school's own tradition, to whoever held the doctrine's living authority at the time.

For Sephtis, that call had been the student's first, made alone, uncertain, understanding only in the act of making it that this was the one reading no one could hand to anyone else. This time, she made it without that same trembling uncertainty -- not because the loss weighed any less, but because she had already learned, years earlier, exactly what the weight of that particular reading was supposed to feel like, and knew now how to carry it without being surprised by it.

The fourth-generation apprentice stood beside her this time, the way she had once stood alone. "Is it different," he asked, "the second time you have to do this?"

"Different how," she said, not unkindly.

"Easier?"

She thought about it honestly before answering, the way the ledger itself had taught three generations to answer honestly rather than smoothly. "Not easier. I know the shape of it now, so it doesn't ambush me the way it did the first time. That's not the same as easier. It's just familiar in a way that lets me do it properly instead of just surviving it."

The reading held clear and calm for the full window the rites required, the same sky, by the ledger's own record, that had held clear for Sephtis's burial decades earlier, though no one claimed that as anything more than coincidence, least of all the student herself.

Kanja stood at the water's edge through the whole of it, present the way he had been present for nearly every turn this lineage had taken across three generations, and thought of how much of this alias's whole history had been built from exactly this -- people teaching each other, across decades, how to be the one who reads the sky when someone they loved could no longer read it for themselves.

"Three generations," he said quietly, to no one in particular, watching the water take her out. "And two of them, now, I've watched bury the one before."
