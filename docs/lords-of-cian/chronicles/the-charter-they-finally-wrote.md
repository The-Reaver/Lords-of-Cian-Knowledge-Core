# The Charter They Finally Wrote

*Locked canon, Batch 240, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1056`). Captain Alias Chronicle LVIII, wave 20. Not a
territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Eight months after the surrender, Garren Hask called the dispute council back into session the way
he'd promised he would — once the peace had settled enough, he'd said, to see clearly what the coast
actually needed from a crew that no longer had an enemy telling it what to be. Nobody had forced the
question in the meantime. It had simply sat there, unresolved, while the coast rebuilt around them
and the crew kept doing the ordinary work of a crew without anyone deciding whether it was still
supposed to.

"We're not going to answer all of it today," Hask said, laying a blank ledger page on the table
instead of a finished one. "I don't think we're capable of that, and I don't think pretending
otherwise would serve anybody. But eight months is long enough to know a few things for certain, and
I'd rather write down what's certain than keep waiting for the parts that aren't."

Corren Halst went first, the way she usually did now. "Nobody's here because a war is making them
stay. That's been true for months and nobody's left. That's not nothing."

"Say it as a rule, then," Efa Gol said. "Not a feeling. A rule somebody new can read."

So they wrote it as a rule. Membership was chosen, not assigned, and stayed chosen — anyone could
leave at any time, for any reason or none, owing no explanation to the ledger or the council or to
Kanja personally, the same standing the crew had already extended to Maret Vos months before the war
even ended. The dispute council's authority stood on its own, independent of any war that had once
made it necessary, empowered to settle disputes, admit new members, and govern the ship's affairs
whether or not there was ever another enemy to justify it. And Kanja sat in council as one voting
seat among five, his word carrying no more formal weight than Hask's or Breck's or Halst's, a
standing the council had been training itself toward for years before it ever needed writing down.

"That's three things," Callum Breck said, reading it back. "There's a fourth nobody's said yet."

Kanja didn't wait for someone else to raise it. "What happens when the ones who remember why we
started this are gone, and I'm the only one left who was there." He said it plainly, the way he'd
learned to say the things that didn't have comfortable answers. "I don't have that one. I told you
that already, at the last meeting. I still don't have it."

"Then leave it blank," Hask said, and did — a fourth line on the page, headed and dated, with
nothing written under it. "Not every question gets an answer the day you're brave enough to ask it.
Some of them just get a place held for when you're ready."

The council signed the first three lines and left the fourth exactly as blank as Hask had made it,
and Hask filed the page in front of every ledger that came after, where the newest recruit and the
oldest hand would both see it before they saw anything else. It didn't resolve what the crew was for
in a world finally at peace — nobody in that room believed one page could. It gave the question
somewhere to live instead of nowhere, and that, Hask said, closing the session the way he closed
every entry he wasn't finished with, was as much honesty as any of them owed a page that wasn't done
being written.
