# The Days Without Wind

*Locked canon, Batch 192, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-561`). The Storm That Walks Alias Chronicle XVI, wave 6 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

Sephtis's forecast for the fortnight read, in its entirety: nothing. No system building, no front
moving, no pressure change worth marking on the chart. Flat air over flat water, as far out as his
method could reliably see.

The blockade runner Kanja needed to intercept had chosen its window for exactly that reason. Without
weather to hide inside or use as leverage, the crossing would be decided by oars, sails that couldn't
fill, and whichever side ran out of patience first.

"You've built your whole reputation on reading the sky," the sailing master said, watching the
becalmed water sit as still as poured glass. "What happens when the sky has nothing to say?"

"Then it's not my fight to win," Kanja said. "It's everyone else's. Mine included."

There was no storm to time an approach around, no driving rain to vanish into, no wind shear to turn
a chase in his favor. The interception became a rowed pursuit across nine hours of flat calm, hull
against hull, men taking turns at the oars until their hands split, the blockade runner's captain
gambling correctly that a fleet known for reading weather would be slower to adapt to a fight with no
weather in it at all.

He gambled correctly for most of the nine hours. Kanja's crew held the pace anyway — not through any
gear, any prediction, any advantage the alias was known for, but through the same plain endurance
that had gotten them onto the water in the first place. The runner's rowers broke first, arms giving
out an hour before Kanja's did.

"I kept waiting for the trick," the runner's captain admitted afterward, hands still raw from the
oars. "The storm you'd somehow already known was coming. It never came."

"Some days there isn't one," Kanja said. "The name people gave me was never really about the weather.
It was about not needing it to still win."
