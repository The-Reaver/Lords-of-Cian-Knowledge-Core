# The Trade They Wanted to Buy From Him

*Locked canon, Batch 279, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1458`). Blue-Collar Titan Alias Chronicle CI, wave 34. The alias's first cross-border technical-diplomacy register, extending the open-knowledge ethos already established at `MCD-1069` into a foreign-relations context for the first time.*

---

The envoys arrived under a neutral banner Efa Gol didn't recognize, three of them, dressed for travel rather than court, and their spokesman got to the point before he'd finished sitting down.

"We're prepared to pay in full for exclusive instruction," he said. "Your tunnel methods -- the shoring, the tactile reading, the ventilation work. Our settlement has lost people to collapses your methods would have prevented. We'll pay whatever figure makes it worth teaching us alone, and no one else."

Efa Gol, standing watch at the tent's entrance, caught Kanja's expression before he answered and recognized it as something closer to disappointment than interest.

"I'll teach your people gladly," Kanja said. "Not exclusively, and not for a price that buys silence along with the lesson."

The spokesman frowned. "You'd rather we shared it with rivals who might use it against us?"

"I'd rather nobody anywhere loses people to a collapse a lesson could have prevented, including whoever you're worried might learn it too." Kanja set down the level he'd been checking and gave the envoys his full attention. "The methods aren't mine to sell in the first place. I learned half of them from a tunnel digger who never asked me for a coin, and the other half from mistakes that cost real people real harm before anyone corrected them. Charging your settlement to keep them secret from someone else's would make me exactly the kind of tradesman I've spent this whole war refusing to be."

"Then what would you accept?"

"Send me people who'll actually learn it, not just watch me do it once and go home satisfied. I'll teach your engineers directly, the same way the Killane guild taught me -- hands on the tool, tested against real rock, not a lecture and a demonstration." Kanja glanced at Efa Gol, who was watching the exchange with the particular alertness of someone recalculating a threat assessment mid-conversation. "And when your people have it solid enough to teach their own apprentices, they teach it forward the same way, freely. That's the only price that actually matters to me."

The spokesman exchanged a look with his companions, clearly having arrived prepared for a negotiation over gold and finding himself in one over principle instead.

"Our council expected you to name a difficult price," he admitted. "Not an easy one you'd insist on anyway."

"It's not easy. It's just not the kind of difficult that gets solved with money." Kanja extended a hand. "Send your engineers. I'll send crew alongside them to make sure the teaching actually takes, and it costs your settlement nothing but the time it takes people to learn something properly."

Efa Gol waited until the envoys had left the tent before she spoke.

"You could have charged them," she said. "They'd have paid enormously, and it would have gone straight to the association's own funds."

"It would have. And every settlement that couldn't afford what this one could have gone on losing people to the same collapses, because the only lesson available with a price on it went to whoever could pay it first." Kanja watched the envoys' party head back toward the district border. "I've spent long enough building this trade to know exactly what it's worth. That's precisely why I won't sell exclusive rights to it. Worth that valuable doesn't belong locked behind one settlement's coin."
