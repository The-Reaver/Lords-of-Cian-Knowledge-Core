# The Seat They Offered Her

*Locked canon, Batch 286, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-1516`). Captain Alias Chronicle XCVI, wave 32, closing the wave. Mira is offered a formal dispute-council seat, extending her arc from operational courage (`MCD-1421`) into institutional life for the first time.*

---

They didn't ask her at the grain depot, in the noise of what she'd just done. Efa Gol waited a full week, until Mira had stopped checking the same truss twice out of habit, before bringing it to her plainly: the council wanted to offer her a seat.

Mira laughed, briefly and without much humor in it. "I'm twenty. I read a beam wrong for half a second better than anyone else standing there. That's not the same thing as knowing how a council should rule on anything."

"No," Efa Gol agreed. "It isn't. Nobody's saying otherwise." She set down the ledger page she'd been holding — Garren Hask's old hand on it, one of the last pages he'd filled before his heart stopped keeping time. "But you carved two names into that wall with your own hands. You know exactly what this crew owes people, because you've paid a piece of that debt yourself, at an age most of the founding generation hadn't even joined yet."

Kanja found her at the seawall that evening, where she went most evenings now, though nobody had ever asked her to. "You don't have to say yes," he told her. "The seat isn't a debt you owe for surviving what happened to your father."

"I know that." She didn't look away from the wall. "That's actually what's making it hard. If it was owed, I'd know what to do with it. This is just — offered. Because they think I'm ready, not because anyone's keeping score of what I lost."

It was Callum Breck, newly seated himself and still testing the shape of the chair, who found the answer that let her say yes without pretending the hesitation away. He proposed something the charter had never needed before: a seat held in observance for a full year before it carried a vote — not a lesser seat, just an honestly slower one, the same principle the crew had always applied to every new hand it ever trusted, extended for the first time to someone the crew had also, in a real sense, raised.

Mira took it on those terms. "A year of watching before I get to decide anything," she said. "That sounds right. That sounds like how everyone else here actually earned it too."
