# What the Captain's Son Remembered

*Locked canon, Batch 200, 2026-09-11 (`MCD-818`). The Scourge Alias Chronicle XXXIII, wave 11 of the ten-wave sixth-through-fifteenth run. Not a territory Chronicle. Narrated in neutral third-person prose.*

---

The man had never met the Scourge and had spent most of his life certain he never wanted to, because
the story he'd grown up with wasn't one of rescue or reputation but of his own father, standing on a
burning deck sixty years past, choosing a lit spar over surrender and living, afterward, as a man who
never once spoke of that night as anything but a humiliation he hadn't chosen for himself.

"He told it different every time," the son said, an old man himself now, to a trader passing through
who'd made the mistake of mentioning the Scourge's name within his hearing. "Some nights it was the
ghost who broke him. Some nights it was that the ghost refused to even give him the dignity of
begging him to live. He never once told it the way I think it actually happened, which is that
somebody looked at a man about to do something irreversible and just — didn't make him do it alone."

"You don't blame the Scourge, then."

"I did, for years. Blamed him for taking something from my father that I couldn't name, until I got
old enough myself to understand my father had already lost that thing long before the fire — the
trade had cost him whatever he needed to feel right about himself, and losing it in front of a
stranger just made the cost visible for the first time." He said it slowly, working through a
grievance he'd clearly turned over more than once across a long life. "The ghost didn't ruin my
father. My father's own choices ruined my father. The ghost just happened to be standing there when
the bill finally came due."

"Would you thank him, if you ever met him?"

The old man considered the question longer than the trader expected. "I don't know that thanks is the
right shape for it. I'd want him to know my father lived another thirty years after that night, badly
for a while, better toward the end, and died in a bed instead of on a burning deck — and that I don't
know if that's a mercy the ghost meant to give him or just a thing that happened to fall out of a
choice made for entirely different reasons." He shrugged, an old grievance settling into something
closer to plain fact than anger. "Either way. It's mine to carry now, not his to answer for. That's
how debts like that usually end up, I've found — passed down to whoever's left holding them, whether
the one who caused them ever meant to hand them over or not."
