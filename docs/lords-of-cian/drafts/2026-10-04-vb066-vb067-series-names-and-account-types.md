**VB-066**. Series names. "Chronicle" is reserved as a series name for Kanja Haku Rexmar's narrative tracks: his Alias entries, the manuscript Chronicles I-VIII, and his Kanja-version track (VB-026, VB-062, VB-063), whose "Kanja Chronicle" prefix keeps its numerals distinct from the manuscript's; the withdrawn Chronicles IX-XI (replaced by MCD-334 through MCD-336) are excluded from that numbering. In-world documents and in-world usage (ASH-047's Purge Chronicles, a "chronicler") are unaffected. Every other protagonist's series takes a name drawn from its own character or narrator, or, for the homage-era territories, the shared name Annals. Lauris Letitia: the Records (Fermand transcribing her literal archive, MCD-211, VB-064). Daba: the Rolls (named for the private list of names Daba keeps, begun with the Rookery's dead and carrying every name 1804's doctrine has cost since, MCD-1610 and its entry docs/lords-of-cian/chronicles/daba-chronicle-xl-the-tally-he-keeps-alone.md; distinct from the Rookery's rolls Odalys kept, MCD-1572). Ozmund Verehimu: the Testaments (Red Beard's telling of the General's account, VB-022, CC-020, MCD-1730). Ezio Valcari: the Exhibits (his investigative role, VB-020, ARS-405, taking its name from the "exhibit" frame his Game Plan floated; the exhibit-fragment opener itself stays optional). Anirak: the Collections (from her own Debt Collection, ARS-440). The homage-era territories: the Annals, titled by territory (Xaragua Annals, Kazi Annals), Arturo Salvatierra Duho's entries sitting within the Xaragua Annals and Tunji's and Femi's within the Kazi Annals. A single entry is a Record, a Roll, a Testament, an Exhibit, a Collection, or an Annals entry (titled, e.g., Xaragua Annals III). Series names are reader-facing titles, not in-world documents, except where a locked rule already frames the series as an in-world artifact (Lauris's archive, MCD-211, VB-064); the Rolls in particular are not an in-world roster (MCD-1569, MCD-1619). A non-Kanja protagonist launched later is given a series name at the Game Plan step of the launch gate. The Character Chronicle Launch Protocol is renamed the Series Launch Protocol, its three steps unchanged. The rename is carried in the same run of batches to every rule statement, category tag, entry file name, profile, tracker row, and project doc that states the old series names. Historical batch notes and Abad's verbatim approval quotes keep their original wording, with only file paths updated; source-document titles, `source` fields, the World Adaptation Blueprint's own "chronicle" (MCD-148 through MCD-217), and the docs/lords-of-cian/chronicles/ folder name keep their wording. The Drive mirror and the archive app's copy are synced afterward.

**VB-067**. Account types and reliability. Each protagonist's series is to be complemented over time by at least one entry of each further account type available in that protagonist's world and consistent with that protagonist's locked rules. (1) The Series: the protagonist's own named series (VB-066), reliable whoever narrates it. For Kanja, the Series is his Alias entries, the manuscript Chronicles I-VIII, and the Kanja-version track (VB-026), which is the home of his Onyx accounts (VB-062); its Long Mask Onyx accounts are reconstructed retrospectively (VB-062, ARS-437). Types (2) through (5) are entries outside the Series. (2) A Comrade Account: told by an ally or crew member, reliable in good faith and bounded by what the teller saw and knew; where a locked rule records the teller as mistaken or misled (e.g., Draconis, MCD-1730, CC-085), that belief is locked as the teller's belief, not as fact; what the teller relays secondhand is locked only as said. (3) An Adversary Account: unreliable by interest, told by an enemy and bent by what that enemy needs to be true. (4) A Dossier: unreliable by design, an institutional record carrying that institution's standing misreadings -- the Sealbound Directorate (MCD-1727) or the Sovereign Trust (as with its false Rookery record, MCD-1566) on Cian, the hostile institutions of a protagonist's own world elsewhere (MCD-313). The type governs entries, not source documents titled as dossiers. (5) Hearsay: unreliable by vantage, told at a remove by civilians, bystanders, or neutral parties, named or unnamed, through rumor, distance, and legend's drift. Every teller, in a Series or outside it, is bounded by what they could know: a statement, true or false, that a locked rule places beyond that teller's knowledge or attribution (e.g., CC-027, CC-085, MCD-1093, MCD-1569, MCD-1881) may not be made. Types (2) through (5) inherit the drafting conventions of the Series they complement (e.g., PH2-048's unnamed-guest convention for the Annals); for Kanja, VB-063 governs wherever Onyx appears. Types (2) through (5) are titled by type and subject (e.g., "An Adversary Account: Daba") and numbered separately from the Series, and sit behind that protagonist's Series Launch Protocol gate. The frame test applies only to entries outside the Series: an entry filed in a Series is reliable whatever its frame, and an entry meant to be unreliable is never filed in a Series. Outside the Series, account type is set by an entry's frame -- framed as a character's own telling, or as a document within the fiction -- not by whose point of view its narration follows. Reliability attaches to the narration: what characters say, believe, or perceive, and any document quoted inside an entry, is locked only as said, believed, or written, unless the narration or a locked rule confirms it. An unreliable account never establishes a new canon fact: what it states is locked only as what was said, and by whom, and every point where it departs from the truth must trace to a locked rule or a reliable account that holds the truth, a trace the Connective-Tissue Gate checks. No account type relaxes a reserved rule's drafting restrictions (e.g., MCD-314, ARS-437, MCD-1569): what a reserved rule forbids drafted material to assert, imply, foreshadow, or reference, no teller may; a deliberately open question may be repeated as rumor only in forms its reserved rule permits. Nor may an unreliable account add new facts about its own teller's institution; such facts need their own locked rules. From this rule's lock onward, each new entry's header labels its account type and its teller ("teller: none" for close-third or neutral narration); an entry outside any Series with no teller (close-third or neutral narration, e.g., the interstitials MCD-1621, MCD-1622) is reliable under the narration rule and labeled "account type: none"; the narrative never labels its own reliability. Entries locked before this rule are read as reliable under the narration rule above (the Series, or account type none for the interstitials) unless a locked rule marks a claim as contested or false (as SBD-041, SBD-045, and CC-136 do for SBD files), and are not relabeled; where such entries conflict, the reconciliation Abad rules on prevails. Extends and qualifies VB-062: a Comrade Account where a locked rule records the teller as mistaken, or in what it relays secondhand, an Adversary Account, a Dossier, or Hearsay may state what is false, locked only as what was said and by whom.

VB-062 amendment (same batch): append "Qualified by VB-067: a Comrade Account where a locked rule records the teller as mistaken, or in what it relays secondhand, an Adversary Account, a Dossier, or Hearsay may state what is false; such a statement is locked only as what was said and by whom, and its departure from the truth traces to a locked rule or reliable account. Every teller is bounded by what they could know, and no teller may assert, imply, foreshadow, or reference what a reserved rule forbids."
