## (a) Proposed rule

**VB-068**. The account craft standard. From this rule's lock onward, every new entry outside a
Series (each Comrade Account, Adversary Account, Dossier, Hearsay entry, and entry of account type
none, `VB-067`) is drafted to the standard at docs/lords-of-cian/voice/account-craft-standard.md,
and so is every new scene inside any entry in which a character tells a story aloud. Each new
entry's header carries its account type and its teller as `VB-067` sets out. Entries locked before
`VB-067` are not relabeled. Before drafting a Comrade Account, an Adversary Account, or a Hearsay
entry, the writer fixes its teller state (informed, uninformed, mistaken, or deliberately lying; a
Comrade Account never deliberately lying, `VB-067`), an identified listener (named, or identified
by description where a locked rule keeps them unnamed, `PH2-048`, `MCD-1093`), and its setting
module (or, where no module fits, the setting stated, S1-S3 applied, the nearest module used as a
guide). In a Hearsay entry with more than one teller, each teller's state and listener are fixed;
the header names every teller. The page shows the teller state and the listener, and no label names
either. Both are none for an entry of account type none, and neither applies to a Dossier. A
storytelling scene inside any entry fixes its teller state, its listener, and its setting only,
since account types govern entries and never scenes. A lie or a misreading is shown through the
pattern of the telling or through contradiction with outside fact or locked canon; an honest error
through contradiction alone. None of them is ever shown through gaze, fidgeting, or any other
body-language tell. Honest drift runs in memory's directions (rounder, larger, more famous, more
vivid), within `VB-067`'s Comrade bound. A coordinated Comrade story must still be true. A liar's
drift runs toward his own defense. A Hearsay entry makes its number of removes legible. No account,
Series narration included, states a fact about an institution that no locked rule holds; such a
fact is drafted as its own rule. A fact about an institution here means a fact about a standing
structure, office, form, procedure, vocabulary, or practice of a named institution, as distinct
from an event involving one. This institution bar is checked in every entry's Connective-Tissue
review, Series entries included. An unreliable teller's false claim about an institution is locked
only as said (`VB-067`), and its trace is a locked rule that holds the truth. The standard governs
how an account is told. What an account may say is set by `VB-067` (its reliability rules, its
Comrade Account bounds, its knowledge bound, its trace rule, and its reserved-rule bar), by this
rule's institution bar, and by the Voice Bible's Pillars, hard constraints, exclusion list, and
track voice rulings (`VB-063` for Onyx, `VB-022` for the Testaments, `VB-064` for the Records,
`VB-065` for the Collections, `VB-020` for the close-third Rolls and Annals, and `CC-034` with
`VB-024` for the Exhibits). Where the standard and any of these differ, the locked rule controls.
The standard's research evidence grades are craft guidance and assert nothing in-world. Its
proposed venue and vocabulary names are usable only after each is locked by its own rule. Its
section 7 checklist is part of the Connective-Tissue Gate's independent review for every entry and
scene it covers. This rule's approval covers the standard's handling of the part (c) items on
greetings carried by action (R0.2) and on spoken Spanish (M1) only once Abad rules on those items;
until then those clauses are pending his ruling. The standard's comic registers (R0.4, R15, R18,
M1, M4, M7, M9) are written through irony and understatement only (`VB-004`) until Abad rules on
the part (c) item on comic registers. A change to the standard needs Abad's approval
in his own words, like a change to any rule. Extends `VB-067`; qualifies nothing in it.

## (b) Naming table

**How each name was checked** (re-run 2026-10-04, against `ledger_version` 37.6, 2,693 rules).
- **L and C counts.** Each candidate was matched case-insensitively on word boundaries against
  every rule statement in `canon-ledger.json` and every file under `docs/lords-of-cian/chronicles/`
  (subfolders included). A space in a multi-word candidate matches a space or a hyphen; a hyphen in
  a hyphenated candidate matches only a hyphen. **L** counts matching rule statements. **C** counts
  matching entry files. C was counted against the folder as it stood on 2026-10-04: 1,508 files,
  before the renames that move non-Kanja series files to Record, Roll, Testament, Exhibit,
  Collection, and Annals names. The renames change file names and headers, so C should be re-run
  once they land.
- **Near-collisions.** A near-collision is a capitalized name in either corpus that shares the
  candidate's first letter and sits within edit distance 2 of it (for a multi-word candidate, of
  any one of its words). For words of four letters or fewer, distance 2 matches most short names,
  so only one-edit matches count there. Names counted are in-world proper names and words inside a
  locked proper name (a person, place, ship, institution, artifact, ability, technique, era, or
  event). Not counted: entry titles, rank and stage labels (Tier 2, Stage 3), and ordinary words
  capitalized at the start of a sentence. Real-world homage anchors named inside `PH2-` rules
  (Raby, Staten, Sandino, Brazier and the like) are not in-world names and are not listed, except
  where a candidate relies on one.
- **Also noted.** Overlaps the method does not catch (a different first letter, a name contained
  inside the candidate, a word the candidate shares with a locked name, a two-edit match on a short
  word, a sound-alike, a real place) are listed under "Also noted" or "Real place."
- "Generic" means every hit is the ordinary English word, never a proper name.
- "Clean" means L0, C0, and no near-collision by the method.

**Constraints applied.**
- **Mainline.** Germanic or industrial register. The words listed as taken or overloaded in
  `00-canon-venue-inventory.md` §10 are avoided: Ledger, Table, Hollow, Crucible, Exchange,
  Compact, Quiet, Gallery, Hall, Mirror, Knot, Vise, Rest, Slab, Pit, Circuit, Cut, Standard,
  Register, Stone, and the Anchor and Anvil. Also avoided:
  - the banned words of `VB-013` and `VB-050`
  - "Cian"
  - Iron and Rust, Onyx's verdict words
  - Crow (the Crow King)
  - Long (Long Mask, Long Walk, Long Currents, Long Pursuit, Long Correction, Long Tenure)
  - Vigil (the Vigil Standard)
  - Deposition (`WC-020`)
  - Cinder (Cinderhilt, `MCD-1729`)
  - Marker (the Marker Rebellion)
  - Shape (Shaper)
- **Homage World** (`PH2-034`). Invented names drawn from real Taíno, Yoruba, Akan, Kikongo,
  Nahuatl, or Swahili vocabulary. No word-initial clusters English doesn't use (Mb-, Mw-, Ml-,
  Ms-, Mk-, Nz-, Ng-), no Spanish (`PH2-061`'s colonial-name reading), and no real-world proper
  noun used as an in-world name. Every row flags real-place homonyms, and overlaps with existing
  PH2 names (Guaní/Guanín, Kasa/Kasi, Tunji/Tunde are the standing examples). Names already taken
  in PH2 were rejected outright:
  - Kesho, Bohio, and Ile (`PH2-` supporting casts)
  - Kiti (a territory)
  - Batey (the city)
  - Kalunga (`MCD-095`)
- **Homage street terms.** These are street vocabulary, not replacements. Plain English nouns (bar,
  barbershop, runner, corner) stay usable in narration; the terms below are what the people
  themselves say.

**What the re-run changed.** Several rows earlier marked clean are not clean by the method:
the Slack Hawser (Stack), the Last Bell (the Belly), the Call Yard (Yara), the Copper Wheel
(Cooper), roof-boy, chanuo, pembe and oriki. The Tallow Lamp shares "Tallow" with a locked place
name. Several counts were wrong and are corrected below. Three short words (igun, ogba, ofo) fall
under the four-letter rule, so their two-edit matches move to "Also noted."

   "no formal rites borrowed from any Trust or Kingdom." The Chronicle locked at `MCD-1073` has
   Kanja speak "the words of the rite himself" at sea. The two are compatible if those words are the

   VIII; the header of Kanja Chronicle I) and has no ledger rule. A lock would let the standard cite
   it by rule.

   *el que no necesita nada*, while `PH2-061` frames Spanish as the imposed colonial tongue and
